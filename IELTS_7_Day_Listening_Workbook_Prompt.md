# Prompt: Create an IELTS 7-Day Listening Spelling Workbook

Create a comprehensive, practical **7-day IELTS Listening Spelling Practice Workbook** for a student who frequently makes spelling mistakes in IELTS Listening.

## Main objective

Help the learner recognise, remember, and correctly spell commonly confused English words—especially words that sound similar but have different endings.

The workbook should be suitable for independent daily practice and should include explanations, word banks, exercises, dictation practice, and answer keys.

## Required content

### Day 1: -ant vs -ent
Teach common words ending in **-ant** and **-ent**. Include a broad word list, meanings, example sentences, commonly confused pairs, listening-style gap fills, and dictation.

### Day 2: -ance vs -ence
Teach words ending in **-ance** and **-ence**. Include vocabulary lists, word families, meanings, examples, IELTS-style sentence-completion questions, and dictation.

### Day 3: -able vs -ible
Explain common spelling patterns for **-able** and **-ible**. Provide examples, meanings, and focused exercises.

### Day 4: Other commonly confused endings
Cover useful patterns such as:
- -ary vs -ery
- -ory vs -ery
- -tion vs -sion
- -cial vs -tial
- Other relevant IELTS Listening spelling traps.

Include examples, short explanations, and practice questions.

### Day 5: Double letters, silent letters, and vowel confusion
Cover double consonants, silent letters, single vs double vowels, confusing letter sequences, and words often misspelled after hearing them. Include exercises and dictation.

### Day 6: IELTS Listening vocabulary in context
Provide useful vocabulary and realistic sentence-completion exercises in contexts such as:
- Education
- Work and employment
- Accommodation
- Travel and transport
- Health and fitness
- Environment
- Research and technology
- Places, facilities, and services

### Day 7: Mixed spelling assessment
Create a final assessment covering all patterns from the week. Include gap-fill questions, short dictation passages, a review checklist, and a personal mistake bank.

## Special section: Singular and plural forms

Include nouns whose singular and plural forms are identical, for example:
- sheep
- deer
- fish
- aircraft
- species
- series
- means

Also include common irregular plurals (child/children, person/people) and uncountable nouns commonly encountered in listening tests. Explain how context and grammar help identify the correct form.

## Exercise design

For each day:
1. Explain the spelling pattern in simple language.
2. Give a substantial vocabulary list.
3. Provide short, natural example sentences.
4. Include listening-style gap-fill exercises.
5. Include a dictation activity.
6. Provide an answer key.

Do not place answers immediately beside the questions. Put answer keys in a separate section so the learner can attempt the exercises first.

## Formatting and presentation

- Create a polished, printable A4 PDF workbook.
- Use clear section headings and consistent spacing.
- Make it easy to follow for independent study.
- Include page numbers.
- Use readable tables or organised word lists.
- Avoid unnecessary decoration.
- Make the answer key easy to locate.

## Large-print edition

For a large-print version:
- Use a smooth, readable font such as DejaVu Sans.
- Use approximately **16–17 pt** for main body text.
- Use approximately **17–21 pt** for headings.
- Keep supporting text comfortably readable.
- Preserve all exercises, word lists, and answer keys.
- Allow more pages rather than shrinking the text.
- Keep tables structured and readable wherever possible.

## Quality checks

Before delivering:
- Check spelling in every word list and exercise.
- Ensure every answer key matches its question.
- Avoid accidental duplicates unless repetition is intentional for revision.
- Make sure examples are grammatically correct and natural.
- Ensure the workbook is practical for a seven-day IELTS Listening spelling routine.
