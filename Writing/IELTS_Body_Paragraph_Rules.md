# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Body Paragraph Rules

> **Goal:** Band 7+ with body paragraphs that are clear, natural and academic.
> **Use it for:** every essay type. The Agree/Disagree, Advantages/Disadvantages and Discuss Both Views guides still decide your fixed sentence openers.
> **Most important idea:** these are guides, not a test. If following a rule makes a paragraph sound robotic, keep the paragraph natural.

---

## 1. The Memory Card

```text
PEEL    P  Point     Your idea, as the topic sentence.
        E  Explain   Why is it true? How does it happen?
        E  Example   One real situation that shows the explanation.
        L  Link      Back to your topic sentence or introduction.

PEERL   P  Point
        E  Explain
        E  Example
        R  Result    What happens because of this?
           or
           Response  What does the other side say, and why is my side stronger?
        L  Link

LENGTH  90-120 words. Aim for 100-120.
ONE RULE TO REMEMBER: every sentence continues the one before it.
```

---

## 2. The 10 Main Rules

| # | Rule | Remember |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Length** | At least 90 words, at most 120 words |
| 2 | **Point → Explain → Example** | The example must be based on the explanation |
| 3 | **Result or Response** | Add one when it helps, between the example and the Link |
| 4 | **Link** | Return to your introduction point or your topic sentence |
| 5 | **Structure** | A clear structure supports your Task Response and Coherence marks |
| 6 | **Sentence variety** | Mix sentence types for Grammatical Range |
| 7 | **Long sentences** | Use long sentences that you can control |
| 8 | **Idea Bank ideas** | Take your ideas from the topic-wise Idea Bank PDF |
| 9 | **Idea Bank sentences** | Use its exact sentences when they fit your paragraph |
| 10 | **Natural vocabulary** | Avoid too much repetition, but never sound robotic |

### Rule 1 — Length: 90 to 120 words
- Aim for **100–120 words**. Treat 90 as your safe minimum.
- **Under 90?** Add one more Explain sentence by asking *"why?"* or *"what happens next?"*.
- **Over 120?** Shorten the example. It should be one sentence, not a story.
- **Check your total too.** The whole essay must reach **250 words**. If your bodies are only 90 words each, your introduction and conclusion must add at least 70 words together.

### Rule 2 — The example must be based on the explanation
Your topic sentence gives the Point, the next sentence explains it, and the example shows that explanation happening.

| ❌ Same topic, different idea | ✅ Based on the explanation |
|---|---|
| **Explain:** People must pay bills online. **Example:** *A school teaches children to protect their passwords.* | **Explain:** People must pay bills online. **Example:** *A person who never learned computer skills has to queue at an office to pay an electricity bill.* |

**The 5-second test:** read the Explain and Example sentences together. If the same situation is not in both, change the example.

If you write two Explain sentences, the example should show the **second** one, because it comes right before it.

### Rule 3 — Result or Response
Choose by what the paragraph needs:

| Choose | When | How it starts |
|---|---|---|
| **Result** | You want to show what the example leads to | *As a result, …* · *…, and this …* · *…, which …* |
| **Response** | The other side is strong, and you can answer it easily | *Although some people argue that …, [my side].* |
| **Neither** | The paragraph is already complete | Go straight to the Link |

- A short result can sit at the end of the example (*…, which makes life abroad less lonely*). You do not always need a separate sentence.
- A Response answers the other side, so it does not have to come from your example. It must still be about the same question.
- Give the other side **one clause only**, and put your side after the comma.
- Never write *but* after *Although*.

### Rule 4 — The Link
End by returning to your **introduction point** or your **topic sentence**.

- Use general words. Do not copy the Point again.
- Add **no new idea**. A new solution or a new argument in the last sentence has no explanation behind it.

| ❌ Brings a new idea | ✅ Returns to the Point |
|---|---|
| **Point:** Celebrities' families are harmed by the press. **Link:** *The best approach is to create new privacy laws.* | **Point:** Celebrities' families are harmed by the press. **Link:** *Consequently, the press should keep celebrities' children out of its stories.* |

### Rule 5 — Focus on structure, because it supports your score
Structure alone does not earn a band score, but it makes your good ideas easy to follow.

| Band 7 criterion | How the body paragraph helps |
|---|---|
| **Task Response** | Your ideas answer the question, and they are explained and supported. The band 7 description warns about supporting ideas that *"lack focus"*, which is why Rule 2 matters so much. |
| **Coherence & Cohesion** | Each paragraph has one clear central topic, and each sentence follows from the one before it. |

### Rule 6 — Sentence variety
Mix these naturally. You do not need all of them in every paragraph.

| Type | Pattern | Where it fits well |
|---|---|---|
| **Long simple** | *[long subject] + verb + object* | Link |
| **Compound** | *…; as a result, …* · *…, and …* | Explain |
| **Complex** | *When …, …* · *…, which …* · *Although …, …* | Explain, Response |
| **Compound-complex** | *a … who …, and this …* | Example |

**Accuracy comes first.** A correct compound sentence scores higher than a broken complex sentence. Use the structures you have practised.

### Rule 7 — Long sentences that you can control
- Most of your sentences should be **about 15–30 words**.
- Over 35 words, a sentence usually needs splitting, because this is where mistakes begin.
- A long sentence is good only if it stays correct and clear. The examiner rewards control, not length itself.
- One shorter sentence is fine when it makes your point stronger.

### Rule 8 — Pick your ideas from the Idea Bank
Your ideas are in `Writing/Output/T2_XX_<Topic>_Idea_Bank.pdf`.

| You need | Look in |
|---|---|
| Main ideas | Part 2D *Quick Idea Bank* |
| Explanations and examples | Part 3 *Category-Wise Free Writing* |
| Collocations | Part 4 *Vocabulary & Collocation Bank* |
| Sentence patterns | Part 5 *Sentence Structure Toolkit* |

Choose the idea that you can **explain and give an example for**, not the one that sounds most impressive. Simple ideas are enough for band 7.

### Rule 9 — Use exact Idea Bank sentences when they fit
- If a sentence fits your Point and your structure, use it exactly.
- If it nearly fits, **change the words to fit the question**. A sentence that does not answer the question earns nothing, however good it is.
- Use a few sentences or phrases in a paragraph, not the whole paragraph.
- Always write the Link and the connection between sentences yourself, because they must fit your own essay.

### Rule 10 — Avoid repetition, but stay natural
- Do not check every sentence for repeated words. That makes writing slow and robotic.
- **Topic words may repeat.** In an essay about technology, you can say *technology* several times.
- Change a word only when you have used it too often and a natural choice exists. *Reference words* also help: *this, these, such, they*.
- Do not choose a rare word that you are not sure about. Formal and easy to understand is the target.

## 3. Worked Examples

**Question:** *Technology helps our modern life. Do you agree or disagree?*
**My plan:** Agree → Idea 1: makes life convenient → Idea 2: makes communication easier.
🟩 marks an exact Idea Bank sentence from Chapter 02 Technology.

### PEEL paragraph (110 words)

| Part | Sentence |
|---|---|
| **Point** | The main reason why I agree with this idea is that technology has made everyday tasks far more convenient. |
| **Explain** | 🟩 Instead of standing in long queues and filling in piles of paper forms, citizens can apply for documents online, pay bills and check information from their phones. |
| **Explain** | This saves time for people, who can use those hours for work, study or family. |
| **Example** | For example, a worker who once lost half a day paying a bill at a bank can now pay it by phone in two minutes, and this makes the working day less stressful. |
| **Link** | Therefore, technology has clearly made modern life easier, because ordinary tasks now demand far less effort. |

**Why it works:** *pay bills* and *saves time* in the explanation become *paying a bill* and *two minutes* in the example, and the Link returns to *convenient* in new words.

### PEERL paragraph with a Response (107 words)

| Part | Sentence |
|---|---|
| **Point** | Another important reason for my agreement is that technology has made communication much easier. |
| **Explain** | When friends and relatives live far apart, 🟩 smartphones, messaging apps, social media and video calls allow people to stay in touch instantly and almost free of charge. |
| **Example** | For instance, a Bangladeshi worker in Dubai who calls home by video every evening can see their children and help with homework, which makes life abroad less lonely. |
| **Response** | Although some people argue that online contact is less warm than a real meeting, it is far better than the silence that long distance once caused. |
| **Link** | Consequently, technology has brought people closer together, whatever the distance between them. |

### The same paragraph with a Result (98 words)
Keep the other sentences and replace the Response with this:

> **Result:** As a result, families stay close across borders, and long periods apart become much easier to bear.

## 4. Extra Rules I Added

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | **One main idea in one paragraph** | The examiner should be able to say what the paragraph is about in a few words. Advantages/Disadvantages paragraphs are the exception, with two short points each. |
| 12 | **Support your opinion in every sentence** | A paragraph must not drift towards the other side, except in one short Response clause. Discuss Both Views essays are the exception: BP1 reports the other view on purpose, as *their* belief, not yours. |
| 13 | **Keep your introduction's promise** | Body paragraph 1 develops your first idea from the introduction, and body paragraph 2 develops the second, in the same order. In Discuss Both Views essays, BP1 develops the other view and BP2 develops your view, in the order of your introduction. |
| 14 | **Use realistic examples, not fake facts** | *A worker who pays a bill by phone* is safe. Never invent a statistic, a study or an expert. |
| 15 | **Use one linker in a sentence** | Never stack them (*~~Moreover, in addition,~~*), and do not start every sentence with one. |
| 16 | **Be careful with big claims** | *Many people may* is more academic and more accurate than *everybody always*. Use *may, can, often, many*. |
| 17 | **Plan the example first** | In your plan, write the idea and its example, then write the explanation that leads to it. This is the easiest way to keep them matched. |

## 5. How Strict Are These Rules?

You do not need to tick every rule in every paragraph.

| Try to keep every time | Use when it fits |
|---|---|
| One clear Point as the topic sentence | Result or Response |
| An explanation of that Point | A second Explain sentence |
| An example based on that explanation | Exact Idea Bank sentences |
| A Link with no new idea | Every sentence type in Rule 6 |
| 90–120 words | Less common words, if you are sure of them |

**If two rules conflict,** choose this order:
1. Answer the question clearly.
2. Keep your sentences correct.
3. Keep the paragraph natural and easy to read.
4. Then add variety and advanced language.

## 6. How This Works With Your Essay Guides

| Essay type | Body paragraph shape | Words in each body |
|---|---|---|
| **Agree / Disagree** | BP1 P E E X L · BP2 P E X R L, with the openers in the [Agree/Disagree guide](IELTS_Agree_Disagree_Essay_Guide.md) | 90 is your exam minimum. **Model answers use 100–120.** |
| **Advantages / Disadvantages** | 7 sentences with two points, as in the [Advantages/Disadvantages guide](IELTS_Advantages_Disadvantages_Essay_Guide.md) | **120–135**, because each paragraph has two points. This is the one exception to the 120-word limit. |
| **Discuss Both Views** | BP1 (the other view) P E E X L · BP2 (your view) P E X R L, with the openers in the [Discuss Both Views guide](IELTS_Discuss_Both_Views_Essay_Guide.md) | 90–120. **Aim for about 100**, because the whole essay stays under 300 words. |
| **Cause/Problem + Solution** | BP1 P E X P E L · BP2 Solution E X Solution E L, with the openers in the [Cause/Problem + Solution guide](IELTS_Cause_Problem_Solution_Essay_Guide.md) | 90–120. **Aim for about 105**, because the whole essay stays between 260 and 300 words. Each paragraph has two points, as an exception to Rule 11. |
| **Double Question** | List block P E X P E L · Opinion block P E X R L, chosen for each question, with the openers in the [Double Question guide](IELTS_Double_Question_Essay_Guide.md) | 90–120. **Aim for about 105**, because the whole essay stays between 260 and 300 words. A List block has two points, as an exception to Rule 11. |
| **Other essay types** | PEEL or PEERL from this file | 90–120 |

## 7. 30-Second Check Before You Move On

- [ ] Can I say this paragraph's main idea in a few words?
- [ ] Does the example show the explanation right before it?
- [ ] Does the Link return to my Point or introduction, with no new idea?
- [ ] Does the paragraph support my opinion all the way through?
- [ ] Is it about 90–120 words?
- [ ] Does it read naturally when I say it quietly in my head?
