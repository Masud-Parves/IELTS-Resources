# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Discuss Both Views Essay Guide

> **Goal:** Band 7+ with one fixed structure you can remember under exam pressure.
> **Length:** minimum **260 words**, **always under 300**, target **about 280 words**.
> **Rule:** same 4 paragraphs every time. **The other view first, your view last. One clean opinion, never a partial answer.**
> **Ideas:** simple ideas from your topic-wise idea bank, developed well.

---

## 1. The Memory Card (learn this first)

```text
ORDER   Other view first, my view last, in every paragraph of the essay.
LENGTH  260-299 words. Plan: 40 + 100 + 100 + 40 = 280.

INTRO   (35-45)    [General statement: optional, see Section 6]
                   While some people argue that OTHER VIEW, others believe that MY VIEW.
                   I completely support the latter view because MAIN REASON.

BP1     (90-120)   THE OTHER VIEW. Report it fairly. Do not write "I".
                   P  On the one hand, people who support ... argue that ...
                   E  This is because ... / Using ..., ...
                   E  ...; as a result, ...
                   X  For example, a ... who ..., and this ...
                   L  For this reason, many people believe that ...

BP2     (90-120)   MY VIEW.
                   P  On the other hand, I firmly believe that ... because MAIN REASON.
                   E  When ..., ...
                   X  For instance, a ... who ..., which ...
                   R  Although some people argue that [BP1's point], [my side].
                   L  Consequently, ...

CONCL   (35-45)    In conclusion, people hold different views on whether ... or ...
                   While OTHER VIEW, I firmly believe that MY VIEW because MAIN REASON.
```

**Same shape as your Agree/Disagree essay:** BP1 is P E E X L and BP2 is P E X R L, so you do not have to learn a new paragraph plan.

**The one big difference:** in this essay, BP1 explains the view you do **not** support.

## 2. Know the Question Type (30 seconds)

| Use this guide when the question | Do **not** use this guide when it says |
|---|---|
| Gives two views and says *Discuss both views/sides and give your opinion.* | *To what extent do you agree or disagree?* after **one** claim (Agree/Disagree guide) |
| Gives two views and asks *What is your opinion?* | *What is your opinion?* after **one** claim (Agree/Disagree guide) |
| Gives two views and asks *Do you agree or disagree?* (HOU-02) | *Discuss the advantages and disadvantages.* (Advantages & Disadvantages guide) |
| Gives **one** statement and says *Discuss both views* (LEI-02) | *Globalisation has both advantages and disadvantages. Discuss both and give your opinion.* (SOC-05, Advantages & Disadvantages guide) |

**The test:** can you finish the sentence *"Some people think ___, while others think ___"*? If yes, use this guide.

### When the views are not clear

Sometimes the question does not state both views clearly. Write out both views before you plan.

| Question | View 1 | View 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **LEI-02:** *Having more money and less free time is better than earning less money and having more free time.* | More money matters more | More free time matters more |
| **MED-01:** *Some people think it is good, while others think there are disadvantages to this.* | Sports sponsorship is good | Sports sponsorship has disadvantages |

## 3. Task Response — Your First Target

The band 7 Task Response descriptor asks you to address the main parts of the question, give *"a clear and developed position"*, and extend and support your main ideas. In this essay, that means four jobs:

| # | Job | Where you do it | If you miss it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Discuss the **first** view | One body paragraph | Part of the question is not answered |
| 2 | Discuss the **second** view | The other body paragraph | Part of the question is not answered |
| 3 | Give **your opinion**, the same one every time | Introduction, BP2, conclusion | Your position is unclear |
| 4 | **Develop** both views with an explanation and an example | BP1 and BP2 | Ideas stay as a list |

**Answer the exact question, not the general topic.** EDU-19 asks where schools should **spend money**. An essay about whether technology is good for children is on the right topic, but it does not answer that question. Before you finish each body paragraph, ask: *does this still answer what the question asked?*

### A clean opinion is safe

Choosing one side does not make your essay one-sided, because BP1 still discusses the other view in full. **Discussing a view is not the same as agreeing with it.** That is why you can write a whole paragraph about the other view and still have one clean opinion.

### Banned phrases

| ❌ Never write | ✅ Write instead |
|---|---|
| *I partly agree with both views.* | *I completely support the latter view because …* |
| *Both views have their merits.* | Delete it |
| *It depends on the situation.* | Choose one side before you start |
| *Schools should spend money on both.* | Choose one |
| *…, but [the other view] also matters.* in your opinion sentence | Only your own side appears there |

> ⚠️ **Idea Bank warning.** Many Part 3 passages end with a balanced answer, such as *"schools need both technology and teachers"* or *"the most effective approach involves everyone"*. Those endings are good reading, but do not copy them into this essay. Take the ideas and the reasons, and leave out the "both" ending.

## 4. Choose Your Side (60 seconds) and Set the Order

### The side rule

Do not ask *"What do I truly believe?"* Ask:

> **Which view gives me one simple reason that I can explain with an example?**

That view is your side. Then find one simple reason for the other view, because BP1 needs it.

### ⭐ The order rule: your view always goes last

This is the same emphasis rule as your Advantages & Disadvantages guide: the reader takes the **last idea** as your position. So the other view always comes first, in all four paragraphs.

**The order does not depend on the question. It depends only on your opinion.**

| You support | Introduction | BP1 | BP2 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The **second** view in the question (Essay A) | First view → second view | First view | Second view | First view → second view |
| The **first** view in the question (Essay B) | Second view → first view | Second view | First view | Second view → first view |

This gives you three free benefits:
- *"the latter view"* in your opinion sentence is **always correct**, because your view is always the one you mentioned last.
- If you support the first view, the order of the question changes automatically, which helps your paraphrase.
- BP2 flows straight from your introduction into your conclusion.

## 5. The Fixed Structure

| Paragraph | Content | Sentences | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | General statement (optional)<br>a. Paraphrase both views<br>b. Clear opinion + main reason | 2 (or 3) | **35–45** |
| **Body Paragraph 1** | The view you do **not** support — **P E E X L** | 5 | **90–120** |
| **Body Paragraph 2** | The view you **support** — **P E X R L** | 5 | **90–120** |
| **Conclusion** | a. Statement in new words<br>b. Theme of both views + your opinion restated | 2 | **35–45** |
| **Total** | | 14 | **260–299** (target about 280) |

### ⭐ The under-300 rule

**Your essay is always 260–299 words.** The simple plan is **40 + 100 + 100 + 40 = 280**.

| Why this rule helps | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | You can finish and still have 6 minutes to check your work |
| **Accuracy** | Fewer sentences give you fewer chances to make mistakes |
| **Practice** | With limited time, the same short plan is quicker to learn |
| **No lost marks** | 250 is the official minimum, and extra length does not earn marks by itself |

**Staying inside the limits**
- **Body paragraphs:** 90–120 is your limit, but **aim for about 100**. Five sentences × about 20 words = 100. If every part reaches its maximum, the total goes over 300.
- **Too long?** Cut extra details and repeated ideas. **Never cut the example or the explanation**, because they are your Task Response marks.
- **Too short?** The smallest sizes add up to only 250 words (35 + 90 + 90 + 35), so add one more *why?* or *what happens next?* detail.
- **Do not count every word in the exam.** In practice, find out how many lines 280 words fills in your handwriting. Then you can check the length at a glance.

> **This is your practice rule, not an IELTS rule.** IELTS has no maximum, and going over 300 is not penalised. If you reach 305 words in the exam, do not cross out a good sentence in a hurry. Use that time to check your grammar.

## 6. Introduction Template

**Default: 2 long sentences.**

**a. Paraphrase both views (~22–28 words)** — other view first

> *While some people argue that [other view in new words], others believe that [my view in new words].*

**b. Opinion + main reason (~12–17 words)**

> *I completely support the latter view because [main reason].*

Keep sentence b the same in every essay. Only the reason changes. The reason is the Point of your BP2, so write it in a few words here and develop it there.

### The general statement (optional)

Forty-five words is not enough for three long sentences, so a general statement makes your other sentences shorter. Use it only when **the question gives you a background sentence** that you can paraphrase. Otherwise, skip it.

| Part | EDU-04: *Discipline is an ever increasing problem in modern schools. Some people think that discipline should be the responsibility of teachers, while others think that this is the role of parents.* | Words |
|---|---|---|
| General statement | Poor behaviour has become a growing concern in many schools. | 10 |
| Paraphrase | While some people argue that teachers should control it, others believe that this is the duty of parents. | 18 |
| Opinion | I completely support the latter view because discipline begins at home. | 11 |
| **Total** | | **39** |

| ❌ Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| *Nowadays, this is a very controversial topic in our society.* | Fits every question, so it shows no language or idea of your own |
| *This essay will discuss both views and give my opinion.* | A plan sentence sounds memorised and adds no idea |
| *Some people think that schools should invest more money in technology, while others think …* | Copies the question. Copied words are not counted |
| *In my opinion, the first view is good, but the second view is also good.* | A partial opinion |

## 7. Body Paragraph 1 — The View You Do Not Support

> **General rules for every essay type:** see [IELTS_Body_Paragraph_Rules.md](IELTS_Body_Paragraph_Rules.md). One topic sentence, one main idea, and an example based on the explanation. Those rules matter more than ticking every box here. If a sentence opener sounds unnatural in your essay, change it.

| # | Role | Safe starter | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **P** | *On the one hand, people who support [view] argue that [reason].* | Topic sentence. Says whose view it is and gives its main reason |
| 2 | **E** | *This is because …* / *Using …, [people] can …* | Why do they think so? |
| 3 | **E** | *…; as a result, …* | What happens because of it? |
| 4 | **X** | *For example, a … who …, and this …* | **Shows the second Explain sentence happening** |
| 5 | **L** | *For this reason, many people believe that [view in new words].* | Returns to the view, still as **their** belief |

### ⭐ The BP1 rule: report it, do not join it

Write BP1 as an honest reporter. Give the other view a real reason and a real example, because the examiner must see that you have discussed it. But show that the idea belongs to **them**.

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| Use *people who support … argue that …*, *in their view*, *many people believe that …* | The reader can see whose idea it is |
| Do not write *I*, *my* or *in my opinion* | Your opinion belongs in the other three paragraphs |
| Give BP1 as much development as BP2 | A weak BP1 means the first view is not really discussed |
| Do not attack the view here | Your answer to it comes in BP2, in the R sentence |

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right |
|---|---|
| *Technology makes lessons more engaging, and I think this is very important.* (You have changed sides.) | *People who support investment in technology argue that it makes lessons more engaging.* |
| *Some people wrongly think that computers help, but they are expensive.* (This attacks the view and leaves it undiscussed.) | *…argue that it makes lessons more engaging. Using videos and animations, teachers can …* |

## 8. Body Paragraph 2 — The View You Support

| # | Role | Safe starter | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **P** | *On the other hand, I firmly believe that [my view] because [main reason].* | Topic sentence. The same reason as your introduction |
| 2 | **E** | *When …, …* | Why is it true? How does it happen? |
| 3 | **X** | *For instance, a … who …, which …* | **Shows the Explain sentence happening** |
| 4 | **R** | *Although some people argue that [BP1's point], [my side].* | Answers the other view |
| 5 | **L** | *Consequently, …* | Returns to your point and the question |

### ⭐ The R sentence: reuse BP1's point

You do not have to think of anything new for the Although clause. **Take the main point of your BP1 and answer it.** This is the sentence that makes your essay a real discussion, because it shows why your view is stronger.

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right |
|---|---|
| *Although teachers are important, technology makes lessons more engaging.* (Ends on the other view.) | *Although some people argue that technology makes lessons more engaging, even the best equipment is wasted if teachers have never been trained to use it.* |

- Your side always goes **after the comma**.
- Never write *but* after *Although*.
- Give the other view **one clause only**. You already explained it in BP1.
- **Your emergency exit:** if you cannot think of an R sentence, write another *Explain* sentence. The paragraph is still complete.

### The Example rule — for both paragraphs

The example must show the Explain sentence right before it, and the same situation must appear in both.

| Step | Essay A, BP2 |
|---|---|
| **E** | Students *lose confidence*; a teacher can *notice the problem and adapt the lesson*. |
| **X** | A pupil *has lost confidence in mathematics*; a patient teacher can *notice the difficulty and explain it differently*. |

**The 5-second test:** read E and X together. If the same situation is not in both, change the example. In your plan, write the example first, then the Explain sentence that leads to it.

## 9. Sentence Variety and Linking Devices

### Sentence variety — the key to band 7 in Grammar

The band 7 descriptor asks for *a variety of complex structures* and *frequent error-free sentences*. Most sentences should be about 15–25 words, and your example can reach about 30. You do not need every type in every paragraph.

| Type | Pattern | From the model essays |
|---|---|---|
| **Long simple** | *[long subject] + verb + object* | *Consequently, **funding well-trained teachers is** the most effective way to improve students' learning.* |
| **Compound** | *Clause; as a result, clause.* | *Difficult processes can be shown on a screen**; as a result,** students understand them more quickly.* |
| **Complex** | *When / Although / because / who / which / that* | ***Although** some people argue that …, even the best equipment is wasted **if** teachers have never been trained to use it.* |
| **Compound-complex** | *a … who …, and this …* | *For example, a science class **that** cannot safely perform …, **and** this experience often stays longer in students' memories.* |
| **Passive** | *be + past participle* | *Difficult processes **can be shown** on a screen …* · *… strict laws and large investments in clean energy **are required** …* |
| **Impersonal passive** | *It is often argued that …* | Another safe way to start your paraphrase: ***It is often argued that** schools should spend more money on computers.* |
| **Verb+ing to start** | *Using …, [subject] + verb* · *By using …, [subject] + verb* | ***Using** videos, animations and simulations, teachers can bring difficult topics to life.* |
| **Verb+ing as subject** | *Funding …, Protecting …* + verb | ***Funding** well-trained teachers **is** the most effective way …* |

**Four accuracy rules**
- **Verb+ing needs the right subject.** The person doing the *-ing* must come right after the comma.
  ❌ *Using computers, lessons become interesting.* ✅ *Using computers, teachers can make lessons interesting.*
- **Use the passive when the doer is not important,** or when you want the result to be the subject. One or two in a paragraph is plenty.
- **The example is never a short simple sentence.** Add *who / which / while / when / whereas*.
- **Accuracy comes first.** A correct compound sentence scores higher than a broken complex sentence. Use the structures you have practised.

### Linking devices — Coherence & Cohesion

| Job | Device | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Show two views | *While … others …* | Introduction, conclusion |
| Open each view | *On the one hand, …* / *On the other hand, …* | BP1 P / BP2 P |
| Explain | *This is because …* · *When …, …* | E |
| Show a result | *…; as a result, …* · *…, which …* | E, X |
| Give an example | *For example, …* · *For instance, …* | X |
| Contrast inside a sentence | *…, whereas …* · *…, but …* | X |
| Answer the other view | *Although some people argue that …, …* | R |
| Close the paragraph | *For this reason, …* · *Consequently, …* | L |
| Close the essay | *In conclusion, …* | Conclusion |

**Reference words also link sentences:** *this, these, they, it, such*. *"…, and **this** experience …"* connects two ideas without using a linker. The band 7 descriptor calls this mix *"a range of cohesive devices"*.

> ⚠️ **Use one linker in a sentence.** Never stack them (*~~Moreover, in addition,~~*), and do not start every sentence with one.

## 10. Conclusion Template

Three things, in two sentences: **the statement in new words, the theme of both views, your opinion restated.**

**a. The statement in new words (~15–18 words)**

> *In conclusion, people hold different views on whether [view 1 in new words] or [view 2 in new words].*

**b. Both themes + your opinion (~22–27 words)** — other view first, your view last

> *While [theme of the other view], I firmly believe that [my view] because [main reason in new words].*

| ❌ Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| *In conclusion, both views are important.* | A partial opinion, and it does not match your introduction |
| *While technology can help, **but** teachers are important.* | Never use *but* after *While* |
| *Governments should also train more teachers in villages.* | A new idea has no support, so leave it out |
| Repeating your introduction word for word | Say the same meaning in new words |

## 11. Model Essay A — You Support the Second View

**Question (EDU-19):** *Some people think that schools should invest more money in technology, such as more computers, while others think more money should be spent on teachers. Discuss both views and give your opinion.*

**5-minute plan**
- **My side:** teachers (the second view), so the question's order stays the same.
- **Other view (BP1):** technology makes lessons engaging → a dangerous chemical reaction shown on a smart board.
- **My view (BP2):** technology cannot replace a teacher → a pupil who has lost confidence in mathematics.
- **R:** answers BP1's *engaging* point with *untrained teachers waste equipment*.
- **Idea bank:** Chapter 01 Education → *Technology in Schools: Computers or Teachers?*

**Introduction** (39 words)
> While some people argue that schools should spend more of their budgets on computers and other technology, others believe that this money should go to teachers. I completely support the latter view because technology cannot replace a good teacher.

**Body Paragraph 1 — the other view** (98 words)
> On the one hand, people who support investment in technology argue that it makes lessons more **interactive and engaging**. Using videos, animations and simulations, teachers can bring difficult topics to life, which a textbook alone cannot do. Difficult processes can be shown on a screen; as a result, students understand them more quickly. For example, a science class that cannot safely perform a chemical reaction can watch it on a smart board, and this experience often stays longer in students' memories. For this reason, many people believe that computers are a sensible use of a school's limited money.

**Body Paragraph 2 — my view** (110 words)
> On the other hand, I firmly believe that schools should fund teachers first because technology cannot replace their human qualities. When students lose confidence, a teacher can notice the problem and adapt the lesson, and no computer program can offer the same support. For instance, a village pupil who has lost confidence in mathematics may give up on recorded lessons, whereas a patient teacher can notice the difficulty and explain it differently. Although some people argue that technology makes lessons more engaging, even the best equipment is wasted if teachers have never been trained to use it. Consequently, funding well-trained teachers is the most effective way to improve students' learning.

**Conclusion** (42 words)
> In conclusion, people hold different views on whether schools should fund digital equipment or teaching staff. While technology can make lessons more interesting, I firmly believe that teachers deserve the investment first because no device can offer the same guidance and encouragement.

**Total: 289 words** ✅

### Sentence map of Essay A

| Part | Role | Linker | Sentence structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro a | Paraphrase | *While … others* | Complex (*While* + *that* clauses) |
| Intro b | Opinion + reason | *because* | Complex (reason) |
| BP1 1 | **P** | *On the one hand* | Complex (*who* + *that*) |
| BP1 2 | **E** | *Using …* · *which* | Verb+ing to start + relative *which* |
| BP1 3 | **E** | *as a result* | Compound + passive (*can be shown*) |
| BP1 4 | **X** | *For example* · *this experience* | Compound-complex (*that* + *and*) |
| BP1 5 | **L** | *For this reason* | Complex (*that*) |
| BP2 1 | **P** | *On the other hand* | Complex (*that* + *because*) |
| BP2 2 | **E** | *When* | Compound-complex (*When* + *and*) |
| BP2 3 | **X** | *For instance* · *whereas* | Complex (*who* + *whereas*) |
| BP2 4 | **R** | *Although some people argue that* | Complex + passive (*is wasted*, *been trained*) |
| BP2 5 | **L** | *Consequently* | Long simple (verb+ing subject) |
| Concl a | Statement in new words | *In conclusion* | Complex (*whether*) |
| Concl b | Both themes + opinion | *While* · *because* | Complex (*While* + *that* + *because*) |

## 12. Model Essay B — You Support the First View

**Question (ENV-13):** *Some people think that current environmental issues are global problems and should therefore be dealt with by the government while others believe that these problems can only be tackled by individuals. Discuss both sides and give your opinion.*

**5-minute plan**
- **My side:** governments (the first view), so **the order flips**: individuals come first and governments last.
- **Other view (BP1):** small actions add up → a Dhaka family using buses and cloth bags.
- **My view (BP2):** only governments have the power → a polluting factory and a strict law.
- **R:** answers BP1's *small actions add up* with *green choices must be available and affordable*.
- **Idea bank:** Chapter 03 Environment → *Climate Change: Causes and Who Is Responsible* · *Environmental Education and Individual Action*.

**Introduction** (38 words)
> While some people believe that environmental damage can only be reduced by ordinary citizens, others argue that such worldwide problems are the responsibility of governments. I completely support the latter view because only governments have the necessary power.

**Body Paragraph 1 — the other view** (98 words)
> On the one hand, people who support individual action argue that every person contributes to the problem through the energy they use and the products they buy. Individual actions may seem small, but they **add up** when millions of people take part. By using public transport and avoiding single-use plastic, ordinary families can reduce pressure on the environment. For example, a family in Dhaka that travels by bus and carries cloth bags produces less waste, and this change costs them very little. For this reason, many people believe that protecting the environment must begin with the ordinary citizen.

**Body Paragraph 2 — my view** (112 words)
> On the other hand, I firmly believe that governments should deal with these problems because they alone have the power to act on a large scale. When pollution comes mainly from power stations and factories, strict laws and large investments in clean energy are required, and a single person cannot provide them. For instance, a factory owner who pollutes a river may ignore residents' complaints, but a law with heavy fines can force every factory to clean up its waste. Although some people argue that small actions add up, people can only make green choices if those choices are available and affordable. Consequently, individual effort cannot succeed unless governments **take the lead**.

**Conclusion** (43 words)
> In conclusion, people hold different views on whether citizens or national leaders should tackle environmental damage. While individual choices can reduce waste and pollution, I firmly believe that governments should solve these problems because only they can make changes on the required scale.

**Total: 291 words** ✅

**Notice:** the question put governments first, but this essay put them last, because that is the side it supports. *"The latter view"* still points to the right side.

| "governments must **take the lead**, because only they have the power to make changes on the scale that is required." | Essay B, BP2 P and L, conclusion |

**Rules for choosing ideas**
1. **Find the category first** (e.g. *computers or teachers* → Education → Technology in Schools).
2. **Find both sides in the same passage.** Your BP1 idea comes from one side, your BP2 idea from the other.
3. **Simple beats clever.** Pick the idea you can explain and give an example for.
4. **Change the words to fit the question.** Use exact sentences when they fit, but never copy a whole paragraph.
5. **Leave out the balanced ending** (Section 3).

> ✅ **Your Model Answer Sheets follow this guide.** Every essay marked *Discussion* in a sheet's Contents table uses this exact structure, so you can study them as extra models. The Bangla Discussion essays in Part 6 of your Idea Banks are translations of the same model answers. The side chosen in a sheet is one good answer, not the only right one. In the exam, choose the side that gives **you** an easy reason and example.

## 14. Useful Language Bank (simple and safe)

| Paraphrase "others think" | *others believe that*, *others argue that*, *others are convinced that* |

| Your opinion | *I completely support the latter view*, *I firmly believe that* |
| Explaining | *This is because …*, *When …, …*, *This means that …* |
| Giving an example | *For example, …*, *For instance, …* |

## 15. 40-Minute Exam Plan

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| **0–2 min** | Confirm the type. Write out View 1 and View 2 in your own words. |
| **2–5 min** | Choose your side. Write one reason + example for each view. Mark the order: other view first, my view last. |
| **5–9 min** | Introduction (Paraphrase both views → Opinion + reason) |
| **9–19 min** | Body Paragraph 1 — the other view — P E E X L |
| **19–29 min** | Body Paragraph 2 — my view — P E X R L |
| **29–34 min** | Conclusion (Statement in new words → *While* + both themes + opinion) |
| **34–40 min** | Check: spelling, articles (a/the), plurals (-s), subject–verb agreement, word count |

## 16. Final Checklist

- [ ] I found **both views**, and I discussed each one in its own body paragraph.
- [ ] I chose **one side** and never wrote *partly, both views are right, it depends*.
- [ ] My opinion is the **same** in the introduction, BP2 and the conclusion.
- [ ] The **other view comes first and my view comes last** in the introduction, the body paragraphs and the conclusion.
- [ ] BP1 reports the other view fairly, with **no *I***, and it has a real reason and example.
- [ ] Each body paragraph has **one topic sentence** and one main idea.
- [ ] Each example **shows the Explain sentence right before it**.
- [ ] My R sentence answers BP1's point, with **my side after the comma** and no *but*.
- [ ] My Link sentences add no new idea.
- [ ] I used a **mix of structures**: simple, compound, complex, passive and verb+ing.
- [ ] Each sentence connects to the one before it, with one linker or reference word at most.
- [ ] My ideas come from the matching **idea bank chapter**, without its balanced ending.
- [ ] My conclusion has the statement in new words, both themes and my opinion, and **no new idea**.
- [ ] Introduction 35–45, each body paragraph 90–120 (aim for about 100), conclusion 35–45, **total 260–299**.

## 17. Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Writing about your own view in both paragraphs | BP1 must explain the other view (Section 7) |
| Writing *I think* or *in my opinion* in BP1 | Use *people who support … argue that …* |
| Giving BP1 only two or three weak sentences | Give it a real reason, explanation and example |
| Attacking the other view in BP1 | Save your answer for the R sentence in BP2 |
| Ending with *both views are important* | One clean opinion, the same as your introduction |
| Copying a balanced ending from the idea bank | Take the ideas and leave out the ending |
| Following the question's order when you support the first view | Your view always goes last (Section 4) |
| Choosing a side, then finding no idea for the other view | Plan one reason for **each** view before you write |
| Writing three short sentences to include a general statement | Skip it, and write two long sentences |
| *While …, but …* / *Although …, but …* | Remove *but* |
| An example that fits the topic but not the Explain sentence | Use the 5-second test (Section 8) |
| *Using computers, lessons become interesting.* | The doer comes after the comma: *Using computers, teachers can …* |
| Writing a new idea in the conclusion | Only the two themes and your opinion |
| A total under 260 words | Aim for about 100 in each body paragraph, not 90 |
| A total of 300 words or more in practice | Cut extra details, but keep the explanation and the example |
