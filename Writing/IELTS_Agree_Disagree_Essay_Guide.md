# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Agree / Disagree Essay Guide

> **Goal:** Band 7+ with one fixed structure you can remember under exam pressure.
> **Length:** minimum **260 words**, target **about 300–320 words**.
> **Rule:** same 4 paragraphs every time. **One clear side from the first line to the last. Never a partial answer.**
> **Ideas:** simple ideas from your topic-wise idea bank, developed well. No complex ideas.

---

## 1. The Memory Card (learn this first)

```text
INTRO   (35-50)    Paraphrase the statement.
                   I completely agree/disagree with this view because IDEA 1 and IDEA 2.

BP1     (100-120)  P  The main reason why I agree/disagree with this idea is that IDEA 1.
                   E  This is because ...
                   E  ...; as a result, ...
                   X  For example, a ... who ..., and this ...
                   L  Therefore, ...

BP2     (100-120)  P  Another important reason for my agreement/disagreement is that IDEA 2.
                   E  When ..., ...
                   X  For instance, a ... who ..., which ...
                   R  Although some people argue that ..., [my side].
                   L  Consequently, ...

CONCL   (35-55)    In conclusion, I firmly believe that [statement in new words].
                   IDEA 1 in new words, and IDEA 2 in new words.
```

**The only difference between BP1 and BP2:** BP2 replaces the second Explain with a Response.

**The golden rule:** Point → Explain → Example → Link must all talk about the **same thing**. The example shows the Explain sentence right before it (Section 6).

## 2. Know the Question Type (30 seconds)

| Use this guide when the question ends with | Do **not** use this guide when it says |
|---|---|
| *To what extent do you agree or disagree?* | *Discuss both views and give your opinion.* (use the [Discuss Both Views guide](IELTS_Discuss_Both_Views_Essay_Guide.md)) |
| *To what extent do you agree?* | *Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?* (use the Advantages & Disadvantages guide) |
| *Do you agree or disagree?* | *What are the causes? What are the solutions?* (Problem–Solution) |
| *Do you agree with this opinion?* | *Do you think it is the best way? What other solutions are there?* (use the [Double Question guide](IELTS_Double_Question_Essay_Guide.md)) |
| *What is your opinion?* after **one** claim | *Some people think X, while others think Y. What is your opinion?* (use the [Discuss Both Views guide](IELTS_Discuss_Both_Views_Essay_Guide.md)) |

## 3. Choose One Side (60 seconds) — and Keep It

### The side rule

Do not ask *"What do I truly believe?"* Ask:

> **Which side gives me 2 simple ideas that I can explain with an example?**

Choose that side, even if it is not your personal opinion. The examiner marks how well you support your position, not whether the position is right.

### A one-sided answer is safe

*To what extent* does **not** force you to write about both sides. The band 7 Task Response descriptor asks for *"a clear and developed position"*. **"I completely disagree"** is a clear position. Your Response sentence in BP2 (Section 6) shows that you understand the other side, without moving your position.

### Check what "agree" means first

**Agree = support the statement exactly as it is written.** This matters most when the statement is negative. Find the main claim, which is usually the sentence with *should*, *will*, *best* or *only*.

| Statement | If you **agree**, you are saying | If you **disagree**, you are saying |
|---|---|---|
| *Schools should cut art and music from the curriculum.* | Remove them | **Keep them** |
| *Health care should **not** be provided for free.* | People should **pay** | Health care should be **free** |
| *The best way to improve health is to exercise daily.* | Exercise is the best way | Something else is better or equally important |

**Quick tip:** statements with strong words (*all, every, only, never, best, banned, cut*) are usually easier to **disagree** with.

### Banned phrases

These make your position partial, or make it sound as if you are changing sides.

| ❌ Never write | ✅ Write instead |
|---|---|
| *I partly agree* · *I largely agree* · *I agree to some extent* | *I completely agree with this view* |
| *It depends on the situation.* | Choose one side before you start |
| *There are arguments on both sides.* | Delete it |
| *On the other hand, …* to start BP2 | *Another important reason for my …* |
| *…, but [the other side also has a point].* in the introduction or conclusion | Only your own side appears there |

> ⚠️ **Do not mix up your two guides.** The Advantages & Disadvantages essay uses *Although* in the introduction, *While* in the conclusion, and *On the other hand* in BP2. **This essay uses none of them in those places.** Its only *Although* is the Response sentence in BP2.

## 4. The Fixed Structure

| Paragraph | Content | Sentences | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | a. Paraphrase the statement<br>b. Clear opinion + Idea 1 + Idea 2 | 2 | **35–50** |
| **Body Paragraph 1** | Idea 1 — **P E E X L** | 5 | **100–120** |
| **Body Paragraph 2** | Idea 2 — **P E X R L** | 5 | **100–120** |
| **Conclusion** | a. Statement + opinion in new words<br>b. Idea 1 + Idea 2 in new words | 2 | **35–55** |
| **Total** | | 14 | **270–345** (target 300–320) |

### ⭐ The same two ideas run through the whole essay

Introduction, body paragraphs and conclusion all carry the **same two ideas in the same order**. Only the words change. This is what keeps your position clear, and it also means you only have to think of two ideas.

| | Idea 1 | Idea 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | *develop children's creativity* | *help them to manage stress* |
| **Body Paragraph** | BP1: *encourage creativity and self-expression, which are increasingly valued by employers* | BP2: *protect children's mental well-being* |
| **Conclusion** | *build the imaginative skills that employers want* | *keep young learners emotionally healthy* |

## 5. Introduction Template

The introduction is the examiner's first impression, so write it slowly and check it before you continue.

**a. Paraphrase (1 sentence, ~18–28 words)** — choose one opener:

| When the question | Formula |
|---|---|
| Is one direct claim | *It is often argued that [statement in new words].* |
| Gives background first | *Nowadays, [background in new words], and many believe that [claim in new words].* |

**b. Opinion + two ideas (1 sentence, ~15–22 words)**

> *I completely **agree / disagree** with this view because [Idea 1] and [Idea 2].*

Keep this sentence the same in every essay. Only the two ideas change.

### What makes a good first impression

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| Change the words **and** the sentence order of the question | Copied words are not counted and show weak vocabulary |
| Say your opinion in sentence b, by word 30–35 | The examiner knows your position at once |
| Write both ideas in the **same grammar shape** | *develop … and help …* reads smoothly (see below) |
| Keep each idea to 3–6 words | Details belong in the body paragraphs |

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Better |
|---|---|
| *…because of creativity and also children can manage their stress.* (the two ideas have different shapes) | *…because these subjects develop children's creativity and help them to manage stress.* |
| *This essay will explain why I disagree.* (a plan sentence sounds memorised and adds no idea) | Stop after your two ideas |

## 6. Body Paragraph Rules — PEEL(R)

> **General rules for every essay type:** see [IELTS_Body_Paragraph_Rules.md](IELTS_Body_Paragraph_Rules.md). In the exam, 90 words is your minimum for a body paragraph. This guide and your Model Answer Sheets use 100–120 as the target.

| Letter | Meaning | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Point | States the idea from your introduction. This is also the topic sentence. |
| **E** | Explain | Why is it true? How does it happen? |
| **X** | Example | A specific, realistic situation that **shows the Explain sentence right before it happening**. Never invent statistics or studies. |
| **R** | Response | Mentions what the other side says, then answers it. |
| **L** | Link | Brings **this paragraph's idea** back to your opinion or to the question, with no new idea. |

### ⭐ The Example rule — one idea from Point to Link

**Your rule:** we share our Point, explain it, give an example of that explanation, then link back. Every sentence continues the one before it.

| Step | Job | EDU-02, BP2 |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Say the idea | Computer literacy is necessary for everyday life. |
| **E** | Say why | People must pay bills and book tickets online, so those without training feel excluded. |
| **X** | Show **that E** in one real situation | Someone without computer skills queues at an office to pay a bill, while a neighbour pays by phone in two minutes. |
| **R** | Answer the other side (BP2 only) | Computers do not weaken traditional skills, because children use them on a computer. |
| **L** | Return to the idea and the question | So computer skills should stand beside the basics, and no child should be unable to manage these tasks. |

| ❌ Same topic, different idea | ✅ Shows the explanation |
|---|---|
| **E:** People must pay bills online. **X:** *a school that teaches children how to protect their passwords helps them to avoid online fraud.* (The explanation is about daily tasks, but the example is about safety.) | **E:** People must pay bills online. **X:** *a person who never learned computer skills may have to travel to an office and queue to pay an electricity bill, while a neighbour does it on a phone in two minutes.* |

**The 5-second test:** read E and X together.
1. Underline the key situation in E (*pay bills online*).
2. Look for the same situation in X (*pay an electricity bill*).
3. If it is not there, change the example. An example that only fits the general topic does not count.

**Four small rules**
- **BP1 has two Explain sentences.** The example comes right after the second one, so it must show the second one.
- **R is the one exception.** It answers the other side, so it does not come from your example. It must still be about the same question.
- **L adds nothing new.** If your Link introduces a new solution or a new idea, cut it back to the idea of this paragraph.
- **Plan the example first.** In your 3-minute plan, write the idea and its example, then write the Explain sentence that leads to it.

> **Why it matters for band 7:** the Task Response descriptor asks you to *"extend and support"* main ideas, and it warns that supporting ideas *"may lack focus"*. One weak example will not cost you the band, but a focused example in every paragraph is the safest way to show development.

### The two paragraphs, sentence by sentence

| # | BP1 (Idea 1) | BP2 (Idea 2) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **P** — *The main reason why I agree/disagree with this idea is that …* | **P** — *Another important reason for my agreement/disagreement is that …* |
| 2 | **E** — *This is because …* | **E** — *When …, …* |
| 3 | **E** — *…; as a result, …* | **X** — *For instance, a … who …, which …* |
| 4 | **X** — *For example, a … who …, and this …* | **R** — *Although some people argue that …, [my side].* |
| 5 | **L** — *Therefore, …* | **L** — *Consequently, …* |

**Word target:** 5 sentences × about 22 words ≈ **110 words**. Sentences can run from about 14 to 30 words.

> **Under 100 words?** Add one more *Explain* sentence by asking *"why?"* or *"what happens next?"*. Six sentences is fine.
> **Over 120 words?** Shorten the example. It should be one sentence, not a story.

### The Response sentence (R) — why it does not make your answer partial

R goes in **BP2 only**, once per essay. It follows the emphasis rule from your other guide: **the other side goes in the *Although* clause, and your side goes after the comma.** The last idea is the one the reader takes as your position.

> **Although some people argue that** [other side], **[why my side is still right].**

| ❌ Wrong (ends on the other side) | ✅ Right (ends on your side) |
|---|---|
| *Although a tired child cannot learn effectively, some people argue that school hours should be spent only on job-related skills.* | *Although some people argue that limited school hours should be spent only on job-related skills, a tired and unhappy child cannot learn any subject effectively.* |

- Never write *but* after *Although*.
- Give the other side **one clause only**. Do not explain it or give it an example.
- **Your emergency exit:** if you cannot think of an R sentence in the exam, write another *Explain* sentence instead. The paragraph is still complete.

### The Link sentence (L)

The Link closes the paragraph in **general words**. It does not repeat the Point.

| ❌ Repeats the Point | ✅ Links back to the question |
|---|---|
| *Therefore, art and music encourage creativity.* | *Therefore, removing these lessons would leave children less prepared for the modern world, not more prepared.* |

### Sentence variety — the key to band 7 in Grammar

The band 7 descriptor asks for *a variety of complex structures* and *frequent error-free sentences*. This fixed plan gives you the variety automatically:

| Sentence | Structure | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Complex | *… is that …, which …* |
| **E** (*This is because*) | Complex | *This is because [people] who …* |
| **E** (*as a result*) | Compound | *Clause; as a result, clause.* |
| **E** (*When*) | Complex or compound-complex | *When …, …, and …* |
| **X** | Compound-complex | *a … who …, and this …* / *a … who …, which …* |
| **R** | Complex (concession) | *Although some people argue that …, …* |
| **L** | Long simple or complex | *Therefore, [long subject] + verb + object.* |

**Rules**
- **The example is never a short simple sentence.** Add *who / which / while / when*.
- Do not start two sentences in a row with the same word.
- **Accuracy first:** a correct compound sentence scores higher than a broken complex sentence. Only use structures you have practised.
- One linker per sentence, taken from the plan above. Never stack them (*~~Moreover, in addition,~~*).

## 7. Conclusion Template

Same size as your introduction: **2 sentences, 35–55 words.** Do not make it a single short line, and do not add a new idea.

**a. Statement + opinion in new words (~18–26 words)**

> *In conclusion, I firmly believe that [your position, using the topic in new words].*

**b. Idea 1 + Idea 2 in new words (~15–25 words)**

> *[Subject] + [Idea 1 in new words], and they also + [Idea 2 in new words].*

| ❌ Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| *In conclusion, I completely disagree because these subjects develop creativity and help to manage stress.* | Repeats the introduction word for word and is too short |
| *…Governments should also train more art teachers.* | A new idea has no support, so leave it out |
| *…although technology is also important.* | Weakens your position at the last moment |

## 8. Model Essay A — Disagree

**Question (EDU-07):** *The role of education is to prepare children for the modern world. Schools should cut art and music out of the curriculum so that children can focus on useful subjects such as information technology. To what extent do you agree?*

**5-minute plan**
- **Main claim:** schools should cut art and music. **My side: disagree** (keep them).
- **Idea 1:** creativity, because employers value it → student who draws designs websites.
- **Idea 2:** reduce stress, because exams are tiring → anxious pupil relaxes in the music group.
- **Other side for R:** school hours should go to job skills.
- **Idea bank:** Chapter 01 Education → *Arts, Music, Sport and Physical Education*.

**Introduction** (43 words)
> It is often argued that schools should remove art and music from their timetables so that pupils can concentrate on practical subjects such as computing. I completely disagree with this view because these subjects develop children's creativity and help them to manage stress.

**Body Paragraph 1** (115 words)
> The main reason why I disagree with this idea is that art and music **encourage creativity and self-expression**, which are increasingly valued by employers. This is because a modern workplace needs people who can produce new ideas, not only people who can operate a computer. Machines can now perform routine tasks quickly; as a result, original thinking has become one of the most useful skills that a worker can offer. For example, a student who learns to draw at school may later design attractive websites for a company, and this job requires artistic ability as well as technical knowledge. Therefore, removing these lessons would leave children less prepared for the modern world, not more prepared.

**Body Paragraph 2** (112 words)
> Another important reason for my disagreement is that creative lessons protect children's mental well-being. When pupils spend long hours preparing for exams, they often feel tired and anxious, and art and music give them a healthy way to express emotions and **reduce stress**. For instance, a pupil who feels anxious before exams may relax by playing in the school music group, which helps them to return to their studies with a calmer mind. Although some people argue that limited school hours should be spent only on job-related skills, a tired and unhappy child cannot learn any subject effectively. Consequently, these lessons support academic learning and deserve their place in a **well-rounded education**.

**Conclusion** (41 words)
> In conclusion, I firmly believe that art and music should keep their place in the school curriculum instead of being replaced by technical subjects. These lessons build the imaginative skills that employers want, and they also keep young learners emotionally healthy.

**Total: 311 words** ✅

### Sentence map of Essay A

| Part | Role | Linker | Sentence structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro a | Paraphrase | *It is often argued that* | Complex (*that* + *so that*) |
| Intro b | Opinion + 2 ideas | *because* | Complex (reason) |
| BP1 1 | **P** | *The main reason why … is that* | Complex (*why* + *that* + *which*) |
| BP1 2 | **E** | *This is because* | Complex (*because* + *who*) |
| BP1 3 | **E** | *as a result* | Compound (semicolon + linker) |
| BP1 4 | **X** | *For example* · *this job* | Compound-complex (*who* + *and*) |
| BP1 5 | **L** | *Therefore* | Long simple (*removing these lessons …*) |
| BP2 1 | **P** | *Another important reason* | Complex (*that*) |
| BP2 2 | **E** | *When* | Compound-complex (*When* + *and*) |
| BP2 3 | **X** | *For instance* | Complex (*who* + *which*) |
| BP2 4 | **R** | *Although some people argue that* | Complex (concession) |
| BP2 5 | **L** | *Consequently* · *these lessons* | Simple (two verbs) |
| Concl a | Statement + opinion | *In conclusion* | Complex (*that*) |
| Concl b | 2 ideas in new words | *These lessons* · *and* | Compound-complex (*that* + *and*) |

## 9. Model Essay B — Agree

**Question (TEC-10):** *It is common nowadays for each member of the family to have their own piece of modern technology. Some people think this will lead to a break down in family relationships and communication. To what extent do you agree?*

**5-minute plan**
- **Main claim:** personal technology will damage family relationships. **My side: agree.**
- **Idea 1:** less conversation → teenager never mentions a school problem.
- **Idea 2:** less shared time → family scatters after dinner.
- **Other side for R:** technology keeps relatives in other cities in contact.
- **Idea bank:** Chapter 02 Technology → *Technology and Modern Lifestyle*.

**Introduction** (45 words)
> Nowadays, it has become normal for every person in a household to own a smartphone, tablet or laptop, and many believe that this damages family ties. I completely agree with this view because these devices reduce conversation at home and take away shared family time.

**Body Paragraph 1** (110 words)
> The main reason why I agree with this idea is that personal devices reduce the amount of real conversation between family members. This is because people who are absorbed in a separate screen pay little attention to those around them, even when they are sitting in the same room. Parents may be answering work emails while children watch videos; as a result, meaningful conversation becomes rare. For example, a teenager who spends every evening on a phone may never mention a problem at school, and the parents may only discover it when it has become serious. Therefore, the daily talks that keep families close are slowly disappearing from many homes.

**Body Paragraph 2** (118 words)
> Another important reason for my agreement is that individual devices leave family members with less time together. When everyone can watch their own films and play their own games, people no longer need to share an activity, so **family bonds** weaken over time. For instance, a family in Dhaka that once watched television together after dinner may now scatter to different rooms as soon as the meal ends, which leaves children feeling distant from their parents. Although some people argue that technology helps relatives in different cities stay in contact, this benefit does little for family members who live under the same roof. Consequently, the home becomes a place where relatives live side by side but rarely connect.

**Conclusion** (41 words)
> In conclusion, I firmly believe that giving each family member a personal device harms relationships and communication within the home. Smartphones and tablets limit everyday talking between parents and children, and they also replace the activities that relatives once enjoyed together.

**Total: 314 words** ✅

## 10. Use Your Idea Bank in Every Answer

Do not invent ideas in the exam. Recall ones you have already read and practised.

| What you need | Where it is |
|---|---|
| 2 simple main ideas | Part 2D *Quick Idea Bank* table — one row per category |
| Explanations and examples | Part 3 *Category-Wise Free Writing* — the passage for that category |
| Collocations | Part 4 *Vocabulary & Collocation Bank* |
| Sentence patterns | Part 5 *Sentence Structure Toolkit* |
| Files | `Writing/Output/T2_XX_<Topic>_Idea_Bank.pdf` (source text in `Writing/source/`) |

**How the model essays used the idea bank**

| Idea bank sentence | Used in |
|---|---|
| "Art, music and drama **encourage creativity and self-expression**, which are increasingly valued by employers …" | Essay A, BP1 P |
| "… they can provide a way to express emotions and **reduce stress**." | Essay A, BP2 E |
| "With limited school hours, they ask why students should spend time painting or singing instead of learning skills that lead directly to jobs." | Essay A, BP2 R |
| "… a family sitting in the same room, with each person absorbed in a separate screen." | Essay B, BP1 E |
| "Parents may be answering work emails while children watch videos, so meaningful conversation becomes rare." | Essay B, BP1 E |
| "Although technology helps relatives in different cities stay in contact …" | Essay B, BP2 R |

**Rules for choosing ideas**
1. **Find the category first** (e.g. *art and music at school* → Education → Arts, Music, Sport).
2. **Take the side with more ready material.** The free writing usually gives both sides: your two ideas come from one side, and your R sentence comes from the other.
3. **Simple beats clever.** Pick the idea you can explain and give an example for, not the most impressive one.
4. **Change the words to fit the question.** Never copy a whole paragraph.

> ✅ **Your Model Answer Sheets follow this guide.** Every essay marked *Agree / Disagree* in a sheet's Contents table uses this exact structure, so you can study them as extra models. The side chosen in a sheet is one good answer, not the only right one. In the exam, choose the side that gives **you** 2 easy ideas.

## 13. Final Checklist

- [ ] I checked what "agree" means for this statement, especially if it is negative.
- [ ] I chose **one side** and never wrote *partly, largely, to some extent, it depends*.
- [ ] My opinion is the **same** in the introduction, both body paragraphs and the conclusion.
- [ ] Both ideas appear in the introduction, in the **same order** as BP1 and BP2.
- [ ] BP1 is **P E E X L**; BP2 is **P E X R L**.
- [ ] My R sentence puts the other side in the *Although* clause and **my side after the comma**, with no *but*.
- [ ] Each example **shows the Explain sentence right before it**: the same situation appears in both.

- [ ] My Link sentences return to the paragraph's idea in general words, add no new idea and do not repeat the Point.
- [ ] My conclusion uses **new words** and adds **no new idea**.

## 14. Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Choosing "partly agree" because both sides seem true | Choose the side with 2 easy ideas and write only that side |
| Agreeing in the introduction and arguing the opposite in BP2 | Plan both ideas before you write, and check they support the same side |
| Misreading a negative statement | Say the meaning to yourself first: *"Agree means …"* |
| Starting BP2 with *On the other hand* | That belongs to your Advantages & Disadvantages essay. Use *Another important reason …* |
| An example about the same topic but a different idea from the Explain sentence | Use the 5-second test (Section 6) and change the example |
| A Link sentence that brings in a new idea or solution | Return to the idea of this paragraph |
| Giving the other side a full paragraph | One *Although* clause only, in BP2 |
| *Although …, but …* | Remove *but* |
| Writing a third idea in the conclusion | Only Idea 1 and Idea 2, in new words |
| Choosing a clever idea that you cannot explain | Choose a simple idea from the idea bank |
| Copying the question words in the introduction | Change the words **and** the sentence order |
| A body paragraph under 100 words | Add one more *Explain* sentence |
