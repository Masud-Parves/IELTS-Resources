# IELTS Writing Band 7 --- 6-Week Master Preparation System

## 1. Purpose

This is the master instruction file for a six-week IELTS Writing
preparation program. It can be used by the learner or shared with
another AI tutor so future study sheets follow the same method.

**Primary target:** IELTS Writing Band 7.

The learner can understand good essays and usually has basic ideas, but
needs to improve: - fast and natural paraphrasing; - developing simple
ideas into complete arguments; - sentence structure and grammatical
control; - topic-based idea generation; - active use of vocabulary and
collocations; - timed Task 1 and Task 2 performance.

The central learning cycle is:

**READ → NOTICE → UNDERSTAND → TRANSFORM → PRODUCE → CORRECT**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Core Weaknesses

1.  Can understand model essays but struggles to paraphrase
    introductions independently.
2.  Has ideas but struggles to develop them into long, logical,
    well-structured arguments.
3.  Needs a reliable idea bank for common IELTS topics.
4.  Needs exposure to Band 7+ sentence patterns and linking devices.
5.  Needs vocabulary learned in context rather than isolated word lists.
6.  Needs a structured six-week progression from guided practice to exam
    conditions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Core Idea-Development Formula

Use:

**IDEA → WHY → HOW → RESULT → EXAMPLE**

Example basic idea:

> Online shopping saves time.

Development: - **Why?** Consumers do not need to travel to physical
stores. - **How?** They can search, compare prices, and purchase
products from home. - **Result?** The saved time can be used for work,
study, family, or leisure. - **Example?** A full-time employee can order
groceries online rather than travelling to a supermarket and waiting in
queues.

Model development:

> One significant advantage of online shopping is that it can save
> consumers a considerable amount of time. Since customers can compare
> prices and purchase products from home, they no longer need to travel
> to several physical stores in search of suitable products. This is
> particularly beneficial for people with demanding work schedules, as
> the time saved can be devoted to professional responsibilities, family
> commitments, or leisure activities. For instance, a full-time employee
> can order groceries online after work instead of spending an hour
> travelling to a supermarket and waiting in queues. Therefore, the
> convenience provided by e-commerce can contribute to a more efficient
> lifestyle.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Sentence Development Ladder

Training should repeatedly move from simple to developed English.

**Level 1:** Technology improves education.

**Level 2:** Technology improves education by giving students easier
access to learning materials.

**Level 3:** Technology improves education by giving students easier
access to digital textbooks, lectures, and other learning resources.

**Level 4:** Technology can improve education by giving students easier
access to digital resources, particularly when high-quality teaching
materials are unavailable locally.

**Level 5:** Consequently, learners in remote communities may gain
educational opportunities that would otherwise be inaccessible.

Band 7 does **not** require every sentence to be long. Use an accurate
mixture of simple, compound, and complex sentences.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Paraphrasing System

Do not paraphrase by mechanically replacing every word with a synonym.
Preserve meaning while changing vocabulary and/or grammatical structure
naturally.

### Method A --- Change reporting structure

Original: \> Some people believe that online learning is more effective
than classroom education.

Paraphrase: \> It is often argued that studying through digital
platforms is more effective than receiving instruction in a conventional
classroom.

### Method B --- Change word forms

Original: \> Technology has changed the way people communicate.

Paraphrase: \> Technological developments have brought significant
changes to the way individuals communicate with one another.

### Method C --- Reconstruct the idea

Original: \> More people are buying products online rather than visiting
shops.

Paraphrase: \> The rapid growth of e-commerce has significantly changed
consumer behaviour, with an increasing number of people choosing digital
marketplaces over traditional stores.

### Method D --- Change sentence focus

Original: \> Governments should spend more money on public transport.

Paraphrase: \> Greater public investment in transportation systems is
often regarded as an important government responsibility.

### Paraphrasing checklist

-   Is the original meaning unchanged?
-   Have important words/concepts been transformed naturally?
-   Has the grammatical structure changed?
-   Is the new sentence accurate?
-   Have unnatural synonyms been avoided?
-   Has no new opinion been accidentally added?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. The 5-Minute Task 2 Introduction System

A strong introduction normally needs:

1.  **Sentence 1 --- Paraphrase the question**
2.  **Sentence 2 --- Give the position and/or essay direction**

Example:

> In recent years, technological advancements have significantly changed
> the way people purchase goods and services, with online shopping
> becoming increasingly popular. Although this development has several
> disadvantages, I believe its benefits, particularly in terms of
> convenience and consumer choice, are considerably greater.

Avoid memorized hooks, unnecessary definitions, irrelevant background,
and very long introductions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Useful Sentence Structures

### Cause and effect

-   Because X, Y.
-   Since X, Y.
-   X, thereby leading to Y.
-   X, which can result in Y.
-   Consequently, ...
-   As a result, ...

### Concession

-   Although X, Y.
-   While X can provide several benefits, it may also...
-   Despite the fact that X, ...

### Condition

-   If governments invest in X, ...
-   Unless appropriate measures are introduced, ...
-   Provided that X, ...

### Relative clauses

-   Students who live in remote areas...
-   Renewable energy, which produces fewer emissions,...

### Participle structures

-   By reducing commuting time, remote work can...
-   Having access to digital resources, students can...

Use complexity naturally. Accuracy is more important than complexity.

## 9. Required Format for Every Topic/Category Sheet

Every category must follow this architecture.

### Part A --- Topic Map

Show the major directions from which IELTS may test the issue.

### Part B --- 500--1000 Word IELTS-Focused Reading Passage

The passage must: - be written for IELTS Writing preparation; - include
positive and negative perspectives where relevant; - explain causes,
effects, consequences, solutions, and examples; - use natural Band 7+
language; - contain varied sentence structures and cohesive devices; -
avoid unnecessarily obscure vocabulary; - naturally include the 10
selected vocabulary items; - provide reusable ideas without encouraging
memorization.

### Part C --- 10 High-Value Vocabulary Items

For every item give: - word/phrase; - simple meaning; - useful
collocation; - IELTS-style example sentence.

### Part D --- Collocation Bank

Teach phrases, not only isolated words.

Example: - reduce educational inequality; - widen economic inequality; -
address social inequality; - unequal access to resources.

### Part E --- Idea Bank

Use:

**Idea → Why → How → Result → Example**

Where relevant include positive, negative, individual, government,
business/society, and solution perspectives.

### Part F --- Reusable Sentence Patterns

Give approximately 5--8 useful patterns. For each: 1. pattern; 2.
function; 3. new example.

### Part G --- Paraphrasing Practice

Provide approximately five IELTS-style prompts. For each: - original
prompt; - key concepts; - possible transformations; - model
paraphrase; - short explanation.

### Part H --- Idea-Development Exercises

Give at least three basic ideas and ask the learner to develop each
through:

**WHY → HOW → RESULT → EXAMPLE**

Put model development after the exercises so the learner can attempt
them first.

### Part I --- IELTS Question Bank

Provide approximately 5--10 realistic Task 2 questions across different
question types.

## 10. Vocabulary Rules

1.  Prioritize useful academic vocabulary and collocations over rare
    words.
2.  Avoid language that sounds memorized or unnatural.
3.  Include about 10 core vocabulary items for every major category.
4.  Use those items naturally in the reading passage.
5.  Teach common collocations.
6.  Recycle high-value language across related chapters.
7.  Distinguish formal writing vocabulary from conversational language.
8.  Never encourage forcing every new word into an essay.
9.  Accuracy and appropriateness matter more than rarity.
10. Include active-production exercises.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Cohesion Rules

Use linking expressions naturally, including: - Furthermore, -
Moreover, - In addition, - However, - Nevertheless, - By contrast, -
Consequently, - As a result, - For instance, - In particular, -
Therefore, - While... - Although...

Do not overload paragraphs with connectors. Cohesion should also come
from logical progression, referencing, repeated key concepts, synonyms,
and grammatical connections.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Task 2 Essay Types

Train: 1. Opinion / Agree--Disagree 2. Discuss Both Views + Opinion 3.
Advantages--Disadvantages 4. Advantages Outweigh Disadvantages 5.
Problems/Causes + Solutions 6. Two-Part / Direct Questions

For every type cover: - question analysis; - position; - planning; -
introduction; - body organization; - conclusion; - common mistakes; -
timed practice.

Use flexible frameworks, not rigid memorized templates.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Task 1 Preparation

Recommended study allocation:

**Task 2: approximately 70%**\
**Task 1: approximately 30%**

For Academic IELTS train: - line graphs; - bar charts; - pie charts; -
tables; - mixed charts; - processes; - maps.

Focus on selecting key features, writing a clear overview, making
comparisons, grouping information, accurate data language, and avoiding
unsupported explanations/opinions.

## 14. Six-Week Roadmap

### Week 1 --- Paraphrasing + Sentence Development

Goals: - learn assessment requirements; - master IDEA → WHY → HOW →
RESULT → EXAMPLE; - practise sentence expansion; - practise
paraphrasing; - build accurate complex sentences; - start an error log.

Output: - 10+ paraphrased questions; - 15--20 developed ideas; - 5+
short paragraph exercises; - introductory Task 1 practice.

### Week 2 --- Body Paragraph Construction

Goals: - topic sentences; - explanation; - cause/effect; - examples; -
paragraph unity; - cohesion; - grammatical accuracy; - continued
paraphrasing.

Output: - 7--10 complete body paragraphs; - several introductions; -
daily topic reading; - Task 1 overview/comparison exercises.

### Week 3 --- Topic Mastery I

Study: 1. Education 2. Technology & Internet 3. Environment 4. Health &
Lifestyle 5. Government & Public Spending

For each: read, collect language, transform sentence patterns, develop
arguments, and write at least one paragraph.

### Week 4 --- Topic Mastery II

Study: 6. Work & Employment 7. Crime & Punishment 8. Family & Society 9.
Media & Advertising 10. Culture & Globalization 11. Universal themes

Continue paraphrasing, correction, Task 1, and complete essays.

### Week 5 --- Essay-Type Mastery

-   Day 1: Opinion / Agree--Disagree
-   Day 2: Discuss Both Views
-   Day 3: Advantages / Disadvantages
-   Day 4: Problems/Causes + Solutions
-   Day 5: Two-Part Questions
-   Day 6: Mixed Task 2 practice
-   Day 7: Timed Task 2 + detailed review

### Week 6 --- Exam Mode

Focus on: - timed writing; - rapid planning; - accurate introductions; -
developed body paragraphs; - concise conclusions; - Task 1 + Task 2
simulations; - proofreading; - recurring-error review.

Learn very little new vocabulary during the final week.

## 15. Daily Routine

### Office / Break Session --- 25--35 minutes

1.  Read the day's topic sheet.
2.  Collect:
    -   3 reusable ideas;
    -   3 vocabulary items/collocations;
    -   2 sentence structures.
3.  Paraphrase one IELTS question.
4.  If time permits, write one topic sentence.

### Home Session --- 35--45 minutes

1.  Select one simple idea.
2.  Ask WHY?
3.  Ask HOW?
4.  Identify the RESULT.
5.  Create an EXAMPLE.
6.  Turn the chain into one coherent paragraph.
7.  Compare with feedback/model.
8.  Add repeated mistakes to the error log.

On selected days, replace paragraph practice with Task 1 or a timed Task
2 essay.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 16. Weekly Review

At the end of each week check:

### Ideas

Can the learner generate 2--3 arguments for studied topics without
notes?

### Vocabulary

Can the learner use new vocabulary in original sentences?

### Structures

Can the learner reuse grammatical patterns with different topics?

### Paraphrasing

Can the learner paraphrase accurately and quickly?

### Errors

Track recurring problems: - articles; - tense; - subject--verb
agreement; - singular/plural; - prepositions; - word forms; -
punctuation; - fragments; - run-on sentences; - awkward vocabulary; -
unclear referencing.

Prioritize recurring errors.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 17. AI Tutor Correction Protocol

When correcting writing, do not immediately replace it with a perfect
essay.

### Step 1 --- Diagnostic assessment

Comment separately on: - Task Response; - Coherence and Cohesion; -
Lexical Resource; - Grammatical Range and Accuracy.

Any band estimate must be presented as an approximate diagnostic, not an
official score.

### Step 2 --- Identify the 3--5 highest-priority problems

### Step 3 --- Specific correction format

**Learner sentence:**\
...

**Problem:**\
...

**Improved version:**\
...

**Why:**\
...

### Step 4 --- Idea-development check

For each body paragraph ask: - Is there one clear central idea? - Is it
explained? - Is the cause/mechanism clear? - Is the consequence clear? -
Is the example relevant?

### Step 5 --- Model rewrite only after analysis

### Step 6 --- Targeted exercise

Give a short exercise based on the learner's recurring weakness.

## 18. Active Reading Method

The 500--1000-word passages are training material, not material to
memorize.

### First read --- Meaning

Understand the arguments.

### Second read --- Language

Mark collocations, connectors, and sentence patterns.

### Third stage --- Extraction

Collect: - 3 ideas; - 3 collocations; - 2 sentence patterns.

### Fourth stage --- Transformation

Create new sentences using the patterns.

### Fifth stage --- Recall

Close the sheet and explain the topic in writing from memory.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 19. Exam-Condition Idea Generation

Before writing, ask:

### WHO?

Individuals? Students? Workers? Parents? Businesses? Governments?
Communities?

### WHAT AREA?

Economic? Educational? Environmental? Social? Cultural? Health-related?
Technological?

### POSITIVE OR NEGATIVE?

What is the benefit/problem?

### WHY?

Why does it happen?

### CONSEQUENCE?

What happens as a result?

### EXAMPLE?

What realistic general example demonstrates it?

Example:

**Automation → repetitive jobs replaced → some workers lose employment →
income insecurity → retraining programs needed.**

# Final Principle

The objective is not:

> "I have read many Band 8 essays."

The objective is:

> **"When I receive an unfamiliar IELTS question, I can quickly
> understand it, generate relevant ideas, paraphrase it naturally,
> develop those ideas logically, express them using accurate and varied
> English, and complete the task within the exam time."**

Every future lesson, chapter, exercise, correction, and PDF should serve
that objective.
