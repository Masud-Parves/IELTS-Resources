# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Advantages & Disadvantages Essay Guide

> **Goal:** Band 7+ with one fixed structure you can remember under exam pressure.
> **Length:** minimum **260 words**, target **about 330–360 words** (a topic sentence plus long, varied sentences in the body paragraphs push it well above 260 naturally).
> **Rule:** same 4 paragraphs every time. Only the *opinion sentence* changes with the question type.

---

## 1. Know the Question Type First (30 seconds)

Read the instruction line carefully. It tells you whether you need an opinion.

| Type | Typical instruction | Opinion needed? | Your answer in one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type 1 — Discuss** | *Discuss the advantages and disadvantages.* / *What are the advantages and disadvantages?* | **No** | No opinion sentence — skip it |
| **Type 2 — Outweigh** | *Do you think the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?* / *To what extent do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?* | **Yes** | "In my opinion, the advantages clearly outweigh the disadvantages." |
| **Type 3 — More / Positive or negative** | *Are there more advantages or disadvantages?* / *Is this a positive or negative development?* | **Yes** | "In my view, this trend has more benefits than drawbacks." |

**Memory trick:** if the question contains **"you"**, **"outweigh"**, **"more"** or **"positive/negative"** → give your opinion in the introduction **and** the conclusion.

> **Which side should I choose?** Choose the side you can explain more easily. For most questions, **"advantages outweigh"** is the safest choice, because Body Paragraph 1 (advantages) already supports it and the BP2 summary sentence can show that the problems are manageable.
>
> **Whichever side you support goes after the comma** in your *Although* (intro) and *While* (conclusion) sentences. See Section 2, the emphasis rule.

## 2. The Fixed Structure (memorise this)

| Paragraph | Content | Sentences | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | a. Paraphrase the question<br>b. **Although** + 2 points of the side you do **not** support, + 2 points of the side you **support**<br>c. Opinion (Type 2 & 3 only — **skip for Type 1**) | 3 (Type 1: 2) | **40–50** (Type 1: 30–40) |
| **Body Paragraph 1** | **Always advantages** — topic sentence + 2 advantages (PEEL) + summary sentence | 7 | **120–135** |
| **Body Paragraph 2** | **Always disadvantages** — topic sentence + 2 disadvantages (PEEL) + summary sentence | 7 | **120–135** |
| **Conclusion** | a. New paraphrase of the topic<br>b. **While** + the side you do **not** support, + the side you **support**<br>c. Restate opinion (Type 2 & 3 only — **skip for Type 1**) | 3 (Type 1: 2) | **35–45** (Type 1: 25–35) |
| **Total** | | | **≈ 330–360** (Type 1: ≈ 310–340) |

### Why two points in each body paragraph?
Almost every question says **advantages** and **disadvantages** (plural). Writing **two** of each fully answers the question, which protects your **Task Response** score.

### Why "Although" and "While"?
- **Intro, sentence 2 → always start with *Although*.**
- **Conclusion, sentence 2 → always start with *While*.**

They are the same grammar pattern (a complex sentence with a contrast), so you only have to remember **one** sentence shape. It also mentions both sides in one sentence, which looks balanced and shows the examiner a complex structure straight away.

### ⭐ The emphasis rule: your side always goes LAST

In an *Although / While* sentence, the **first clause is the weaker idea** (the one you accept but do not support) and the **main clause after the comma is the stronger idea** (the one you agree with). The reader always takes the **last idea** as your position.

> **Although** [the side I do NOT support], **[the side I SUPPORT]**.

| Your opinion | *Although / While* clause (weaker side) | Main clause after the comma (your side) | Opinion sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Advantages outweigh** (Type 2) / **More advantages, positive** (Type 3) | 2 **disadvantages** | 2 **advantages** | *…the advantages clearly outweigh the disadvantages.* |
| **Disadvantages outweigh** (Type 2) / **More disadvantages, negative** (Type 3) | 2 **advantages** | 2 **disadvantages** | *…the disadvantages outweigh the advantages.* |
| **Type 1 — Discuss** (no opinion) | 2 **disadvantages** (default) | 2 **advantages** (default) | None — skip the opinion sentence |

**The same order is used in both places:** the *Although* sentence in the introduction and the *While* sentence in the conclusion must point to the **same side**. If they point to different sides, your position becomes unclear and Task Response drops.

> **What never changes:** BP1 is always advantages and BP2 is always disadvantages, whatever your opinion. Only three things change with your opinion: the order inside *Although / While*, the opinion sentences, and the weighing in the BP2 summary sentence (Section 4B).

| ❌ Wrong (contradicts the opinion) | ✅ Right |
|---|---|
| *Although studying abroad builds independence and improves careers, it causes culture shock and language problems. In my opinion, the **advantages** outweigh the disadvantages.* (The main clause stresses the problems, but the opinion supports the benefits.) | *Although studying abroad can cause culture shock and language problems, it builds independence and improves careers. In my opinion, the **advantages** outweigh the disadvantages.* |

> ⚠️ **Grammar warning:** never use *but* after *Although* or *While*.
> ❌ *Although students struggle with language, **but** they gain experience.*
> ✅ *Although students struggle with language, they gain experience.*

## 3. Introduction Template

### Sentence formulas

**a. Paraphrase (1 sentence, ~15–20 words)**
> *Nowadays / In recent years,* **[paraphrase of the topic using synonyms + different word order].**

**b. Although sentence (1 sentence, ~20–25 words)** — the side you do **not** support first, the side you **support** last (the emphasis rule, Section 2)

| Your opinion | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Advantages outweigh** (and Type 1 default) | **Although** [topic] **can/may** [disadvantage 1] **and** [disadvantage 2], **it also** [advantage 1] **and** [advantage 2]. |
| **Disadvantages outweigh** | **Although** [topic] **can/may** [advantage 1] **and** [advantage 2], **it often** [disadvantage 1] **and** [disadvantage 2]. |

*Example (same question, both opinions):*
- **Advantages outweigh:** *Although they often struggle with cultural and linguistic challenges, they also gain valuable life experience and better career prospects.*
- **Disadvantages outweigh:** *Although studying abroad can build independence and improve career prospects, it often exposes students to serious cultural and linguistic difficulties.*

**c. Third sentence — choose by question type**

| Type | Sentence c |
|---|---|
| Type 1 — Discuss | **No sentence c.** Stop after the *Although* sentence. |
| Type 2 — Advantages outweigh | *In my opinion, the advantages of [topic] clearly outweigh the disadvantages.* |
| Type 2 — Disadvantages outweigh | *In my opinion, the disadvantages of [topic] outweigh the advantages.* |
| Type 3 — More advantages / positive | *In my view, this development brings more benefits than drawbacks.* |
| Type 3 — More disadvantages / negative | *In my view, this development brings more drawbacks than benefits.* |

> 🚫 **Type 1 has no opinion sentence and no "plan" sentence.** If the question does not ask for your opinion, simply leave sentence c out. The introduction has 2 sentences: paraphrase + *Although*.
>
> Do **not** write a plan sentence such as *"This essay will examine both the benefits and the drawbacks of this trend."* because:
> - it sounds **formal and template-like**, not academic, and examiners recognise it as a **memorised sentence**. Memorised language is not credited and can lower your Lexical Resource score;
> - it adds **no idea**, because your *Although* sentence has already shown both sides, which is all the outline a Type 1 essay needs;
> - it uses words you could spend on a better explanation or example in the body paragraphs.

## 4. Body Paragraph Rules (for both BP1 and BP2)

> **General rules for every essay type:** see [IELTS_Body_Paragraph_Rules.md](IELTS_Body_Paragraph_Rules.md). This essay type is the one exception to its 120-word limit, because each paragraph has two points.

Every body paragraph has the **same 7-sentence shape**:

| # | Role | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| S0 | **Topic sentence** | Introduces the paragraph's side (advantages / disadvantages) in **general** words — does **not** name the two points (the introduction has already done that) |
| S1 | **Point 1** | States the first advantage/disadvantage |
| S2 | **Explain** | Why / how it happens |
| S3 | **Example** | A specific, realistic situation that **shows S2 happening** — written as a **complex or compound-complex** sentence, never a short simple one |
| S4 | **Point 2** | States the second advantage/disadvantage |
| S5 | **Explain + Result** | Why **Point 2** matters and what it leads to |
| S6 | **Summary sentence** | Restates the paragraph's main idea in *general* words (see 4B) |

**Word target:** 7 sentences × 15–25 words ≈ **120–135 words**.

### ⭐ The Example rule — one idea from Point to Example

**Your rule:** we share our Point, explain it, then give an example of that explanation. Each sentence continues the one before it. This paragraph has two points, but only Point 1 has an example, so **S1 → S2 → S3 must be one chain** and **S4 → S5 must be another**.

| ❌ Same topic, different idea | ✅ Shows the explanation |
|---|---|
| **S2:** *Without safe parks or playgrounds nearby, they often stay indoors; as a result, they spend more time on screens.* **S3:** *a boy who lives beside a busy road may develop breathing problems.* (S2 is about staying indoors, but S3 is about pollution.) | **S2:** the same sentence. **S3:** *a boy in a small city flat with no playground nearby may spend every evening watching videos, which can leave him unfit and unhealthy.* |

**The 5-second test:** read S2 and S3 together.
1. Underline the key situation in S2 (*no parks, stays indoors, screens*).
2. Look for the same situation in S3 (*no playground, every evening watching videos*).
3. If it is not there, change the example. An example that only fits the general topic does not count.

**Plan the example first.** In your 3-minute plan, write Point 1 and its example, then write the S2 sentence that leads to it.

> **Why it matters for band 7:** the Task Response descriptor asks you to *"extend and support"* main ideas, and it warns that supporting ideas *"may lack focus"*. One weak example will not cost you the band, but a focused example in every paragraph is the safest way to show development.

### Fixed topic sentences (S0) — learn these two by heart

| Paragraph | Template | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **BP1** | *There is little doubt that [topic] offers considerable advantages to [people].* | Complex (*that*-clause) |
| **BP2** | *On the other hand, it cannot be denied that [topic] also has some drawbacks.* | Complex (*that*-clause) |

**Why these work**
- **Clear and short:** the introduction's *Although* sentence has already named all four points, so the topic sentence only has to say *which side* this paragraph covers.
- **No overlap with S6:** the summary sentence does the general restating at the end; the topic sentence stays neutral at the start, so the paragraph does not open and close with the same idea.
- **Not basic:** both are complex sentences (*There is little doubt **that** …* / *it cannot be denied **that** …*), so the paragraph starts with grammar range, not a plain simple sentence.
- **Reusable:** only *[topic]* and *[people]* change from question to question.

| ❌ Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| *Studying abroad offers two key benefits for young people.* | Counting points sounds mechanical and simple |
| *Nowadays, studying abroad offers significant benefits …* | *Nowadays* belongs to the introduction (trend), and repeats its first word |
| *Studying abroad has personal and career benefits.* | Names the points — repeats the introduction and the summary |

### 4A. Sentence variety — the key to band 7 in Grammar (GRA)

The band 7 descriptor asks for **"a variety of complex structures"** and **"frequent error-free sentences"**. Short, simple sentences (*"The first problem is culture shock."*) keep you at band 6. Use this mix in **every** body paragraph — it comes from Part 5 (Sentence Structure Toolkit) of your idea bank chapters:

| Sentence type | How many per paragraph | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Long simple** (rich noun phrase) | 0–1 (usually S6) | [long subject] + verb + object | *Overall, living and learning abroad prepares young people for both adult life and the global job market.* |
| **Compound** | 1 | Clause, **and / but / so** clause · Clause**; as a result,** clause | *…; as a result, their mental health and academic performance may suffer.* |
| **Complex** | 3–4 | **that / because / since / as / if / when / although / which / who** | *There is little doubt that …* · *If they cannot express …, they may …* |
| **Compound-complex** | 1 (ideal for S3) | Complex + compound in one sentence | *For example, a … student who … while …, soon learns …, and this skill …* |
| **Special structure** (optional, max 1) | 0–1 | *not only … but also*, participle phrase, cleft (*It is … that …*) | *…an international degree not only broadens … but also improves …* |

**Rules for long, clear sentences**
- Aim for **15–25 words** per sentence. Under 12 words looks basic; over 35 words usually creates errors.
- **The example (S3) must never be a short simple sentence.** Add a relative clause (*who / which*), a time clause (*while / when*) or a result (*, and this …*). An example is where examiners check whether you can control longer sentences.
- Do **not** start two sentences in a row with the same word (*Students … Students …*).
- **Accuracy first:** a correct compound sentence scores higher than a broken complex sentence. Only use structures you have practised.

### 4B. The final summary sentence (S6)

After you have discussed both points with PEEL, the **last sentence of each body paragraph restates the main idea of the paragraph — without repeating the exact points.** Repeating the same words sounds mechanical and makes the paragraph feel badly structured; a general summary closes the paragraph naturally.

**How:** turn each specific point into a **broader category**.

| Specific point in the paragraph | General word in the summary |
|---|---|
| personal growth / independence | *adult life* |
| career prospects / jobs | *the global job market* |
| culture shock / homesickness | *social challenges* |
| language barrier | *academic challenges* |
| cost / debt | *financial pressure* |
| pollution / traffic | *environmental problems* |

| ❌ Boring (repeats the exact points) | ✅ Better (restates in general words) |
|---|---|
| *Therefore, personal growth and career opportunities are the two advantages of studying abroad.* | *Overall, living and learning abroad prepares young people for both adult life and the global job market.* |
| *So culture shock and language barrier are the disadvantages.* | *Although these social and academic challenges can make the first year stressful, most students overcome them once they adapt.* |

**Summary starters:** *Overall, …* · *In short, …* · *Taken together, these …* · *All in all, …* · *Although these … can …, …*

**BP2 summary sentence changes with the question type:**

| Type | BP2 last sentence |
|---|---|
| **Type 1 — Discuss** | Summary only → *Overall, these social and academic challenges can make the first year abroad stressful.* |
| **Type 2 & 3 — Advantages side** | Summary **+ weighing** (problems in the *Although* clause, "they are manageable" after the comma) → *Although these social and academic challenges can make the first year stressful, most students overcome them once they adapt.* |
| **Type 2 & 3 — Disadvantages side** | Summary **+ weighing** ("some people cope" in the *Although* clause, "the problems are serious" after the comma) → *Although some students eventually adapt, these social and academic challenges often cause lasting damage to their well-being and studies.* |

> The BP2 summary follows the **same emphasis rule** as the introduction: the weaker idea goes in the *Although* clause, and your side goes after the comma.
>
> **Why the weighing matters:** in an "outweigh" essay, the examiner wants to see *why* one side is stronger. Adding it to the summary sentence keeps your position clear through the whole essay — a key requirement for Task Response band 7 — without adding an extra sentence.

### 4C. Linkers — the chain that holds the paragraph together (Coherence & Cohesion)

Every sentence should connect to the one before it. Use **one linker or one reference word per sentence**, following this fixed chain:

| # | BP1 linker chain | BP2 linker chain | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 | *There is little doubt that …* | *On the other hand, it cannot be denied that …* | Opens the side; BP2's linker marks the switch |
| S1 | *To begin with, …* | *Firstly, …* | Signals Point 1 |
| S2 | *This is because …* | *…; as a result, …* | Explains / shows effect |
| S3 | *For example, …* | *For instance, …* | Introduces the example |
| S4 | *In addition, …* | *Secondly, …* | Signals Point 2 |
| S5 | *As a result of …, … which …* | *If …, … and, consequently, …* | Explanation + result |
| S6 | *Overall, …* | *Although these …, …* | Closes the paragraph |

**Reference words also link sentences** — *this, these, such, they, them*: *"…, and **this** skill benefits them throughout life."* / *"Although **these** social and academic challenges …"*. Mixing linkers and reference words is what the band 7 descriptor calls *"a range of cohesive devices"*.

> ⚠️ **Do not overuse linkers.** Never stack two (*~~Moreover, in addition,~~*) and never start every sentence with *Moreover / Furthermore*. One per sentence, chosen from the chain above, is enough.

## 6. Conclusion Template

**a. New paraphrase (1 sentence, ~10–15 words)** — must be *different* from your intro paraphrase but keep the same meaning.
> *In conclusion, [topic said in a new way] has become increasingly common.*

**b. While sentence (1 sentence, ~20–25 words)** — repeat both sides with new words, **in the same order as your intro *Although* sentence** (your side last)

| Your opinion | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Advantages outweigh** (and Type 1 default) | **While** [people] **may/will** [disadvantage 1 & 2 paraphrased], [advantage 1 & 2 paraphrased]. |
| **Disadvantages outweigh** | **While** [topic] **can** [advantage 1 & 2 paraphrased], [disadvantage 1 & 2 paraphrased]. |

*Example (disadvantages outweigh):* *In conclusion, completing a degree overseas has become a common choice. While it can promote personal growth and a successful career, the social and language barriers that students face often damage their well-being and academic progress. Therefore, I believe the drawbacks of studying abroad outweigh its benefits.*

**c. Restate opinion**

| Type | Sentence c |
|---|---|
| Type 1 — Discuss | **No sentence c.** End the essay with the *While* sentence. |
| Type 2 — Advantages outweigh | *Therefore, I firmly believe that the benefits far outweigh the drawbacks.* |
| Type 2 — Disadvantages outweigh | *Therefore, I believe that the drawbacks outweigh the benefits.* |
| Type 3 — Positive / more advantages | *Overall, I believe this is a positive development.* |
| Type 3 — Negative / more disadvantages | *Overall, I believe this is a negative development.* |

## 7. Full Model Essay (Type 2 — Outweigh)

**Question:** *University students are increasingly studying abroad as part of their study. Do you think the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?*

**Idea bank used:** Chapter 01 Education → *Studying Abroad, Exchange Visits and Foreign Languages* (see Section 8).

**Introduction** (47 words)
> Nowadays, a growing number of university students are completing part of their education at institutions overseas. Although they often struggle with cultural and linguistic challenges, they also gain valuable life experience and better career prospects. In my opinion, the advantages of studying abroad clearly outweigh the disadvantages.

**Body Paragraph 1 — Advantages** (134 words)
> There is little doubt that studying overseas offers considerable advantages to young people. To begin with, the most significant benefit is the personal growth that comes from living independently. This is because students living away from their parents must manage money and solve everyday problems, which builds confidence and maturity. For example, a Bangladeshi student who lives in Canada while studying soon learns to plan a budget and solve problems, and this skill benefits them throughout life. In addition, an international degree not only broadens students' horizons but also improves their career prospects. As a result of daily contact with native speakers, these students develop strong language skills and a global outlook, which multinational employers highly value. Overall, living and learning abroad prepares young people for both adult life and the global job market.

**Body Paragraph 2 — Disadvantages** (135 words)
> On the other hand, it cannot be denied that studying overseas also has some drawbacks. Firstly, many students experience culture shock when they suddenly face unfamiliar food, customs and social rules. Without their families nearby, they often feel lonely and homesick; as a result, their mental health and academic performance may suffer. For instance, a student who spends every weekend alone in a dormitory may lose the motivation to attend classes, which can lead to poor grades. Secondly, the language barrier can be a serious obstacle, as even students with good English may struggle to follow fast lectures. If they cannot express their ideas clearly, they may avoid class discussions and, consequently, fall behind their classmates. Although these social and academic challenges can make the first year stressful, most students overcome them once they adapt.

**Conclusion** (41 words)
> In conclusion, studying overseas has become increasingly common. While learners will inevitably face social and language barriers, overcoming them leads to personal growth and a successful career. Ironically, it is these very hardships that make the experience more advantageous than disadvantageous.

**Total: 357 words** ✅ (well above the 260 minimum)

| BP2 S1 | Point 1 | *Firstly* | Complex (time *when*) |

**Rules for borrowing**
1. **Same theme:** find the category that matches the question first (e.g. *studying abroad* → Education → Studying Abroad).
2. **Same main ideas:** take your 2 advantages and 2 disadvantages from that category's Quick Idea Bank row or free writing.
3. **Same sentence patterns:** reuse the toolkit structures, but **change the words to fit the question** — never copy a whole paragraph.
4. **Practise before the exam:** for each category, memorise 2–3 flexible sentences (one Point, one Explain, one Summary) that you can adapt to many questions.
5. **Spare ideas:** if a point does not fit, the same passage usually has a back-up (here: *cost and inequality*, *brain drain*).

## 9. Same Structure, Other Question Types

### Type 1 — Discuss (no opinion)

**Question:** *More and more people are working from home instead of in an office. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this trend.*

**Intro**
> These days, more and more employees are working from home rather than in an office. Although remote work can cause isolation and weaker teamwork, it also saves commuting time and gives workers greater flexibility.

*Only 2 sentences: the question does not ask for an opinion, so there is no sentence c.*

**Body paragraphs:** BP1 = saving time & money on commuting / flexible schedule. BP2 = loneliness / weaker teamwork and communication. **BP2 summary sentence without weighing** — e.g. *Overall, these social and professional difficulties can make remote work less satisfying for some employees.* (Ideas: Chapter 06 Work idea bank.)

**Conclusion**
> In conclusion, remote working has become common in many industries. While home-based employees may feel lonely and find cooperation harder, they enjoy shorter travel times and more control over their day.

*Only 2 sentences: no opinion and no general "both sides" sentence. The* While *sentence closes the essay.*

### Type 3 — More advantages or disadvantages?

**Question:** *Many young people now spend a year travelling or working before starting university. Are there more advantages or disadvantages to this?*

**Intro**
> In recent years, many school leavers have started taking a year off to travel or work before university. Although this break may delay their studies and be expensive, it also builds independence and gives them clearer goals. In my view, this choice brings more benefits than drawbacks.

**Conclusion**
> In conclusion, taking a gap year before university is now a popular decision. While it can postpone graduation and be financially demanding, it helps young people mature and choose their courses with confidence. Overall, I believe this is a positive development.

## 11. Useful Language Bank (simple and safe)

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Paraphrase "increasingly" | *a growing number of*, *more and more*, *an increasing number of*, *it has become common for* |
| Paraphrase "advantages" | *benefits*, *positive effects*, *strengths*, *merits* |
| Paraphrase "disadvantages" | *drawbacks*, *negative effects*, *problems*, *downsides* |
| Adding a point | *Another major advantage is …*, *In addition, …*, *Furthermore, …* |
| Explaining | *This is because …*, *This means that …*, *In other words, …* |
| Giving an example | *For example, …*, *For instance, …*, *A clear example of this is …* |
| Showing a result | *As a result, …*, *Consequently, …*, *Therefore, …*, *This leads to …* |
| Contrasting | *On the other hand, …*, *However, …*, *Although …*, *While …* |

## 12. 40-Minute Exam Plan

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| **0–2 min** | Identify the type (Discuss / Outweigh / More). Underline the topic words. |
| **2–5 min** | Write 2 advantages + 2 disadvantages, each with a quick example. Decide your opinion (Type 2 & 3 only). |
| **5–9 min** | Introduction (Paraphrase → Although → Opinion; Type 1 stops after Although) |
| **9–19 min** | Body Paragraph 1 — advantages |
| **19–29 min** | Body Paragraph 2 — disadvantages (+ weighing sentence) |
| **29–34 min** | Conclusion (Paraphrase → While → Opinion; Type 1 stops after While) |
| **34–40 min** | Check: spelling, articles (a/the), plurals (-s), subject–verb agreement, word count |

**Quick word count:** count the words in 3 average lines, divide by 3 to get words per line, then multiply by your total lines.

## 13. Final Checklist (before you finish)

- [ ] I identified the type correctly and gave an opinion **only** if the question asked for one. For Type 1, there is no opinion sentence and no *This essay will …* sentence.
- [ ] My opinion is the **same** in the introduction, the BP2 summary sentence and the conclusion.
- [ ] Intro sentence 2 starts with **Although**; conclusion sentence 2 starts with **While** — and neither has *but*.
- [ ] In both sentences, the side I **support** comes **after the comma** (last), and both point to the same side.
- [ ] BP1 = **2 advantages**, BP2 = **2 disadvantages**, each explained with an example or result.
- [ ] Each example (S3) **shows the Explain sentence right before it** (S2): the same situation appears in both.
- [ ] Each body paragraph starts with the fixed **topic sentence** (*There is little doubt that … / On the other hand, it cannot be denied that …*).
- [ ] Each body paragraph ends with a **summary sentence in general words** (not repeating the exact points).
- [ ] Every body sentence has **one linker or reference word** from the chain in Section 4C.
- [ ] Each body paragraph mixes **compound, complex and compound-complex** sentences of 15–25 words, and the **example is never a short simple sentence**.
- [ ] My ideas and sentence patterns come from the matching **idea bank chapter**.
- [ ] My conclusion paraphrase is **different** from my introduction paraphrase.
- [ ] No new ideas in the conclusion.
- [ ] **260+ words**.
- [ ] Spelling of key words checked: *career, struggle, advantage, disadvantage, benefit, environment, opportunity, opinion*.

## 14. Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Copying the question words in the intro | Change words **and** sentence order |
| *Although …, but …* / *While …, but …* | Remove *but* |
| Putting the side you support in the *Although / While* clause | Your side always goes **after the comma**, because the last idea carries the emphasis (Section 2) |
| Adding an opinion, or a plan sentence such as *This essay will examine both the benefits and the drawbacks…*, to a Type 1 question | Skip sentence c in the introduction and conclusion; the *Although / While* sentence already covers both sides |
| Saying "advantages outweigh" but writing BP2 that sounds stronger than BP1 | Add the **weighing** to the BP2 summary sentence |
| Ending a body paragraph by repeating the exact points | Restate them in **general words** (Section 4B) |
| Many short sentences (*The first problem is culture shock.*) | Join ideas with *which / because / so / as a result* (Section 4A) |
| Inventing new ideas in the exam | Reuse the matching **idea bank** category (Section 8) |
| Listing 3–4 ideas without explaining them | Only 2 per paragraph, each **explained + example** |
| An example about the same topic but a different idea from S2 | Use the 5-second test (Section 4) and change the example |
| Repeating the intro sentences word-for-word in the conclusion | Paraphrase again with new words |
| Writing under 260 words | Follow the word plan: 45 + 128 + 128 + 40 ≈ 340 |
| Topic sentence that counts or names the points (*… offers two key benefits: …*) | Use the fixed general topic sentence (Section 4) |
| Sentences that do not connect to each other | Follow the linker chain (Section 4C) — one linker or reference word per sentence |
