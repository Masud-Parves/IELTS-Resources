## Part 1 — Idea Generation Flow {: .part}

<div class="flow">Culture → Globalisation: Benefits &amp; Harms → Traditional Culture Disappearing → Western Influence &amp; Global Pop Culture → Local Film, Music &amp; Arts → International Tourism: Benefits → Tourism &amp; Local Communities → Tourism &amp; Historical Sites → Adapting to Local Customs &amp; Tolerance → English as a Global Language → Learning Foreign Languages → Multinational vs Local Business → History, Heritage &amp; Passing Culture On</div>

When you see **any** culture, tourism, language or globalisation question, run through this chain from left to right and ask: *"Does this question touch this area?"* Then **stop at the 2 links that fit the question best**. Those 2 links become your two body paragraphs.

**Memory trick — every culture question is about the balance between LOCAL and GLOBAL. Use P-S-A: Protect – Share – Adapt**

| Action | Key question | Typical ideas |
|---|---|---|
| **P — Protect** | How can we keep local culture alive? | festivals, mother tongue, museums, school lessons, support for local artists |
| **S — Share** | What do we gain from contact with other cultures? | tourism, foreign films, languages, trade, tolerance, new ideas |
| **A — Adapt** | How should cultures change without losing their identity? | modernise traditions, respect local customs as a visitor, combine old and new |

Most culture essays work well with **Share** in one body paragraph (benefits of globalisation, tourism, languages) and **Protect** in the other (risks to local identity and how to protect it). **Adapt** makes a mature conclusion.

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Example:** *"Some people think tourism should be banned in some areas to protect local culture."*

S → tourism brings income, jobs and cultural exchange ✔ — P → crowds and commercialisation can damage sacred sites and traditions ✔ — A → limit visitor numbers and train local guides instead of a total ban ✔.
Chain links: **Tourism & Local Communities** + **Tourism & Historical Sites**.

</div>

## Part 2 — Exam-Hall Brainstorming System {: .part}

### 2A. The 5-Minute Plan

| Minute | What to do | What you write on the question paper |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | **Decode the question.** Underline the topic words and the instruction words. Decide which of your five essay guides it uses (Part 2G). | e.g. *globalisation / advantages & disadvantages / opinion → Advantages / Disadvantages guide (Type 2, with opinion)* |
| 1–3 | **P-S-A grid.** Protect, Share, Adapt — one or two notes each. | 4–6 one-line notes |
| 3–4 | **Choose the 2 strongest ideas.** Add one reason and one example to each. | Idea 1 + why + example; Idea 2 + why + example |
| 4–5 | **Decide your position (one clear side, only if the question asks) and use the guide's word plan.** | Thesis / BP1 / BP2 / Conclusion in 4 lines |

### 2B. WHO is affected?

- **Young people** — global pop culture, social media trends, foreign films, fashion, language choice
- **Older generations** — keepers of traditions, stories, crafts and languages
- **Local communities** — festivals, crafts, community identity, effects of tourism
- **Tourists and travellers** — learn about cultures, must respect local customs
- **Local businesses and craftspeople** — compete with multinational brands; sell to tourists
- **Artists, musicians and filmmakers** — local creative industries, funding, global audiences
- **Language learners and speakers of minority languages** — English, language loss
- **Migrants** — adapting to a new culture while keeping their own
- **Governments** — cultural policy, heritage protection, tourism regulation
- **The world** — cultural diversity, international understanding

### 2E. How One Idea Becomes a Full Paragraph (PEEL)

**P**oint → **E**xplain (why? how?) → **E**xample → **L**ink / Result

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### বাংলায় চিন্তা (Bangla thinking)

**Point:** আন্তর্জাতিক পর্যটন স্থানীয় অর্থনীতিকে শক্তিশালী করে।
**Explain:** কারণ পর্যটকেরা হোটেল, খাবার, পরিবহন ও হস্তশিল্পে অর্থ ব্যয় করেন, যা স্থানীয় মানুষের আয় বাড়ায়।
**Example:** যেমন, কক্সবাজারে পর্যটনের কারণে হাজার হাজার মানুষ হোটেল, রেস্টুরেন্ট ও গাইড হিসেবে কাজ পেয়েছেন।
**Link/Result:** ফলে সরকারও রাস্তা ও অন্যান্য অবকাঠামো উন্নয়নে বিনিয়োগ করতে উৎসাহিত হয়।

</div>

**The same idea in English (one paragraph):**

> **Point:** International tourism strengthens local economies.
> **Explain:** This is because tourists spend money on hotels, food, transport and handicrafts, which increases local people's incomes.
> **Example:** For example, tourism in Cox's Bazar has given thousands of people jobs in hotels and restaurants and as guides.
> **Link:** As a result, the government is also encouraged to invest in roads and other infrastructure.

Count it: about **55 words** from one idea. Add a second explanation (for example, cultural exchange between visitors and local people) and a second example to reach the **100–120 words** that one body paragraph needs.

### 2F. Worked Brainstorm Demo

<div class="question">Some people think that when a person travels to a different culture, they should adapt to the local practices and customs. To what extent do you agree?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · TOU-07 (also in the Part 7 Question Bank)*

**My 5-minute notes:**

```
Type: Agree / Disagree (to what extent) → ONE clear side: I completely agree
      (Agree/Disagree guide: never "largely" or "partly")
P-S-A: Adapt ✔ (main idea)  Protect ✔ (respect local culture)
       Share → goes into the R sentence (visitors keep their own beliefs)

Intro (35–50w): paraphrase + "I completely agree … because adapting shows
                respect for local people and gives visitors a better experience"
BP1 (100–120w) Idea 1 – P E E X L: respect for local people
   why: every society has rules about dress, greetings, religious sites
   ex: tourists at a mosque in Bangladesh dress modestly, remove shoes
   L: Therefore, adapting avoids offence
BP2 (100–120w) Idea 2 – P E X R L: a better travel experience
   why: locals welcome visitors who make an effort
   ex: a tourist who greets people in Bangla is invited to a family meal
   R: Although some argue adapting means giving up one's identity, visitors
      can follow customs in public and keep their beliefs in private
   L: Consequently, …
Conclusion (35–55w): In conclusion, I firmly believe … + Idea 1 + Idea 2
```

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**কেন এই অবস্থান?** "To what extent" প্রশ্নেও "largely agree" নয়, একটিই স্পষ্ট পক্ষ নাও (Agree/Disagree guide)। নিজের বিশ্বাস ধরে রাখার যুক্তিটি ফেলে দিও না, বরং BP2-এর Response (R) বাক্যে ব্যবহার করো, তাহলে উত্তর পরিণত দেখায় কিন্তু আংশিক হয় না। P-S-A কৌশলের **Adapt** ও **Protect** এখানে কাজে লেগেছে। Idea chain-এর **Adapting to Local Customs & Tolerance** থেকে idea নেওয়া হয়েছে।

### 2G. Essay-Type Paragraph Plans (your five essay guides)

| Essay type (guide) | Introduction | Body Paragraph 1 | Body Paragraph 2 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agree / Disagree** (*to what extent? / do you agree?*) | **35–50:** paraphrase + *I completely agree/disagree with this view because* Idea 1 and Idea 2. Never "partly". | **100–120:** Idea 1 — P E E X L | **100–120:** Idea 2 — P E X R L (*Although some people argue that …, [my side]*) | **35–55:** *In conclusion, I firmly believe that …* + both ideas in new words |
| **Discuss Both Views** (*some think X, others think Y*) | **35–45:** *While some people argue that [other view], others believe that [my view]. I completely support the latter view because …* | **90–120:** the view you do **not** support, no "I" — P E E X L | **90–120:** **your view** — P E X R L | **35–45:** *In conclusion, people hold different views on whether … While …, I firmly believe that …* |
| **Advantages – Disadvantages** | **40–50:** paraphrase + *Although* [side you do not support], [side you support] + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **30–40**, 2 sentences) | **120–135:** advantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **120–135:** disadvantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **35–45:** *While …* + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **25–35**) |
| **Cause/Problem + Solution** | **30–40:** paraphrase + *This is mainly because C1 and C2, but S1 and S2 can tackle this problem.* No opinion. | **90–120:** 2 causes or problems — P E X P E L | **90–120:** 2 solutions, each fixing one cause (WHO + WHAT + HOW) | **30–40:** causes + solutions in new words |
| **Double Question** (*two questions*) | **30–40:** paraphrase + *[Answer 1], and [answer 2].* | **90–120:** answer to Q1 — List block (P E X P E L) or Opinion block (P E X R L) | **90–120:** answer to Q2 — List or Opinion block | **30–40:** *In conclusion,* answer 1 + answer 2 in new words |

*Full rules: `Writing/IELTS_*_Essay_Guide.md`. Questions asking only for solutions or only for problems use the Cause/Problem + Solution guide, Section 14: one idea in each body paragraph (P E E X L), no opinion.*

## Part 3 — Category-Wise Free Writing {: .part}

Each passage below is an **idea store**. Read it slowly, notice how each idea is explained and supported with an example, and pay attention to the **bold collocations**. After each passage, try to say 4–5 of its ideas aloud in your own words.

### Globalisation: Benefits and Harms

**Globalisation** refers to the growing connection between countries through trade, investment, technology, travel and culture. Products made in one country are sold all over the world, people communicate instantly across continents, and films, music and food from many cultures are available almost everywhere. Globalisation has transformed economies and societies, bringing both important benefits and serious concerns.

The most significant benefit of globalisation is **economic growth**. When countries trade freely, businesses can sell their products to much larger markets, which creates jobs and raises incomes. Many developing countries, including Bangladesh, have grown rapidly by exporting goods such as clothing to international markets. Foreign investment brings capital, technology and management skills, and consumers benefit from a wider choice of products at lower prices.

Globalisation also spreads **knowledge and technology**. Medical advances, new farming techniques, renewable energy and digital tools can quickly reach countries around the world. Students can study abroad or take online courses from foreign universities, and scientists can cooperate on global problems such as disease and climate change.

Culturally, globalisation encourages **exchange and understanding**. People can enjoy foreign music, films, literature and cuisine, travel more easily and make friends from different backgrounds. This contact can reduce prejudice and help people see that, despite cultural differences, they share many hopes and values.

However, globalisation also has serious disadvantages. **Multinational companies** with huge budgets can dominate markets, forcing small local businesses to close. Some companies move production to countries where wages are low and labour laws are weak, which can lead to poor working conditions. The benefits of global trade are not always shared fairly; the gap between rich and poor, both within and between countries, can grow wider.

There are also cultural concerns. Many people fear **cultural homogenisation**, in which the world becomes more and more similar as global brands, Western films and international fast food spread everywhere. Young people may become more interested in global celebrities and fashions than in their own traditions, languages and festivals. Over time, some local customs and smaller languages may disappear.

Globalisation can also increase environmental problems, because transporting goods around the world consumes large amounts of fuel and produces pollution. In addition, global economic connections mean that a crisis in one country can quickly affect many others.

The key is to manage globalisation wisely. Governments can protect workers' rights, support small businesses and invest in education so that more people benefit from new opportunities. At the same time, they can promote local culture through schools, the media and support for artists, ensuring that openness to the world does not mean **losing cultural identity**.

In conclusion, globalisation has brought economic growth, technological progress and cultural exchange, but it has also created inequality, environmental pressure and threats to local culture. With sensible policies, societies can **enjoy its benefits** while reducing its harms.

### Traditional Culture and Ways of Life Disappearing

As societies modernise, many traditional ways of life are disappearing. Young people move to cities, adopt global lifestyles and spend much of their time online, while older traditions, crafts, languages and festivals receive less attention. Many people are concerned about this loss and ask whether it is important to preserve traditional culture, and how this can be done.

There are several reasons why traditional ways of life are declining. **Urbanisation** is one of the most important. When people move from villages to cities for work and education, they often leave behind traditional occupations, such as farming, fishing and handicrafts, and the community events connected with them. **Globalisation and the media** also play a major role. Global films, music, fashion and food are widely available and heavily promoted, and many young people find them more exciting than local traditions, which they may see as old-fashioned.

Economic pressures contribute too. Traditional crafts, such as hand-weaving, pottery and woodwork, often take a long time to produce and cannot compete on price with factory-made goods. As a result, craftspeople may struggle to earn a living, and their children choose other careers. Busy modern lifestyles also leave families with less time to celebrate festivals or pass on traditional skills.

Preserving traditional culture is important for several reasons. Traditions form part of a nation's **identity**; they connect people to their history and give them a sense of belonging. Festivals such as Pohela Boishakh in Bangladesh bring people of different ages and backgrounds together and strengthen community ties. Traditions also contain **valuable knowledge**, such as sustainable farming methods, traditional medicine and ways of living in harmony with nature, that has been developed over generations. In addition, cultural diversity makes the world richer and more interesting, and traditional culture can attract tourists and support local economies.

However, preserving tradition does not mean refusing all change. Cultures have always evolved, and some traditional practices, such as those that are unfair to women or harmful to health, should change. The goal should be to keep the valuable parts of tradition while adapting to modern life.

There are many ways to protect traditional culture. **Schools** can teach local history, folk music, dance and crafts, and organise cultural events. **Governments** can support traditional artists and craftspeople through grants, training and markets for their products, and protect cultural heritage sites. **Tourism** can create demand for traditional performances, food and handicrafts. **Families** play the most important role by celebrating festivals together, telling traditional stories and speaking their mother tongue at home. Modern technology can also help, as traditions can be recorded, shared and taught online.

In conclusion, traditional ways of life are disappearing mainly because of urbanisation, globalisation and economic change. Because traditions are an essential part of identity and knowledge, societies should make a conscious effort to **preserve and adapt** them for future generations.

### Western Influence and Global Popular Culture

In many parts of the world, Western culture, especially American and European culture, has a strong influence on daily life. Hollywood films, Western music, fast-food chains, fashion brands and social media platforms are popular almost everywhere. Some people welcome this influence as a sign of openness and modernity, while others worry that it threatens local cultures and values.

There are several reasons for the strong influence of Western culture. Western countries have large and wealthy entertainment industries that can produce high-quality films, music and television with huge budgets and global marketing. English, the main language of much of this content, is widely studied around the world. In addition, many people associate Western products and lifestyles with success, modernity and freedom.

Western influence can bring some **positive effects**. It can introduce new ideas about individual rights, gender equality, democracy and personal freedom. Exposure to foreign films and music can broaden people's horizons and encourage creativity. Learning about other cultures can also help people become more open-minded and tolerant. Moreover, local artists sometimes combine Western styles with traditional elements, creating new and exciting forms of music, fashion and film.

However, there are also **negative effects**. When young people are constantly exposed to Western media, they may begin to see their own culture as less attractive or outdated. They may prefer Western clothes to traditional dress, celebrate foreign festivals more enthusiastically than local ones, or speak English mixed with their mother tongue. Over time, this can weaken **cultural identity** and lead to the decline of local traditions.

Western influence can also affect values. Some films and programmes promote lifestyles, attitudes and behaviour that may conflict with local family values or religious beliefs. Consumerism, in particular, is often spread through Western advertising and media, encouraging people to buy branded products and fast food.

Foreign films are a clear example. Some people argue that they have a negative impact on local culture, because local film industries cannot compete with Hollywood's budgets and audiences prefer foreign productions. Others point out that foreign films can also inspire local filmmakers and help audiences understand other cultures.

The best response to Western influence is not to reject it completely, which would be unrealistic in a connected world, but to maintain a **healthy balance**. People can enjoy global culture while also valuing their own traditions. Governments can support local media, music and film, and schools can teach students to appreciate their cultural heritage. Families can encourage children to take part in local festivals and speak their mother tongue.

In conclusion, Western culture has a strong global influence, bringing new ideas and opportunities but also challenges to local identity. By combining **openness with pride** in their own culture, societies can benefit from global influences without losing what makes them unique.

### Local Film, Music and Arts Industries

Every society has its own music, art, literature, dance and film, which express its history, values and imagination. However, in a globalised world, local arts often face strong competition from international entertainment. Many people prefer to watch foreign films, listen to international music and buy global brands, which raises questions about how local creative industries can survive and whether governments should support them.

There are several reasons why people often prefer foreign films and music. International productions, especially from Hollywood and other large industries, usually have **much bigger budgets**, allowing them to use famous actors, advanced special effects and global marketing. They may also offer a wider variety of stories and genres. In contrast, local films may have limited budgets, simpler production quality and fewer cinemas willing to show them. In addition, young people who follow global trends on social media may see foreign entertainment as more fashionable.

Yet local arts are extremely important. Music and art are essential for both **society and the individual**. For society, they preserve cultural identity, record history and pass on values from one generation to the next. Traditional songs, such as folk and Baul music in Bangladesh, tell stories about village life, love, faith and struggle. Festivals with music and dance bring communities together and strengthen social bonds. For individuals, art provides a way to express emotions, relieve stress and develop creativity. Learning an instrument or taking part in a drama group can build confidence and discipline.

Local films tell stories that reflect local people's lives, problems and dreams in their own language. They can raise awareness of social issues, such as poverty, gender inequality or corruption, in a way that audiences can relate to. A successful local film can also attract international attention and present the country's culture to the world.

Many people therefore believe that governments should **support local creative industries**. Governments can provide funding and loans for filmmakers, build or renovate cinemas, offer training for young artists and organise film and music festivals. Some countries require cinemas or television channels to show a certain amount of local content. Others offer tax benefits for companies that invest in local productions.

However, government support should be used carefully. Funding should be distributed fairly and transparently, and governments should not use it to control the content of art or promote only views that suit them. Artists need **creative freedom** to explore difficult and sometimes controversial ideas, although this freedom should not be used to spread hatred or harm others.

At the same time, local artists can use modern technology to reach audiences directly through streaming platforms and social media, often at low cost.

In conclusion, local film, music and arts face strong international competition, but they play a vital role in preserving identity and enriching individual lives. With sensible government support, creative freedom and the use of new technology, local arts can **thrive alongside global entertainment**.

### International Tourism: Benefits

Tourism is one of the largest industries in the world. Millions of people travel every year to visit beaches, mountains, historical sites and cities in other countries. For many nations, including Bangladesh, tourism offers great potential for economic and social development. It brings important benefits to both individuals and society.

The most obvious benefit of tourism is **economic**. Tourists spend money on accommodation, food, transport, entertainment and souvenirs, which provides income for local businesses. Tourism creates **jobs** for hotel staff, tour guides, drivers, cooks, artists and craftspeople, many of whom may have limited opportunities in other industries. In areas such as Cox's Bazar, tourism supports thousands of families. Tourism also brings foreign currency into the country, which strengthens the national economy.

Tourism can also lead to better **infrastructure**. To attract visitors, governments often invest in roads, airports, electricity, water supply and communication networks. These improvements also benefit local residents, making it easier for them to travel, run businesses and access services.

Another important benefit is **cultural exchange**. When tourists visit a country, they learn about its history, traditions, food and way of life. At the same time, local people meet visitors from different backgrounds and learn about other cultures. This contact can reduce stereotypes and increase understanding and tolerance between nations. Many people believe that those who travel abroad become more open-minded and understanding of others.

For individuals, travel offers many personal benefits. It allows people to relax, escape from daily stress and enjoy new experiences. Travelling to new places can **broaden horizons**, build confidence and develop problem-solving skills, as travellers learn to deal with unfamiliar situations, languages and customs. Many people remember their journeys as some of the most valuable experiences of their lives.

Tourism can also encourage the **protection of culture and nature**. When local communities see that tourists are interested in their traditions, crafts and natural environment, they may feel prouder of their heritage and more motivated to protect it. Entrance fees to national parks and historical sites can provide money for conservation.

However, the benefits of tourism depend on good management. Without proper planning, tourism can damage the environment, increase prices for local people and turn traditions into commercial shows. Governments and communities must ensure that tourism is **sustainable**, respecting both nature and local culture.

With more people now watching travel programmes on television and social media, some people suggest that fewer people will want to pay to travel abroad. However, watching a video cannot replace the real experience of tasting local food, meeting people and seeing famous places in person, so tourism is likely to remain popular.

In conclusion, international tourism brings significant economic, cultural and personal benefits. When it is managed responsibly, it can create jobs, improve infrastructure, promote understanding between cultures and help preserve heritage.

### Tourism and Local Communities

Tourism can bring wealth and opportunities to local communities, but it can also create serious problems, especially when large numbers of visitors arrive in small towns, villages or remote areas. **Eco-tourism**, which involves visiting natural environments and traditional communities, has become popular, but some people worry that it may harm the very cultures and places that tourists come to see.

One major concern is that tourism can turn local culture into a **commercial product**. Traditional dances, ceremonies and crafts may be performed or produced mainly to entertain tourists, losing their original meaning. For example, a sacred ritual may be shortened and repeated several times a day for visitors, becoming more of a show than a spiritual event. Over time, local people may begin to value traditions only for the money they bring.

Tourism can also change local lifestyles and values. Contact with large numbers of visitors may encourage young people to copy foreign fashions, behaviour and consumer habits. Tourists who do not respect local customs, for example by wearing inappropriate clothing at religious sites or behaving loudly, can cause offence and tension.

There are also **economic and social problems**. When tourism grows rapidly, the cost of land, housing and food may rise, making life more expensive for local residents. Many tourism jobs are seasonal and low-paid, while much of the profit goes to large hotel chains and tour companies from outside the area. Local people may be pushed out of their traditional land to make space for hotels and resorts.

Environmental damage is another concern, especially in eco-tourism destinations. Large numbers of visitors can produce waste, disturb wildlife, pollute water and damage fragile ecosystems such as forests, coral reefs and mountains.

Because of these problems, some people argue that tourism should be **banned** in certain areas to protect local culture and nature. Others believe that change is inevitable and that banning tourism would remove important income and opportunities from poor communities.

A balanced approach is usually best. Instead of complete bans, governments can **limit visitor numbers** in sensitive areas, require permits and set rules for tourist behaviour. Local communities should be involved in planning and decision-making, so that tourism meets their needs and respects their values. Training local people as guides, cooks and hosts ensures that more of the income stays in the community. Tourists can be educated about local customs before they visit, and a portion of tourism income can be used to protect cultural sites and the environment.

In conclusion, tourism can harm local communities by commercialising culture, raising living costs and damaging the environment. However, with careful management, community involvement and responsible behaviour by visitors, tourism can **benefit local people** while preserving their culture and natural surroundings.

### Tourism and Historical Sites

Historical buildings and sites, such as ancient temples, mosques, palaces, forts and archaeological ruins, are among the most popular tourist attractions in the world. They tell the story of past civilisations and are a source of national pride. However, as tourism grows, many of these sites are being damaged, sometimes **beyond repair**.

There are several ways in which tourism damages historical sites. The sheer number of visitors can cause physical wear: millions of feet walking on ancient floors and stairs gradually wear them down, and people touching walls, carvings and paintings can damage delicate surfaces. Some visitors climb on monuments, carve their names into walls or take small pieces as souvenirs. **Litter, pollution and vandalism** are also common problems.

The development of tourist facilities can also cause harm. Hotels, restaurants, shops and roads built close to historical sites can spoil their appearance and, in some cases, damage their foundations. Air pollution from traffic can slowly erode stone buildings. In addition, the humidity created by large crowds in enclosed spaces, such as tombs and caves, can damage ancient paintings.

Protecting historical sites is important for several reasons. They are part of a nation's **cultural heritage** and identity, helping people understand their history and roots. They provide valuable information for historians and archaeologists. They also attract tourists, who bring income to local communities and the national economy. Once a historical site is destroyed, it can never be fully replaced.

There are many measures that can be taken to prevent damage. Governments can **limit the number of visitors** allowed at one time, for example through timed tickets or daily quotas. Some areas of a site can be closed to the public, and barriers, walkways and glass screens can protect fragile parts. Strict rules against touching, climbing and littering, enforced by guards and fines, can reduce careless behaviour.

Money from **entrance fees** can be used for maintenance, restoration and research. Higher fees for foreign tourists, as used in many countries, can raise extra funds while keeping access affordable for local people. Governments can also control construction near historical sites to protect their surroundings.

**Education** is also important. Guides, signs and information centres can explain why sites are fragile and how visitors should behave. Schools can teach children to respect their heritage. Technology offers new possibilities: virtual tours and digital models allow people to explore sites online, and replicas of particularly fragile areas can be built for visitors, as has been done at some famous caves.

In conclusion, tourism is causing serious damage to many historical sites, through overcrowding, careless behaviour and nearby development. By controlling visitor numbers, enforcing rules, investing entrance fees in conservation and educating visitors, societies can **protect their heritage** while still sharing it with the world.

### Adapting to Local Customs and Cultural Tolerance

When people travel to another country, either as tourists or to live and work, they encounter customs, beliefs and practices that may be very different from their own. This raises the question of whether visitors should adapt to local practices. It is also often said that people who travel are more tolerant and understanding of others.

There are strong reasons why visitors should **respect and adapt to local customs**. Every society has its own rules about dress, greetings, food, religious practices and social behaviour. When visitors follow these rules, they show respect for their hosts and avoid causing offence. For example, tourists visiting a mosque in Bangladesh are expected to dress modestly and remove their shoes. Respecting such customs helps to build good relationships between visitors and local people.

Adapting to local customs also improves the travel experience. Visitors who learn a few words of the local language, try local food and follow local manners are often welcomed more warmly. They are more likely to make friends, learn about the culture in depth and have meaningful experiences, rather than staying in a "tourist bubble".

For people who move abroad to live and work, adapting to the local culture is even more important. Learning the language, understanding workplace norms and taking part in community life can help migrants integrate, find jobs and feel at home.

However, adapting does not mean giving up one's own identity. Visitors can respect local customs in public behaviour while keeping their own beliefs, values and traditions in private. In some cases, a visitor may feel unable to follow a particular local practice, for example because of religious beliefs or health reasons. In such situations, a polite explanation is usually better than either ignoring the custom or acting against one's conscience.

Preparation makes adaptation much easier. Before travelling, visitors can read about the country's history, religion and etiquette, learn simple greetings and find out what clothing is appropriate. Many misunderstandings happen not because tourists want to be disrespectful, but simply because they do not know the local rules.

Travel can certainly make people more **tolerant and open-minded**. Meeting people from different backgrounds, seeing how they live and understanding their values can break down stereotypes and prejudices. A person who has experienced being a foreigner may become more understanding of immigrants and minorities in their own country.

However, travel does not automatically lead to tolerance. Some tourists spend their entire holiday in international hotels, eating familiar food and interacting only with other tourists. Others travel with closed minds and return with their prejudices confirmed. Tolerance depends more on a person's attitude and willingness to learn than on the act of travelling itself.

In conclusion, visitors should generally adapt to local customs out of respect for their hosts, while maintaining their own core identity. Travel can make people more tolerant, but only when they approach other cultures with **curiosity, humility and respect**.

### English as a Global Language and Language Loss

English has become the world's most widely used international language. It is the main language of international business, science, aviation, diplomacy, the internet and much of global entertainment. Tourism and globalisation have contributed greatly to its spread. Some people even predict that, in the future, everyone will choose to speak the same global language.

Having a common global language has clear **advantages**. It makes international communication much easier. Business people from different countries can negotiate deals, scientists can share research and tourists can travel more easily. A common language can help to reduce misunderstandings between nations and support international cooperation on global issues such as climate change and health. For individuals, speaking English can open up **career opportunities**, access to higher education abroad and a vast amount of information online.

However, the dominance of one language also has serious **disadvantages**. The most significant is the risk of **language loss**. Experts believe that many of the world's languages are endangered, and some disappear completely every year when their last speakers die. When young people see English, or another dominant national language, as the key to success, they may stop using their minority or local languages. Their children may then grow up unable to speak the language of their grandparents.

Losing a language means more than losing words. Each language contains a unique way of seeing the world, including knowledge about nature, medicine, history and relationships, as well as stories, songs, proverbs and humour. When a language disappears, much of this **cultural heritage** is lost forever. In Bangladesh, people understand the deep connection between language and identity because of the Language Movement of 1952, in which people gave their lives to protect the right to speak Bangla.

Language loss is also happening within countries. In Bangladesh, for example, several indigenous communities in the hill tracts and other regions have their own languages, but young people increasingly use Bangla or English at school and work. Without written materials, schools and media in these languages, they may gradually disappear.

A single global language could also give an unfair advantage to native English speakers and countries, both economically and culturally. People who cannot speak English well may be excluded from opportunities.

The best solution is **multilingualism**, in which people learn a global language such as English for international communication while continuing to use and value their mother tongue. Governments can support this by teaching local languages in schools, using them in media and public life, and recording endangered languages. Families play a crucial role by speaking their own language at home and passing on stories and songs to their children.

In conclusion, a global language like English brings real benefits for communication, business and education. However, if it replaces other languages, the world will lose valuable cultural diversity. People should therefore learn English while **protecting their own languages**.

### Learning Foreign Languages

In a globalised world, learning foreign languages has become increasingly important. Many schools teach English and sometimes a second foreign language, and millions of adults study languages for work, travel or personal interest. However, with the development of fast and accurate translation apps, some people argue that learning foreign languages is becoming a waste of time.

There are many benefits to learning a foreign language. The most practical is **career advantage**. Employers in international companies, tourism, diplomacy, trade and many other fields value employees who can communicate with foreign clients and partners. Speaking English or another major language can also allow students to study at foreign universities and access academic resources.

Learning a language also has **cognitive benefits**. It trains memory, concentration and problem-solving skills, and many people find that speaking two languages helps them switch more easily between tasks. In addition, learning a language helps people understand the structure of their own language better.

Perhaps most importantly, learning a language is a way to understand another **culture**. Language reflects how people think, what they value and how they relate to each other. Some people argue that it is impossible to fully understand a country's culture without speaking its language, because jokes, idioms, literature and many social customs depend on language. While it is possible to learn a lot about a culture through translation, speaking the language allows deeper and more personal connections with people.

**Translation technology** has improved greatly, and apps can now translate text and speech almost instantly. These tools are very useful for tourists and for quick communication. However, they still make mistakes, especially with complex sentences, idioms and cultural meanings. More importantly, relying on a machine cannot build the same trust and relationship as speaking directly to someone in their language. In business and personal relationships, the effort to learn someone's language shows respect.

There is also debate about the best age to start learning a language. Many experts believe that **children** learn languages more easily than adults, especially pronunciation, because their brains are more flexible and they are less afraid of making mistakes. For this reason, many schools introduce foreign languages in primary school. However, others argue that young children should first master their mother tongue, and that older learners can study grammar more efficiently.

Some people also believe that it is impossible to become fluent without living in a country where the language is spoken. Living abroad certainly provides constant practice and exposure. However, with online resources, films, music and conversation partners available on the internet, many people now reach a high level without leaving their own country.

In conclusion, despite advances in translation technology, learning foreign languages remains valuable for careers, brain development and cultural understanding. Language learning is not a waste of time but an **investment in personal and professional growth**.

### Multinational Companies versus Local Business

In many countries, large multinational companies, such as international fast-food chains, supermarkets, clothing brands and technology firms, are opening branches and factories. Some governments actively encourage foreign companies to invest, while many people believe that governments should focus on supporting local businesses instead.

Foreign companies can bring significant **benefits**. They invest large amounts of money, build factories and offices, and create thousands of jobs. They often introduce new technology, management skills and international standards, which local workers and businesses can learn from. Foreign investment can increase exports, bring in foreign currency and help a country integrate into the global economy. Consumers also benefit from a wider range of products and, in some cases, lower prices and higher quality.

However, multinational companies can create problems for **local businesses**. With huge budgets for marketing, low prices and well-known brands, they can attract customers away from small local shops, restaurants and producers. For example, when a large international supermarket opens in a town, nearby family-run grocery shops may lose customers and eventually close. As a result, local entrepreneurs may lose their livelihoods, and the character of local communities may change.

There are also concerns that much of the **profit** made by multinational companies goes back to their home countries rather than staying in the local economy. Some companies may exploit cheap labour, avoid taxes or damage the environment, especially in countries where regulations are weak. In addition, if a multinational company decides to move its operations elsewhere, thousands of workers may suddenly lose their jobs.

Culturally, the spread of multinational brands can contribute to **cultural homogenisation**. When the same fast-food restaurants, coffee shops and clothing stores appear in every city, local food traditions and fashions may decline. Young people may see international brands as more fashionable and prestigious than local products.

Multinational companies can, however, also support local businesses indirectly. They often buy materials and services from local suppliers, train local staff who later start their own companies, and introduce higher standards of quality and safety that local firms can learn from. The overall effect depends greatly on government policy and how companies are regulated.

**Local businesses** have their own strengths. They usually keep profits within the community, employ local people, use local suppliers and preserve local traditions, such as traditional food, crafts and textiles. Supporting them can make an economy more resilient and maintain cultural diversity.

The best approach is a balance between the two. Governments can welcome foreign investment that brings jobs, technology and skills, while setting clear rules on wages, taxes and environmental protection. At the same time, they can support local businesses through low-interest loans, training, reduced taxes for small enterprises and campaigns encouraging people to **buy local products**.

In conclusion, multinational companies bring investment and jobs but can threaten local businesses and culture. Governments should welcome responsible foreign investment while actively supporting local enterprises, so that the economy benefits from both.

### History, Heritage and Passing Culture On

Every culture has recorded its history in some way, whether through oral stories, songs, written documents, monuments, art or, more recently, photographs, films and digital archives. Knowing our history helps us understand who we are and where we come from. An important question for every society is how to pass history and culture on to the next generation, and why this matters.

There are many ways in which history can be **passed on**. **Families** are the first teachers of history. Grandparents and parents tell children stories about their own childhoods, family origins and important events they experienced. In Bangladesh, many families share memories of the Liberation War of 1971, keeping the experiences of that generation alive.

**Schools** play a central role through history lessons, which teach students about their nation's past and the wider world. Good history teaching does not only involve memorising dates; it helps students understand causes and consequences, analyse evidence and think critically. School trips to museums, monuments and historical sites make history more real and memorable.

**Museums, libraries and archives** preserve objects, documents, photographs and artworks that tell the story of the past. They allow people of all ages to see and learn from real historical evidence. Historic buildings and sites are also living reminders of history.

**Festivals, ceremonies and national days** help to keep history alive in the public memory. For example, International Mother Language Day on 21 February, which began in Bangladesh, reminds people of the sacrifices made during the Language Movement and the importance of linguistic rights.

Modern technology offers new ways to pass on history. Documentaries, films, websites, podcasts and virtual museum tours can reach large audiences, especially young people who spend much of their time online. Digital archives can preserve old photographs, recordings and documents for future generations.

History is important to society for several reasons. Firstly, it gives people a sense of **identity and belonging**, helping them understand their culture, values and traditions. Secondly, it allows societies to **learn from the past**. By studying wars, injustices and mistakes, as well as successes and achievements, people can make better decisions in the future. Thirdly, history promotes understanding between different groups by explaining the origins of conflicts and differences. Finally, it honours the people who came before us and their contributions.

However, history can also be misused. Governments or groups may present a one-sided or false version of history to support their own interests. It is therefore important that history is taught honestly and that people learn to examine different sources and perspectives.

In conclusion, history can be passed on through families, schools, museums, festivals and modern media. It is vital for society because it builds identity, teaches lessons from the past and promotes understanding, as long as it is **told truthfully**.

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Paragraph mix formula:** প্রতিটি body paragraph-এ রাখার চেষ্টা করো → **1টি long simple + 1–2টি compound + 2–3টি complex + 1টি compound-complex** sentence। 5–6টি বাক্য × গড়ে 20 শব্দ = **100–120 words**।

</div>

#### 1. Long simple sentence (rich noun phrase)

**Formula:** [long noun phrase] + verb + object
*একটাই clause, কিন্তু subject-টা বড় ও তথ্যপূর্ণ।*

- The rapid spread of international fast-food chains and clothing brands **has changed** young people's lifestyles.
- Traditional festivals such as Pohela Boishakh **bring** people of all ages and religions together.

#### 2. Compound with *and / but / so / yet*

- Tourism creates jobs, **but** it can also damage historical sites.
- English opens many doors, **so** parents want their children to learn it early.

#### 3. Compound with semicolon + linker

- Globalisation brings economic growth**; however,** it can weaken local traditions.
- Many young people no longer speak their grandparents' language**; as a result,** it may die out.

#### 4. Complex — concession (*Although / Even though / While*)

- **Although** foreign films are entertaining, they may reduce interest in local cinema.
- **Even though** translation apps are useful, they cannot replace real language skills.

#### 5. Complex — reason (*Because / Since / As*)

- **Since** traditions connect people to their past, they should be preserved.
- **Because** tourists spend money locally, tourism supports many small businesses.

#### 6. Complex — condition (*If / Unless / Provided that*)

- **If** children do not learn their mother tongue, it **may** disappear within a few generations.
- Tourism can benefit local people, **provided that** it is carefully managed.

#### 7. Relative clause (*which / who / where / whose*)

- Craftspeople **who** make traditional products often struggle to earn a living.
- Many visitors go to Sylhet, **where** they can see tea gardens and hills.

#### 8. Compound-complex

**Formula:** Although/Because/If + clause, Clause A, and/but Clause B

- **Although** globalisation spreads Western culture, it also introduces new ideas, **and** many people benefit from them.
- **If** tourists respect local customs, they are welcomed warmly, **and** both sides learn from each other.

#### 9. Participle phrase

- **Exposed** to global media, many young people prefer foreign music to local songs.
- **Visiting** historical sites, students learn more than they could from textbooks.

#### 10. *Not only … but also …*

- Tourism **not only** creates jobs **but also** encourages cultural exchange.
- Learning a foreign language **not only** improves career prospects **but also** helps people understand other cultures.

#### 11. *The more …, the more …*

- **The more** young people consume global media, **the less** interest they show in local traditions.
- **The more** visitors a site receives, **the faster** it is damaged.

#### 12. Cleft sentence (emphasis)

- **It is** families, more than schools, **that** keep a language alive.
- **What** societies need **is** a balance between modern progress and tradition.

#### 13. Impersonal passive

- **It is often said that** travel broadens the mind.
- **It is widely believed that** globalisation threatens local cultures.

#### 14. Contrasting views

- **While some people believe that** globalisation destroys local culture, **others argue that** it enriches it.
- **While** English helps international communication, **local languages** carry unique cultural knowledge.

#### 15. Result / consequence

- Many people now move to cities, **which means that** traditional village life is disappearing.
- Global brands are everywhere. **As a result,** cities around the world look increasingly similar.

### How to Translate These Essays

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### অনুবাদের ৭টি ধাপ

1. **পুরো অনুচ্ছেদটি আগে পড়ো।** প্রতিটি শব্দ নয়, মূল idea-টা বোঝো।
2. **Tag দেখো** (Simple / Compound / Complex / Compound-Complex)। লেখার আগেই ঠিক করো ইংরেজি বাক্যটির গঠন কেমন হবে।
3. **সঠিক linker বেছে নাও:** যদিও → *Although*, যখন → *When / As*, কারণ → *because*, যদি → *If*, যা / যারা → *which / who*, স্বীকার করতেই হয় → *Admittedly*, উদাহরণস্বরূপ → *For example*।
4. **Word Bank ব্যবহার করো।** প্রতিটি essay-র আগে দেওয়া শব্দগুলোর মধ্যে অন্তত 8টি ব্যবহার করার চেষ্টা করো।
5. **Word target মেনে চলো:** প্রতিটি heading-এর পাশে লেখা শব্দসংখ্যা অনুবাদের পর গুনে দেখো। কম হলে বাক্যে ব্যাখ্যা বা উদাহরণ যোগ করো।
6. **Grammar যাচাই করো:** tense, subject–verb agreement (globalisation **has**, traditions **are**), এবং article (*the* world, *a* sense of belonging)।
7. **শব্দে শব্দে অনুবাদ করবে না।** অর্থটা স্বাভাবিক ইংরেজিতে প্রকাশ করো। ঘড়ি ধরো: প্রতিটি essay 40 মিনিটে।

</div>

### Essay 1 — Advantages Outweigh Disadvantages?

<div class="question">Globalisation has both advantages and disadvantages. Discuss both and give your opinion.</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · SOC-05 · Common topic ★ Globalization: benefits or harms*

**Essay type:** Advantages–Disadvantages, Type 2 (discuss both + opinion) · **Position:** The advantages clearly outweigh the disadvantages.<br>
**অবস্থান:** সুবিধাগুলো অসুবিধার চেয়ে স্পষ্টভাবেই বেশি, বিশেষ করে উন্নয়নশীল দেশের জন্য। তাই Although ও While বাক্যে আগে অসুবিধা, comma-র পরে সুবিধা।<br>
**কাঠামো:** IELTS_Advantages_Disadvantages_Essay_Guide.md অনুযায়ী — Type 2 (মতামতসহ) — Although-introduction ও While-conclusion-এ যে দিক সমর্থন করো তা comma-র পরে, প্রতিটি body paragraph-এ ঠিক 7টি বাক্য। মোট 330–360 শব্দ।

**পরিকল্পনা (Plan)**

- **Introduction (40–50):** বিশ্বায়ন কী → Although + অসুবিধা (স্থানীয় দোকান বন্ধ, সংস্কৃতি দুর্বল), + সুবিধা (কর্মসংস্থান, জ্ঞান) → In my opinion, সুবিধা বেশি।
- **Body Paragraph 1 (120–135) — সুবিধা, 7টি বাক্য:** অর্থনৈতিক প্রবৃদ্ধি, বড় বাজার → রপ্তানিতে আয় → উদাহরণ: বাংলাদেশের পোশাকশিল্প → জ্ঞান, প্রযুক্তি ও সস্তা পণ্য → চিকিৎসা ও কৃষির অগ্রগতি → Overall…
- **Body Paragraph 2 (120–135) — অসুবিধা, 7টি বাক্য:** বহুজাতিক কোম্পানির দাপট → উদাহরণ: সুপারমার্কেটের পাশে মুদি দোকান → সাংস্কৃতিক একরূপতা → স্থানীয় রীতি ও ভাষা হারায় → শেষ বাক্য: Although these… ন্যায্য আইন ও সাংস্কৃতিক কর্মসূচি দিয়ে কমানো যায়।
- **Conclusion (35–45):** নতুন paraphrase → While + অসুবিধা, + সুবিধা → Therefore, I firmly believe…

**Fixed openers:** Although… (but ছাড়া) · In my opinion, … · There is little doubt that… · To begin with, … · This is because… · For example, … · In addition, … not only… but also… · As a result, … · Overall, … · On the other hand, it cannot be denied that… · Firstly, … · …; as a result, … · For instance, … · Secondly, … · If…, …consequently… · Although these/some… · In conclusion, … · While…, … · Therefore, I believe that…

#### Introduction (40–50 words)

বিশ্বায়ন (globalisation), অর্থাৎ বাণিজ্য, প্রযুক্তি ও সংস্কৃতির মাধ্যমে দেশগুলোর মধ্যে ক্রমবর্ধমান সংযোগ, পৃথিবীকে বদলে দিয়েছে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> যদিও এটি স্থানীয় দোকান বন্ধ করে দিতে এবং ঐতিহ্যবাহী সংস্কৃতিকে দুর্বল করতে পারে, এটি কর্মসংস্থান তৈরি করে এবং উপকারী জ্ঞান ছড়িয়ে দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> আমার মতে, বিশ্বায়নের সুবিধাগুলো অসুবিধার চেয়ে স্পষ্টভাবেই বেশি। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — সুবিধা (120–135 words)

এতে কোনো সন্দেহ নেই যে বিশ্বায়ন উন্নয়নশীল দেশের মানুষের জন্য উল্লেখযোগ্য সুবিধা বয়ে আনে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> প্রথমত, সবচেয়ে বড় সুবিধা হলো অর্থনৈতিক প্রবৃদ্ধি, কারণ দেশগুলো যখন মুক্তভাবে বাণিজ্য করে, তখন ব্যবসাগুলো অনেক বড় বাজারে পণ্য বিক্রি করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এর কারণ হলো, বিদেশি ক্রেতাদের কাছে বিক্রি করলে দেশে টাকা আসে, যা কর্মসংস্থান তৈরি করে এবং আয় বাড়ায়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, সারা বিশ্বে পোশাক রপ্তানি করা বাংলাদেশের পোশাকশিল্পে লাখ লাখ মানুষ কাজ করেন, এবং তাদের অনেকেই নারী। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> এছাড়া, বিশ্বায়ন শুধু জ্ঞান ও প্রযুক্তিই ছড়িয়ে দেয় না, ক্রেতাদের কম দামে আরও বেশি পণ্য বেছে নেওয়ার সুযোগও দেয়। <span class="tag">Simple</span> এই আদান-প্রদানের ফলে চিকিৎসাবিজ্ঞানের অগ্রগতি, চাষাবাদের নতুন কৌশল, নবায়নযোগ্য জ্বালানি ও ডিজিটাল সরঞ্জাম দ্রুত সারা বিশ্বের দেশে পৌঁছে যেতে পারে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> সব মিলিয়ে, বৈশ্বিক বাণিজ্য ও সহযোগিতা দরিদ্র দেশগুলোকে আরও ধনী, স্বাস্থ্যবান ও সংযুক্ত হতে সাহায্য করে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — অসুবিধা (120–135 words)

অন্যদিকে, এটি অস্বীকার করা যায় না যে বিশ্বায়নের কিছু অসুবিধাও আছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> প্রথমত, অনেক স্থানীয় ব্যবসায়ী ক্ষতিগ্রস্ত হন, কারণ বিপুল বাজেটের বহুজাতিক কোম্পানি (multinational companies) বাজার দখল করে নিতে পারে এবং ছোট স্থানীয় ব্যবসাগুলোকে বন্ধ হতে বাধ্য করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> দামে প্রতিযোগিতা করার মতো অর্থ না থাকায় পারিবারিক ব্যবসাগুলো প্রায়ই ক্রেতা হারায়; ফলে মালিকেরা তাদের আয় হারান। <span class="tag">Compound</span> যেমন, কাছেই একটি আন্তর্জাতিক সুপারমার্কেট খুললে একটি ছোট মুদি দোকান তার ক্রেতা হারাতে পারে, যা মালিককে ঋণে ডুবিয়ে দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> দ্বিতীয়ত, সাংস্কৃতিক একরূপতা (cultural homogenisation) একটি গুরুতর সমস্যা হতে পারে, কারণ বৈশ্বিক ব্র্যান্ড, পশ্চিমা চলচ্চিত্র ও আন্তর্জাতিক fast food সবখানে ছড়িয়ে পড়ছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> তরুণরা যদি শুধু বৈশ্বিক trend অনুসরণ করে, তাহলে তারা নিজেদের উৎসবকে অবহেলা করতে পারে, এবং ফলস্বরূপ কিছু স্থানীয় রীতিনীতি ও ছোট ভাষা হারিয়ে যেতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> যদিও এই অর্থনৈতিক ও সাংস্কৃতিক চ্যালেঞ্জগুলো বাস্তব, ন্যায্য আইন ও সাংস্কৃতিক কর্মসূচির মাধ্যমে এগুলো কমানো যায়। <span class="tag">Complex · Passive</span>

#### Conclusion (35–45 words)

পরিশেষে, দেশগুলোর মধ্যে ঘনিষ্ঠ সংযোগ এখন আধুনিক জীবনের অংশ। <span class="tag">Simple</span> যদিও (While) বৈশ্বিক বাজার ছোট ব্যবসা ও স্থানীয় রীতিনীতির ক্ষতি করতে পারে, এটি উন্নয়নশীল দেশগুলোতে কর্মসংস্থান, সস্তা পণ্য ও নতুন প্রযুক্তি নিয়ে আসে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> তাই আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে সুবিধাগুলো অসুবিধার চেয়ে অনেক বেশি। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

</div>

### Essay 2 — Agree / Disagree

<div class="question">Some people think that foreign films have a negative impact on local culture. To what extent do you agree or disagree?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · MED-13 · Common topic ★ Western influence*

**Essay type:** Agree / Disagree · **Position:** I completely agree.<br>
**অবস্থান:** আমি সম্পূর্ণ একমত — বিদেশি চলচ্চিত্র তরুণদের মূল্যবোধ বদলে দেয় এবং দেশের চলচ্চিত্রশিল্পকে দুর্বল করে। "partly agree" বা আংশিক উত্তর নয়।<br>
**কাঠামো:** IELTS_Agree_Disagree_Essay_Guide.md অনুযায়ী — শুরু থেকে শেষ পর্যন্ত একটিই স্পষ্ট পক্ষ, কখনো আংশিক উত্তর নয়। মোট 270–345 শব্দ।

**পরিকল্পনা (Plan)**

- **Introduction (35–50):** প্রশ্নের paraphrase → I completely agree + Idea 1 (তরুণদের মূল্যবোধ বদলায়) + Idea 2 (দেশের চলচ্চিত্রশিল্প দুর্বল)।
- **Body Paragraph 1 (100–120) — Idea 1:** বড় বাজেটের রোমাঞ্চকর ছবি → বিদেশি পোশাক, ভাষা, আচরণ অনুকরণ → উদাহরণ: উৎসবের চেয়ে পশ্চিমা উদযাপন পছন্দ করা কিশোর → নিজেকে দেখার ধরনে প্রভাব।
- **Body Paragraph 2 (100–120) — Idea 2:** হলে বিদেশি ছবি, স্থানীয় নির্মাতার আয় কমে → উদাহরণ: তরুণ বাংলাদেশি পরিচালক → Response: সহনশীলতা বাড়ে, কিন্তু নিজের গল্প হারালে মূল্য নেই → সুরক্ষা প্রয়োজন।
- **Conclusion (35–55):** In conclusion, I firmly believe… → Idea 1 ও Idea 2 নতুন শব্দে।

#### Introduction (35–50 words)

#### Body Paragraph 1 — Idea 1 (100–120 words)

এই ধারণার সাথে আমার একমত হওয়ার প্রধান কারণ হলো, বিদেশি চলচ্চিত্র তরুণদের মূল্যবোধ (values) বদলে দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এর কারণ হলো, হলিউড ও অন্যান্য বড় চলচ্চিত্রশিল্পের বাজেট বিশাল, তাই তাদের ছবি প্রায়ই স্থানীয় ছবির চেয়ে বেশি রোমাঞ্চকর হয়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> তরুণরা যখন নিয়মিত এই ছবিগুলো দেখে, তখন তারা বিদেশি পোশাক, ভাষা ও আচরণ অনুকরণ করতে পারে; ফলে স্থানীয় গল্প ও ঐতিহ্য কম মনোযোগ পায়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, বাংলাদেশের কিছু কিশোর এখন ঐতিহ্যবাহী উৎসবের চেয়ে পশ্চিমা ধাঁচের পোশাক ও উদযাপন বেশি পছন্দ করে, এবং এটি ধীরে ধীরে তাদের নিজের সংস্কৃতি থেকে দূরে সরিয়ে দিতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound</span> তাই তরুণরা নিজেদের কীভাবে দেখে, তার ওপর বিদেশি চলচ্চিত্রের জোরালো প্রভাব রয়েছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — Idea 2 (100–120 words)

আমার একমত হওয়ার আরেকটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ কারণ হলো, বিদেশি চলচ্চিত্র দেশের চলচ্চিত্রশিল্পকে (film industry) দুর্বল করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> স্থানীয় সিনেমা হলগুলো যখন মূলত বিদেশি ছবি দেখায়, তখন স্থানীয় নির্মাতারা দর্শক ও আয় হারান, তাই নতুন ছবি বানানোর জন্য তাদের হাতে কম টাকা থাকে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> যেমন, একজন তরুণ বাংলাদেশি পরিচালক হয়তো দেখেন যে সিনেমা হলগুলো একটি স্থানীয় গল্পের চেয়ে একটি আমেরিকান অ্যাকশন ছবিকে বেশি পছন্দ করে, যা তার কাজ চালিয়ে যাওয়া খুব কঠিন করে তোলে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যদিও কেউ কেউ যুক্তি দেন যে বিদেশি চলচ্চিত্র সহনশীলতা ও পারস্পরিক বোঝাপড়া বাড়াতে পারে, কোনো দেশের নিজের গল্পই যদি আর বলা না হয়, তাহলে এই সুবিধার খুব একটা মূল্য থাকে না। <span class="tag">Complex</span> ফলে দেশের চলচ্চিত্রশিল্পের সুরক্ষা প্রয়োজন, এবং বিদেশি চলচ্চিত্র স্থানীয় সংস্কৃতির জন্য একটি বাস্তব হুমকি হয়েই থাকে। <span class="tag">Compound</span>

#### Introduction (30–40 words)

আধুনিক উন্নয়নের কারণে অনেক ঐতিহ্যবাহী জীবনধারা হারিয়ে যাচ্ছে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে এই ঐতিহ্যগুলো ধরে রাখা গুরুত্বপূর্ণ, এবং স্কুল ও পরিবার তরুণদের কাছে এগুলো পৌঁছে দিতে সাহায্য করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — Q1: Opinion block (90–120 words)

আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে ঐতিহ্যবাহী জীবনধারা ধরে রাখা গুরুত্বপূর্ণ, কারণ এগুলো একটি জাতির পরিচয়ের (a nation's identity) অপরিহার্য অংশ। <span class="tag">Complex</span> উৎসব, সংগীত, কারুশিল্প, খাবার ও ভাষা মানুষকে তাদের ইতিহাসের সাথে যুক্ত করে এবং তাদের মধ্যে একাত্মতার অনুভূতি তৈরি করে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, বাংলাদেশের পহেলা বৈশাখের মতো উৎসব বিভিন্ন বয়স ও পটভূমির মানুষকে একত্র করে এবং সামাজিক বন্ধন মজবুত করে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> যদিও কেউ কেউ যুক্তি দেন যে পুরোনো রীতিনীতি অগ্রগতি ধীর করে দেয়, একটি দেশ তার অনন্য ঐতিহ্য না হারিয়েও আধুনিক হতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> তাই এই ঐতিহ্যগুলো হারানো মানে আমাদের অতীতের সাথে একটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ যোগসূত্র হারানো। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — Q2: List block (90–120 words)

সবচেয়ে কার্যকর উপায় হলো, স্কুলগুলো ঐতিহ্যবাহী সংস্কৃতি শেখাবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> স্কুলগুলো যখন পাঠ্যক্রমে স্থানীয় ইতিহাস, লোকসংগীত (folk music) ও ঐতিহ্যবাহী কারুশিল্প যুক্ত করে এবং সাংস্কৃতিক অনুষ্ঠানের আয়োজন করে, তখন শিশুরা ছোটবেলা থেকেই নিজেদের ঐতিহ্যকে মূল্য দিতে শেখে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যেমন, স্কুলের উৎসবের জন্য একটি লোকবাদ্যযন্ত্র বাজাতে শেখা একজন শিক্ষার্থী বড় হয়েও সেটি বাজিয়ে যেতে পারে, যা ওই সংগীতকে বাঁচিয়ে রাখে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এছাড়া, পরিবারগুলো ঘরে ঐতিহ্য পরবর্তী প্রজন্মের হাতে তুলে দিতে (pass on traditions) পারে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> বাবা-মা যখন একসাথে উৎসব পালন করেন এবং ঘরে মাতৃভাষায় কথা বলেন, তখন শিশুরা এই রীতিনীতিকে দৈনন্দিন জীবনের অংশ হিসেবে দেখেই বড় হয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এই পদক্ষেপগুলো একসাথে আধুনিক জীবনের পাশাপাশি ঐতিহ্যকে টিকে থাকতে সাহায্য করবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

পরিশেষে, আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে ঐতিহ্যবাহী জীবনধারা ধরে রাখার যোগ্য, কারণ এগুলো মানুষকে পরিচয়ের অনুভূতি দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> স্কুলে সাংস্কৃতিক শিক্ষা এবং ঘরে প্রতিদিনের চর্চা নিশ্চিত করবে যে এগুলো টিকে থাকবে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

**Common mistakes in culture essays (এগুলো এড়িয়ে চলো):**

- ❌ *Our culture is very rich and we must protect it.* (too general) → ✅ *Traditional crafts such as nakshi kantha embroidery represent centuries of rural history.*
- ❌ *Western culture is bad.* (judgemental) → ✅ *Some aspects of global popular culture may conflict with local values.*
- ❌ *the tourism* (general meaning) → ✅ **tourism** brings income… (no article)
- ❌ *learn English language* → ✅ learn **English** / learn **the English language**
- ❌ *foreigner people* → ✅ **foreigners** / **foreign visitors**
- ❌ *traditional culture are disappearing* → ✅ traditional culture **is** disappearing
- **"culture" প্রতিশব্দ:** heritage, traditions, customs, way of life, identity, cultural practices, values

</div>

### Homework 1 — Discussion

<div class="question">Some people believe that, to protect local culture, tourism should be banned in some areas, while others think that change is inevitable and banning tourism will have no benefits. Discuss both sides and give your opinion.</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · TOU-05 · ★ international tourism*

**Essay type:** Discussion (both views + opinion) · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–299 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 35–45 · BP1 (the other view) 90–120 · BP2 (your view) 90–120 · Conclusion 35–45<br>
**Use ideas from:** Tourism & Local Communities; Tourism & Historical Sites; International Tourism: Benefits

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- নিষেধের পক্ষে 2টি যুক্তি কী (পবিত্র স্থান ও রীতিনীতির বাণিজ্যিকীকরণ, ভিড় ও পরিবেশের ক্ষতি)?
- নিষেধের বিপক্ষে 2টি যুক্তি কী (স্থানীয় মানুষের আয় হারানো, পরিবর্তন অনিবার্য, সাংস্কৃতিক আদান-প্রদান)?
- তোমার মত: একটিই পক্ষ বেছে নাও, কখনো আংশিক নয়। সেটি সবসময় BP2-তে রাখো। একটি বাস্তব উদাহরণ ভাবো।
- P-S-A কৌশল কোথায় ব্যবহার করবে?

</div>

**Try to use:** protect local culture · commercialise traditions · mass tourism · income for local communities · change is inevitable · limit visitor numbers · respect local customs · sustainable tourism

**Sentence target:** use *While some people argue that…, others believe that…* in the introduction and *provided that* once.

</div>

### Homework 2 — Problem–Solution

<div class="question">Eco-tourism usually involves people visiting remote areas. Some people think this will have a negative impact on local cultures and communities. What problems can result from eco-tourism? What measures can be taken to protect local culture and communities?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · TOU-03*

**Essay type:** Problem and Solution · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–300 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 30–40 · BP1 2 causes/problems 90–120 · BP2 2 solutions 90–120 · Conclusion 30–40<br>
**Use ideas from:** Tourism & Local Communities; Chapter 03 (Air Travel & Tourism)

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- Problem + Solution: BP1-এ 2টি সমস্যা (সংস্কৃতির বাণিজ্যিকীকরণ; বাসিন্দাদের জন্য খরচ বেড়ে যাওয়া) — উদাহরণ: পার্বত্য অঞ্চলের কোনো আদিবাসী গ্রামে পর্যটকের ভিড়।
- BP2 (Mirror rule): বাণিজ্যিকীকরণ ↔ স্থানীয়দের পরিকল্পনায় যুক্ত করা; খরচ ↔ পর্যটনের আয় স্থানীয়দের কাছে পৌঁছানো। প্রতিটিতে WHO + WHAT + HOW।
- Introduction: This can lead to P1 and P2, but S1 and S2 can reduce these effects.
- মতামত নয়; উপসংহারে নতুন idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** eco-tourism · remote areas · local communities · become commercialised · rising prices · fragile ecosystems · involve local people in planning · educate visitors

**Sentence target:** use *If…, …will…* twice and one relative clause with *who*.

</div>

### Homework 3 — Advantages–Disadvantages

<div class="question">Scientists predict that all people will choose to speak the same global language in the future. Do you think this is a positive or negative development?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · LAN-02 · ★ globalization & culture*

**Essay type:** Advantages–Disadvantages, Type 3 (positive or negative?) · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 330–360 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 40–50 · BP1 advantages 120–135 · BP2 disadvantages 120–135 · Conclusion 35–45<br>
**Use ideas from:** English as a Global Language & Language Loss; Learning Foreign Languages

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- একটিই রায় দাও: positive অথবা negative। "mostly negative, if other languages disappear" লিখবে না।
- Body 1 (ঠিক 7টি বাক্য): একটি বৈশ্বিক ভাষার 2টি সুবিধা (ব্যবসা ও বিজ্ঞান; ভ্রমণ ও বোঝাপড়া)।
- Body 2 (ঠিক 7টি বাক্য): 2টি অসুবিধা (ভাষার বিলুপ্তি; সাংস্কৃতিক জ্ঞান ও পরিচয় হারানো) — উদাহরণ: ভাষা আন্দোলন ও ২১ ফেব্রুয়ারি।
- নেতিবাচক রায় হলে Although ও While বাক্যে আগে সুবিধা, comma-র পরে অসুবিধা; উপসংহারে "multilingualism" নতুন সমাধান যোগ করবে না।

</div>

**Try to use:** a global language · international communication · endangered languages · die out · cultural heritage · mother tongue · multilingualism · protect their own languages

**Sentence target:** use one cleft sentence (*It is… that…*) and *not only… but also…* once.

</div>

### Homework 4 — Double Question

<div class="question">Some people prefer to watch foreign films rather than locally produced films. Why is this? Do you think the government should spend more money supporting the local film industry?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · MED-12 · ★ Western influence*

**Essay type:** Double Question — List + Opinion · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–300 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 30–40 · BP1 = Q1 (List block) 90–120 · BP2 = Q2 (Opinion block) 90–120 · Conclusion 30–40<br>
**Use ideas from:** Local Film, Music & Arts; Western Influence; Chapter 05 (Arts vs Science Funding)

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- Q1 "Why is this?" কারণ জানতে চায়, তাই List block: 2টি কারণ (বড় বাজেট ও special effects; ফ্যাশনেবল মনে হওয়া), "I" ছাড়া।
- Q2 Opinion block: হ্যাঁ অথবা না, "শর্তসাপেক্ষে" নয়। প্রশ্নের ঠিক কথাটি মনে রাখো: সরকার কি **বেশি অর্থ ব্যয়** করবে।
- Opinion block: কারণ (স্থানীয় গল্প ও সংস্কৃতি রক্ষা) + উদাহরণ (তরুণ পরিচালকের সরকারি অনুদান) + Response (Although some people argue that টাকা হাসপাতালে যাওয়া উচিত, [তোমার পক্ষ])।
- Introduction: paraphrase + [উত্তর 1], and [উত্তর 2]। Conclusion-এ দুটি উত্তর নতুন শব্দে, নতুন idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** big budgets · special effects · global marketing · the local film industry · tell local stories · preserve cultural identity · funding and training · creative freedom

**Sentence target:** use *The more…, the more…* once and *Although…, …* once.

</div>
