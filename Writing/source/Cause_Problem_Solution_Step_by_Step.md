# Cause/Problem + Solution: Step by Step

**IELTS Writing Task 2 · Band 7 target · 260–300 words**

Follow these 9 steps in order for every Cause + Solution or Problem + Solution essay. Each step shows both essay types side by side, so you can see exactly what changes.

| Step | What you do | Exam time |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Find the essay type | 0–1 min |
| **2** | Underline the exact topic | 1–2 min |
| **3** | Find 2 causes or 2 problems | 2–3 min |
| **4** | Match them with 2 solutions, and plan your examples | 3–5 min |
| **5** | Write the introduction (30–40 words) | 5–9 min |
| **6** | Write Body Paragraph 1 (90–120 words) | 9–19 min |
| **7** | Write Body Paragraph 2 (90–120 words) | 19–29 min |
| **8** | Write the conclusion (30–40 words) | 29–34 min |
| **9** | Check your essay | 34–40 min |

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Word plan: 35 + 105 + 105 + 35 = 280.** Do not write the minimum in every paragraph, because 30 + 90 + 90 + 30 is only 240.

**No opinion is needed** unless the question directly asks for one, so do not write *I think* or *In my opinion*.

</div>

#### The two questions used in every step

<div class="question">Cause essay (HEA-09): Walking is known to be beneficial for health and yet fewer and fewer people are walking these days. What are the reasons for this? What can be done to tackle this problem?</div>

<div class="question">Problem essay (TOU-02): As a result of tourism and the increasing number of people travelling, there is a growing demand for more flights. What problems does this cause for the environment? What measures could be taken to solve the problems?</div>

## Step 1: Find the essay type

**Do this:** read the **first** question only, and ask what it wants you to find.

| The first question says | It wants | Essay type |
|---|---|---|
| *What are the causes / reasons?* · *Why is this?* · *Why do you think this is?* | **Cause** = কারণ। ঘটনাটি কেন ঘটছে? | **Cause + Solution** |
| *What problems does this cause?* · *What are the effects / consequences?* | **Problem** = সমস্যা বা ক্ষতি। ঘটনাটির ফলে কী ক্ষতি হচ্ছে? | **Problem + Solution** |

The second question always wants a **Solution** = সমাধান।

**Remember the order:**

```text
   CAUSE      ->      SITUATION      ->      PROBLEM
comes before       the question topic      comes after
```

<div class="bn" style="break-inside:avoid" markdown="1">

**সাবধান:** প্রশ্নে *problem* শব্দটি থাকলেই সেটি Problem essay হয় না। প্রথম প্রশ্নটি কী জানতে চাইছে, শুধু সেটি দেখো।

</div>

| Question | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *What are the **reasons** for this? What can be done to tackle this problem?* | **Cause** | The first question asks for reasons. *This problem* only means the topic. |
| *What **problems** does this cause for the environment? What measures could be taken?* | **Problem** | The first question asks for the harm. |

**Not sure about an idea? Put it into a test sentence:**

| Test sentence | If it makes sense, your idea is a |
|---|---|
| *This situation happens **because** ___.* | **Cause** |
| ***Because of** this situation, ___.* | **Problem** |

## Step 2: Underline the exact topic

**Do this:** underline the words that limit your answer. Your ideas must fit these words, not only the general topic.

| Question | Underline | So your answer must be about |
|---|---|---|
| HEA-09 | *fewer people are **walking*** | Why people walk less, not why they exercise less in general |
| TOU-02 | *problems **for the environment*** | Harm to nature, not expensive tickets or busy airports |

## Step 3: Find 2 causes or 2 problems {: .part}

**Do this:** write 2 ideas, using only a few words for each. Take them from your Idea Bank whenever you can.

| | Cause essay | Problem essay |
|---|---|---|
| **Ask yourself** | Why does this happen? | What harm does this do? |
| **Idea 1** | City streets are unsafe | High carbon emissions |
| **Idea 2** | Technology removes the need to walk | Noise and land loss near airports |

**Cannot find an idea?** Check these areas one by one: **money, technology, work and time, education, government, family and society, health, environment.**

**Choose simple ideas.** Pick the ones you can explain with an example, not the ones that sound most impressive.

## Step 4: Match them with 2 solutions, and plan your examples {: .part}

**Do this:** ask of each idea, *"What would fix this?"* Then write **WHO** will act and **WHAT** they will do.

**In a Cause essay, the solution removes the reason. In a Problem essay, the solution reduces the harm.**

| | Cause essay | Problem essay |
|---|---|---|
| **Idea 1 → Solution 1** | Unsafe streets → **governments** build safe footpaths | Carbon emissions → **governments** introduce fair taxes |
| **Idea 2 → Solution 2** | Technology → **workplaces and individuals** bring walking back | Noise and land loss → **governments** invest in high-speed trains |

**Then plan one example for each paragraph.** It goes with Idea 1 and Solution 1. Your finished plan should be this short:

```text
CAUSE ESSAY (HEA-09)
BP1  C1 unsafe streets     -> parent takes a rickshaw
     C2 technology         -> lifts, online shopping
BP2  S1 safe footpaths     -> same parent walks to the market
     S2 walking in routine -> stairs at work, walk to shops

PROBLEM ESSAY (TOU-02)
BP1  P1 carbon emissions   -> tourist's return flight
     P2 noise, land loss   -> residents, farmland
BP2  S1 fair taxes         -> business traveller uses online calls
     S2 high-speed trains  -> fewer short flights
```

**Your planning is finished. You now know every idea in your essay.**

## Step 5: Write the introduction {: .part}

**30–40 words · 2 sentences.** Sentence 1 paraphrases the situation. Sentence 2 gives your whole answer.

| | Cause essay | Problem essay |
|---|---|---|
| **Sentence 1:** the situation in your own words | *Despite its well-known health benefits, walking is becoming less common in modern life.* | *As tourism expands, the demand for air travel continues to rise around the world.* |
| **Sentence 2 formula** | *This is mainly because [C1] and [C2], but [S1] and [S2] can tackle this problem.* | *This growth [P1] and [P2], but [S1] and [S2] can reduce these effects.* |
| **Sentence 2** | *This is mainly because city streets are unsafe and technology has made it unnecessary, but safer footpaths and better daily habits can tackle this problem.* | *This growth increases carbon emissions and damages areas near airports, but fair environmental taxes and high-speed trains can reduce these effects.* |

**Do not write:** *This essay will discuss the causes and solutions.* · *In my opinion, …* · *Although …, but …*

## Step 6: Write Body Paragraph 1 {: .part}

**90–120 words · 6 sentences · aim for about 105 words.** Your first idea gets an example. Your second idea does not, so the paragraph stays within the limit.

**Ask yourself:** *Explain* = how does it happen? · *Example* = who does it happen to? · *Link* = what is the overall result?

<div class="steps" markdown="1">

| Part | Cause essay | Problem essay |
|---|---|---|
| <span class="pl pl-P">Point</span> | *One of the main causes of this trend is that walking in large cities has become unpleasant and dangerous.* | *The most serious environmental problem caused by this growth is the high level of carbon emissions.* |
| <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span> | *Heavy traffic, air pollution and broken footpaths make short journeys on foot stressful, so many people avoid them.* | *Aeroplanes burn large amounts of fuel, and there are few clean alternatives for long-distance flights at present.* |
| <span class="pl pl-X">Example</span> | *For example, a parent who must walk beside fast buses on a broken footpath may take a rickshaw for a five-minute journey, and this soon becomes a habit.* | *For example, a tourist who takes one long-distance return flight can produce as much carbon as several months of driving, and this damage grows as more people travel.* |
| <span class="pl pl-P">Point</span> | *Another important cause is technology, which has removed much of the physical effort from daily life.* | *Another problem is the harm that airports cause to the areas around them.* |
| <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span> | *Lifts replace stairs, and online shopping means that people do not even need to walk to the shops.* | *They create noise pollution for nearby residents and take up large areas of land, which may once have been farmland or forest.* |
| <span class="pl pl-L">Link</span> | *Consequently, walking has gradually disappeared from the daily routines of many people.* | *Consequently, more flights damage both the global climate and local communities.* |

</div>

**Check your direction:**

- **Cause essay:** your explanation moves **towards** the situation: *unsafe streets → stress → people avoid walking.*
- **Problem essay:** your explanation moves **away from** the situation: *more flights → more fuel burned → more carbon.*

**The example must show the explanation right before it.** *Broken footpaths* appears in both the Explain and the Example sentences above. If the same situation is not in both, change the example.

| Too short or too long? | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Under 90 words** | Ask *"why?"* or *"what happens next?"* about Idea 2, and add that detail |
| **Over 120 words** | Shorten the example. It should be one sentence, not a story |

**Do not write solutions in this paragraph.** Keep them for Step 7.

## Step 7: Write Body Paragraph 2 {: .part}

**90–120 words · 6 sentences · aim for about 105 words.** The shape is the same as BP1, and both essay types work in the same way here.

**Every solution needs three things: WHO + WHAT + HOW.** *Example* = what would happen to one person? · *Link* = what is the final result?

| | ❌ Vague | ✅ Developed |
|---|---|---|
| **WHO + WHAT** | *Governments should take strict action.* | *The most effective solution is for governments to make streets safer for pedestrians.* |
| **HOW** | *Then the problem will be solved.* | *When city planners build safe footpaths and separate them from traffic, walking becomes pleasant and practical rather than stressful.* |

<div class="steps" markdown="1">

| Part | Cause essay | Problem essay |
|---|---|---|
| <span class="pl pl-S">Solution</span> | *The most effective solution is for governments to make streets safer for pedestrians.* | *The most effective way to reduce these emissions is to introduce fair environmental taxes, especially for people who fly very frequently.* |
| <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span> | *When city planners build safe footpaths and separate them from traffic, walking becomes pleasant and practical rather than stressful.* | *Higher prices could reduce unnecessary flights, and the money raised could be invested in clean energy.* |
| <span class="pl pl-X">Example</span> | *For instance, a parent who can reach the local market on a wide, well-lit footpath may no longer need a rickshaw, which adds exercise to their day at no cost.* | *For instance, a business traveller who pays more for every extra trip may replace some meetings with online calls, which reduces emissions without affecting families who rarely fly.* |
| <span class="pl pl-S">Solution</span> | *In addition, employers and individuals can bring walking back into everyday routines.* | *In addition, governments could invest in high-speed trains for shorter journeys.* |
| <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span> | *Workplaces can encourage staff to use the stairs, while people can walk to nearby shops instead of ordering online, and these small changes quickly add up.* | *When passengers have a fast and comfortable alternative, short flights become less necessary, so airports cause less noise and need less new land.* |
| <span class="pl pl-L">Link</span> | *Together, these measures would make walking a normal part of modern life again.* | *Together, these measures would allow people to travel while causing far less environmental damage.* |

</div>

**Use careful language.** Solutions have not happened yet, so use *should, could, can, would*. Do not write *This will completely solve the problem.*

**Use a realistic example, not a fake fact.** Describe what would happen to one person. Never invent a statistic, a study or an expert.

## Step 8: Write the conclusion {: .part}

**30–40 words · 2 sentences.** Use the same ideas in new words, and add no new idea.

| | Cause essay | Problem essay |
|---|---|---|
| **Sentence 1 formula** | *In conclusion, [situation] because [C1] and [C2].* | *In conclusion, [situation] harms [P1] and [P2].* |
| **Sentence 1** | *In conclusion, people walk less because urban streets are hazardous and technology has reduced physical effort.* | *In conclusion, rising demand for flights harms the climate and the communities living near airports.* |
| **Sentence 2 formula** | *[S1] and [S2] would [final result].* | *[S1] and [S2] would [final result].* |
| **Sentence 2** | *Safer pedestrian routes and small changes to everyday habits would encourage more people to walk regularly.* | *Well-designed taxes and better rail connections would limit this damage without preventing people from travelling.* |

## Step 9: Check your essay {: .part}

**Use your last 6 minutes in this order.**

**1. Check your answer**

☐ BP1 answers the first question, and BP2 answers the second.  
☐ My causes or problems pass the test sentence from Step 1.  
☐ BP1 has no solutions, and BP2 has no new causes or problems.  
☐ Every solution has WHO + WHAT + HOW.  
☐ Each example shows the explanation right before it.  
☐ I wrote no opinion, and no *This essay will …* sentence.

**2. Check your language**

☐ Articles (*a, an, the*) and plurals (*-s*)  
☐ Subject–verb agreement (*people are, a person is*)  
☐ No *but* after *Although* or *While*  
☐ Spelling: *conclusion, sentence, government, environment*

**3. Check your length**

☐ About 280 words in total: at least 260, and no more than 300 in practice.

### Essay A: Cause + Solution (294 words)

<div class="question">Walking is known to be beneficial for health and yet fewer and fewer people are walking these days. What are the reasons for this? What can be done to tackle this problem?</div>

<div class="model" markdown="1">

<p class="plabel">Introduction <span class="wc">· 38 words</span></p>

Despite its well-known health benefits, walking is becoming less common in modern life. This is mainly because city streets are unsafe and technology has made it unnecessary, but safer footpaths and better daily habits can tackle this problem.

<p class="plabel">Body Paragraph 1: Causes <span class="wc">· 111 words</span></p>

<span class="pl pl-P">Point</span>One of the main causes of this trend is that walking in large cities has become unpleasant and dangerous. <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span>Heavy traffic, air pollution and broken footpaths make short journeys on foot stressful, so many people avoid them. <span class="pl pl-X">Example</span>For example, a parent who must walk beside fast buses on a broken footpath may take a rickshaw for a five-minute journey, and this soon becomes a habit. <span class="pl pl-P">Point</span>Another important cause is technology, which has removed much of the physical effort from daily life. <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span>Lifts replace stairs, and online shopping means that people do not even need to walk to the shops. <span class="pl pl-L">Link</span>Consequently, walking has gradually disappeared from the daily routines of many people.

<p class="plabel">Body Paragraph 2: Solutions <span class="wc">· 113 words</span></p>

<span class="pl pl-S">Solution</span>The most effective solution is for governments to make streets safer for pedestrians. <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span>When city planners build safe footpaths and separate them from traffic, walking becomes pleasant and practical rather than stressful. <span class="pl pl-X">Example</span>For instance, a parent who can reach the local market on a wide, well-lit footpath may no longer need a rickshaw, which adds exercise to their day at no cost. <span class="pl pl-S">Solution</span>In addition, employers and individuals can bring walking back into everyday routines. <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span>Workplaces can encourage staff to use the stairs, while people can walk to nearby shops instead of ordering online, and these small changes quickly add up. <span class="pl pl-L">Link</span>Together, these measures would make walking a normal part of modern life again.

<p class="plabel">Conclusion <span class="wc">· 32 words</span></p>

In conclusion, people walk less because urban streets are hazardous and technology has reduced physical effort. Safer pedestrian routes and small changes to everyday habits would encourage more people to walk regularly.

</div>

### Essay B: Problem + Solution (285 words) {: .part-break}

<div class="question">As a result of tourism and the increasing number of people travelling, there is a growing demand for more flights. What problems does this cause for the environment? What measures could be taken to solve the problems?</div>

<div class="model" markdown="1">

<p class="plabel">Introduction <span class="wc">· 35 words</span></p>

As tourism expands, the demand for air travel continues to rise around the world. This growth increases carbon emissions and damages areas near airports, but fair environmental taxes and high-speed trains can reduce these effects.

<p class="plabel">Body Paragraph 1: Problems <span class="wc">· 107 words</span></p>

<span class="pl pl-P">Point</span>The most serious environmental problem caused by this growth is the high level of carbon emissions. <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span>Aeroplanes burn large amounts of fuel, and there are few clean alternatives for long-distance flights at present. <span class="pl pl-X">Example</span>For example, a tourist who takes one long-distance return flight can produce as much carbon as several months of driving, and this damage grows as more people travel. <span class="pl pl-P">Point</span>Another problem is the harm that airports cause to the areas around them. <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span>They create noise pollution for nearby residents and take up large areas of land, which may once have been farmland or forest. <span class="pl pl-L">Link</span>Consequently, more flights damage both the global climate and local communities.

<p class="plabel">Body Paragraph 2: Solutions <span class="wc">· 113 words</span></p>

<span class="pl pl-S">Solution</span>The most effective way to reduce these emissions is to introduce fair environmental taxes, especially for people who fly very frequently. <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span>Higher prices could reduce unnecessary flights, and the money raised could be invested in clean energy. <span class="pl pl-X">Example</span>For instance, a business traveller who pays more for every extra trip may replace some meetings with online calls, which reduces emissions without affecting families who rarely fly. <span class="pl pl-S">Solution</span>In addition, governments could invest in high-speed trains for shorter journeys. <span class="pl pl-E">Explain</span>When passengers have a fast and comfortable alternative, short flights become less necessary, so airports cause less noise and need less new land. <span class="pl pl-L">Link</span>Together, these measures would allow people to travel while causing far less environmental damage.

<p class="plabel">Conclusion <span class="wc">· 30 words</span></p>

In conclusion, rising demand for flights harms the climate and the communities living near airports. Well-designed taxes and better rail connections would limit this damage without preventing people from travelling.
