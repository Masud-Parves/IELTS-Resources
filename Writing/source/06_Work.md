## Part 1 — Idea Generation Flow {: .part}

<div class="flow">Work → Job Satisfaction vs High Salary → Work–Life Balance → Working from Home → Job Security vs Career Change → Automation &amp; AI → Education, Skills &amp; Getting a Job → Young People &amp; Part-Time Work → Gender Equality at Work → Older Workers &amp; Retirement → Pay, Promotion &amp; Motivation → Self-Employment &amp; Entrepreneurship → Migration for Work &amp; Brain Drain</div>

When you see **any** work question, run through this chain from left to right and ask: *"Does this question touch this area?"* Then **stop at the 2 links that fit the question best**. Those 2 links become your two body paragraphs.

**Memory trick — the 4 things people want from a job: M-M-B-S**

| Letter | What workers want | Typical ideas |
|---|---|---|
| **M — Money** | salary, bonuses, promotion | supports family, security, status; but money alone does not bring happiness |
| **M — Meaning** | job satisfaction, interest, respect | motivation, mental health, productivity |
| **B — Balance** | time for family, rest, health | long hours, stress, remote work, always "on call" |
| **S — Security** | a stable job, pension, safe future | fear of automation, contracts, career change |

Most work questions ask you to compare two of these (e.g. *Money vs Meaning*, *Security vs Change*), or to explain how a trend (technology, globalisation, remote work) affects them.

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Example:** *"Is money an important factor when choosing a job? What other factors should be considered?"*

M-M-B-S → **Money** ✔ (BP1: yes, important — family, security) → **Meaning + Balance + Security** ✔ (BP2: satisfaction, working hours, career growth).
Run the chain → **Job Satisfaction vs High Salary** + **Work–Life Balance**.

</div>

## Part 2 — Exam-Hall Brainstorming System {: .part}

### 2A. The 5-Minute Plan

| Minute | What to do | What you write on the question paper |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | **Decode the question.** Underline the topic words and the instruction words. Decide which of your five essay guides it uses (Part 2G). | e.g. *working from home / advantages / disadvantages → Advantages / Disadvantages guide (Type 1, no opinion)* |
| 1–3 | **WHO + M-M-B-S grid.** Employee, employer, society — Money, Meaning, Balance, Security. | 4–6 one-line notes |
| 3–4 | **Choose the 2 strongest ideas.** Add one reason and one example to each. | Idea 1 + why + example; Idea 2 + why + example |
| 4–5 | **Decide your position (one clear side, only if the question asks) and use the guide's word plan.** | Thesis / BP1 / BP2 / Conclusion in 4 lines |

- **Employers / companies** — productivity, costs, recruitment, staff loyalty
- **Young people / graduates** — first job, unemployment, skills, part-time work
- **Women** — equal pay, maternity leave, childcare, career breaks
- **Older workers** — retirement age, retraining, experience, discrimination
- **Families** — time together, income, children's care
- **Self-employed people / entrepreneurs** — risk, independence, irregular income
- **Migrant workers** — better pay abroad, separation from family, remittances
- **Governments** — unemployment, taxes, labour laws, education and training
- **Society / the economy** — economic growth, inequality, brain drain

### 2E. How One Idea Becomes a Full Paragraph (PEEL)

**P**oint → **E**xplain (why? how?) → **E**xample → **L**ink / Result

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### বাংলায় চিন্তা (Bangla thinking)

**Point:** ঘরে বসে কাজ করার সবচেয়ে বড় সুবিধা হলো যাতায়াতের সময় বাঁচে।
**Explain:** কারণ বড় শহরে অফিসে যাওয়া-আসায় প্রতিদিন কয়েক ঘণ্টা নষ্ট হয়।
**Example:** যেমন, ঢাকার একজন কর্মী প্রতিদিন যানজটে দুই-তিন ঘণ্টা কাটান, যা তিনি ঘরে থেকে পরিবার বা বিশ্রামের জন্য ব্যবহার করতে পারেন।
**Link/Result:** ফলে কর্মীরা কম ক্লান্ত থাকেন এবং কাজে বেশি মনোযোগ দিতে পারেন।

</div>

**The same idea in English (one paragraph):**

> **Point:** The greatest advantage of working from home is that it saves commuting time.
> **Explain:** In large cities, travelling to and from the office can waste several hours every day.
> **Example:** For example, an office worker in Dhaka may spend two or three hours in traffic each day, time that could instead be spent with family or resting.
> **Link:** As a result, remote workers are often less tired and can concentrate better on their work.

Count it: about **70 words** from one idea. Add one more explanation or a second short example and you reach the **100–120 words** that one body paragraph needs.

### 2G. Essay-Type Paragraph Plans (your five essay guides)

| Essay type (guide) | Introduction | Body Paragraph 1 | Body Paragraph 2 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agree / Disagree** (*to what extent? / do you agree?*) | **35–50:** paraphrase + *I completely agree/disagree with this view because* Idea 1 and Idea 2. Never "partly". | **100–120:** Idea 1 — P E E X L | **100–120:** Idea 2 — P E X R L (*Although some people argue that …, [my side]*) | **35–55:** *In conclusion, I firmly believe that …* + both ideas in new words |
| **Discuss Both Views** (*some think X, others think Y*) | **35–45:** *While some people argue that [other view], others believe that [my view]. I completely support the latter view because …* | **90–120:** the view you do **not** support, no "I" — P E E X L | **90–120:** **your view** — P E X R L | **35–45:** *In conclusion, people hold different views on whether … While …, I firmly believe that …* |
| **Advantages – Disadvantages** | **40–50:** paraphrase + *Although* [side you do not support], [side you support] + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **30–40**, 2 sentences) | **120–135:** advantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **120–135:** disadvantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **35–45:** *While …* + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **25–35**) |
| **Cause/Problem + Solution** | **30–40:** paraphrase + *This is mainly because C1 and C2, but S1 and S2 can tackle this problem.* No opinion. | **90–120:** 2 causes or problems — P E X P E L | **90–120:** 2 solutions, each fixing one cause (WHO + WHAT + HOW) | **30–40:** causes + solutions in new words |
| **Double Question** (*two questions*) | **30–40:** paraphrase + *[Answer 1], and [answer 2].* | **90–120:** answer to Q1 — List block (P E X P E L) or Opinion block (P E X R L) | **90–120:** answer to Q2 — List or Opinion block | **30–40:** *In conclusion,* answer 1 + answer 2 in new words |

*Full rules: `Writing/IELTS_*_Essay_Guide.md`. Questions asking only for solutions or only for problems use the Cause/Problem + Solution guide, Section 14: one idea in each body paragraph (P E E X L), no opinion.*

## Part 3 — Category-Wise Free Writing {: .part}

Each passage below is an **idea store**. Read it slowly, notice how each idea is explained and supported with an example, and pay attention to the **bold collocations**. After each passage, try to say 4–5 of its ideas aloud in your own words.

### Job Satisfaction versus High Salary

When people choose a job, they often face a difficult decision: should they choose the job that pays the most, or the job that they will enjoy the most? In an ideal world, a job would offer both a good salary and personal satisfaction, but in reality many people must choose between them. The answer often depends on a person's financial situation, values and stage of life.

There are strong reasons why money is important. A good salary allows people to **provide for their families**, pay for their children's education, buy or rent a decent home and save for the future. In developing countries, where there is often little social security, a well-paid job may be the only protection against illness, unemployment or old age. For a young person who must support parents and younger siblings, choosing a low-paid job simply because it is interesting may not be a realistic option. This is one reason why job satisfaction is sometimes described as a **luxury** in poorer countries.

Money also brings status and independence. In many societies, people with high incomes are respected, and a well-paid job can increase a person's confidence and freedom to make their own choices.

However, a high salary does not guarantee happiness. People spend a large part of their adult lives at work, often more than forty hours a week. If they find their work boring, meaningless or stressful, they may feel unhappy every day, regardless of how much they earn. Over time, this can lead to **stress, burnout and health problems**. Many people who earn large salaries in demanding jobs, such as banking or corporate law, eventually leave to do work they find more meaningful, even for less money.

**Job satisfaction** also benefits employers. Workers who enjoy their jobs are usually more motivated, more creative and more loyal. They work harder without being forced, take fewer sick days and are less likely to leave the company, which reduces the cost of recruiting and training new staff. In contrast, unhappy workers may do only the minimum required.

Satisfaction comes from many sources besides the work itself: a supportive manager, friendly colleagues, opportunities to learn, recognition for good work and a sense that the job **makes a difference** to other people. Teachers, nurses and social workers, for example, often earn modest salaries but feel proud that they help others.

The best approach is to look for a balance. People should earn enough to live comfortably and securely, but once this basic need is met, extra money tends to add less to happiness than interesting work, good relationships and free time. Young people choosing a career should therefore consider their abilities and interests, not only the starting salary.

In conclusion, money is an important factor in choosing a job, especially for people with financial responsibilities. However, long-term happiness and success depend largely on job satisfaction. Ideally, people should **strike a balance** between earning a decent income and doing work that they find meaningful.

### Work–Life Balance and Long Working Hours

**Work–life balance** means having enough time and energy for both work and personal life, including family, friends, health, rest and hobbies. In many modern societies, this balance has become difficult to achieve. Long working hours, long commutes and constant contact through smartphones mean that many people feel that work has taken over their lives.

There are several reasons for this problem. In competitive economies, many employees work long hours because they fear losing their jobs or want to gain promotion. Some companies expect staff to stay late as a sign of commitment, even when there is little extra work. In large cities, workers may spend two or three hours a day travelling to and from the office, which leaves little time for anything else. Financial pressure also plays a role: people with low wages may need to work overtime or take a second job just to **make ends meet**.

Technology has made the problem worse. With smartphones and email, employees can be contacted at any time, including evenings, weekends and holidays. Some employers want to be able to reach their staff at all times. While this can be useful in emergencies and allows quick decisions, it also means that workers never fully relax. A manager who answers office calls during a family holiday may be physically with their family but mentally at work.

The consequences of poor work–life balance are serious. Constant pressure can lead to **stress, anxiety and burnout**, as well as physical problems such as poor sleep, high blood pressure and back pain. Family relationships may suffer when parents have little time for their children or partners. Ironically, overworked employees are often less productive, because tired people make more mistakes and lose creativity.

Fortunately, there are many ways to improve the situation. Employers can offer **flexible working hours**, allowing staff to start and finish at different times, and allow some work to be done from home to reduce commuting. They can set clear rules that employees are not expected to answer emails outside working hours, except in genuine emergencies. Some countries have introduced laws giving workers the "right to disconnect" after work.

Governments can also help by limiting maximum working hours, enforcing paid holidays and investing in public transport to reduce commuting time. Some companies have experimented with a four-day working week and found that staff were happier and just as productive.

Individuals have a role too. They can learn to manage their time more effectively, set boundaries, switch off work notifications in the evening and make time for exercise, family and rest. Learning to say no to unnecessary extra work can be difficult but important.

In conclusion, many people struggle to balance work with other parts of life because of long hours, commuting, financial pressure and constant digital contact. With flexible policies from employers, sensible laws and better personal habits, it is possible to achieve a **healthier balance** that benefits both workers and companies.

### Working from Home and Remote Work

In the past, almost all office work took place in an office. Today, thanks to fast internet, laptops and video-conferencing software, millions of people **work from home**, either full-time or for part of the week. This trend grew rapidly during the global pandemic, when many companies were forced to send their employees home, and it has continued in many industries since then.

For employees, the most obvious advantage is that working from home **saves commuting time** and money. In large cities with heavy traffic, workers may save several hours a day, which they can spend with their families, exercising or resting. Remote work also offers flexibility. Parents can take their children to school, and people with disabilities or health problems can work more comfortably. Many employees report that they can concentrate better at home, without the noise and interruptions of a busy office.

Employers can also benefit. Companies can **reduce office costs**, such as rent and electricity, and they can hire talented employees who live far away, even in other countries, rather than only those living near the office. Many companies have found that productivity can stay the same or even improve when staff are trusted to work from home, although results vary between jobs.

Remote work has wider benefits as well. Fewer people travelling to work means less traffic congestion and air pollution. It may also help smaller towns, because people no longer need to live in expensive city centres to have good jobs.

However, working from home has significant disadvantages. Many people feel **lonely and isolated** because they miss daily contact with colleagues. Communication can be slower and misunderstandings more frequent when teams cannot talk face to face. Young employees may find it harder to learn from experienced colleagues, and they may have fewer chances to be noticed for promotion.

Another problem is that the boundary between work and home life can disappear. When the office is the kitchen table, it can be difficult to stop working in the evening, and some people end up working longer hours than before. Others find home full of distractions, such as children, housework or television. Not everyone has a quiet room, a good desk or a fast internet connection.

For these reasons, many organisations now prefer a **hybrid model**, in which employees work from home for part of the week and in the office for the rest. This combines the flexibility and time savings of remote work with the teamwork and social contact of the office. Clear rules, regular online meetings and occasional team events can help remote staff feel connected.

In conclusion, working from home offers real benefits for both employees and employers, including flexibility, time savings and lower costs. However, it can also cause isolation and blur the line between work and home. A balanced, hybrid approach is likely to provide **the best of both worlds** for many workers.

### Job Security versus Career Change

In previous generations, many people stayed with the same employer for their whole working life. A secure job with a regular salary and a pension was seen as the ideal. Today, however, the job market is changing quickly, and people are more likely to change jobs, employers and even careers several times. This raises the question of whether it is better to value **job security** or to be willing to change careers.

Job security has clear advantages. A stable job provides a regular income, which allows people to plan their lives, take out loans to buy a house and support their families with confidence. Long-term employees often receive benefits such as pensions, health insurance and paid holidays. They also become experts in their roles and build strong relationships with colleagues. For people with family responsibilities, the **peace of mind** that comes from knowing their job is safe can be extremely valuable.

However, staying in the same job for many years can also have disadvantages. Some people become bored or lose motivation when they do the same tasks every day. They may stop learning new skills, which can make them vulnerable if their employer closes or their job is replaced by technology. In addition, people sometimes stay in jobs they dislike simply out of fear of change, which can lead to unhappiness and stress.

A **career change** can bring new energy and opportunities. People who move into a different field may find work that better matches their interests and values, leading to greater job satisfaction. Changing jobs can also lead to higher salaries, because employers often pay more to attract experienced staff. Moreover, people with experience in different fields can bring fresh ideas and a broad range of skills, which many employers value.

At the same time, changing careers involves risk. A person may need to retrain, which takes time and money, and they may have to start at a lower level with a lower salary. There is no guarantee that the new career will be more satisfying or successful. For people with heavy financial responsibilities, such risks can be difficult to accept.

In today's economy, the idea of "a job for life" is becoming less realistic in many industries. Automation, globalisation and new technologies mean that some jobs disappear while new ones appear. Therefore, true security may come less from staying in one job and more from **continuously updating one's skills**, so that one can find work in different situations.

Governments and employers can support workers by offering affordable training and retraining programmes, career advice and unemployment support during periods of transition.

In conclusion, job security offers stability and peace of mind, while career change offers growth and new opportunities. The right choice depends on personal circumstances, but in a fast-changing world, the ability to learn new skills and **adapt to change** may be the most valuable form of security.

### Automation, Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Work

Technology has always changed the way people work, from the invention of the steam engine to the arrival of computers. Today, automation and artificial intelligence (AI) are transforming the workplace at a faster rate than ever before. Robots assemble cars, software handles customer enquiries, and AI programs can write reports and analyse data. These changes create both opportunities and serious concerns about the **future of work**.

On the positive side, automation can **increase productivity** and reduce costs for businesses. Machines can work continuously and perform repetitive tasks accurately, which allows companies to produce more goods at lower prices. Automation can also make work safer by taking over dangerous jobs, such as working in mines, handling chemicals or lifting heavy loads. AI tools can save workers time on routine paperwork, allowing them to focus on more creative and meaningful tasks.

New technologies also **create new jobs**. Just as computers created jobs in software development, IT support and digital marketing, AI and automation are creating demand for data analysts, cybersecurity experts, robot technicians and many other roles that did not exist a few decades ago. In addition, cheaper products can increase demand, which may create employment in other parts of the economy.

However, many workers are worried about losing their jobs. Those whose work involves **routine and predictable tasks**, such as factory workers, cashiers, call-centre staff and data-entry clerks, are particularly at risk. In countries like Bangladesh, where millions of people work in labour-intensive industries such as garment manufacturing, increased automation could affect large numbers of workers. Older workers and those with less education may find it especially difficult to move into new types of work.

There is also a concern that the benefits of automation may not be shared fairly. Company owners and highly skilled workers may become richer, while low-skilled workers face unemployment or lower wages. This could **widen the gap** between rich and poor and create social tension.

To prepare for these changes, education and training are essential. Schools and universities should focus on skills that machines find difficult, such as creativity, critical thinking, communication and teamwork. Governments and employers should provide **retraining programmes** so that workers can learn new skills before their old jobs disappear. Lifelong learning should become a normal part of every career.

Governments can also strengthen social safety nets, such as unemployment benefits, to support workers during periods of change, and they may need to consider how to tax and regulate new technologies fairly.

In conclusion, automation and AI will continue to change the world of work, removing some jobs while creating others. The challenge is not to stop technological progress but to manage it wisely, so that workers are prepared and the benefits are **widely shared**. With the right policies, technology can make work safer, more productive and more interesting.

### Education, Skills and Getting a Job

For many young people, the main purpose of education is to find a good job. Parents often believe that a university degree is the key to a secure and well-paid career. However, in today's competitive job market, employers increasingly look for more than qualifications. Experience, soft skills and performance in interviews also play a major role in **securing a good job**.

A university degree is certainly valuable. Many professions, such as medicine, engineering, law and teaching, require formal qualifications. A degree shows employers that a candidate has knowledge in a particular field and the discipline to complete a long course of study. Graduates generally have access to a wider range of jobs and tend to earn higher salaries over their careers than people without degrees.

However, a degree alone **does not guarantee a job**. In many countries, the number of graduates has grown faster than the number of suitable jobs, so competition is intense. Employers often complain that graduates have theoretical knowledge but lack practical experience and **soft skills**, such as communication, teamwork, problem-solving and time management. As a result, a candidate with a degree and relevant work experience usually has a much stronger chance than one with a degree alone.

Experience can be gained in many ways: internships, part-time jobs, volunteering, student clubs and personal projects. A student who has worked in a shop has learned to deal with customers, handle responsibility and work under pressure. Someone who has organised a university event has shown leadership and planning skills. These experiences make a strong impression on employers.

Job interviews are another important step. **First impressions** matter: candidates who are well prepared, dress appropriately and communicate confidently are more likely to succeed. However, interviews have limitations. Some highly capable people are nervous in interviews and perform poorly, while others are skilled at presenting themselves but less effective in the actual job. For this reason, many companies now use additional methods, such as practical tests, group tasks and trial periods, to assess candidates more fairly.

Personal connections also influence hiring in many countries. While recommendations can help employers find reliable staff, recruitment based mainly on personal contacts can be unfair to talented candidates without connections. Transparent and merit-based recruitment is better for both fairness and company performance.

To improve their chances, students should combine their studies with practical experience and develop their soft skills. Universities can help by including internships and career guidance in their programmes, and employers can offer more training opportunities for young graduates.

In conclusion, a good education provides an important foundation for a career, but it is not enough on its own. Employers look for candidates who combine **knowledge with experience**, strong soft skills and the ability to present themselves well. The most successful job seekers are those who prepare in all of these areas.

### Young People and Part-Time Work

In many countries, teenagers and university students take part-time jobs while they are still studying. They may work in cafés, shops, restaurants or delivery services, or give private tuition to younger students. Some parents encourage their children to work, believing that it teaches valuable lessons, while others worry that work may harm their studies and well-being.

There are several advantages to young people having part-time jobs. Firstly, work teaches **responsibility and discipline**. Young workers must arrive on time, follow instructions, serve customers politely and complete tasks properly. These habits are useful in every future job. Secondly, earning their own money helps young people **understand the value of money**. When a teenager has to work several hours to buy a new phone, they are more likely to spend carefully and save.

Part-time work also provides **valuable work experience**. Employers often prefer candidates who have some experience, even in a simple job, because it shows maturity and reliability. Young people develop soft skills such as communication, teamwork and problem-solving, and they may discover what kind of work they enjoy or dislike, which can help them choose a career. In addition, a part-time job can help students from poorer families pay for their education and reduce the financial burden on their parents.

However, there are also disadvantages. The most serious is that work may take time and energy away from **studying**. A student who works long hours in the evening may be too tired to concentrate in class or complete homework, and their exam results may suffer. Children and teenagers also need time to rest, play, take part in sports and spend time with family and friends, which are important for healthy development.

Another concern is that young workers may be **exploited**. Some employers pay very low wages, ask young people to work long or late hours, or provide unsafe working conditions, knowing that young workers may not understand their rights. In some developing countries, child labour remains a serious problem, and children who work full-time may leave school completely.

There is also a risk that young people may become too focused on earning money and lose interest in long-term education, especially if a job offers immediate income while education seems distant and difficult.

To gain the benefits and avoid the risks, part-time work should be limited and appropriate to the young person's age. Governments should enforce laws on minimum working age, maximum working hours for students and safe working conditions. Parents should make sure that work does not interfere with schoolwork and health. Employers can offer flexible hours that fit around school schedules.

In conclusion, part-time work can teach young people responsibility, financial skills and valuable experience. However, it must be carefully balanced with education, rest and personal development. When hours are reasonable and conditions are fair, the **advantages generally outweigh the disadvantages**.

### Gender Equality at Work

Over the past century, women's participation in the workforce has increased dramatically. In many countries, women now work as doctors, engineers, managers, politicians and entrepreneurs, roles that were once almost entirely held by men. In Bangladesh, the garment industry employs millions of women, giving many of them their own income for the first time. Nevertheless, **gender inequality** at work remains a significant problem in many parts of the world.

One major issue is the **gender pay gap**. In many countries, women on average earn less than men. This is partly because women are more likely to work in lower-paid sectors or part-time jobs, and partly because of discrimination, where women are paid less than men for similar work. Women are also under-represented in senior positions, such as company directors and top managers, a problem sometimes described as the "glass ceiling".

Another challenge is the balance between work and family responsibilities. In many societies, women are still expected to do most of the housework and childcare, even when they work full-time. This **double burden** can cause exhaustion and limit women's career progress. Some employers are reluctant to hire young women because they expect them to take maternity leave in the future. Such discrimination is not only unfair but also illegal in many countries.

There are strong reasons to promote gender equality at work. Firstly, it is a matter of **fairness and human rights**: people should be judged on their ability, not their gender. Secondly, it benefits businesses and the economy. Companies that recruit from the whole population, rather than only half of it, have access to more talent and different perspectives, which can improve decision-making and creativity. When women earn their own income, families have more financial security, and children, especially daughters, gain positive role models.

Women's employment can also have wider social benefits. Women with their own income often have a stronger voice in family decisions and are more likely to invest in their children's education and health.

Several measures can help to achieve equality. Governments can pass and enforce laws requiring **equal pay for equal work** and banning discrimination in recruitment and promotion. Paid maternity and paternity leave, along with affordable childcare, can help both parents to combine work and family life. Companies can offer flexible working hours, mentoring programmes for women and transparent promotion systems. At home, sharing housework and childcare more equally between men and women is also essential.

Changing attitudes is equally important. Schools and the media can challenge stereotypes about "men's jobs" and "women's jobs", encouraging girls to study subjects such as science and engineering and boys to consider careers such as nursing and teaching.

In conclusion, although women have made great progress in the workplace, inequality in pay, promotion and family responsibilities remains. Achieving gender equality requires fair laws, supportive workplace policies and changes in social attitudes, and it benefits **not only women but society as a whole**.

### Older Workers and Retirement

As people live longer and healthier lives, the question of when and how people should retire has become increasingly important. In many countries, the population is ageing, which means there are more retired people and fewer young workers. This has led to discussions about raising the retirement age and encouraging companies to **employ older people**.

There are many advantages for companies that employ older workers. Older employees usually have years of **experience and knowledge** that cannot be learned quickly from books. They have dealt with many different situations, and they often have strong judgement and problem-solving skills. They also tend to be loyal and reliable; they are less likely to change jobs frequently, which reduces recruitment and training costs. In addition, older workers can act as **mentors**, passing on their knowledge to younger colleagues and helping them develop professionally.

Older employees can also improve customer relations. Many customers appreciate dealing with mature, patient and experienced staff, and a workforce that includes people of different ages can better understand the needs of a varied customer base. Teams that mix young and older workers often combine new ideas with practical wisdom.

However, there are also some disadvantages. Older workers may expect higher salaries because of their experience, which increases costs for the company. Some may have health problems that lead to more sick days. In addition, some older workers may find it harder to learn new technology or adapt to rapid changes in working methods, although this is not true for everyone and can be addressed through training. There is also a concern that if older people continue working longer, there may be fewer opportunities for young people entering the job market.

For society, encouraging people to work longer can **reduce pressure on pension systems**, because people pay taxes for longer and receive pensions for a shorter time. Many older people also benefit personally from continuing to work, as it keeps them mentally active, provides social contact and gives them a sense of purpose. On the other hand, people who do physically demanding jobs, such as construction or farming, may not be able to work safely into their late sixties.

A flexible approach is therefore the most sensible. Rather than forcing everyone to retire at a fixed age, governments can allow people to choose when to retire within a reasonable range, and companies can offer **part-time or flexible roles** for older employees. Training programmes can help older workers keep their skills up to date.

In conclusion, employing older people brings companies valuable experience, loyalty and mentoring, although it may involve higher costs and the need for training. With flexible retirement policies and support for lifelong learning, both companies and society can **make the most of** the skills of older workers.

### Pay, Promotion and Motivation

Why do people work hard? For many, the answer seems simple: money and promotion. Higher salaries and senior positions bring financial rewards, status and respect. However, everyday experience in the workplace shows that motivation is more complex. Pay, promotion, recognition, a pleasant work environment and a sense of purpose all play a role.

**Promotion** is one of the strongest motivations in the modern workplace. A promotion usually brings a higher salary, which allows people to support their families and improve their standard of living. It also brings status: in many societies, a person's job title strongly affects how they are seen by family, friends and the community. Promotion can also give people more responsibility and influence, which many find rewarding. For these reasons, the desire to be promoted encourages many employees to **work hard** and develop their skills.

Money is certainly important, particularly for people who are struggling financially. Some argue that people who are "hungry for money" make the most dedicated workers, especially in sales jobs where income depends on results. However, money alone is not always the strongest motivator. Once people earn enough to live comfortably, other factors often become more important, such as **interesting work**, appreciation from managers and good relationships with colleagues.

The **work environment** has a strong influence on motivation. Employees who feel respected, listened to and valued are more likely to give their best effort. A supportive manager who praises good work and helps staff to improve can motivate people more than a small pay rise. In contrast, a workplace with constant criticism, unfair treatment or bullying can destroy motivation, even if salaries are high. Good facilities and equipment are also helpful, but they cannot replace a culture of respect.

Fairness in pay is another important issue. Some people suggest that paying all workers the same salary would promote harmony among colleagues. While this might reduce jealousy, it could also reduce motivation, because hard-working and highly skilled employees would receive no extra reward for their effort. Most people believe pay should reflect skills, responsibility and performance, as long as the system is transparent and fair.

There is also a wider debate about how society values different jobs. Doctors, nurses and teachers make an enormous contribution to society, yet they often earn far less than entertainment and sports celebrities. Many people feel that this is unfair and that essential workers deserve better pay and recognition, although celebrity incomes are largely determined by the market, not by governments.

In conclusion, promotion and money are powerful motivators, but they are not the only reasons people work hard. Recognition, respect, meaningful work and a positive environment are equally important. Employers who understand this can **create a motivated workforce** that performs well and stays loyal.

### Self-Employment and Entrepreneurship

Not everyone wants to work for an employer. A growing number of people, especially young people, are choosing to start their own businesses or work as freelancers. Thanks to the internet and social media, it is now easier than ever to sell products, offer services and reach customers without a large office or shop. In Bangladesh and many other countries, online businesses selling clothes, food, handicrafts and services have become very popular.

There are many attractions to **self-employment**. The most obvious is independence. Self-employed people are their own bosses; they can make their own decisions, choose their working hours and decide what kind of work to do. This freedom can bring great job satisfaction. Entrepreneurs who succeed may also earn much more than they would as employees, because they keep the profits of their own business.

Entrepreneurship also benefits society. New businesses **create jobs** for other people, introduce new products and services and encourage competition, which can lower prices and improve quality for consumers. Many of today's large companies started as small businesses run by one or two people with an idea. Self-employment can also provide opportunities for people who find it difficult to get traditional jobs, such as women who need flexible hours to care for children, or people in areas with few employers.

However, self-employment involves considerable **risks**. Many new businesses fail within their first few years, and the owner may lose their savings or fall into debt. Self-employed people usually have no fixed income; in a bad month, they may earn very little. They also lack benefits such as paid holidays, sick pay and pensions, which employees often receive.

In addition, running a business can be extremely demanding. Entrepreneurs often work very long hours and must handle everything themselves, from production and marketing to accounting and customer service. The stress of being responsible for everything can affect their health and family life. Without colleagues, some self-employed people also feel isolated.

Young people who want to start a business often face specific challenges, such as a lack of capital, experience and business knowledge. Banks may be unwilling to lend them money, and they may not know how to manage finances or legal requirements.

Governments and society can support entrepreneurs in several ways. They can offer **small loans with low interest**, business training and advice, simplify the process of registering a business and reduce taxes for new small businesses. Schools and universities can teach basic business skills, and successful entrepreneurs can mentor young people.

In conclusion, self-employment offers independence, flexibility and the chance to create something new, and entrepreneurs play a valuable role in the economy. However, it also involves financial risk and heavy workloads. With proper preparation and support, more people can **turn their ideas into successful businesses**.

### Migration for Work and Brain Drain

Throughout history, people have moved in search of better work and a better life. Today, this happens on two main levels. Within countries, many people move from rural areas to cities to find jobs. Internationally, millions of people move to other countries for work, from construction workers and domestic workers to highly qualified doctors, engineers and IT specialists.

The reasons for **rural-to-urban migration** are mainly economic. In many developing countries, farming provides low and uncertain incomes, and rural areas offer few other jobs. Cities, by contrast, offer factories, offices, shops and services, as well as better schools and hospitals. In Bangladesh, for example, many young people move from villages to Dhaka or Chattogram to work in garment factories or the service sector. This movement can help families escape poverty, but it also creates problems: cities become overcrowded, housing becomes expensive, slums grow, and traffic and pollution increase. Meanwhile, villages may lose their young and energetic workers, leaving older people behind.

International migration offers similar opportunities on a larger scale. Workers can earn much higher wages abroad and send money home to their families. These **remittances** are a major source of income for many developing countries, including Bangladesh, helping families pay for education, healthcare and housing. Migrant workers also gain new skills and experience that may be useful if they return home.

However, migrant workers often face serious difficulties. Some work long hours in difficult conditions, and some are exploited by dishonest recruitment agents or employers. Living far from their families for many years can cause loneliness and family problems.

When highly educated professionals move abroad permanently, their home countries suffer from **brain drain**. Doctors, nurses, engineers and scientists are often trained at great public expense, and when they leave, their skills are lost to their own country. Skilled people move abroad for many reasons: higher salaries, better working conditions, modern equipment, opportunities for research and career development, political stability and a better future for their children. Brain drain can weaken healthcare and education systems and slow economic development.

There are also debates in the countries that receive workers. Some people argue that bringing in foreign workers to do jobs that local people are unwilling to do causes problems for local communities. Others point out that migrant workers fill important gaps in the labour market, support the economy and often do essential but difficult work.

Several solutions can help. Developing countries can improve salaries, working conditions and research opportunities for professionals, and they can encourage those living abroad to return or to share their expertise. Rural development, such as better roads, schools and small industries, can reduce pressure to move to cities. Governments should also **protect migrant workers' rights** through fair recruitment and agreements with destination countries.

In conclusion, migration for work brings income and opportunities but also creates challenges, such as overcrowded cities, family separation and brain drain. With fair policies and investment at home, countries can **maximise the benefits** of migration while reducing its costs.

## Part 5 — Sentence Structure Toolkit {: .part}

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Paragraph mix formula:** প্রতিটি body paragraph-এ রাখার চেষ্টা করো → **1টি long simple + 1–2টি compound + 2–3টি complex + 1টি compound-complex** sentence। 5–6টি বাক্য × গড়ে 20 শব্দ = **100–120 words**।

</div>

#### 1. Long simple sentence (rich noun phrase)

**Formula:** [long noun phrase] + verb + object
*একটাই clause, কিন্তু subject-টা বড় ও তথ্যপূর্ণ।*

- The growing number of employees working from home **has changed** the way companies manage their staff.
- A well-paid job with regular working hours **gives** people financial security and time for their families.

#### 2. Compound with *and / but / so / yet*

- A high salary brings security, **but** it does not always bring happiness.
- Many graduates lack experience, **so** employers prefer candidates with internships.

#### 3. Compound with semicolon + linker

- Working from home saves time**; however,** it can make employees feel isolated.
- Older workers have valuable experience**; therefore,** companies should not ignore them.

#### 4. Complex — concession (*Although / Even though / While*)

- **Although** money is important, job satisfaction matters more in the long term.
- **Even though** automation removes some jobs, it creates new ones in technology.

#### 5. Complex — reason (*Because / Since / As*)

- **Since** people spend most of their adult lives at work, they should choose a job they enjoy.
- **Because** commuting takes hours in big cities, many workers prefer remote work.

#### 6. Complex — condition (*If / Unless / Provided that*)

- **If** employees feel respected, they **are more likely to** stay with the company.
- **Unless** workers update their skills, they **may** lose their jobs to machines.

#### 7. Relative clause (*which / who / where / whose*)

- Employees **who** enjoy their work are usually more productive.
- Many people work overtime, **which** leaves them little time for their families.

#### 8. Compound-complex

**Formula:** Although/Because/If + clause, Clause A, and/but Clause B

- **Although** remote work is convenient, some employees feel lonely, **and** others work longer hours.
- **If** companies offer flexible hours, parents can care for their children, **and** productivity often improves.

#### 9. Participle phrase

- **Working** long hours every day, many employees have no time for exercise.
- **Trained** in their home country, many doctors later move abroad for better pay.

#### 10. *Not only … but also …*

- A good manager **not only** sets clear goals **but also** recognises hard work.
- Part-time jobs **not only** teach responsibility **but also** provide useful experience.

#### 11. *The more …, the more …*

- **The more** valued employees feel, **the harder** they work.
- **The longer** people work without rest, **the more** mistakes they make.

#### 12. Cleft sentence (emphasis)

- **It is** job satisfaction, not salary, **that** keeps many teachers in their profession.
- **What** most workers want **is** fair pay and respect.

#### 13. Impersonal passive

- **It is widely believed that** a university degree guarantees a good job.
- **It is often argued that** remote work reduces productivity.

#### 14. Contrasting views

- **While some people choose** jobs for the salary, **others prefer** work that they find meaningful.
- **While** employers benefit from constant contact, **employees** may suffer from stress.

#### 15. Result / consequence

- Many skilled doctors move abroad, **which means that** hospitals at home are short of staff.
- Automation is spreading quickly. **As a result,** routine jobs are disappearing.

7. **শব্দে শব্দে অনুবাদ করবে না।** অর্থটা স্বাভাবিক ইংরেজিতে প্রকাশ করো। ঘড়ি ধরো: প্রতিটি essay 40 মিনিটে।

#### Introduction (30–40 words)

বাড়ি থেকে অনলাইনে কাজ করা এখন অনেক দেশে ক্রমেই সাধারণ হয়ে উঠছে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> যদিও এই প্রবণতা একাকীত্ব তৈরি করতে পারে এবং কাজ ও ঘরের সীমারেখা অস্পষ্ট করে দিতে পারে, এটি যাতায়াতের সময় বাঁচায় এবং কর্মীদের আরও নমনীয়তা দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — সুবিধা (120–135 words)

এতে কোনো সন্দেহ নেই যে বাড়ি থেকে কাজ করা কর্মী ও নিয়োগকর্তা উভয়ের জন্য উল্লেখযোগ্য সুবিধা বয়ে আনে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> প্রথমত, সবচেয়ে বড় সুবিধা হলো, বাড়ি থেকে কাজ করলে যাতায়াতের (commuting) সময় ও টাকা বাঁচে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এর কারণ হলো, তীব্র যানজটের বড় শহরগুলোতে কর্মীরা দিনে কয়েক ঘণ্টা বাঁচাতে পারেন, যা তারা বিশ্রামে কাটাতে পারেন। <span class="tag">Complex</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, ঢাকার যে অফিসকর্মী একসময় দিনে তিন ঘণ্টা যানজটে কাটাতেন, তিনি এখন সেই সময় বিশ্রাম নিতে পারেন, এবং তিনি অনেক কম ক্লান্ত বোধ করেন। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> এছাড়া, দূর থেকে কাজ (remote work) শুধু কর্মীদের নমনীয়তাই দেয় না, কোম্পানিগুলোকে অফিসের খরচ কমাতেও সাহায্য করে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> এই নমনীয়তার ফলে বাবা-মা সন্তানদের স্কুলে পৌঁছে দিয়েও নিজেদের কাজ শেষ করতে পারেন, যা ঘরের মানসিক চাপ কমায়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> সব মিলিয়ে, বাড়ি থেকে কাজ মানুষকে নিজের সময়ের ওপর বেশি নিয়ন্ত্রণ দেয় এবং কর্মী ও ব্যবসা দুই পক্ষেরই টাকা বাঁচায়। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — অসুবিধা (120–135 words)

অন্যদিকে, এটি অস্বীকার করা যায় না যে বাড়ি থেকে কাজ করার কিছু অসুবিধাও আছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> প্রথমত, অনেকে একাকী ও বিচ্ছিন্ন বোধ করেন, কারণ তারা সহকর্মীদের সাথে প্রতিদিনের যোগাযোগ হারান। <span class="tag">Complex</span> সামনাসামনি কথোপকথন ছাড়া দলগত কাজ (teamwork) ধীর হয়ে যায়; ফলে তরুণ কর্মীরা অভিজ্ঞ সহকর্মীদের কাছ থেকে শেখার সুযোগ কম পান। <span class="tag">Compound</span> যেমন, বাড়িতে একা কাজ করা একজন নতুন স্নাতক হয়তো সিনিয়র সহকর্মীদের কাছ থেকে দ্রুত পরামর্শ পান না, যা তার অগ্রগতি ধীর করে দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> দ্বিতীয়ত, নির্দিষ্ট কর্মদিবস না থাকা একটি বড় বাধা হতে পারে, কারণ কাজ ও পারিবারিক জীবনের সীমারেখা (boundary) মুছে যেতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> ছোট সন্তান আছে এমন একজন বাবা বা মা যদি শান্ত কোনো ঘর ছাড়া কোলাহলপূর্ণ বাড়িতে কাজ করেন, তাহলে তাকে হয়তো অনেক রাত পর্যন্ত কাজ চালিয়ে যেতে হয়, এবং ফলস্বরূপ তিনি ক্লান্ত হয়ে পড়েন। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> সব মিলিয়ে, এই সামাজিক ও বাস্তব সমস্যাগুলো কিছু কর্মীর জন্য দূর থেকে কাজকে চাপপূর্ণ ও কম ফলপ্রসূ করে তুলতে পারে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Conclusion (25–35 words)

পরিশেষে, দূর থেকে কাজ এখন কর্মজীবনের একটি স্বাভাবিক অংশ। <span class="tag">Simple</span> যদিও (While) বাড়ি থেকে কাজ করা কর্মীরা বিচ্ছিন্ন বোধ করতে পারেন এবং কাজ থেকে মন সরাতে তাদের কষ্ট হতে পারে, তারা দীর্ঘ যাতায়াত এড়াতে পারেন এবং বেশি স্বাধীনতা উপভোগ করেন। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Introduction (30–40 words)

কেউ কেউ মনে করেন যে চাকরি মূলত বেতন দেখেই বেছে নেওয়া উচিত, কারণ ভালো আয় পরিবারকে সুরক্ষা দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে টাকা একটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ বিষয়, এবং কাজের সন্তুষ্টি (job satisfaction) ও ক্যারিয়ারে উন্নতির সুযোগও বিবেচনা করা উচিত। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — Q1: Opinion block (90–120 words)

আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে চাকরি বেছে নেওয়ার সময় টাকা একটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ বিষয়, কারণ এটি পরিবারকে নিরাপত্তা দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> ভালো বেতন মানুষকে পরিবারের ভরণপোষণ করতে, সন্তানদের পড়াশোনার খরচ দিতে, একটি ভালো বাসা কিনতে বা ভাড়া নিতে এবং ভবিষ্যতের জন্য সঞ্চয় করতে সাহায্য করে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, যে তরুণকে বাবা-মা ও ছোট ভাইবোনদের দায়িত্ব নিতে হয়, তার জন্য শুধু আকর্ষণীয় বলে কম বেতনের চাকরি বেছে নেওয়া বাস্তবসম্মত না-ও হতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যদিও কেউ কেউ যুক্তি দেন যে টাকা দিয়ে সুখ কেনা যায় না, টাকার অভাব বাসাভাড়া, বিল ও স্কুলের বেতন নিয়ে সারাক্ষণ দুশ্চিন্তা তৈরি করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> তাই ক্যারিয়ারের যেকোনো সিদ্ধান্তে আয়কে গুরুত্বের সাথে বিবেচনা করা উচিত। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — Q2: List block (90–120 words)

আরেকটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ বিষয়, যা বিবেচনা করা উচিত, তা হলো কাজের সন্তুষ্টি। <span class="tag">Complex</span> কাজ যদি মানুষের কাছে একঘেয়ে, অর্থহীন বা চাপপূর্ণ মনে হয়, তাহলে তারা যত টাকাই আয় করুক না কেন, প্রতিদিন অসুখী বোধ করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যেমন, ভালো বেতনের একজন হিসাবরক্ষক যদি নিজের কাজ অপছন্দ করেন, তিনি প্রতি সোমবার সকালকে ভয় পেতে পারেন, যা ধীরে ধীরে তার স্বাস্থ্য ও পারিবারিক জীবনের ক্ষতি করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> আরেকটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ বিষয় হলো ক্যারিয়ারে উন্নতির সুযোগ (career growth)। <span class="tag">Simple</span> ভালো প্রশিক্ষণ ও পদোন্নতির সুযোগ আছে এমন চাকরি পরে অনেক বেশি বেতনের দিকে নিয়ে যেতে পারে; ফলে শুরুর কম বেতন সবসময় খারাপ সিদ্ধান্ত নয়। <span class="tag">Compound</span> ফলে একজন বুদ্ধিমান চাকরিপ্রার্থী প্রথম বেতনের বাইরেও ভবিষ্যৎ দেখেন। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Introduction (30–40 words)

আজকাল অনেক মানুষ চাকরির পাশাপাশি পরিবার, স্বাস্থ্য ও অবসরের জন্য সময় বের করতে হিমশিম খান। <span class="tag">Simple</span> এর মূল কারণ দীর্ঘ কর্মঘণ্টা ও সারাক্ষণ অনলাইনে যোগাযোগ, কিন্তু নমনীয় চাকরি ও অফিসের message-এর স্পষ্ট সীমা এই সমস্যার মোকাবিলা করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — 2টি কারণ (90–120 words)

এই সমস্যার একটি প্রধান কারণ হলো অনেক আধুনিক চাকরির দীর্ঘ কর্মঘণ্টা। <span class="tag">Simple</span> প্রতিযোগিতামূলক অর্থনীতিতে অনেক কর্মী দীর্ঘ সময় কাজ করেন, কারণ তারা চাকরি হারানোর ভয় পান বা পদোন্নতি (promotion) পেতে চান। <span class="tag">Complex</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, পদোন্নতির আশায় থাকা একজন ব্যাংককর্মী প্রতিদিন অনেক রাত পর্যন্ত অফিসে থাকতে পারেন, এবং এতে সন্তানদের জন্য খুব কম সময় থাকে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> আরেকটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ কারণ হলো প্রযুক্তি, যা নিয়োগকর্তাদের যেকোনো সময় ফোন ও email-এর মাধ্যমে কর্মীদের সাথে যোগাযোগ করার সুযোগ দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> কর্মীরা যখন গভীর রাতে বা ছুটির দিনে message পান, তখন তারা পুরোপুরি বিশ্রাম নিতে পারেন না, এবং তাদের মানসিক চাপ বাড়ে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> ফলে একসময় জীবনের অন্য দিকগুলোর জন্য রাখা সময়ের অনেকটাই এখন কাজে ভরে যায়। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — 2টি সমাধান (90–120 words)

সবচেয়ে কার্যকর সমাধান হলো, নিয়োগকর্তারা নমনীয় কর্মঘণ্টা (flexible working hours) চালু করবেন। <span class="tag">Simple</span> কর্মীরা যখন নিজেদের কর্মঘণ্টা বেছে নিতে বা কিছু দিন বাড়ি থেকে কাজ করতে পারেন, তখন তারা পরিবারের জন্যও সময় পান। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যেমন, যে ব্যাংককর্মী সকালে আগে কাজ শুরু করে বিকেল চারটায় অফিস ছাড়েন, তিনি স্কুল থেকে সন্তানদের নিয়ে আসতে পারেন, যা পারিবারিক জীবনকে মজবুত করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এছাড়া, ব্যবস্থাপক ও কর্মী উভয়েই অফিসের message-এর ক্ষেত্রে স্পষ্ট সীমা নির্ধারণ করতে পারেন। <span class="tag">Simple</span> সত্যিকারের জরুরি অবস্থা ছাড়া অফিস সময়ের পরে ব্যবস্থাপকদের কর্মীদের সাথে যোগাযোগ করা উচিত নয়, এবং কর্মীরা সন্ধ্যায় কাজের notification বন্ধ রাখতে পারেন। <span class="tag">Compound</span> এই পদক্ষেপগুলো একসাথে মানুষকে কাজের বাইরের জীবন উপভোগ করার সময় ও শক্তি দেবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Conclusion (30–40 words)

পরিশেষে, অনেকের কাছে এই ভারসাম্য রক্ষা কঠিন, কারণ চাকরি দীর্ঘ সময় নিয়ে নেয় এবং প্রযুক্তির মাধ্যমে বাড়ি পর্যন্ত তাদের পিছু ছাড়ে না। <span class="tag">Complex</span> নমনীয় সময়সূচি এবং অফিস-পরবর্তী message-এর ওপর কঠোর সীমা তাদের বিশ্রাম নিতে সাহায্য করবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

**Common mistakes in work essays (এগুলো এড়িয়ে চলো):**

- ❌ *I want to do a good job in future.* → ✅ I want to get a good job **in the future**.
- ❌ *Workers do overtime work.* → ✅ Workers **work overtime**.
- ❌ *a work / works* (meaning a job) → ✅ **a job**; "work" is usually uncountable.
- ❌ *He got promotion.* → ✅ He **got a promotion** / He **was promoted**.
- ❌ *The salary of doctors are low.* → ✅ The salary of doctors **is** low / Doctors' salaries **are** low.
- ❌ *Employer and employee* mixed up → ✅ **employer** = the company/boss; **employee** = the worker.
- **"job" প্রতিশব্দ:** position, post, occupation, profession, career, employment, role

</div>

### Homework 1 — Agree / Disagree

<div class="question">Doctors, nurses and teachers make a great contribution to society and should be paid more than entertainment and sports celebrities. Do you agree or disagree?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · WRK-08*

**Essay type:** Agree / Disagree — one clear side · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 270–345 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 35–50 · BP1 Idea 1 100–120 · BP2 Idea 2 100–120 · Conclusion 35–55<br>
**Use ideas from:** Pay, Promotion & Motivation; Job Satisfaction vs High Salary; Migration & Brain Drain

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- একটিই পক্ষ নাও। "বেশি বেতন প্রাপ্য, কিন্তু তারকাদের আয় বাজার ঠিক করে" ধরনের আংশিক উত্তর লিখবে না।
- Idea 1: ডাক্তার, নার্স ও শিক্ষকদের অবদান (উদাহরণ: মহামারির সময় স্বাস্থ্যকর্মী)। Idea 2: কম বেতনে মেধাবীরা পেশা ছাড়ে বা বিদেশে যায় (brain drain)।
- তারকাদের পক্ষের যুক্তি (ছোট ক্যারিয়ার, কোটি দর্শক) BP2-এর Response বাক্যে Although অংশে রাখো।
- উপসংহার: In conclusion, I firmly believe that… + দুটি idea নতুন শব্দে। নতুন কোনো সুপারিশ বা idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** make a great contribution to society · essential workers · deserve better pay · attract talented people · brain drain · determined by the market · public funding · recognition

**Sentence target:** use *It is… that…* once and *While…, …* once.

</div>

### Homework 2 — Discussion

<div class="question">Completing university education is thought by some to be the best way to get a good job. On the other hand, other people think that getting experience and developing soft skills is more important. Discuss both sides and give your opinion.</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · WRK-03*

**Essay type:** Discussion (both views + opinion) · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–299 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 35–45 · BP1 (the other view) 90–120 · BP2 (your view) 90–120 · Conclusion 35–45<br>
**Use ideas from:** Education, Skills & Getting a Job; Young People & Part-Time Work; Chapter 01 (University & Employment)

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- ডিগ্রির পক্ষে 2টি যুক্তি কী (কিছু পেশায় বাধ্যতামূলক, জ্ঞান ও চিন্তার দক্ষতা, বেশি বেতন)?
- অভিজ্ঞতা ও soft skills-এর পক্ষে 2টি যুক্তি কী (ডিগ্রি চাকরির নিশ্চয়তা দেয় না, নিয়োগকর্তারা দলগত কাজ ও যোগাযোগ দক্ষতা চান)?
- তোমার মত: একটিই পক্ষ বেছে নাও, কখনো আংশিক নয়। সেটি সবসময় BP2-তে রাখো। একটি বাস্তব উদাহরণ দাও (internship করা বনাম না করা স্নাতক)।
- উপসংহারে নতুন কোনো idea বা সুপারিশ নয়: প্রশ্ন নতুন শব্দে, দুই মতের মূল কথা, আর একই মত।

</div>

**Try to use:** a university degree · does not guarantee a job · soft skills such as teamwork · gain work experience · internships · a competitive job market · employers look for

**Sentence target:** use *While some people argue that…, others believe that…* in the introduction and one relative clause with *who*.

</div>

### Homework 3 — Advantages–Disadvantages

<div class="question">Some employers want to be able to contact their staff at all times, even on holidays. Does this development have more advantages than disadvantages?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · WRK-01 · ★ work–life balance*

**Essay type:** Advantages–Disadvantages, Type 3 (positive or negative?) · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 330–360 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 40–50 · BP1 advantages 120–135 · BP2 disadvantages 120–135 · Conclusion 35–45<br>
**Use ideas from:** Work–Life Balance; Working from Home; Pay, Promotion & Motivation

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- একটিই রায় দাও: positive অথবা negative। অসুবিধার পক্ষে হলে Although ও While বাক্যে আগে সুবিধা, comma-র পরে অসুবিধা।
- Body 1 (ঠিক 7টি বাক্য): নিয়োগকর্তার জন্য 2টি সুবিধা (জরুরি সমস্যার দ্রুত সমাধান, বিদেশি ক্লায়েন্টের সাথে কাজ)।
- Body 2 (ঠিক 7টি বাক্য): কর্মীর জন্য 2টি অসুবিধা (বিশ্রাম নেই ও burnout, পারিবারিক সময় নষ্ট); অসুবিধার পক্ষে হলে শেষ বাক্য "Although some…"।
- Introduction-এ "In my view, …" ও Conclusion-এ "Overall, I believe…" — দুই জায়গায় একই রায়।

</div>

**Try to use:** contact staff at all times · deal with emergencies quickly · never fully relax · suffer from burnout · take a toll on family life · the right to disconnect · reduce productivity · staff turnover

**Sentence target:** use *not only… but also…* once and *Unless…, …* once.

</div>

### Homework 4 — Double Question

<div class="question">Some people with a good education and experience in their field decide to move abroad to work. Why do you think that is? What problems does this cause?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · WRK-14*

**Essay type:** Double Question — List + List · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–300 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 30–40 · BP1 = Q1 (List block) 90–120 · BP2 = Q2 (List block) 90–120 · Conclusion 30–40<br>
**Use ideas from:** Migration for Work & Brain Drain; Pay, Promotion & Motivation

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- দুটি প্রশ্নই কিছুর নাম জানতে চায় ("Why do you think…?" একটি ফাঁদ), তাই দুটিই List block; essay-র কোথাও "I" নয়।
- BP1: 2টি কারণ (বেশি বেতন; ভালো কাজের পরিবেশ ও গবেষণার সুযোগ) — উদাহরণ: একজন বাংলাদেশি ডাক্তার যুক্তরাজ্যে চলে যান।
- BP2: 2টি সমস্যা (brain drain ও গ্রামের হাসপাতালে ডাক্তার সংকট; পরিবার বিচ্ছিন্ন হওয়া)।
- প্রশ্নে সমাধান চাওয়া হয়নি, তাই উপসংহারে কোনো সমাধান নয়। Introduction: paraphrase + [উত্তর 1], and [উত্তর 2]। Conclusion-এ দুটি উত্তর নতুন শব্দে, নতুন idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** highly qualified professionals · better working conditions · career development · brain drain · trained at public expense · a shortage of doctors · remittances · family separation

**Sentence target:** use *The more…, the more…* once and a participle phrase (*Trained in…, many doctors…*) once.

</div>
