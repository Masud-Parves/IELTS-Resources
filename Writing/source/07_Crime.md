## Part 1 — Idea Generation Flow {: .part}

<div class="flow">Crime → Causes of Crime (Poverty, Education, Family) → Prison vs Rehabilitation → Repeat Offending → Community Service &amp; Alternatives → Capital Punishment → Sentencing &amp; Fixed Punishments → Youth Crime &amp; Parents → Media &amp; Violence → Police, Technology &amp; Surveillance → Cybercrime → Drugs &amp; Alcohol → Crime in Cities &amp; Prevention</div>

When you see **any** crime question, run through this chain from left to right and ask: *"Does this question touch this area?"* Then **stop at the 2 links that fit the question best**. Those 2 links become your two body paragraphs.

**Memory trick — every crime question is about one of 3 stages: BEFORE → DURING → AFTER**

| Stage | Key question | Typical ideas |
|---|---|---|
| **BEFORE** the crime | Why do people commit crime? How can we prevent it? | poverty, unemployment, poor education, family problems, drugs, media; education, jobs, youth programmes |
| **DURING / catching** | How do we catch criminals and keep people safe? | police patrols, CCTV, technology, community watch, cybercrime units |
| **AFTER** the crime | How should criminals be punished or changed? | prison, rehabilitation, community service, death penalty, fixed sentences, reoffending |

Most good crime essays combine two stages: for example, *"BEFORE: education and jobs prevent crime"* + *"AFTER: rehabilitation stops reoffending"*.

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Example:** *"Some people think that more police on the streets is the only way to reduce crime."*

This is a **DURING** idea (police). The word **"only"** invites you to add **BEFORE** ideas (education, jobs, youth clubs) and **AFTER** ideas (rehabilitation).
Chain links: **Police, Technology & Surveillance** (BP1) + **Causes of Crime** / **Crime Prevention** (BP2).

</div>

## Part 2 — Exam-Hall Brainstorming System {: .part}

### 2A. The 5-Minute Plan

| Minute | What to do | What you write on the question paper |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | **Decode the question.** Underline the topic words and the instruction words. Decide which of your five essay guides it uses (Part 2G). Look for absolute words: *only, best, always, all*. | e.g. *prison / better ways / your opinion → Discuss Both Views guide (two views given)* |
| 1–3 | **B-D-A grid.** Before – During – After: one or two notes each. | 4–6 one-line notes |
| 3–4 | **Choose the 2 strongest ideas.** Add one reason and one example to each. | Idea 1 + why + example; Idea 2 + why + example |
| 4–5 | **Decide your position (one clear side, only if the question asks) and use the guide's word plan.** | Thesis / BP1 / BP2 / Conclusion in 4 lines |

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**পরীক্ষার হলে মনে রাখো:** প্রশ্নে **only, best, main, all** শব্দ থাকলে সেটা একটা ফাঁদ। প্রায় সবসময় সবচেয়ে ভালো উত্তর হলো **"এটা একটা উপায়, কিন্তু একমাত্র/সবচেয়ে ভালো উপায় নয়"**। যেমন: "Is poverty the **main** cause of crime?" → "একটি বড় কারণ, তবে একমাত্র নয় — শিক্ষা, পরিবার ও মাদকও দায়ী।"

### 2E. How One Idea Becomes a Full Paragraph (PEEL)

**P**oint → **E**xplain (why? how?) → **E**xample → **L**ink / Result

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### বাংলায় চিন্তা (Bangla thinking)

**Point:** দারিদ্র্য অপরাধের একটি বড় কারণ।
**Explain:** কারণ যারা চাকরি পায় না এবং পরিবারের খাবার জোগাতে পারে না, তারা মরিয়া হয়ে চুরি বা অন্য অপরাধের দিকে ঝুঁকতে পারে।
**Example:** যেমন, বড় শহরে কাজ না পাওয়া একজন তরুণ মোবাইল ফোন ছিনতাইয়ের মতো অপরাধে জড়িয়ে পড়তে পারে।
**Link/Result:** ফলে দারিদ্র্য কমানো ও চাকরি সৃষ্টি অপরাধ কমানোর একটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ উপায়।

</div>

**The same idea in English (one paragraph):**

> **Point:** Poverty is one of the major causes of crime.
> **Explain:** People who cannot find work or feed their families may become desperate and turn to theft or other crimes.
> **Example:** For example, a young man who has failed to find a job in a big city may become involved in snatching mobile phones.
> **Link:** Therefore, reducing poverty and creating jobs is an important way to reduce crime.

Count it: about **65 words** from one idea. Add a second explanation (for example, poor areas often lack good schools and youth activities) and you reach the **100–120 words** that one body paragraph needs.

### 2G. Essay-Type Paragraph Plans (your five essay guides)

| Essay type (guide) | Introduction | Body Paragraph 1 | Body Paragraph 2 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agree / Disagree** (*to what extent? / do you agree?*) | **35–50:** paraphrase + *I completely agree/disagree with this view because* Idea 1 and Idea 2. Never "partly". | **100–120:** Idea 1 — P E E X L | **100–120:** Idea 2 — P E X R L (*Although some people argue that …, [my side]*) | **35–55:** *In conclusion, I firmly believe that …* + both ideas in new words |
| **Discuss Both Views** (*some think X, others think Y*) | **35–45:** *While some people argue that [other view], others believe that [my view]. I completely support the latter view because …* | **90–120:** the view you do **not** support, no "I" — P E E X L | **90–120:** **your view** — P E X R L | **35–45:** *In conclusion, people hold different views on whether … While …, I firmly believe that …* |
| **Advantages – Disadvantages** | **40–50:** paraphrase + *Although* [side you do not support], [side you support] + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **30–40**, 2 sentences) | **120–135:** advantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **120–135:** disadvantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **35–45:** *While …* + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **25–35**) |
| **Cause/Problem + Solution** | **30–40:** paraphrase + *This is mainly because C1 and C2, but S1 and S2 can tackle this problem.* No opinion. | **90–120:** 2 causes or problems — P E X P E L | **90–120:** 2 solutions, each fixing one cause (WHO + WHAT + HOW) | **30–40:** causes + solutions in new words |
| **Double Question** (*two questions*) | **30–40:** paraphrase + *[Answer 1], and [answer 2].* | **90–120:** answer to Q1 — List block (P E X P E L) or Opinion block (P E X R L) | **90–120:** answer to Q2 — List or Opinion block | **30–40:** *In conclusion,* answer 1 + answer 2 in new words |

*Full rules: `Writing/IELTS_*_Essay_Guide.md`. Questions asking only for solutions or only for problems use the Cause/Problem + Solution guide, Section 14: one idea in each body paragraph (P E E X L), no opinion.*

## Part 3 — Category-Wise Free Writing {: .part}

Each passage below is an **idea store**. Read it slowly, notice how each idea is explained and supported with an example, and pay attention to the **bold collocations**. After each passage, try to say 4–5 of its ideas aloud in your own words.

### Causes of Crime: Poverty, Education and Family

Crime exists in every society, but its level and type vary greatly between countries, cities and neighbourhoods. Understanding why people commit crimes is the first step towards preventing them. Although every criminal makes a personal choice, social and economic conditions strongly influence those choices. The most commonly discussed causes are poverty, lack of education, family problems, drugs and the influence of friends.

**Poverty and unemployment** are often linked to crime. When people cannot find work or earn enough to feed their families, they may become desperate and turn to theft, robbery or illegal trade. Young men who have no job and no hope for the future may also join gangs, which offer money, protection and a sense of belonging. In many cities, crime rates are higher in poor areas, where unemployment is high and public services are weak.

However, poverty alone does not explain all crime. Most poor people are honest and never break the law. At the same time, wealthy and powerful people commit serious crimes, such as fraud, corruption, tax evasion and bribery, which can cause enormous harm to society. These **white-collar crimes** are driven by greed rather than need. Therefore, while poverty is an important factor, it is not the only cause, and perhaps not the main cause, of most crimes.

**Lack of education** is another important factor. Young people who leave school early often have fewer job opportunities and may spend their time on the streets, where they are more likely to be influenced by criminals. Education not only provides skills for legal employment but also teaches values, discipline and the ability to think about the consequences of one's actions.

**Family background** also plays a major role. Children who grow up without proper care, love and supervision, or who witness violence and crime at home, are more likely to develop behavioural problems. When both parents work long hours, or when a parent is absent, children may receive little guidance, and they may look for attention and acceptance elsewhere, sometimes among dangerous groups.

**Peer pressure** is particularly strong among teenagers. Young people often want to fit in with their friends, and if those friends are involved in drug use, fighting or theft, they may join in to avoid being rejected. In addition, drug and alcohol addiction can lead people to steal in order to pay for their habit or to commit violent acts while intoxicated.

Because crime has many causes, it requires many solutions. Governments can reduce poverty by creating jobs and providing a social safety net, improve schools and keep children in education, support families through parenting programmes and childcare, and offer youth clubs and sports activities that give young people a positive alternative.

In conclusion, crime is caused by a combination of social, economic and personal factors. Poverty is a significant cause of some crimes, but education, family, peer pressure, addiction and greed are also important. **Tackling the root causes** of crime is more effective in the long term than relying only on punishment.

### Prison versus Rehabilitation

When a person commits a crime, society must decide how to respond. The traditional answer has been to send offenders to prison. Prison removes criminals from society, punishes them for their actions and, it is hoped, discourages others from committing similar crimes. However, many people now argue that prison alone does not work well, and that **rehabilitation**, which aims to change offenders' behaviour, is a more effective approach.

Prison has several important functions. First, it **protects the public** from dangerous criminals. People who commit serious violent crimes, such as murder, rape or armed robbery, need to be kept away from society so that they cannot harm others. Second, prison acts as a **deterrent**: the fear of losing one's freedom may stop some people from committing crimes. Third, prison provides a sense of justice for victims and their families, who want to see that the offender has been punished.

However, prison has serious weaknesses. It is extremely expensive, because governments must pay for buildings, guards, food and healthcare for every prisoner. In many countries, prisons are **overcrowded**, with poor conditions that can damage prisoners' physical and mental health. More importantly, prisons often fail to change behaviour. Young or first-time offenders who are imprisoned for minor crimes may mix with experienced criminals and learn new criminal skills. For this reason, prisons are sometimes described as "schools of crime".

After release, former prisoners face many difficulties. A criminal record makes it hard to find a job or a place to live, and some families and communities reject them. Without support, many return to crime, creating a cycle of **reoffending** that is costly for society.

Rehabilitation tries to break this cycle. Inside prison, offenders can receive education, vocational training, counselling and treatment for addiction. For less serious crimes, offenders can be given **alternative punishments**, such as community service, probation or electronic monitoring, which allow them to keep their jobs and family relationships while paying back to society. The goal is to help offenders become law-abiding citizens who will not commit crimes again.

Critics argue that rehabilitation is too soft and does not give victims a sense of justice. They also point out that some offenders do not want to change, and that rehabilitation programmes cost money. These concerns are valid, but rehabilitation usually costs less than repeated imprisonment, and it can make communities safer in the long run.

The best approach depends on the type of crime and the offender. Dangerous and violent criminals should be imprisoned to protect the public, but even they should have access to education and treatment. For minor and non-violent offenders, especially young people, rehabilitation and community-based punishments are often more effective.

In conclusion, prison is necessary for serious crimes, but it should not be the only response to crime. A system that combines punishment with **genuine rehabilitation** is more likely to reduce reoffending, save public money and create a safer society.

### Repeat Offending

One of the biggest problems in criminal justice systems around the world is **repeat offending**, or reoffending. Many people who are released from prison commit new crimes within a short time, and some spend their whole lives moving in and out of prison. This suggests that prisons are not always succeeding in their aim of changing behaviour, and it raises important questions about how society deals with offenders.

There are several reasons why former prisoners return to crime. The most important is the difficulty of **finding legal employment**. Many employers are unwilling to hire someone with a criminal record, because they fear that the person may steal or cause trouble. Without a job, former prisoners cannot pay rent or support their families, and crime may appear to be the only way to survive.

Another reason is the experience of prison itself. In overcrowded prisons with few programmes, prisoners may spend years doing nothing useful. They may form friendships with other criminals and learn new illegal skills, and they may lose contact with family members and law-abiding friends. When they are released, they often return to the same neighbourhoods and the same groups of people who influenced them before.

**Addiction** is also a major factor. Many prisoners have problems with drugs or alcohol, and if they do not receive treatment, they may return to their habits after release. Addiction is expensive, and some people steal to pay for drugs, which quickly leads them back to prison.

Mental health problems, low levels of education and a lack of housing can make the situation even worse. Former prisoners who have nowhere to live are much more likely to reoffend than those who have a stable home and family support.

To reduce reoffending, the focus must shift from punishment alone to **preparation for life after prison**. Inside prison, offenders should have access to education, literacy classes and vocational training in skills such as carpentry, cooking, tailoring or computing. Counselling and addiction treatment can help them deal with the problems that led to their crimes.

Support after release is equally important. Governments and charities can provide temporary housing, help with finding work and regular meetings with probation officers. Employers can be encouraged to hire former prisoners through tax benefits or special programmes. Mentoring schemes, in which former offenders who have successfully changed their lives guide others, can also be very effective.

Society's attitude matters too. If former prisoners are always treated as criminals, they may feel there is no point in trying to change. Giving them a genuine **second chance**, while still holding them responsible for their actions, can encourage them to build a new life.

In conclusion, repeat offending is caused by unemployment, the negative effects of prison, addiction and a lack of support. By investing in education, treatment and help after release, governments can **break the cycle of crime**, which benefits both former prisoners and the whole community.

### Community Service and Alternative Punishments

Not every crime needs to be punished with prison. Many countries now use **alternative punishments** for less serious offences, such as community service, fines, probation, electronic tagging and restorative justice programmes. Supporters believe these methods are cheaper and more effective than prison, while critics worry that they are too soft on crime.

**Community service** requires offenders to do unpaid work that benefits the community, such as cleaning parks, painting public buildings, helping in charity shops or assisting elderly people. This form of punishment has several advantages. It allows offenders to **pay back to society** for the harm they have caused. A young person who has damaged public property, for example, might be required to repair or clean it, which helps them understand the consequences of their actions.

Community service is also much cheaper than prison. Keeping a person in prison is expensive, while community service requires only supervision. In addition, offenders can **keep their jobs and family relationships**, which are important for staying away from crime. Unlike prison, community service does not expose minor offenders to serious criminals, so it reduces the risk that they will learn new criminal behaviour.

Other alternatives include **probation**, in which offenders live in the community under the supervision of a probation officer and must follow certain rules, and **electronic tagging**, which tracks offenders' movements. Fines can be effective for minor offences, although they may be unfair if the same amount is a small sum for a rich person but a huge burden for a poor one.

**Restorative justice** is another approach. It brings offenders and victims together, with the victim's agreement, so that the offender can hear how their actions affected the victim, apologise and agree on ways to repair the harm. Many victims find this process helpful, and offenders who face their victims often feel genuine regret.

However, alternative punishments have limitations. Critics argue that they do not provide enough **deterrence** or justice, especially if the public sees them as an easy option. Community service may not be appropriate for violent or dangerous offenders, who need to be separated from society. In addition, these programmes must be well organised and properly supervised; otherwise, offenders may fail to complete their work without facing any consequences.

For these reasons, alternative punishments should be used carefully. They are most suitable for first-time and non-violent offenders, such as people convicted of minor theft, vandalism or traffic offences, while prison should be kept for serious crimes.

In conclusion, community service and other alternatives can be effective, fair and economical ways to punish minor offenders. They allow offenders to make amends, reduce the cost of prisons and lower the risk of reoffending. When used appropriately, they can make the justice system both **more humane and more effective**.

### Capital Punishment

Capital punishment, or the death penalty, is the most extreme form of punishment that a state can impose. It is still used in some countries for crimes such as murder, terrorism and drug trafficking, while many other countries have abolished it. The death penalty is one of the most controversial topics in debates about crime and justice.

Supporters of capital punishment put forward several arguments. Firstly, they believe that it acts as the **ultimate deterrent**. If people know that they could be executed for certain crimes, they may be less likely to commit them. Secondly, they argue that some crimes, such as the murder of children or acts of terrorism that kill many innocent people, are so terrible that death is the only **fitting punishment**. From this point of view, the death penalty delivers justice for victims and their families. Thirdly, some argue that executing dangerous criminals permanently protects society, because they can never escape or reoffend.

However, opponents raise serious objections. The most important is the **risk of executing innocent people**. No justice system is perfect. Witnesses can make mistakes, evidence can be misunderstood, and police or courts can be influenced by corruption or prejudice. In several countries, people who were sentenced to death have later been proved innocent, sometimes through new DNA evidence. Once a person has been executed, such a mistake can never be corrected.

Opponents also question whether the death penalty actually deters crime. Many serious crimes are committed in moments of anger, under the influence of drugs or alcohol, or by people who do not expect to be caught, so the threat of execution may not affect their decisions. Many criminologists argue that there is no clear evidence that countries with the death penalty have lower murder rates than similar countries without it.

There are also **moral and human rights** arguments. Many people believe that the state should not take a human life, even as punishment, and that executing someone sends a message that killing can be acceptable. In addition, the death penalty is sometimes applied unfairly; poor people and members of minority groups, who cannot afford good lawyers, may be more likely to be sentenced to death.

Some people suggest that **life imprisonment** without the possibility of release is a better alternative. It protects society from dangerous criminals, punishes them severely and allows mistakes to be corrected if new evidence appears. It also gives offenders the possibility of reflecting on their crimes.

In conclusion, the death penalty is a deeply divisive issue. While supporters see it as a just and deterrent punishment for the worst crimes, the risk of executing innocent people and the lack of clear evidence that it reduces crime are strong arguments against it. Many people therefore believe that **life imprisonment** is a more appropriate and safer form of punishment for the most serious offences.

### Sentencing: Fixed Punishments or Judges' Discretion

When a person is found guilty of a crime, the court must decide on the punishment, or **sentence**. In some systems, judges have considerable freedom, or discretion, to choose a sentence based on the details of each case. Others have proposed **fixed punishments**, in which every crime of a particular type receives exactly the same penalty. Both approaches have advantages and disadvantages.

The main argument for fixed punishments is **consistency and fairness**. If the same crime always leads to the same sentence, people cannot complain that one judge was harsher than another, or that rich and powerful people received lighter punishments than the poor. This can increase public trust in the justice system and reduce the influence of corruption, personal bias or prejudice. Fixed punishments are also clear and easy to understand. When people know exactly what penalty a crime will bring, they may think more carefully before breaking the law.

Fixed punishments can also make the justice system more **efficient**. Courts would spend less time debating sentences, which could reduce delays and costs. In countries where cases take years to be completed, this could be a significant benefit.

However, fixed punishments have a serious weakness: they **ignore individual circumstances**. Crimes that look the same on paper can be very different in reality. A hungry person who steals food to feed their children is not the same as a professional criminal who steals expensive goods to sell. A teenager who makes one mistake is different from an adult with a long criminal record. Mental illness, age, the level of harm caused and whether the offender shows genuine regret are all relevant factors that a fair system should consider.

If every offender received the same punishment regardless of these factors, many sentences would be unjust, either too harsh or too lenient. This could damage public respect for the law rather than strengthen it. In addition, fixed sentences may encourage offenders to deny their crimes, because they gain no benefit from admitting guilt or cooperating with the police.

Judicial discretion allows judges to **take into account** all the circumstances of a case and to choose a punishment that fits both the crime and the offender. Judges can give a second chance to a young first-time offender, while imposing a longer sentence on a repeat offender who has shown no remorse. This flexibility is one of the key features of a fair justice system.

The best solution is a balance between the two approaches. Many countries use **sentencing guidelines**, which set a recommended range of punishments for each type of crime. Judges must follow these guidelines, but they can choose a sentence within the range, or explain their reasons if they go outside it. This ensures consistency while still allowing justice to be applied to individual cases.

In conclusion, fixed punishments offer consistency, clarity and efficiency, but they risk treating very different situations in the same way. Clear guidelines combined with **reasonable judicial discretion** provide the fairest approach to sentencing.

### Youth Crime and Parents' Responsibility

In many societies, people are concerned about the number of crimes committed by teenagers and young adults. Youth crime can range from minor offences, such as vandalism and shoplifting, to serious crimes such as robbery, drug dealing and violent attacks. Understanding why young people commit crimes, and how society should respond, is essential for building safer communities.

There are several reasons for youth crime. **Peer pressure** is one of the most important. Teenagers want to be accepted by their friends, and if their friends are involved in crime, they may join in to prove themselves. Some young people join **gangs** because gangs offer a sense of belonging, protection and money, especially in poor neighbourhoods where opportunities are limited. Boredom and a lack of positive activities, such as sports and clubs, can also lead young people into trouble.

**Family problems** play a major role. Children who lack parental supervision, love and discipline are more likely to behave badly. When parents work long hours, are absent or have problems of their own, such as addiction, children may receive little guidance about right and wrong. Leaving school early and unemployment can further increase the risk, because young people with no education or job may see crime as the only way to earn money or gain respect.

There is debate about how young offenders should be punished. Some people believe that teenagers who commit serious crimes, such as murder, should receive the **same punishment as adults**, because the harm to victims is the same, and harsh punishment may deter others. However, most experts argue that young people are still developing, both emotionally and mentally. They are more likely to act without thinking about the consequences and more open to change. For this reason, many legal systems treat young offenders differently, focusing on education and **rehabilitation** rather than long prison sentences. Placing teenagers in adult prisons can expose them to experienced criminals and make them more likely to reoffend.

Another question is whether **parents should be punished** for their children's crimes. Supporters argue that parents are responsible for raising and supervising their children, and that holding them accountable, for example through fines or compulsory parenting classes, will encourage them to take this responsibility seriously. Critics respond that many parents do their best but still cannot control a rebellious teenager, and that punishing them may cause further harm to struggling families. Parenting classes and family support are often seen as fairer than fines or imprisonment.

Preventing youth crime is better than punishing it. Schools can identify children at risk early and provide counselling. Governments and communities can offer youth clubs, sports programmes, vocational training and mentoring, giving young people **positive role models** and alternatives to crime.

In conclusion, youth crime is caused by peer pressure, family problems, lack of education and limited opportunities. Society should hold young offenders responsible, but it should focus on rehabilitation, support for families and **early prevention**, because young people have the greatest capacity to change.

### Media, Video Games and Violence

Television, films, video games and social media are a major part of modern life, especially for young people. Many of them contain violent content, such as fights, shootings and crime stories. This has led to a long debate about whether violence in the media causes violence in real life, particularly among children and teenagers.

Those who believe that media violence leads to real violence make several arguments. Firstly, children often **imitate** what they see. If they constantly watch characters solving problems through violence, they may begin to see violence as normal and acceptable. Secondly, repeated exposure to violent images may make people less sensitive to the suffering of others. Some violent video games reward players for attacking or killing, which critics believe can encourage aggressive attitudes. In some cases, young criminals have said that they copied ideas from films or games.

Violent content is also very easy to access. Through smartphones and the internet, children can watch extremely violent videos without their parents' knowledge. Social media can spread videos of real fights and crimes, which may encourage others to copy them in order to gain attention.

However, many people argue that the link between media violence and real crime is weak. The vast majority of people who watch violent films or play violent games **never become violent** in real life. In some countries where violent video games are extremely popular, crime rates have fallen in recent decades. This suggests that other factors are much more important.

Experts point out that the main causes of youth crime are social and personal problems, such as poverty, family breakdown, abuse, lack of education, peer pressure, drug use and mental health issues. A child who grows up in a violent home or a dangerous neighbourhood is far more likely to become violent than a child who simply watches action films. Media violence may have a small influence, but it is rarely the **main cause** of crime.

Nevertheless, it is sensible to protect children from inappropriate content. Governments can use **age ratings** for films and games, and broadcasters can avoid showing very violent programmes at times when children are likely to be watching. Parents play the most important role; they can use parental controls, monitor what their children watch and talk to them about the difference between fiction and reality.

The media can also play a positive role. News reports can raise awareness of crime and help police find suspects. Films and documentaries can show the real consequences of crime and violence, which may discourage young people from making bad choices.

In conclusion, media violence may influence some vulnerable young people, but it is not the main cause of crime. Tackling the **underlying social problems** and supporting parents in guiding their children are more effective ways to reduce youth violence.

### Police, Technology and Surveillance

The police play a central role in preventing crime and catching criminals. In recent decades, technology has transformed police work, from fingerprint and DNA analysis to CCTV cameras, mobile phone tracking and computer databases. Some people believe that modern technology has made crime easier to prevent and solve, while others worry about its effect on privacy. There is also debate about whether more police officers are the best way to reduce crime.

A **visible police presence** can deter crime. When police officers patrol streets regularly, potential criminals are less likely to act, and residents feel safer. Police can also respond quickly to emergencies and build trust with local communities. In areas with high crime, increasing the number of officers can have a noticeable effect, at least in the short term.

However, more police is not the **only** way to reduce crime. Police can catch criminals, but they cannot solve the underlying causes of crime, such as poverty, unemployment, poor education and drug addiction. Hiring more officers is also expensive. In addition, if police behave unfairly or use excessive force, they may lose the trust of the public, which makes crime harder to solve. Many experts therefore argue that police work must be combined with social programmes and community involvement.

**Technology** has given police powerful new tools. CCTV cameras in public places can deter crime and provide evidence to identify suspects. DNA testing has helped solve many crimes, including old cases, and has also proved that some convicted people were innocent. Mobile phone records, digital payments and computer databases allow police to track criminals' movements and connections. As a result, some people believe that crime is becoming harder to commit and easier to solve.

At the same time, technology raises **privacy concerns**. Extensive CCTV networks and facial recognition systems mean that ordinary citizens may be watched and recorded constantly. If such data is misused by governments or falls into the wrong hands, it could threaten personal freedom. Criminals have also used technology to commit new types of crime, such as online fraud and hacking, so technology is both a tool for police and a weapon for criminals.

Another debate concerns who should work in the police force. Some people believe that women should not serve as police officers because the work can be physically dangerous. However, modern policing involves many skills, including communication, investigation and working with victims, and **female officers** are often particularly effective in cases involving women and children. Most countries now recruit both men and women.

In conclusion, the police and modern technology play an important role in fighting crime, and they have made many crimes easier to solve. However, they should be used responsibly, with respect for privacy, and combined with efforts to **address the root causes** of crime.

### Cybercrime and Online Fraud

As more people use the internet for shopping, banking, communication and work, crime has also moved online. **Cybercrime** includes hacking, online fraud, identity theft, spreading computer viruses, online harassment and the sale of illegal goods on the internet. It is one of the fastest-growing types of crime in the world, and it affects individuals, businesses and governments.

One common form of cybercrime is **online fraud**. Criminals send fake emails, text messages or phone calls pretending to be from banks, mobile payment services or government offices, asking people to share passwords or PIN numbers. Once they have this information, they can steal money from victims' accounts within minutes. Elderly people and those who are new to digital technology are often targeted because they may be less aware of these tricks.

**Hacking** is another serious threat. Hackers break into computer systems to steal personal data, credit card details or company secrets. Some use ransomware, which locks a company's or hospital's computers until a payment is made. Such attacks can stop important services, cause huge financial losses and put people's safety at risk.

**Identity theft** and **online harassment** are further problems. Criminals may steal personal details, such as national ID numbers or photographs, and use them to open bank accounts, take loans or create fake social media profiles. Others use the internet to threaten, blackmail or bully people, especially young women and teenagers, which can cause serious emotional harm and even lead victims to withdraw from school or work.

Cybercrime is difficult to tackle for several reasons. Criminals can operate from anywhere in the world, often from countries where laws are weak or difficult to enforce. They can hide their identities using fake accounts and new technologies. In addition, many victims do not report cybercrime because they feel embarrassed or do not know where to go for help.

Several measures can help to reduce cybercrime. Governments should create specialist **cybercrime units** with trained officers and modern equipment, and they should pass strong laws against online fraud and data theft. Because cybercrime crosses borders, **international cooperation** between police forces is essential for tracking and arresting criminals.

Banks and technology companies also have a responsibility. They can use stronger security systems, such as two-step verification, monitor unusual transactions and quickly block suspicious accounts. They should also educate customers about common scams.

Individuals can protect themselves through simple habits: using strong passwords, never sharing PINs or one-time codes, checking that websites are secure, and being careful about clicking on links in messages from unknown senders. Public awareness campaigns, especially for elderly people and new internet users, can make a big difference.

In conclusion, cybercrime is a growing threat as more of our lives move online. Tackling it requires cooperation between governments, businesses and individuals. Strong laws, modern policing, secure technology and **public awareness** together can make the internet a safer place.

### Drugs, Alcohol and Crime

Drug and alcohol abuse are closely connected with crime. Many crimes, including theft, violence and traffic accidents, are committed by people who are under the influence of drugs or alcohol, or who need money to pay for their addiction. As a result, some people believe that strict bans on drugs and alcohol are the best way to reduce crime, while others argue that treatment and education are more effective.

There are several links between substance abuse and crime. Firstly, drugs and alcohol can **reduce self-control**. People who are drunk or high may become aggressive, take dangerous risks or commit acts of violence that they would never commit when sober. Domestic violence, fights and dangerous driving are often linked to alcohol. Secondly, addiction is expensive. People who are addicted to drugs may steal, rob or sell drugs themselves in order to pay for their habit. Thirdly, the illegal drug trade is controlled by criminal gangs, which use violence to protect their business and often recruit young people.

Some people argue that **banning alcohol** completely would reduce crime. In theory, if alcohol were not available, alcohol-related violence and accidents would decrease. However, history shows that complete bans often create new problems. When alcohol is banned, illegal markets appear, controlled by criminals, and people may drink dangerous homemade alcohol. Strict bans can also be difficult and expensive to enforce. Many experts therefore believe that controlling the sale of alcohol, through age limits, taxes and licensing hours, is more realistic than a total ban.

With illegal drugs, many countries have traditionally relied on **strict punishment**, including long prison sentences for drug users and dealers. However, prisons filled with drug users do not always reduce drug use, and addicts often return to drugs after release. For this reason, some countries now treat drug addiction mainly as a **health problem**, offering treatment and rehabilitation to users while continuing to punish dealers and traffickers severely.

Prevention is also essential. Schools can educate young people about the dangers of drugs and alcohol, and communities can provide sports, arts and youth programmes that offer positive activities. Families play an important role by talking openly with children and noticing early signs of problems.

For people who are already addicted, **treatment and rehabilitation** programmes, including medical care, counselling and support groups, can help them recover and return to normal life. Helping them find work and housing can reduce the chance that they return to drugs and crime.

In conclusion, drugs and alcohol are a major cause of crime, but simply banning them is not the most effective solution. A combination of sensible regulation, strong action against drug gangs, education and accessible treatment can **reduce both addiction and crime**.

### Crime in Cities and Crime Prevention

In most countries, crime rates are higher in large cities than in rural areas. Cities offer many opportunities, but they also bring together conditions that can encourage crime. Understanding these conditions helps governments and citizens to prevent crime more effectively. A related question is whether crime prevention is mainly the responsibility of the government or of individuals.

There are several reasons why crime is more common in cities. **Population density** is one: with millions of people living close together, there are more potential victims and more opportunities for crime, such as pickpocketing in crowded markets or burglary in large housing areas. Cities also show strong **inequality**, with wealthy and poor neighbourhoods side by side, which can create resentment and temptation. In addition, people in cities often do not know their neighbours, so criminals can act **anonymously**, without being recognised.

Migration also plays a role. Many people move from villages to cities in search of work, but not all find jobs. Unemployed young people living in crowded slums, far from their families and traditional communities, may be drawn into gangs or crime. In rural areas, by contrast, people usually know each other, community ties are strong and it is harder for criminals to hide.

The **government** has the main responsibility for preventing crime. It must provide an effective police force, fair courts and a working justice system. Good urban planning can also reduce crime: well-lit streets, CCTV in public places, safe public transport and well-maintained parks make it harder for criminals to act unnoticed. Most importantly, governments can tackle the causes of crime by creating jobs, improving schools and providing youth programmes and social support in poor neighbourhoods.

However, individuals also have a role in **protecting themselves** and their communities. People can lock their doors, avoid displaying valuable items in public, use strong passwords online and be careful at night. Communities can organise neighbourhood watch groups, in which residents look out for suspicious activity and report it to the police. Parents can supervise their children and teach them good values.

Some people argue that crime prevention should be entirely the government's job, because citizens pay taxes for protection. Others believe that individuals must take responsibility for their own safety, because police cannot be everywhere. In reality, the most effective approach is a **partnership** between the state and citizens. Police are more successful when communities trust them and share information, and individual precautions work best when the wider environment is safe.

In conclusion, crime is higher in cities because of crowding, inequality, anonymity and unemployment. Preventing it requires strong government action on policing, planning and social problems, supported by careful behaviour and **community cooperation** from individuals.

## Part 5 — Sentence Structure Toolkit {: .part}

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Paragraph mix formula:** প্রতিটি body paragraph-এ রাখার চেষ্টা করো → **1টি long simple + 1–2টি compound + 2–3টি complex + 1টি compound-complex** sentence। 5–6টি বাক্য × গড়ে 20 শব্দ = **100–120 words**।

</div>

#### 1. Long simple sentence (rich noun phrase)

**Formula:** [long noun phrase] + verb + object
*একটাই clause, কিন্তু subject-টা বড় ও তথ্যপূর্ণ।*

- A lack of education and job opportunities in poor neighbourhoods **can push** young people towards crime.
- The use of CCTV cameras and DNA testing **has made** many crimes easier to solve.

#### 2. Compound with *and / but / so / yet*

- Prison protects the public, **but** it rarely changes criminals' behaviour.
- Many ex-prisoners cannot find work, **so** they return to crime.

#### 3. Compound with semicolon + linker

- Community service is cheaper than prison**; moreover,** it benefits society.
- The death penalty is irreversible**; therefore,** an innocent person could be executed.

#### 4. Complex — concession (*Although / Even though / While*)

- **Although** poverty can lead to crime, most poor people never break the law.
- **Even though** violent films are popular, few viewers become violent.

#### 5. Complex — reason (*Because / Since / As*)

- **Since** teenagers are still developing, they should not be punished like adults.
- **Because** criminals can operate from abroad, cybercrime is hard to control.

#### 6. Complex — condition (*If / Unless / Provided that*)

- **If** prisoners learn useful skills, they **are less likely to** reoffend.
- **Unless** the root causes are tackled, crime **will** continue to rise.

#### 7. Relative clause (*which / who / where / whose*)

- Young offenders **who** go to adult prisons often learn new criminal skills.
- Many crimes take place in poorly lit areas, **where** criminals can hide easily.

#### 8. Compound-complex

**Formula:** Although/Because/If + clause, Clause A, and/but Clause B

- **Although** prison is expensive, it is necessary for violent criminals, **but** minor offenders need other punishments.
- **If** parents supervise their children, teenagers avoid bad company, **and** youth crime falls.

#### 9. Participle phrase

- **Released** without any support, many prisoners soon return to crime.
- **Influenced** by older friends, some teenagers join gangs.

#### 10. *Not only … but also …*

- Rehabilitation **not only** reduces reoffending **but also** saves public money.
- Drugs **not only** damage health **but also** lead to theft and violence.

#### 11. *The more …, the more …*

- **The longer** young people stay in education, **the less** likely they are to commit crime.
- **The more** visible the police are, **the safer** people feel.

#### 12. Cleft sentence (emphasis)

- **It is** the certainty of being caught, not the severity of punishment, **that** deters most criminals.
- **What** young offenders need most **is** guidance and a second chance.

#### 13. Impersonal passive

- **It is often claimed that** the death penalty deters serious crime.
- **It is widely believed that** poverty is a major cause of crime.

#### 14. Contrasting views

- **While some people argue that** criminals should be punished harshly, **others believe that** rehabilitation works better.
- **While** prison protects society in the short term, **education** prevents crime in the long term.

#### 15. Result / consequence

- Prisons are overcrowded, **which means that** rehabilitation programmes are often ignored.
- Ex-prisoners are rejected by employers. **As a result,** many fall back into crime.

### Essay 1 — Discussion (Both Views + Opinion)

<div class="question">While it is sometimes thought that prison is the best place for criminals, others believe that there are better ways to deal with them. What is your opinion?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · CRI-12 · Common topic ★ Prison vs rehabilitation*

**Essay type:** Discussion · **Position:** There are better ways than prison to deal with criminals.<br>
**অবস্থান:** অপরাধীদের মোকাবিলায় কারাগারের চেয়ে ভালো উপায় আছে।<br>

**পরিকল্পনা (Plan)**

- **Introduction (35–45):** দুই মত (কারাগার আগে, ভালো উপায় শেষে) → আমার মত + কারণ: কারাগার প্রায়ই অপরাধীকে আরও খারাপ করে।
- **Body Paragraph 1 (90–120) — বিপরীত মত, "I" ছাড়া:** সমাজ রক্ষা → সহিংস অপরাধীকে আলাদা রাখা → ভয় দেখিয়ে অপরাধ প্রতিরোধ → উদাহরণ: গাড়ি চুরির কথা ভাবা ব্যক্তি।

#### Introduction (35–45 words)

কেউ কেউ যুক্তি দেন যে অপরাধীদের সবসময় কারাগারে পাঠানো উচিত, অন্যদিকে অনেকে বিশ্বাস করেন যে তাদের মোকাবিলার আরও কার্যকর উপায় আছে। <span class="tag">Complex · Passive</span> আমি শেষের মতটিকে সম্পূর্ণ সমর্থন করি, কারণ কারাগার প্রায়ই অপরাধীদের আরও খারাপ করে তোলে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — বিপরীত মত (90–120 words)

একদিকে, যারা কারাগারকে সমর্থন করেন, তারা যুক্তি দেন যে সমাজকে রক্ষা করতে এটি একটি অপরিহার্য ভূমিকা পালন করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যারা খুন বা সশস্ত্র ডাকাতির মতো গুরুতর সহিংস অপরাধ করে, তাদের সমাজ থেকে আলাদা রাখতে হবে, যাতে তারা আর কারও ক্ষতি করতে না পারে। <span class="tag">Complex · Passive</span> কারাগার অপরাধ প্রতিরোধক (deterrent) হিসেবেও কাজ করে; ফলে স্বাধীনতা হারানোর ভয় কিছু মানুষকে অপরাধ থেকে বিরত রাখতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, গাড়ি চুরির কথা ভাবছে এমন কেউ কয়েক বছর কারাগারে কাটানোর কথা কল্পনা করে তার মত বদলাতে পারে, এবং এতে একজন সম্ভাব্য ভুক্তভোগী রক্ষা পায়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> এ কারণে অনেকে বিশ্বাস করেন যে অপরাধীদের জন্য কারাগারই সবচেয়ে ভালো জায়গা। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — আমার মত (90–120 words)

অন্যদিকে, আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে অপরাধীদের মোকাবিলার আরও ভালো উপায় আছে, কারণ ছোট অপরাধে কারাগারে যাওয়া তরুণ বা প্রথমবারের অপরাধীরা অভিজ্ঞ অপরাধীদের সাথে মিশতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> তারা যখন মুক্তি পায়, তখন অনেকে অপরাধের রেকর্ডের (criminal record) কারণে চাকরি পেতে হিমশিম খায়, এবং তারা শিগগিরই আবার অপরাধে ফিরে যায়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, দোকান থেকে চুরি করে ধরা পড়া একজন কিশোর ছয় মাস কারাগারে থাকার চেয়ে কমিউনিটি সার্ভিস ও কাউন্সেলিং থেকে বেশি শিখতে পারে, যা তাকে চাকরি পাওয়ার ভালো সুযোগ দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যদিও কেউ কেউ যুক্তি দেন যে কারাগার সমাজকে নিরাপদ রাখে, একজন বন্দি যেদিন আবার অপরাধের জীবনে ফিরে যায়, সেদিনই এই সুরক্ষা শেষ হয়ে যায়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> ফলে যে পদ্ধতিগুলো পুনরায় অপরাধ কমায় (reduce reoffending), সেগুলো কারাগারের চেয়ে বেশি কার্যকর। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Conclusion (35–45 words)

পরিশেষে, অপরাধীদের কারাগারে পাঠানো উচিত নাকি অন্যভাবে মোকাবিলা করা উচিত, এ নিয়ে মানুষের মধ্যে ভিন্ন ভিন্ন মত আছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যদিও কারাগার কিছু অপরাধীকে বিরত রাখতে পারে, আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে বিকল্পগুলোই ভালো, কারণ শিক্ষা ও প্রশিক্ষণ মানুষকে অপরাধ ছাড়তে সাহায্য করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

</div>

### Essay 2 — Cause and Solution

<div class="question">Many criminals commit further crimes as soon as they are released from prison. What do you think are the causes of this? What possible solutions can you suggest?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · CRI-02 · Common topic ★ Prison vs rehabilitation*

**Essay type:** Cause and Solution · **Position:** Causes = no legal work after prison + prison fails to change behaviour; Solutions = help with finding jobs + rehabilitation in prison.<br>
**অবস্থান:** কারণ: কারাগার থেকে বের হয়ে বৈধ কাজ না পাওয়া ও কারাগারে আচরণ না বদলানো। সমাধান: চাকরি পেতে সহায়তা ও কারাগারে সংশোধনমূলক প্রশিক্ষণ। মতামত চাওয়া হয়নি।<br>

**পরিকল্পনা (Plan)**

- **Introduction (30–40):** paraphrase → This is mainly because [কারণ 1] and [কারণ 2], but [সমাধান 1] and [সমাধান 2] can tackle this problem.
- **Body Paragraph 1 (90–120) — 2টি কারণ:** বৈধ কাজ না পাওয়া → নিয়োগকর্তারা ভয় পান → উদাহরণ: সবার কাছে প্রত্যাখ্যাত সাবেক কয়েদি → কারাগার আচরণ বদলায় না → অপরাধীদের সাথে থেকে নতুন কৌশল শেখা → Consequently…

- **Conclusion (30–40):** In conclusion, [কারণগুলো নতুন শব্দে]. [সমাধানগুলো নতুন শব্দে] would… নতুন কোনো idea নয়।

**Fixed openers:** This is mainly because…, but… can tackle this problem. · One of the main causes of this… is… · For example, … · Another important cause is… · …; as a result, … · Consequently, … · The most effective solution is for… to… · When / If…, … · For instance, … · In addition, … could… · Together, these measures would… · In conclusion, …

#### Introduction (30–40 words)

অনেক অপরাধী কারাগার থেকে বের হওয়ার কিছুদিনের মধ্যেই আবার অপরাধে ফিরে যায়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এর মূল কারণ হলো, তারা বৈধ কাজ খুঁজে পায় না এবং কারাগার তাদের বদলাতে ব্যর্থ হয়, কিন্তু কর্মসংস্থানে সহায়তা ও সংশোধনমূলক ব্যবস্থা (rehabilitation) এই সমস্যার মোকাবিলা করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — 2টি কারণ (90–120 words)

আবার অপরাধ করার (reoffending) একটি প্রধান কারণ হলো, কারাগার থেকে বের হওয়ার পর বৈধ কাজ খুঁজে পাওয়ার কষ্ট। <span class="tag">Simple</span> অনেক নিয়োগকর্তা অপরাধের রেকর্ড থাকা কাউকে কাজে নিতে চান না, কারণ তারা ভয় পান যে লোকটি চুরি করতে বা ঝামেলা বাধাতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, যে সাবেক কয়েদিকে প্রত্যেক নিয়োগকর্তা ফিরিয়ে দেন, তিনি হয়তো বাসাভাড়া দিতে বা পরিবার চালাতে পারেন না, এবং তখন অপরাধকেই বেঁচে থাকার একমাত্র উপায় মনে হতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> আরেকটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ কারণ হলো, কারাগার নিজেই প্রায়ই অপরাধীদের আচরণ বদলাতে ব্যর্থ হয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যে কয়েদিরা শিক্ষা বা প্রশিক্ষণ ছাড়াই বছরের পর বছর অন্য অপরাধীদের সাথে কাটায়, তারা প্রায়ই নতুন অপরাধের কৌশল শেখে এবং অপরাধী দলে যোগ দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> ফলে অনেকে কারাগারে ঢোকার সময়ের চেয়েও স্বাভাবিক জীবনের জন্য কম প্রস্তুত হয়ে বের হয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — 2টি সমাধান (90–120 words)

সবচেয়ে কার্যকর সমাধান হলো, সরকার সাবেক কয়েদিদের কাজ খুঁজে পেতে সাহায্য করবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> কোম্পানিগুলো যদি সাবেক অপরাধীদের কাজে নেওয়ার জন্য কর-সুবিধা (tax benefits) পায়, তাহলে নিয়োগকর্তারা ঝুঁকি নিয়ে কম চিন্তা করবেন, এবং আরও বেশি সাবেক কয়েদি চাকরি পাবেন। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> যেমন, স্থানীয় একটি কারখানায় চাকরি পাওয়া একজন সাবেক কয়েদি বাসাভাড়া দেওয়ার মতো নিয়মিত আয় পান, যা চুরি করার প্রয়োজন দূর করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এছাড়া, কারাগারগুলো শুধু শাস্তি নয়, সংশোধনের ওপরও জোর দিতে পারে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> কয়েদিরা যখন শিক্ষা এবং কাঠমিস্ত্রির কাজ বা কম্পিউটার দক্ষতার মতো বৃত্তিমূলক প্রশিক্ষণ (vocational training) পায়, তখন তারা কাজে লাগার মতো দক্ষতা নিয়ে বের হয়, এবং কাউন্সেলিং তাদের নিজের কাজের দায় নিতে শেখায়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> এই পদক্ষেপগুলো একসাথে অপরাধের চক্র ভাঙবে এবং সমাজকে আরও নিরাপদ করবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Conclusion (30–40 words)

পরিশেষে, অপরাধীরা প্রায়ই আবার অপরাধ করে, কারণ নিয়োগকর্তারা তাদের ফিরিয়ে দেন এবং কারাগার তাদের আচরণের উন্নতি ঘটায় না। <span class="tag">Complex</span> চাকরি পেতে বাস্তব সহায়তা এবং কারাগারের ভেতরে ভালো প্রশিক্ষণ অপরাধ কমাবে এবং সমাজকে সুরক্ষা দেবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

### Essay 3 — Advantages and Disadvantages

<div class="question">Many people believe that having a fixed punishment for all crimes is more efficient. What are the advantages and disadvantages of having a fixed punishment?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · CRI-09*

**Essay type:** Advantages–Disadvantages, Type 1 (no opinion asked) · **Position:** No opinion is asked for, so both sides are discussed in balance.<br>
**অবস্থান:** প্রশ্নে মতামত চাওয়া হয়নি, তাই মতামত বা plan sentence নেই। Although ও While বাক্যে আগে অসুবিধা, comma-র পরে সুবিধা।<br>
**কাঠামো:** IELTS_Advantages_Disadvantages_Essay_Guide.md অনুযায়ী — Type 1 (মতামত চাওয়া হয়নি) — Although-introduction, প্রতিটি body paragraph-এ ঠিক 7টি বাক্য, While-conclusion। মতামত বা "This essay will…" বাক্য নেই। মোট 310–340 শব্দ।

**পরিকল্পনা (Plan)**

- **Introduction (30–40):** paraphrase → Although + অসুবিধা (ব্যক্তিগত পরিস্থিতি উপেক্ষা, বিচারকের দয়া নেই), + সুবিধা (সামঞ্জস্য, দ্রুত বিচার)। মতামত নেই।
- **Body Paragraph 1 (120–135) — সুবিধা, 7টি বাক্য:** সামঞ্জস্য ও ন্যায্যতা → জনগণের আস্থা, পক্ষপাত কমে → উদাহরণ: ধনী ব্যবসায়ী ও রিকশাচালকের একই সাজা → স্পষ্ট ও দ্রুত → আদালতের দীর্ঘসূত্রতা কমে → Overall…
- **Body Paragraph 2 (120–135) — অসুবিধা, 7টি বাক্য:** ব্যক্তিগত পরিস্থিতি উপেক্ষা → উদাহরণ: ক্ষুধার্ত চোর বনাম পেশাদার অপরাধী → বিচারকের দয়া দেখানোর ক্ষমতা নেই → অনুতপ্ত কিশোর → Overall, these…
- **Conclusion (25–35):** নতুন paraphrase → While + অসুবিধা, + সুবিধা। মতামত নেই।

#### Introduction (30–40 words)

কিছু মানুষ মনে করেন যে প্রতিটি অপরাধের জন্য একটি নির্দিষ্ট শাস্তি (fixed punishment) ঠিক করে দিলে বিচারব্যবস্থা আরও দক্ষ হবে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যদিও নির্দিষ্ট শাস্তি ব্যক্তিগত পরিস্থিতি উপেক্ষা করতে পারে এবং বিচারকদের দয়া দেখানোর সুযোগ কেড়ে নিতে পারে, এটি সামঞ্জস্য ও দ্রুত বিচার নিশ্চিত করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — সুবিধা (120–135 words)

এতে কোনো সন্দেহ নেই যে নির্দিষ্ট শাস্তি বিচারব্যবস্থার জন্য উল্লেখযোগ্য সুবিধা বয়ে আনে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> প্রথমত, নির্দিষ্ট শাস্তির পক্ষে প্রধান যুক্তি হলো সামঞ্জস্য ও ন্যায্যতা (consistency and fairness)। <span class="tag">Simple</span> এর কারণ হলো, একই অপরাধ যে-ই করুক, সবাই একই শাস্তি পায়, যা বিচারব্যবস্থার ওপর জনগণের আস্থা বাড়াতে এবং দুর্নীতি, ব্যক্তিগত পক্ষপাত বা কুসংস্কারের প্রভাব কমাতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, একই অপরাধ করা একজন ধনী ব্যবসায়ী ও একজন দরিদ্র রিকশাচালক একই সাজা পাবেন, যা দেখায় যে আইন সবাইকে সমান চোখে দেখে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এছাড়া, নির্দিষ্ট শাস্তি শুধু স্পষ্টই নয়, আদালতের জন্য দ্রুততরও। <span class="tag">Simple</span> সাজা নিয়ে দীর্ঘ বিতর্ক না থাকার ফলে মামলাগুলো আরও দ্রুত ও কম খরচে শেষ করা যায়, যা আদালতের দীর্ঘসূত্রতা (court delays) কমায়। <span class="tag">Complex · Passive</span> সব মিলিয়ে, নির্দিষ্ট শাস্তি আইনব্যবস্থাকে আরও বিশ্বাসযোগ্য ও দক্ষ করে তুলতে পারে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — অসুবিধা (120–135 words)

অন্যদিকে, এটি অস্বীকার করা যায় না যে নির্দিষ্ট শাস্তির কিছু অসুবিধাও আছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> প্রথমত, সবচেয়ে বড় সমস্যা হলো, এটি প্রত্যেকের ব্যক্তিগত পরিস্থিতি (individual circumstances) উপেক্ষা করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> দুজন মানুষ সম্পূর্ণ ভিন্ন কারণে একই অপরাধ করতে পারে; ফলে একই শাস্তি গভীরভাবে অন্যায্য হতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound</span> যেমন, যে ক্ষুধার্ত মানুষ সন্তানদের খাওয়ানোর জন্য খাবার চুরি করে, সে আর বিক্রি করার জন্য দামি জিনিস চুরি করা পেশাদার অপরাধী এক নয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> দ্বিতীয়ত, কম বয়সী বা মানসিকভাবে অসুস্থ অপরাধীদের প্রতি দয়া দেখানোর (show mercy) ক্ষমতা বিচারকেরা হারাবেন। <span class="tag">Simple</span> সত্যিকারের অনুতপ্ত একজন কিশোর যদি বারবার অপরাধ করা কারও সমান সাজা পায়, তাহলে সে নতুন করে শুরু করার আশা হারাতে পারে, এবং ফলস্বরূপ তার আবার অপরাধ করার সম্ভাবনা বাড়ে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> সব মিলিয়ে, এই সমস্যাগুলোর কারণে অনেক নাগরিকের কাছে নির্দিষ্ট শাস্তির ব্যবস্থা কঠোর ও অন্যায্য মনে হতে পারে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Conclusion (25–35 words)

পরিশেষে, প্রতিটি অপরাধের জন্য একটি নির্দিষ্ট সাজা ঠিক করা এমন একটি ধারণা, যা অনেকেই সমর্থন করেন। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যদিও (While) নির্দিষ্ট শাস্তি ভিন্ন ভিন্ন পরিস্থিতির প্রতি অন্যায্য আচরণ করতে পারে, এটি বিচারকে আরও সামঞ্জস্যপূর্ণ ও দক্ষ করে তোলে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

</div>

| # | Check | Yes / No |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Did I answer **every part** of the question? | |
| 2 | Is my **position clear** in the introduction and the same in the conclusion? | |
| 3 | Does each body paragraph have **one main idea + explanation + example**? | |
| 4 | Did I use at least **5 complex** and **2 compound-complex** sentences? | |
| 5 | Did I use **8+ words or collocations** from the Word Bank / Part 4 correctly? | |
| 6 | Did I check **articles** (a/an/the), **plurals** and **subject–verb agreement**? | |
| 7 | Did I avoid repeating the same word more than **3 times** (e.g. "crime", "criminal")? | |
| 8 | Did I follow my **essay-type guide** (Part 2G): its structure, its word plan (about **280 words**) and no partial opinion, in 40 minutes? | |

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Common mistakes in crime essays (এগুলো এড়িয়ে চলো):**

- ❌ *do a crime / make a crime* → ✅ **commit** a crime
- ❌ *Criminals should be punished strongly.* → ✅ … punished **severely / harshly**.
- ❌ *He was punished for 5 years jail.* → ✅ He was **sentenced to five years in prison**.
- ❌ *The crimes are increasing.* → ✅ **Crime is** increasing / **The crime rate is** rising.
- ❌ *police is* → ✅ the police **are** (plural)
- ❌ *Criminals are animals and should be killed.* (emotional) → ✅ *Supporters of the death penalty argue that…*
- **"criminal" প্রতিশব্দ:** offender, lawbreaker, wrongdoer, convict (after conviction), young offender, repeat offender

</div>

## Part 9 — Homework Essays {: .part}

Write these essays **alone in English**, without looking at Part 6. Plan for 5 minutes, write for 35 minutes, and check with the Part 8 checklist. There is **no model answer** — send your essay to the AI if you want feedback.

### Homework 1 — Agree / Disagree

<div class="question">The death penalty is the best way to control and reduce serious crime. To what extent do you agree?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · CRI-11 · ★ capital punishment*

**Essay type:** Agree / Disagree — one clear side · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 270–345 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 35–50 · BP1 Idea 1 100–120 · BP2 Idea 2 100–120 · Conclusion 35–55<br>
**Use ideas from:** Capital Punishment; Prison vs Rehabilitation; Causes of Crime

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- "best way" একটি দৃঢ় দাবি। একটিই পক্ষ নাও (সম্পূর্ণ দ্বিমত সবচেয়ে সহজ), আংশিক নয়।
- দ্বিমত হলে 2টি idea: নির্দোষ মানুষের মৃত্যুদণ্ডের ঝুঁকি; মৃত্যুদণ্ড থাকলেও অপরাধ কমার স্পষ্ট প্রমাণ নেই। প্রতিটির একটি উদাহরণ।
- পক্ষের যুক্তি (চূড়ান্ত প্রতিরোধক) BP2-এর Response বাক্যে Although অংশে রাখো।
- উপসংহারে নতুন বিকল্প (যাবজ্জীবন) প্রস্তাব করবে না: In conclusion, I firmly believe that… + দুটি idea নতুন শব্দে।

</div>

**Try to use:** capital punishment · the ultimate deterrent · justice for victims · the risk of executing innocent people · irreversible · no clear evidence · life imprisonment · tackle the root causes

**Sentence target:** use one cleft sentence (*It is the certainty of being caught… that…*) and *Although…, …* twice. Keep the tone calm and academic.

</div>

### Homework 2 — Discussion

<div class="question">Some people think that the government should be responsible for crime prevention, while others believe that it is the responsibility of individuals to protect themselves. Discuss both sides and give your opinion.</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · CRI-10*

**Essay type:** Discussion (both views + opinion) · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–299 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 35–45 · BP1 (the other view) 90–120 · BP2 (your view) 90–120 · Conclusion 35–45<br>
**Use ideas from:** Crime in Cities & Prevention; Police, Technology & Surveillance; Cybercrime

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- সরকারের দায়িত্বের পক্ষে 2টি যুক্তি কী (পুলিশ ও আদালত, নাগরিকেরা কর দেয়, মূল কারণ — চাকরি ও শিক্ষা)?
- ব্যক্তির দায়িত্বের পক্ষে 2টি যুক্তি কী (পুলিশ সব জায়গায় থাকতে পারে না, দরজা তালা দেওয়া, অনলাইন নিরাপত্তা, neighbourhood watch)?
- তোমার মত: একটিই পক্ষ বেছে নাও, কখনো আংশিক নয়। সেটি সবসময় BP2-তে রাখো। একটি বাস্তব উদাহরণ দাও।
- উপসংহারে নতুন কোনো idea বা সুপারিশ নয়: প্রশ্ন নতুন শব্দে, দুই মতের মূল কথা, আর একই মত।

</div>

**Try to use:** crime prevention · law enforcement · a visible police presence · tackle the root causes · protect themselves · neighbourhood watch · online fraud

**Sentence target:** use *While some people argue that…, others believe that…* in the introduction and one relative clause with *where*.

</div>

### Homework 3 — Double Question

<div class="question">It is often thought that the increase in juvenile crime can be attributed to violence in the media. Do you agree that this is the main cause of juvenile crime? What solutions can you offer to deal with this situation?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · CRI-03 · ★ youth crime*

**Essay type:** Double Question — Opinion + List · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–300 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 30–40 · BP1 = Q1 (Opinion block) 90–120 · BP2 = Q2 (List block) 90–120 · Conclusion 30–40<br>
**Use ideas from:** Media, Video Games & Violence; Youth Crime & Parents; Causes of Crime

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- Q1-এ "main cause" আছে, তাই Opinion block-এ সবচেয়ে সহজ উত্তর "না": মিডিয়া প্রধান কারণ নয়, পরিবার ও সামাজিক সমস্যা বেশি দায়ী।
- Opinion block: কারণ + উদাহরণ + Response (Although some people argue that children imitate what they see, [তোমার পক্ষ])।
- Q2 List block: 2টি সমাধান (স্কুলে কাউন্সেলিং ও পরিবারকে সহায়তা; youth clubs ও প্রশিক্ষণ); Body 2-তে "I" নয়।
- Introduction: paraphrase + [উত্তর 1], and [উত্তর 2]। Conclusion-এ দুটি উত্তর নতুন শব্দে, নতুন idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** juvenile crime · be attributed to · imitate violent behaviour · the main cause · family breakdown · peer pressure · age ratings · parental controls

**Sentence target:** use *not only… but also…* once and *Unless…, …* once.

</div>

### Homework 4 — Cause–Solution

<div class="question">The crime rate in most countries is often higher in urban areas than in rural areas. Why do you think that is? What can be done to reduce the crime rate?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · CRI-13 · ★ causes of crime*

**Essay type:** Cause and Solution · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–300 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 30–40 · BP1 2 causes/problems 90–120 · BP2 2 solutions 90–120 · Conclusion 30–40<br>
**Use ideas from:** Crime in Cities & Prevention; Causes of Crime; Police, Technology & Surveillance

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- Cause + Solution: BP1-এ 2টি কারণ (ধনী-গরিবের বৈষম্য; শহরে মানুষ প্রতিবেশীকে চেনে না) — গ্রামের সাথে তুলনাটি Explain বাক্যে রাখো।
- একটি বাস্তব উদাহরণ দাও (বড় শহরে ছিনতাই)।
- BP2 (Mirror rule): বৈষম্য ↔ চাকরি ও প্রশিক্ষণ; পরিচয়হীনতা ↔ community policing ও রাস্তার আলো। প্রতিটিতে WHO + WHAT + HOW।
- মতামত বা "This essay will…" নয়; উপসংহারে নতুন idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** population density · inequality · act anonymously · unemployed young people · deprived areas · well-lit streets · youth programmes · community cooperation

**Sentence target:** use *The more…, the more…* once and *which means that…* once.

</div>
