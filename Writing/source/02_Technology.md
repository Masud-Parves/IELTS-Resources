## Part 1 — Idea Generation Flow {: .part}

<div class="flow">Technology → Education → Business/Economy → Communication → Healthcare → Environment → Employment (AI &amp; Automation) → Society &amp; Social Media → Culture → Government → Privacy/Security → Lifestyle → Children &amp; Screen Time</div>

When you see **any** technology or internet question in the exam, run through this chain in your head, from left to right. For each link, ask yourself: *"Does this question touch this area?"* Then **stop at the 2 links that fit the question best**. Those 2 links become your two body paragraphs.

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Example:** *"Some people think mobile phones are making people less sociable. Do you agree?"*

Run the chain → **Communication** ✔ (people talk less face to face) → **Society & Social Media** ✔ (online friends vs real friends) → **Lifestyle** ✔ (families looking at separate screens).
Choose **Communication** for Body Paragraph 1 and **Lifestyle / Family** for Body Paragraph 2.

</div>

**Why this chain works:** almost every technology question is really asking, *"How does technology change one part of life?"* The chain lists every part of life that IELTS examiners usually ask about. If you know 4–5 ideas for each link (Part 3 gives you these), you will never have an empty mind in the exam.

**Short memory trick:** **E-B-C-H-E-E-S-C-G-P-L-C**
*Education, Business, Communication, Health, Environment, Employment, Society, Culture, Government, Privacy, Lifestyle, Children.*

## Part 2 — Exam-Hall Brainstorming System {: .part}

### 2A. The 5-Minute Plan

Do **not** start writing immediately. Spend the first 5 minutes planning. A planned essay is clearer, and clear essays get a higher score for Task Response and Coherence.

| Minute | What to do | What you write on the question paper |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | **Decode the question.** Underline the topic words and the instruction words. Decide which of your five essay guides it uses (Part 2G). | e.g. *mobile phones / less interactive / agree or disagree → Agree / Disagree guide* |
| 1–3 | **WHO + WHAT grid.** Who is affected? What is the effect? | 4–6 one-line notes |
| 3–4 | **Choose the 2 strongest ideas.** Add one reason and one example to each. | Idea 1 + why + example; Idea 2 + why + example |
| 4–5 | **Decide your position (one clear side, only if the question asks) and use the guide's word plan.** | Thesis / BP1 / BP2 / Conclusion in 4 lines |

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**পরীক্ষার হলে মনে রাখো:** সবচেয়ে "বুদ্ধিমান" idea খোঁজার দরকার নেই। এমন 2টি idea বেছে নাও যেগুলো তুমি সহজ ইংরেজিতে **ব্যাখ্যা করতে** এবং **উদাহরণ দিতে** পারবে। সহজ idea + ভালো ব্যাখ্যা = Band 7।

</div>

### 2B. WHO is affected by technology?

- **Students** — online learning, distraction, cheating, research skills
- **Children** — screen time, online safety, games, physical activity
- **Workers / employees** — automation, remote work, new skills, longer working hours
- **Businesses** — productivity, online selling, marketing, competition
- **Consumers** — online shopping, convenience, data collection
- **Families** — less time together, staying in touch with relatives abroad
- **Elderly people** — telemedicine, loneliness, difficulty using new technology
- **Patients and doctors** — diagnosis, robotic surgery, health apps
- **Governments** — online services, cybersecurity, surveillance
- **Society as a whole** — inequality, misinformation, culture, the environment

### 2E. How One Idea Becomes a Full Paragraph (PEEL)

**P**oint → **E**xplain (why? how?) → **E**xample → **L**ink / Result

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### বাংলায় চিন্তা (Bangla thinking)

**Point:** প্রযুক্তি শিক্ষাকে আরও সহজলভ্য করেছে।
**Explain:** কারণ ইন্টারনেট থাকলে যেকোনো জায়গা থেকে, যেকোনো সময় পড়াশোনা করা যায়।
**Example:** যেমন, গ্রামের একজন শিক্ষার্থী শহরে না গিয়েও একটি নামকরা বিশ্ববিদ্যালয়ের অনলাইন কোর্স করতে পারে।
**Link/Result:** ফলে গ্রাম ও শহরের শিক্ষার বৈষম্য কমে আসে।

</div>

**The same idea in English (one paragraph):**

> **Point:** One of the greatest benefits of technology is that it has made education far more accessible.
> **Explain:** Since learning materials are now available online, students can study at any time and from any place, provided that they have an internet connection.
> **Example:** For example, a student living in a remote village can enrol in an online course offered by a well-known university without having to move to a big city.
> **Link:** As a result, technology can help to **narrow the educational gap** between rural and urban areas.

Notice: 1 idea → 4–5 sentences, with a **complex** sentence (*Since…*, *provided that…*) and a **result** sentence (*As a result…*). Every body paragraph in your essay should follow this pattern for 2 ideas, or 1 idea explained deeply.

### 2F. Worked Brainstorm Demo

<div class="question">It is common nowadays for each member of the family to have their own piece of modern technology. Some people think this will lead to a breakdown in family relationships and communication. To what extent do you agree?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · TEC-10 (also in the Part 7 Question Bank)*

**My 5-minute notes:**

```
Type: Agree / Disagree (to what extent) → ONE clear side: I completely agree
      (Agree/Disagree guide: never "partly")
WHO: parents, children, grandparents
WHAT: less real conversation + less shared family time

Intro (35–50w): paraphrase + "I completely agree … because these devices reduce
                conversation at home and take away shared family time"
BP1 (100–120w) Idea 1 – P E E X L: less real conversation
   why: each person absorbed in a separate screen, even at dinner
   ex: teenager on a phone every evening never mentions a problem at school
   L: Therefore, family relationships grow weaker
BP2 (100–120w) Idea 2 – P E X R L: less shared time
   why: everyone watches their own films and plays their own games
   ex: a Dhaka family scatters to different rooms as soon as dinner ends
   R: Although some argue technology helps relatives in other cities stay
      in contact, this does little for people under the same roof
   L: Consequently, family bonds weaken
Conclusion (35–55w): In conclusion, I firmly believe … + Idea 1 + Idea 2
```

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**কেন এই অবস্থান?** "To what extent" প্রশ্নেও একটিই স্পষ্ট পক্ষ নাও (Agree/Disagree guide); "partly agree" লিখলে পরীক্ষায় দ্বিধা বাড়ে। দূরের আত্মীয়ের সাথে video call-এর যুক্তিটি ফেলে দিও না, বরং BP2-এর Response (R) বাক্যে ব্যবহার করো, যাতে তোমার পক্ষ comma-র পরে থাকে এবং উত্তর আংশিক না হয়। Idea chain-এর **Lifestyle** (একসাথে বসে আলাদা screen) থেকে দুটি idea-ই নেওয়া হয়েছে।

### 2G. Essay-Type Paragraph Plans (your five essay guides)

| Essay type (guide) | Introduction | Body Paragraph 1 | Body Paragraph 2 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agree / Disagree** (*to what extent? / do you agree?*) | **35–50:** paraphrase + *I completely agree/disagree with this view because* Idea 1 and Idea 2. Never "partly". | **100–120:** Idea 1 — P E E X L | **100–120:** Idea 2 — P E X R L (*Although some people argue that …, [my side]*) | **35–55:** *In conclusion, I firmly believe that …* + both ideas in new words |
| **Discuss Both Views** (*some think X, others think Y*) | **35–45:** *While some people argue that [other view], others believe that [my view]. I completely support the latter view because …* | **90–120:** the view you do **not** support, no "I" — P E E X L | **90–120:** **your view** — P E X R L | **35–45:** *In conclusion, people hold different views on whether … While …, I firmly believe that …* |
| **Advantages – Disadvantages** | **40–50:** paraphrase + *Although* [side you do not support], [side you support] + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **30–40**, 2 sentences) | **120–135:** advantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **120–135:** disadvantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **35–45:** *While …* + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **25–35**) |
| **Cause/Problem + Solution** | **30–40:** paraphrase + *This is mainly because C1 and C2, but S1 and S2 can tackle this problem.* No opinion. | **90–120:** 2 causes or problems — P E X P E L | **90–120:** 2 solutions, each fixing one cause (WHO + WHAT + HOW) | **30–40:** causes + solutions in new words |
| **Double Question** (*two questions*) | **30–40:** paraphrase + *[Answer 1], and [answer 2].* | **90–120:** answer to Q1 — List block (P E X P E L) or Opinion block (P E X R L) | **90–120:** answer to Q2 — List or Opinion block | **30–40:** *In conclusion,* answer 1 + answer 2 in new words |

*Full rules: `Writing/IELTS_*_Essay_Guide.md`. Questions asking only for solutions or only for problems use the Cause/Problem + Solution guide, Section 14: one idea in each body paragraph (P E E X L), no opinion.*

## Part 3 — Category-Wise Free Writing {: .part}

Each passage below is an **idea store**. Read it slowly, notice how each idea is explained and supported with an example, and pay attention to the **bold collocations**. After each passage, try to say 4–5 of its ideas aloud in your own words.

### Technology and Education

Technology has changed education more in the last twenty years than in the previous hundred. In the past, learning mainly took place inside a classroom, where a teacher explained a lesson from a printed textbook and students took notes. Today, smartphones, laptops and fast internet connections allow students to learn at home, on a bus or in a library, and they can **access a huge amount of information** within seconds. As a result, the role of both teachers and students has started to change.

The most important advantage of educational technology is that it has **made learning more accessible**. Students who live in remote villages, who work full-time or who have a disability can now attend online classes without travelling long distances. A young person in a small town, for example, can take a course from a famous foreign university and receive a certificate at a fraction of the usual cost. In this way, technology can help to **narrow the gap between rural and urban students**, provided that they have a stable internet connection and a suitable device.

Technology can also make learning more personal and more interesting. In a traditional class of fifty students, a teacher usually has to teach everyone at the same speed, even though some students learn quickly and others need more time. Learning apps, by contrast, can give each learner exercises at the right level and **provide instant feedback**. Videos, animations and online quizzes can turn difficult topics, such as science experiments or historical events, into something students can see and remember more easily.

However, technology in education also **creates serious problems**. The first is distraction. A student who opens a laptop to do homework may soon find themselves watching videos or chatting on social media, and an hour can pass without any real study. The second problem is the quality of information. The internet contains a great deal of inaccurate and one-sided material, and young learners do not always know how to **separate reliable sources from unreliable ones**. In addition, it has become very easy to copy answers or use AI tools to write assignments, which may prevent students from developing their own thinking skills.

Another concern is the loss of face-to-face interaction. School is not only a place to gain knowledge; it is also a place where children learn to cooperate, discuss ideas, make friends and respect others. If most lessons move online, students may have fewer chances to **develop social and teamwork skills**, which employers value highly. Teachers also notice that some students feel lonely and lose motivation when they study alone in front of a screen for many hours.

For these reasons, schools should not see technology as a replacement for teachers but as a tool that helps teachers do their job better. Teachers should guide students on how to search for information, check facts and use digital tools responsibly. At the same time, governments should **invest in digital infrastructure** so that poorer students are not left behind.

In conclusion, technology has **opened the door to learning** for millions of people and has made education more flexible and personal. Its benefits, however, depend on how it is used. When digital resources are combined with good teaching and real human contact, students can enjoy the best of both worlds.

### Technology, Business and the Economy

Technological progress has become one of the main forces behind economic growth. Almost every part of business, from production and marketing to banking and customer service, now depends on computers, software and the internet. Companies that once needed large offices, many staff and expensive advertising can now operate online and **reach customers all over the world**. This has created new opportunities, but it has also changed the nature of competition.

The clearest benefit of technology for businesses is **higher productivity**. Machines and software can complete repetitive tasks faster and more accurately than people. A factory with modern equipment can produce more goods in less time, while accounting software can process thousands of payments in a few minutes. Because these systems **reduce operating costs**, companies can offer lower prices to consumers and invest the money they save in new products.

Technology has also **lowered the barriers to starting a business**. In the past, a person who wanted to sell clothes or handmade products needed a shop, a large budget and contacts with distributors. Today, a small entrepreneur can open an online store or a social media page and sell products across the country, or even abroad, from home. Online shopping is convenient for customers as well, because they can compare prices, read reviews and order goods at any time of day.

Communication inside companies has changed too. Business meetings increasingly take place online, which saves time and travel costs and allows companies to work with partners in other countries. Many firms have also discovered that some employees can work effectively from home, which can **reduce office expenses** and give companies access to talented workers who live far away.

However, technological change also brings economic risks. Large technology companies with huge amounts of money and data can **dominate the market**, making it very difficult for smaller competitors to survive. Many small local shops, for instance, have closed because they cannot match the prices and delivery speed of big online retailers. When local businesses disappear, town centres can become empty, and communities lose part of their character.

Another concern is that technology may **widen the gap between rich and poor**. People and countries that own advanced technology, skills and capital tend to benefit the most, while those without internet access or digital skills may fall further behind. Workers in routine jobs may lose their income, while the profits go mainly to company owners and highly skilled employees. On the other hand, cheap smartphones and mobile banking have helped many low-income people in developing countries to send money, save and start small businesses, so technology can also reduce poverty when it is widely available.

For this reason, governments should not try to stop technological progress but should make sure its benefits are shared fairly. Investment in digital skills, fair competition laws and support for small businesses can help more people take part in the digital economy.

Overall, technology is an essential driver of modern economic development. Whether it makes a society richer and fairer, or only richer for a few, depends largely on the decisions of governments and businesses.

### Technology and Communication

Modern technology has completely changed the way people communicate. A few decades ago, people wrote letters that took weeks to arrive or made expensive international phone calls. Now, smartphones, messaging apps, social media and video calls allow people to **stay in touch instantly** and almost free of charge, no matter how far apart they live. In many ways, distance is no longer a barrier to human contact.

The biggest advantage of this change is that families and friends can **maintain close relationships** across long distances. A worker who has moved abroad can see and talk to their children every evening through a video call, and students studying overseas can share daily news with their parents. This can reduce loneliness and homesickness, especially for migrants and elderly people who live alone. In business, too, online communication allows teams in different countries to work together without expensive travel.

Technology has also given ordinary people a voice. Previously, only newspapers, radio and television decided what information the public received. Today, anyone with an internet connection can publish their opinions, report local problems and **draw public attention to social issues**. Online communication also helps people from different countries to exchange ideas, learn about each other's cultures and build international friendships.

However, digital communication has some clear disadvantages. Perhaps the most widely discussed is the **decline of face-to-face interaction**. Many people now prefer sending a message to having a conversation, and it is common to see friends sitting together while each person looks at their own phone. Although people may communicate more often, their conversations are often short and shallow, and young people in particular may not develop the confidence to speak, listen and **read body language** in real situations.

A second problem is the speed at which false information travels. Because a message can be forwarded to thousands of people in minutes, rumours and fake news can **spread like wildfire** before anyone checks whether they are true. In some cases, false messages have caused panic or even violence. Users therefore need to learn to think critically about what they read online.

There is also concern about the quality of language. Text messages and social media posts are usually brief and informal, full of abbreviations and emojis. Some teachers worry that students bring these habits into their academic writing, which **weakens their grammar and spelling**. Others argue that people simply switch between informal and formal styles, as they have always done.

Despite these problems, it would be unrealistic to reject modern communication technology, because it has become part of daily life. The solution is to **use it responsibly**: to keep a balance between online and offline contact, to check information before sharing it, and to make time for real conversations with family and friends.

In conclusion, communication technology has made the world more connected than ever before. If people use it wisely, it can strengthen relationships rather than replace them.

### Technology and Healthcare

Few areas have benefited from technology as much as healthcare. Modern machines, computer systems and medical research have helped doctors to find diseases earlier, treat patients more safely and keep people alive for longer. From scanners and robotic surgery to health apps and online consultations, technology has made medical care more accurate and, in many places, more accessible.

One of the most important advantages is **early diagnosis**. Modern scanning equipment allows doctors to see inside the body in great detail, and computer programs can examine test results and identify warning signs that a human might miss. Early detection is vital because many serious illnesses, such as cancer and heart disease, are much **easier to treat in the early stages**. As a result, technology has helped to **raise survival rates** for many conditions.

Technology has also made treatment more precise. Robotic systems can help surgeons to carry out delicate operations through very small cuts, which means patients feel less pain and recover more quickly. Electronic patient records allow doctors in different hospitals to see a patient's medical history immediately, which reduces mistakes and saves time in emergencies.

Telemedicine is another major benefit. In many countries, people in rural areas live hours away from the nearest specialist. Through video consultations, they can now **seek medical advice** without making a long and expensive journey. This is especially helpful for elderly patients and people with long-term illnesses who need regular check-ups. In addition, wearable devices such as smartwatches can track heart rate, sleep and physical activity, encouraging people to **take responsibility for their own health**.

However, the growing use of technology in healthcare also raises concerns. Medical records contain very private information, and if hospital computer systems are not well protected, hackers may steal or misuse patients' data. There is also the issue of cost. Advanced medical equipment is extremely expensive, so wealthy countries and wealthy individuals often receive better treatment than poorer ones. Unless governments **invest in public healthcare**, technology could make existing health inequalities even wider.

Some people also worry that machines may reduce the human side of medicine. Patients do not only need a correct diagnosis; they also need a doctor who listens to their fears and explains their situation with kindness. If doctors depend too heavily on computers and spend more time looking at screens than at patients, trust between doctors and patients may weaken. Similarly, people who search for their symptoms online may become unnecessarily worried or may try to treat themselves instead of visiting a doctor.

The best approach, therefore, is to use technology to support doctors rather than replace them. Medical staff should be trained to use new tools effectively, strong rules should protect patient data, and governments should try to make modern treatment available to everyone, not only to those who can afford it.

In conclusion, technology has brought enormous improvements to healthcare and has **saved countless lives**. If it is combined with fair access and human care, it can make medical services more accurate, efficient and available to all.

### Technology and the Environment

The relationship between technology and the environment is complicated, because technology is both a cause of environmental problems and an important part of the solution. On the one hand, factories, cars, aeroplanes and power stations have produced huge amounts of pollution and **contributed to climate change**. On the other hand, scientists and engineers have developed cleaner energy, more efficient machines and better ways to monitor the natural world. Whether technology helps or harms the planet depends mainly on how people choose to use it.

The most significant environmental benefit of modern technology is **renewable energy**. Solar panels and wind turbines produce electricity without burning coal, oil or gas, which are the main sources of greenhouse gases. As these technologies become cheaper, more countries can **reduce their dependence on fossil fuels**. Electric vehicles, although not perfect, also offer a cleaner alternative to petrol cars, especially when they are charged with clean electricity.

Technology can also help people **use resources more efficiently**. In agriculture, sensors can measure how much water each part of a field needs, so farmers do not waste water. Smart electricity meters show families how much energy they are using, which encourages them to save power. Online meetings can replace some business travel, which reduces flights and fuel use. In addition, satellites and drones help scientists to track deforestation, melting ice and wildlife populations, giving governments accurate information for making decisions.

However, technology itself places heavy pressure on the environment. Producing smartphones, computers and batteries requires metals and minerals that are often extracted through mining, which can destroy forests and pollute rivers. Large data centres, which store our photos, videos and online services, use enormous amounts of electricity and water for cooling.

Another growing problem is **electronic waste**. Many people replace their phones and other devices every year or two, not because the old ones are broken, but because companies constantly advertise new models. Old devices are often thrown away rather than repaired or recycled, and when they end up in landfill sites, harmful chemicals can **contaminate soil and water**. In some poorer countries, workers dismantle imported electronic waste by hand, which damages both their health and the local environment.

To make technology more environmentally friendly, action is needed at several levels. Governments can **invest in clean energy**, set strict rules on recycling and require companies to make products that last longer and are easier to repair. Businesses should take responsibility for collecting and recycling old devices. Consumers, too, can help by keeping their devices for longer and avoiding unnecessary purchases.

In conclusion, technology should be seen neither as the enemy of the environment nor as a magic solution. It is a powerful tool. If innovation is guided by the goal of **sustainable development**, technology can help societies reduce pollution and protect natural resources; if it is driven only by profit and consumption, it may make environmental problems worse.

### Technology and Employment (AI and Automation)

Automation and artificial intelligence (AI) are changing the world of work faster than ever before. Machines now assemble cars, software answers customer questions, and AI programs can write reports, translate texts and analyse data in seconds. These developments have made many businesses more efficient, but they have also created fear that millions of people could **lose their jobs**. The real picture, however, is more balanced.

From an employer's point of view, automation brings clear benefits. Machines can work day and night without breaks, make fewer errors in repetitive tasks and **cut production costs**. Robots can also take over dangerous or unhealthy jobs, such as working with chemicals or lifting heavy objects, which improves workplace safety. AI can handle routine paperwork, allowing human employees to spend more time on creative and complex tasks that require judgement.

However, the threat to certain jobs is real. Workers whose jobs involve **routine and predictable tasks**, such as cashiers, factory workers, call-centre staff and data-entry clerks, are the most likely to be replaced. Self-service machines in supermarkets and automatic ticket machines at stations are familiar examples. For a company, replacing many workers with a single machine can be financially attractive, so the pressure to automate is strong. As a result, technological progress may cause **short-term unemployment**, especially among older and less educated workers.

At the same time, history shows that technology also **creates new jobs**. Computers removed some office jobs, but they also created entire industries in software, online marketing, cybersecurity and data analysis. AI itself requires people to design, build, check and maintain it. New technologies can also lower prices, which increases demand and creates employment in other areas. The main challenge is therefore not that work disappears, but that workers need different skills from those they had before.

This is why **lifelong learning** has become so important. A person who has done the same task for twenty years may find it very hard to move into a digital job without help. Governments, companies and colleges should work together to offer retraining programmes, so that people can **acquire new skills** before their old jobs vanish. Schools should also focus on abilities that machines find difficult, such as creativity, problem-solving, communication and teamwork.

Technology has also changed where and how people work. Many employees now work from home for part of the week, which saves commuting time and gives them more flexibility. However, because emails and messages can reach them at any hour, the boundary between work and private life can become unclear, and some people feel they are **always on duty**.

In conclusion, AI and automation will certainly **transform the job market**, but they are unlikely to make human work unnecessary. The workers who succeed will be those who can adapt, keep learning and work alongside machines. With good education and fair government policies, technology can create better jobs rather than widespread unemployment.

### Technology, Society and Social Media

Social media has become one of the most powerful forces in modern society. Billions of people use platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube every day to share photos, follow news, chat with friends and express their opinions. For many young people, social media is not just a hobby but a central part of their social life. This has brought people together in new ways, but it has also created new social and psychological problems.

On the positive side, social media helps people to **build communities** around shared interests. Someone who feels isolated in their town can find others who share the same hobby, illness or life experience, and receive advice and emotional support. Social media also allows ordinary citizens to **raise awareness** of social problems. Campaigns for charity, environmental protection or justice can reach millions of people in a few days, and local communities often use online groups to help neighbours during floods or other emergencies.

Online platforms also make it easier to meet new people. Some argue that this brings people closer together, because friendships are no longer limited by geography. Shy people, in particular, may find it easier to start a conversation online before meeting in person.

However, there are serious concerns about the effect of social media on mental health, especially among teenagers. Users constantly see carefully edited photos of other people's holidays, bodies and achievements, and many begin to **compare themselves unfavourably** with others. This can lead to anxiety, low self-esteem and a feeling that one's own life is not good enough. The pressure to collect "likes" and followers can also make young people **seek approval** from strangers rather than from family and real friends.

Another problem is **cyberbullying**. Unlike traditional bullying, which usually stops when a child leaves school, online bullying can continue at home, late at night and in front of a large audience. Victims may feel that there is no escape, and in extreme cases this has led to depression and self-harm.

Social media can also be addictive. The apps are designed to keep users scrolling for as long as possible, with endless videos and frequent notifications. Many people spend several hours a day online without noticing, time that could be used for study, exercise, sleep or face-to-face relationships. Paradoxically, although people have hundreds of online "friends", some feel **more isolated** than ever, because online contact cannot fully replace real companionship.

To reduce these harms, several groups must take action. Parents and schools should teach young people how to **use social media responsibly** and to understand that online images are often unrealistic. Technology companies should remove harmful content more quickly and give users tools to limit their screen time. Individuals, too, can set personal limits, such as switching off notifications or keeping phones out of the bedroom.

In conclusion, social media is a powerful tool that can connect people, spread useful information and support good causes. Yet, if it is used without limits, it can damage mental health and weaken real relationships. The key is a healthy balance between online and offline life.

### Technology and Culture

Technology has had a major influence on how cultures are protected, shared and changed. The internet, streaming services and social media have made it easy for people to watch films, listen to music and learn about traditions from every part of the world. This has increased cultural exchange, but it has also raised fears that strong global cultures may **overshadow local traditions**.

One important benefit is that technology can help to **preserve cultural heritage**. Old songs, stories, manuscripts, photographs and traditional dances can be recorded and stored digitally, so that they are not lost when older generations pass away. Museums put their collections online, allowing students anywhere to study historical objects without travelling. For languages with few speakers, recordings and online dictionaries can be vital in keeping them alive.

Technology also allows local culture to **reach a global audience**. A folk singer, a small film-maker or a traditional craftsperson can now share their work on YouTube or social media and attract viewers from many countries. In the past, only large companies could distribute music or films internationally, but today even a talented young artist from a village can become well known. In this sense, technology can give minority cultures greater visibility and income.

In addition, technology encourages **cultural understanding**. People can learn foreign languages through apps, watch documentaries about other countries and talk directly with people from different backgrounds. These experiences can reduce stereotypes and help people respect cultural differences.

However, the cultural exchange that technology creates is not always equal. A small number of powerful entertainment industries, mostly from Western countries, produce much of the content that people watch online. Their films, music, fashion and lifestyles are promoted heavily, so young people in many countries become more familiar with foreign celebrities than with their own artists and traditions. Over time, this may cause young people to **lose interest in their own traditions**, and local festivals, clothing styles or languages may slowly disappear.

Social media can speed up this process, because trends travel around the world in a matter of hours. A fashion, a dance or a type of food that becomes popular in one country can quickly be copied everywhere, which may make cultures increasingly similar. Some people argue that this is simply a natural part of cultural change, while others fear the loss of **cultural diversity**.

The answer is not to block foreign content, which would be both impossible and unwise, but to support local culture at the same time. Schools can teach traditional history, music and literature; governments can fund local artists and film-makers; and digital platforms can be used to promote local content to young audiences.

In conclusion, technology is both an opportunity and a challenge for culture. It can protect traditions and share them with the world, but it can also spread dominant cultures at the expense of smaller ones. Societies should **embrace global connection** while consciously protecting what makes their own culture unique.

### Technology and Government

Governments around the world increasingly use technology to deliver services, manage information and communicate with citizens. Tax payments, passport applications, land records and even voting systems are moving online. This "digital government" can make public services faster and more transparent, but it also brings new risks related to inequality, security and privacy.

The most obvious benefit is convenience. Instead of standing in long queues and filling in piles of paper forms, citizens can **apply for documents online**, pay bills and check information from their phones. This saves time for people and reduces administrative costs for the state. Digital records can also be searched and updated quickly, which makes government offices more efficient.

Technology can also **increase transparency and accountability**. When governments publish budgets, contracts and statistics online, journalists and ordinary citizens can check how public money is spent. Online payment systems reduce the need for face-to-face cash transactions, which can make it harder for officials to demand bribes. In this way, technology can help to **reduce corruption**.

During emergencies, technology is especially valuable. Governments can send warnings about floods, storms or disease outbreaks to millions of mobile phones within minutes. Data analysis can help authorities to understand where hospitals, schools or roads are most needed and to **allocate public resources** more effectively.

Digital tools can also bring government closer to ordinary people. Many governments now run websites and social media pages where citizens can report problems such as broken roads, power cuts or poor public services, and receive a reply without visiting an office. Some cities even ask residents to vote online on how part of the local budget should be spent. When people feel that their voice is heard, they are more likely to **trust public institutions** and take part in community life.

However, digital government can create a new kind of inequality, often called the **digital divide**. Not everyone has a smartphone, a reliable internet connection or the skills to use online services. Elderly people, low-income families and people in remote areas may find it difficult to access services that are only available online. If governments close their offices completely, these vulnerable groups could be excluded from essential services.

Cybersecurity is another serious concern. Government databases hold sensitive information about citizens' identities, health and finances. A successful cyberattack could expose this data or stop important services from working. Governments therefore need to **invest heavily in cybersecurity** and train their staff to protect information.

There is also the question of surveillance. Cameras, facial recognition and data collection can help police to prevent crime and find criminals, but if they are used without clear limits, they may threaten privacy and personal freedom. Citizens may feel that the state is constantly watching them. A democratic government must therefore **strike a balance between security and freedom**, with clear laws on how data is collected and used.

In conclusion, technology has the potential to make governments more efficient, transparent and responsive. To achieve this, digital services must be introduced in an inclusive way, with offline options for those who need them, strong data protection and clear limits on surveillance.

### Technology, Privacy and Security

Every time people use a search engine, shop online, post on social media or carry a smartphone, they leave a trail of personal data. Companies and governments can collect and analyse this information on a scale that was impossible in the past. While this data can make services more useful and help to fight crime, it also creates serious risks to privacy and personal security.

There are some clear benefits to data collection. Online services can **offer personalised recommendations**, such as films, products or news that match a user's interests. Maps use location data to show traffic in real time, and banks use computer systems to detect unusual payments that might be fraud. Technology also **helps police to solve crimes**: CCTV cameras, mobile phone records and DNA databases have helped to identify criminals who would previously have escaped. For this reason, some people believe that crime has become harder to commit and easier to solve in the digital age.

However, technology has also created new types of crime. **Online fraud** and hacking are increasing rapidly as more people manage their money through the internet. Criminals send fake emails and messages that look as if they come from a bank, encouraging people to share their passwords. A single click on a dangerous link can lead to the loss of a person's savings. Large organisations, including hospitals and banks, have also been attacked by hackers who lock their systems and demand money.

Another concern is how companies use personal data. Many apps collect information about users' locations, contacts and habits, and some sell this information to advertisers. Most people accept long privacy policies without reading them, so they do not know how much information they are giving away. This data can be used to **influence people's choices**, from what they buy to how they vote.

Artificial intelligence adds a new dimension to the problem. AI systems can analyse millions of photos, messages and records, recognising faces and predicting behaviour. If such tools are misused by companies or governments, people may feel that their every move is being watched, which can **threaten individual freedom**.

To deal with these risks, stronger action is needed. Governments should introduce strict **data protection laws** and punish organisations that fail to protect personal information. Companies should explain clearly what data they collect and why, and give users real control over it. Police forces need specialist cybercrime units and international cooperation, because online criminals often operate from other countries. Individuals also have a role to play: using strong passwords, turning on two-step verification and avoiding suspicious links can greatly **reduce the risk of online fraud**.

In conclusion, digital technology has made life more convenient and has given police powerful tools against crime. However, this convenience comes with responsibilities. A safe digital society is one where innovation is encouraged but where clear rules prevent personal information from being exploited.

### Technology and Modern Lifestyle

Technology has transformed the daily routines and habits of people around the world. Washing machines, microwaves, online shopping, food delivery apps and digital payments have made many everyday tasks faster and easier. At the same time, many people now spend a large part of their day looking at screens, and this has changed how they rest, move and relate to others.

The most obvious benefit is convenience. People can buy groceries, pay bills, book tickets and order meals without leaving home. Household machines have **saved a great deal of time** that was once spent washing clothes by hand, cleaning and cooking. This has been particularly important for women in many societies, as it has given them more time to study, work outside the home and **pursue a career**. For elderly people and people with disabilities, home delivery and online services can greatly **improve their independence**.

Technology has also widened people's choices for entertainment. With a smartphone, a person can watch films, listen to music, play games and read books anywhere. This can help people to relax and reduce stress after a long working day. In addition, fitness apps and smartwatches can encourage people to walk more, sleep better and **adopt healthier habits**.

However, these conveniences have a downside. Because so many tasks no longer require physical effort, many people now **lead a sedentary lifestyle**. They sit at a desk all day, sit in a car or bus on the way home, and then sit in front of a screen in the evening. This lack of movement increases the risk of obesity, back pain and heart disease.

Another concern is **dependence on technology**. Many people check their phones dozens of times an hour and feel anxious if they leave their phone at home. Some can no longer find their way without GPS, remember phone numbers or concentrate on a book for long periods. When the internet or electricity fails, ordinary life can suddenly become very difficult.

Family life has also changed. Although technology helps relatives in different cities stay in contact, it can reduce the quality of time that family members spend together at home. It is now common to see a family sitting in the same room, with each person absorbed in a separate screen. Parents may be answering work emails while children watch videos, so meaningful conversation becomes rare. Over time, this can **weaken family bonds**.

The solution is not to give up technology, but to use it consciously. People can set "screen-free" times, such as during meals or the hour before bed, walk or cycle for short journeys, and make time for hobbies and conversations that do not involve devices. Parents, in particular, can set an example for their children.

In conclusion, technology has made modern life far more comfortable and efficient, but comfort should not be confused with well-being. When people **strike a balance** between online and offline activities, they can enjoy the advantages of technology without becoming controlled by it.

### Technology, Children and Screen Time

Children today are growing up in a world that is very different from the one their parents knew. Many children use tablets and smartphones before they can read, and by their teenage years they may spend several hours a day watching videos, playing games and chatting online. This has created new opportunities for learning, but it has also raised serious concerns about children's health, safety and development.

There are real benefits. Educational apps, videos and online games can make learning enjoyable and help children understand difficult subjects. Children can explore topics that interest them, from space to wildlife, far beyond what their school textbooks contain. Using technology from an early age also helps young people **develop digital skills** that they will need in almost every future job. Some children learn to code, design or edit videos, which can **encourage creativity**.

However, too much screen time can harm children's physical health. Children who spend long hours sitting in front of screens often get less exercise and **spend less time playing outdoors**. This is one reason for rising levels of childhood obesity. Using screens late at night can also **disturb sleep patterns**, leaving children tired and unable to concentrate at school.

There are also concerns about mental health and development. Fast-moving videos and games give constant excitement, so some children find it hard to **concentrate on slower activities** such as reading or listening in class. Violent games may make some children more aggressive, and social media can expose young people to pressure and comparison at a very early age. Many parents and teachers have noticed these changes, although experts still debate how strong the effects are.

Online safety is another serious issue. Many children use the internet without adult supervision, and they may come across violent or adult content, or be contacted by strangers who pretend to be friends. Children rarely understand the risks of sharing personal information or photos online. Parents often find it difficult to control their children's internet use, because children may be more skilled with technology than they are, and devices are easy to hide.

Solving these problems requires cooperation. Parents should **set clear limits** on screen time, use parental control software and, most importantly, talk openly with their children about what they do online. Keeping devices in shared family spaces rather than bedrooms can also help. Schools can teach online safety and digital responsibility as part of the curriculum. Technology companies should design safer platforms for young users, and governments should make laws that **protect children online**.

In conclusion, technology is now an unavoidable part of childhood, and it can be a valuable tool for learning and creativity. However, children need adult guidance to use it safely and in moderation. A healthy childhood should still include outdoor play, real friendships, reading and enough sleep.

| surveillance (n) | নজরদারি | **mass surveillance**; **under surveillance** | Mass surveillance may threaten personal freedom. |
| personal data (n) | ব্যক্তিগত তথ্য | **collect / protect / misuse personal data** | Companies must protect the personal data of their users. |
| data breach (n) | তথ্য চুরি/ফাঁস | **suffer a data breach** | The hospital suffered a data breach last year. |
| online fraud (n) | অনলাইন প্রতারণা | **fall victim to** online fraud | Elderly people often fall victim to online fraud. |
| strike a balance | ভারসাম্য বজায় রাখা | **strike a balance between** security and privacy | Governments must strike a balance between security and privacy. |
| cultural heritage (n) | সাংস্কৃতিক ঐতিহ্য | **preserve / protect cultural heritage** | Digital archives help to preserve cultural heritage. |

## Part 5 — Sentence Structure Toolkit {: .part}

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Paragraph mix formula:** প্রতিটি body paragraph-এ রাখার চেষ্টা করো → **1টি long simple + 1–2টি compound + 2–3টি complex + 1টি compound-complex** sentence। একই ধরনের বাক্য পরপর তিনবার ব্যবহার করবে না।

</div>

#### 1. Long simple sentence (rich noun phrase)

**Formula:** [long noun phrase] + verb + object
*একটাই clause, কিন্তু subject-টা বড় ও তথ্যপূর্ণ।*

- The rapid spread of smartphones in developing countries **has transformed** the way people access information.
- Excessive use of social media among teenagers **can lead to** anxiety and low self-esteem.

#### 2. Compound with *and / but / so / yet*

**Formula:** Clause 1, + and/but/so/yet + Clause 2
*দুটি সমান গুরুত্বের বাক্য যুক্ত করো।*

- Online shopping is convenient, **but** it has forced many local shops to close.
- Many routine jobs have been automated, **so** workers must learn new digital skills.

#### 3. Compound with semicolon + linker

**Formula:** Clause 1**; however / therefore / as a result,** Clause 2
*Band 7-এর জন্য খুব কার্যকর গঠন।*

- Technology has made communication instant**; however,** it has also reduced the quality of face-to-face conversation.
- Hackers can steal financial data within seconds**; therefore,** banks must invest heavily in cybersecurity.

#### 4. Complex — concession (*Although / Even though / While*)

**Formula:** Although + clause, main clause
*যদিও… তবুও… ধরনের চিন্তা।*

- **Although** online classes are flexible, they cannot fully replace the social experience of a real classroom.
- **Even though** automation removes some jobs, it also creates new professions in data and software.

#### 5. Complex — reason (*Because / Since / As*)

**Formula:** Because/Since + clause, main clause
*কারণ দেখাতে।*

- **Since** information can be shared instantly, false news often reaches millions before it is checked.
- **Because** children copy adults, parents who are always on their phones set a poor example.

#### 6. Complex — condition (*If / Unless / Provided that*)

**Formula:** If/Unless + clause, main clause (will / can / may)
*শর্ত বোঝাতে।*

- **If** governments do not provide retraining, automation **could** cause long-term unemployment.
- Online learning can reduce inequality, **provided that** poor students have access to devices.

#### 7. Relative clause (*which / who / where / whose*)

**Formula:** Noun + which/who/whose + clause
*দুটি বাক্যকে একটি তথ্যপূর্ণ বাক্যে জুড়ে দেয়।*

- Workers **who** perform routine tasks are the most likely to be replaced by machines.
- Many children spend hours on video games, **which** reduces the time they spend outdoors.

#### 8. Compound-complex

**Formula:** Although/Because/If + clause, Clause A, and/but Clause B
*একটি dependent + দুটি independent clause।*

- **Although** social media helps people stay connected, many users feel lonely, **and** some even develop depression.
- **If** parents set clear rules, children can enjoy technology safely, **but** without guidance they may face serious risks.

#### 9. Participle phrase

**Formula:** Verb-ing / Verb-ed phrase, main clause
*বাক্য শুরু করার উন্নত উপায়।*

- **Living** in a remote village, a student can still attend lectures from a top university online.
- **Exposed** to perfect images every day, many teenagers begin to feel unhappy with their appearance.

#### 10. *Not only … but also …*

**Formula:** Subject + not only + verb… but also + verb…
*একসাথে দুটি সুবিধা/অসুবিধা দেখাতে।*

- Telemedicine **not only** saves patients' travel time **but also** reduces pressure on city hospitals.
- Excessive screen time is **not only** bad for children's eyes **but also** harmful to their sleep.

#### 11. *The more …, the more …*

**Formula:** The more + clause, the more/less + clause
*তুলনামূলক সম্পর্ক দেখাতে।*

- **The more** time young people spend online, **the less** time they spend talking to their families.
- **The more** businesses automate, **the more** important retraining becomes.

#### 12. Cleft sentence (emphasis)

**Formula:** It is … that … / What … is …
*একটি বিষয়ে জোর দিতে।*

- **It is** not technology itself **that** causes harm, but the way people use it.
- **What** worries many parents **is** that their children meet strangers online.

#### 13. Impersonal passive (objective tone)

**Formula:** It is widely believed / argued / often said that …
*নিরপেক্ষ academic ভাব আনে।*

- **It is widely believed that** artificial intelligence will change most jobs within the next twenty years.
- **It is often argued that** mobile phones make people less sociable.

#### 14. Contrasting views

**Formula:** While some people argue that …, others believe that …
*Discussion essay-র introduction ও body-তে খুব কাজের।*

- **While some people argue that** the internet brings people closer, **others believe that** it makes them more isolated.
- **While** supporters of automation focus on efficiency, **critics** point to the loss of jobs.

#### 15. Result / consequence

**Formula:** …, which means that … / As a result, … / This leads to …
*ফলাফল বোঝাতে।*

- Many people work from home now, **which means that** they save hours of commuting each week.
- Companies collect huge amounts of user data. **As a result,** privacy has become a major public concern.

#### Introduction (35–50 words)

প্রায়ই যুক্তি দেওয়া হয় যে মোবাইল ফোনের মতো আধুনিক উদ্ভাবনগুলো মানুষের মধ্যে সত্যিকারের যোগাযোগ কমিয়ে দিচ্ছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> আমি এই মতের সাথে সম্পূর্ণ একমত, কারণ এই যন্ত্রগুলো সত্যিকারের কথোপকথনের জায়গা নিয়ে নিচ্ছে এবং তরুণদের সামাজিক দক্ষতা (social skills) দুর্বল করছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — Idea 1 (100–120 words)

এই ধারণার সাথে আমার একমত হওয়ার প্রধান কারণ হলো, মোবাইল ফোন ঘরে ও বন্ধুদের মধ্যে সামনাসামনি যোগাযোগ (face-to-face interaction) কমিয়ে দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এর কারণ হলো, অনেকে এখন কথা বলার চেয়ে message পাঠাতে বেশি পছন্দ করে, এবং বন্ধুরা একসাথে বসে থাকলেও প্রত্যেকে নিজের ফোনের দিকে তাকিয়ে থাকে — এমন দৃশ্য এখন খুবই সাধারণ। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> বাবা-মা হয়তো অফিসের email-এর উত্তর দিচ্ছেন, আর সেই সময় সন্তানেরা ভিডিও দেখছে; ফলে অর্থপূর্ণ কথোপকথন বিরল হয়ে যায়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, একটি পরিবার একই ঘরে রাতের খাবার খেতে পারে, অথচ প্রত্যেকে আলাদা screen-এ চোখ রাখে, এবং সন্তানেরা কোনো সমস্যায় পড়লেও বাবা-মা হয়তো তা টেরই পান না। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> তাই সময়ের সাথে সাথে এটি পারিবারিক বন্ধন (family bonds) ও বন্ধুত্ব দুর্বল করে দিতে পারে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — Idea 2 (100–120 words)

আমার একমত হওয়ার আরেকটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ কারণ হলো, ফোন তরুণদের সামাজিক দক্ষতা দুর্বল করে দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> কিশোররা যখন মূলত message-এর মাধ্যমে যোগাযোগ করে, তখন তাদের কথোপকথন প্রায়ই ছোট ও অগভীর হয়, এবং বাস্তব পরিস্থিতিতে কথা বলা, মন দিয়ে শোনা ও শরীরী ভাষা (body language) বোঝার আত্মবিশ্বাস তাদের মধ্যে তৈরি না-ও হতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> যেমন, যে কিশোর ঘণ্টার পর ঘণ্টা অনলাইনে chat করে, সে চাকরির interview-তে নার্ভাস বোধ করতে পারে, যা তার ভবিষ্যৎ ক্যারিয়ারের ক্ষতি করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যদিও কেউ কেউ যুক্তি দেন যে বিদেশে কাজ করতে যাওয়া একজন কর্মী প্রতি সন্ধ্যায় video call-এ সন্তানদের দেখতে ও তাদের সাথে কথা বলতে পারেন, যারা পাশে বসে থাকা মানুষকেই উপেক্ষা করে, তাদের জন্য এটি কোনো কাজে আসে না। <span class="tag">Complex</span> ফলে ফোন মানুষকে দৈনন্দিন জীবনের সবচেয়ে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ মুহূর্তগুলোতেই কম মিশুক করে তুলছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Conclusion (35–55 words)

পরিশেষে, আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে smartphone-এর মতো উদ্ভাবন মানুষকে সামাজিকভাবে কম সক্রিয় করে তুলছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> Screen এখন বন্ধু ও আত্মীয়দের মধ্যে সত্যিকারের আলাপের জায়গা নিয়ে নিচ্ছে, এবং এটি তরুণ প্রজন্মকে সামনাসামনি কম আত্মবিশ্বাসী করে তুলছে। <span class="tag">Compound</span>

</div>

### Essay 2 — Discussion (Both Views + Opinion)

<div class="question">An increasing number of people are now using the internet to meet new people and socialise. Some people think this has brought people closer together while others think people are becoming more isolated. Discuss both sides and give your opinion.</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · TEC-07 · Common topic ★ Social media's impact on people*

**Essay type:** Discussion · **Position:** The internet has brought people closer together.<br>
**অবস্থান:** ইন্টারনেট মানুষকে কাছাকাছি এনেছে।<br>
**কাঠামো:** IELTS_Discuss_Both_Views_Essay_Guide.md অনুযায়ী — বিপরীত মত আগে, আমার মত শেষে। মোট 260–299 শব্দ।

**পরিকল্পনা (Plan)**

- **Introduction (35–45):** দুই মত (বিচ্ছিন্নতা আগে, কাছে আনা শেষে) → আমার মত + কারণ: অনলাইন পরিচয় বাস্তব বন্ধুত্বে পরিণত হয়।

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Common mistakes in technology essays (এগুলো এড়িয়ে চলো):**

- ❌ *Technology have many advantages.* → ✅ Technology **has** many advantages.
- ❌ *People are using internet.* → ✅ People are using **the** internet.
- ❌ *informations, equipments, advices* → ✅ information, equipment, advice (uncountable)
- ❌ *It makes people lazy and also it makes people unhealthy and also…* → ✅ *It not only makes people less active but also harms their health.*
- ❌ *Nowadays, technology is very important in our life.* (too general) → ✅ *Over the last two decades, smartphones have changed the way people communicate with one another.*
- **"technology" প্রতিশব্দ:** digital devices, modern gadgets, technological advances, the internet, smartphones, online platforms

</div>

### Homework 1 — Problem–Solution

<div class="question">More and more children are accessing the internet unsupervised and at a younger age. This can sometimes put children at risk. What problems do you think parents face when dealing with their children using the internet? How can this problem be solved?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · TEC-08*

**Essay type:** Problem and Solution · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–300 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 30–40 · BP1 2 causes/problems 90–120 · BP2 2 solutions 90–120 · Conclusion 30–40<br>
**Use ideas from:** Children & Screen Time; Privacy & Security; Society & Social Media

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- প্রথম প্রশ্নটিই ধরন ঠিক করে: সমস্যা জানতে চায়, তাই Problem + Solution essay। মতামত বা "This essay will…" নয়।
- BP1: বাবা-মায়ের 2টি সমস্যা (সন্তান অনলাইনে কী দেখছে জানেন না; প্রযুক্তিতে সন্তানের চেয়ে কম দক্ষ) এবং একটি বাস্তব উদাহরণ।
- BP2 (Mirror rule): সমাধান 1 সমস্যা 1-এর, সমাধান 2 সমস্যা 2-এর। প্রতিটিতে WHO (বাবা-মা, স্কুল, কোম্পানি) + WHAT + HOW।
- Introduction: This can lead to P1 and P2, but S1 and S2 can reduce these effects. উপসংহারে নতুন idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** without parental supervision · put children at risk · inappropriate content · parental control software · set clear limits · talk openly with · online safety · in shared family spaces

**Sentence target:** at least 5 complex sentences (try *If…*, *Because…*, *which…*) and 2 compound-complex sentences.

### Homework 2 — Advantages–Disadvantages

<div class="question">More and more businesses as well as individuals are choosing to communicate either professionally or socially using technology rather than face to face. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of using technology for communicating.</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · COM-11*

**Essay type:** Advantages–Disadvantages, Type 1 (no opinion asked) · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 310–340 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 30–40 · BP1 advantages 120–135 · BP2 disadvantages 120–135 · Conclusion 25–35<br>
**Use ideas from:** Communication; Business & Economy; Society & Social Media

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- প্রশ্ন শুধু সুবিধা-অসুবিধা চায়, মতামত নয় (Type 1): Introduction ও Conclusion-এ 2টি বাক্য, কোনো মতামত বা plan sentence নেই।
- Body 1 (ঠিক 7টি বাক্য): 2টি সুবিধা (সময়, খরচ, দূরত্ব); "businesses" ও "individuals" দুই পক্ষের কথাই রাখো।
- Body 2 (ঠিক 7টি বাক্য): 2টি অসুবিধা (ভুল বোঝাবুঝি, সম্পর্কের গভীরতা); শেষ বাক্য "Overall, these…"।
- Although (Introduction) ও While (Conclusion) বাক্যে আগে অসুবিধা, comma-র পরে সুবিধা, but ছাড়া।

</div>

**Try to use:** save both time and money · stay in touch with · video conferencing · break down geographical barriers · body language · misunderstanding · superficial relationships · a substitute for

**Sentence target:** use *not only… but also…* once, *Although…* once, and a relative clause (*which / who*) at least twice.

</div>

### Homework 3 — Discussion

<div class="question">Some people think that the range of technology currently available is increasing the gap between rich people and poor people. Others think that it is causing the opposite effect. Discuss both views and give your opinion.</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · TEC-04*

**Essay type:** Discussion (both views + opinion) · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–299 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 35–45 · BP1 (the other view) 90–120 · BP2 (your view) 90–120 · Conclusion 35–45<br>
**Use ideas from:** Business & Economy; Education; Government; Employment

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- ধনী মানুষ বা দেশ প্রযুক্তি থেকে কীভাবে বেশি লাভবান হয় (দক্ষতা, পুঁজি, সুযোগ)?
- কম দামি স্মার্টফোন, মোবাইল ব্যাংকিং বা অনলাইন শিক্ষা কি গরিব মানুষকে এগিয়ে যেতে সাহায্য করছে? একটি বাস্তব উদাহরণ ভাবো।
- তোমার নিজের মত কোনটি? সেটি কোন body paragraph-এ রাখবে (সবসময় দ্বিতীয়টিতে)?
- উপসংহারে নতুন কোনো idea বা সুপারিশ নয়: প্রশ্ন নতুন শব্দে, দুই মতের মূল কথা, আর একই মত।

</div>

**Try to use:** widen the gap between rich and poor · digital divide · bridge the digital divide · access to the internet · mobile banking · digital skills · invest in digital infrastructure · open up new opportunities

**Sentence target:** use *While some people argue that…, others believe that…* in the introduction, and *The more…, the more…* once in the body.

</div>

### Homework 4 — Agree / Disagree

<div class="question">Nowadays children watch a lot of TV and play video games. However, some people think that these activities are not good for a child's mental health. To what extent do you agree or disagree?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · TEC-09*

**Essay type:** Agree / Disagree — one clear side · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 270–345 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 35–50 · BP1 Idea 1 100–120 · BP2 Idea 2 100–120 · Conclusion 35–55<br>
**Use ideas from:** Children & Screen Time; Society & Social Media; Lifestyle

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- একটিই পক্ষ নাও: সম্পূর্ণ একমত বা সম্পূর্ণ দ্বিমত। "পরিমাণের ওপর নির্ভর করে" (it depends) লিখবে না।
- একমত হলে 2টি idea: মানসিক স্বাস্থ্যের কোন ক্ষতি লিখবে (মনোযোগ ও ঘুম, আগ্রাসী আচরণ ও একাকীত্ব)? প্রতিটির একটি উদাহরণ।
- ভালো দিকটি (শিক্ষামূলক অনুষ্ঠান) বাদ দিও না; BP2-এর Response বাক্যে Although অংশে রাখো, তোমার পক্ষ comma-র পরে।
- উপসংহার: In conclusion, I firmly believe that… + দুটি idea নতুন শব্দে। নতুন কোনো সুপারিশ বা idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** excessive screen time · mental well-being · concentrate on · disturb sleep patterns · aggressive behaviour · spend less time playing outdoors · in moderation · set clear limits

**Sentence target:** use one cleft sentence (*It is… that…*) and one participle phrase (*Spending hours…, children…*).

</div>
