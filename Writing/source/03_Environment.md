## Part 1 — Idea Generation Flow {: .part}

<div class="flow">Environment → Climate Change &amp; Responsibility → Plastic &amp; Waste → Wildlife &amp; Biodiversity → Renewable Energy → Cities: Air Pollution, Traffic &amp; Green Spaces → Air Travel &amp; Tourism → Deforestation &amp; Natural Places → Water, Farming &amp; Food → Economic Growth vs Environment → Consumerism → International Cooperation (Rich vs Poor Nations) → Education &amp; Individual Action</div>

When you see **any** environment question, run through this chain from left to right and ask: *"Does this question touch this area?"* Then **stop at the 2 links that fit the question best**. Those 2 links become your two body paragraphs.

**Memory trick — every environment essay answers 4 questions: C → D → R → S**

| Step | Question to ask yourself | Example (plastic) |
|---|---|---|
| **C — Cause** | What is causing this problem? | cheap single-use packaging, throw-away habits |
| **D — Damage** | Who or what is harmed, and how? | sea animals, blocked drains, microplastics in food |
| **R — Responsibility** | Who should act: government, companies, individuals, or the world? | governments ban bags; companies redesign packaging |
| **S — Solution** | What exactly should be done? | taxes, recycling systems, reusable bags |

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Example:** *"Some people think environmental problems should be solved by governments, while others think individuals should solve them."*

Run the chain → **Climate Change & Responsibility** ✔ (who acts) → **Renewable Energy** ✔ (only governments can build power systems) → **Education & Individual Action** ✔ (saving energy, recycling).
Use **R — Responsibility** as the main frame: BP1 = what individuals can do; BP2 = why governments must lead.

</div>

## Part 2 — Exam-Hall Brainstorming System {: .part}

### 2A. The 5-Minute Plan

| Minute | What to do | What you write on the question paper |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | **Decode the question.** Underline the topic words and the instruction words. Decide which of your five essay guides it uses (Part 2G). | e.g. *government / individuals / discuss both → Discuss Both Views guide* |
| 1–3 | **C-D-R-S grid.** Cause, Damage, Responsibility, Solution — one line each. | 4–6 one-line notes |
| 3–4 | **Choose the 2 strongest ideas.** Add one reason and one example to each. | Idea 1 + why + example; Idea 2 + why + example |
| 4–5 | **Decide your position (one clear side, only if the question asks) and use the guide's word plan.** | Thesis / BP1 / BP2 / Conclusion in 4 lines |

### 2B. WHO is affected or responsible?

- **Governments** — laws, taxes, public transport, energy policy, protected areas
- **Companies / industries** — factories, packaging, emissions, waste disposal
- **Individuals / consumers** — energy use, shopping habits, transport choices, recycling
- **Farmers** — water use, chemicals, land clearing, but also victims of drought and floods
- **People in poor and coastal countries** — floods, cyclones, rising sea levels (e.g. Bangladesh)
- **Future generations** — will inherit the damage or the solutions
- **Animals and plants** — loss of habitat, hunting, pollution
- **City residents** — air pollution, traffic, lack of green spaces
- **Tourists and the tourism industry** — flights, damage to natural sites, but also money for protection
- **International organisations** — agreements, funding, cooperation between countries

### 2E. How One Idea Becomes a Full Paragraph (PEEL)

**P**oint → **E**xplain (why? how?) → **E**xample → **L**ink / Result

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### বাংলায় চিন্তা (Bangla thinking)

**Point:** পরিবেশ সমস্যা সমাধানে সরকারের ভূমিকাই সবচেয়ে বেশি।
**Explain:** কারণ কেবল সরকারই আইন তৈরি করতে এবং বড় প্রকল্পে বিনিয়োগ করতে পারে।
**Example:** যেমন, সরকার প্লাস্টিক ব্যাগ নিষিদ্ধ করলে বা সৌর বিদ্যুতে বিনিয়োগ করলে লক্ষ লক্ষ মানুষের আচরণ একসাথে বদলে যায়।
**Link/Result:** ফলে একজন ব্যক্তির চেয়ে অনেক বড় পরিবর্তন সম্ভব হয়।

</div>

**The same idea in English (one paragraph):**

> **Point:** The main responsibility for solving environmental problems lies with governments.
> **Explain:** This is because only governments have the power to introduce laws and invest in large-scale projects such as public transport and clean energy.
> **Example:** For example, when a government bans plastic bags or builds solar power stations, the behaviour of millions of people changes at the same time.
> **Link:** As a result, government action can achieve far more than the efforts of any single individual.

Count it: about **75 words** from one idea. Add one more explanation or a second short example and you reach the **100–120 words** that one body paragraph needs.

### 2G. Essay-Type Paragraph Plans (your five essay guides)

| Essay type (guide) | Introduction | Body Paragraph 1 | Body Paragraph 2 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agree / Disagree** (*to what extent? / do you agree?*) | **35–50:** paraphrase + *I completely agree/disagree with this view because* Idea 1 and Idea 2. Never "partly". | **100–120:** Idea 1 — P E E X L | **100–120:** Idea 2 — P E X R L (*Although some people argue that …, [my side]*) | **35–55:** *In conclusion, I firmly believe that …* + both ideas in new words |
| **Discuss Both Views** (*some think X, others think Y*) | **35–45:** *While some people argue that [other view], others believe that [my view]. I completely support the latter view because …* | **90–120:** the view you do **not** support, no "I" — P E E X L | **90–120:** **your view** — P E X R L | **35–45:** *In conclusion, people hold different views on whether … While …, I firmly believe that …* |
| **Advantages – Disadvantages** | **40–50:** paraphrase + *Although* [side you do not support], [side you support] + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **30–40**, 2 sentences) | **120–135:** advantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **120–135:** disadvantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **35–45:** *While …* + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **25–35**) |
| **Cause/Problem + Solution** | **30–40:** paraphrase + *This is mainly because C1 and C2, but S1 and S2 can tackle this problem.* No opinion. | **90–120:** 2 causes or problems — P E X P E L | **90–120:** 2 solutions, each fixing one cause (WHO + WHAT + HOW) | **30–40:** causes + solutions in new words |
| **Double Question** (*two questions*) | **30–40:** paraphrase + *[Answer 1], and [answer 2].* | **90–120:** answer to Q1 — List block (P E X P E L) or Opinion block (P E X R L) | **90–120:** answer to Q2 — List or Opinion block | **30–40:** *In conclusion,* answer 1 + answer 2 in new words |

*Full rules: `Writing/IELTS_*_Essay_Guide.md`. Questions asking only for solutions or only for problems use the Cause/Problem + Solution guide, Section 14: one idea in each body paragraph (P E E X L), no opinion.*

## Part 3 — Category-Wise Free Writing {: .part}

Each passage below is an **idea store**. Read it slowly, notice how each idea is explained and supported with an example, and pay attention to the **bold collocations**. After each passage, try to say 4–5 of its ideas aloud in your own words.

### Climate Change: Causes and Who Is Responsible

Climate change, often called global warming, is widely regarded as one of the most serious threats facing the world today. The average temperature of the planet has been rising, and scientists agree that human activity is the main cause. The effects can already be seen in many places: stronger storms, longer droughts, heavier rainfall, melting ice and rising sea levels. A key question in IELTS essays is not only what causes this problem, but also who should take responsibility for solving it.

The main cause of global warming is the **burning of fossil fuels**. When coal, oil and natural gas are burned in power stations, factories, cars and aeroplanes, they release carbon dioxide and other **greenhouse gases** into the atmosphere. These gases trap heat around the Earth like a blanket. Deforestation makes the problem worse, because trees absorb carbon dioxide; when forests are cut down or burned, this natural protection is lost. Intensive farming, especially large-scale cattle farming, also produces greenhouse gases.

The consequences are severe, and they often fall hardest on people who did least to cause the problem. Low-lying countries such as Bangladesh face more frequent floods and cyclones, and salt water is entering farmland in coastal areas, making it harder to grow crops. Farmers in many regions suffer from unpredictable rainfall, which **threatens food security**. In the long term, millions of people may be forced to leave their homes and become climate migrants.

When it comes to responsibility, some people argue that individuals must change their behaviour. Every person contributes to the problem through the energy they use, the transport they choose and the products they buy. If millions of people switched off unused lights, used public transport and wasted less food, the total effect would be significant. Individual action also sends a message to companies and politicians that people care about the environment.

However, many experts believe that the main responsibility lies with **governments and large companies**. A single person cannot build a solar power station, design a national railway network or change how electricity is produced. Only governments can **introduce strict laws**, set limits on pollution, tax fossil fuels and invest in clean energy on a large scale. Large companies also produce a significant share of emissions, so they must be required to reduce them. In addition, because greenhouse gases spread around the whole planet, no country can solve the problem alone; **international cooperation** is essential.

In practice, the most effective approach involves everyone. Governments should create the rules and the infrastructure that make green choices easy and affordable, such as cheap public transport and incentives for solar panels. Companies should cut emissions and design cleaner products. Individuals, in turn, should take advantage of these options and change their daily habits.

In conclusion, climate change is caused mainly by burning fossil fuels and destroying forests, and its effects are already being felt around the world. Although individuals have a role to play, governments must **take the lead**, because only they have the power to make changes on the scale that is required.

### Plastic Pollution and Waste

Plastic has become one of the most useful materials in modern life. It is cheap, light and strong, and it is used in everything from food packaging and bottles to medical equipment and electronics. However, the same qualities that make plastic useful also make it a serious environmental problem. Because plastic takes hundreds of years to break down, the waste we produce today will remain in the environment for generations.

The main cause of the problem is **single-use plastic**. Shopping bags, drinking straws, water bottles and food containers are often used for only a few minutes before being thrown away. In many countries, waste collection and recycling systems are weak, so large amounts of plastic end up in rivers, fields and streets rather than in proper disposal sites. A **throw-away culture**, in which convenience is valued more than responsibility, has made the situation worse.

The damage is visible everywhere. Plastic waste blocks drains in cities, which can cause flooding during heavy rain. Rivers carry plastic into the sea, where sea animals such as turtles, fish and seabirds **become entangled in it** or mistake it for food and die. Over time, plastic breaks into tiny pieces called microplastics, which have been found in seafood, salt and even drinking water. Although the full effect on human health is still being studied, many people are worried about eating plastic without knowing it.

Burning plastic waste, which is common in some areas, creates another problem. It releases toxic smoke that **pollutes the air** and can harm the health of people living nearby, especially children and elderly people.

There are several practical solutions. Governments can **ban or tax plastic bags** and other single-use items, which has worked well in many countries by encouraging people to bring reusable bags. Deposit schemes, in which customers pay a small extra amount for a bottle and get the money back when they return it, can greatly increase recycling. Governments should also invest in modern waste collection and recycling facilities, especially in fast-growing cities.

Companies have an important responsibility as well. They can use less packaging, choose materials that are easier to recycle, and develop alternatives made from paper, glass or plant-based materials. Supermarkets can sell more products loose or in refillable containers.

Individuals can also make a difference through simple habits. Carrying a cloth bag, using a refillable water bottle, refusing unnecessary straws and **separating waste for recycling** are small actions, but when millions of people do them, the impact is considerable. Community clean-up campaigns, such as cleaning a local beach or canal, can also raise awareness.

In conclusion, plastic pollution is a serious and growing problem caused mainly by single-use products and poor waste management. It can be reduced through a combination of government regulation, responsible business practices and changes in personal habits. Using plastic **wisely rather than wastefully** is the key.

### Wildlife, Endangered Species and Biodiversity

The Earth is home to millions of species of plants and animals, and this variety of life is known as **biodiversity**. Sadly, many species are disappearing much faster than in the past, mainly because of human activity. Tigers, elephants, rhinos, many birds and countless insects are now endangered. This raises two important questions: why should we protect wildlife, and is the money spent on it justified?

The main threat to wildlife is the **loss of natural habitat**. As human populations grow, forests are cleared for farms, roads, housing and industry, leaving animals with nowhere to live and find food. In Bangladesh, for example, the Sundarbans mangrove forest is home to the Bengal tiger, but pressure from human activity and rising sea levels threatens this unique environment. Pollution, climate change and the introduction of foreign species also damage ecosystems.

**Illegal hunting**, or poaching, is another serious problem. Some animals are killed for their skin, horns, tusks or bones, which are sold for decoration or used in traditional medicine. Because these products can be sold for high prices, criminal groups are willing to take great risks. As a result, some species have been hunted to the edge of extinction.

There are strong reasons to protect wildlife. Every species plays a role in its ecosystem; for instance, bees and other insects pollinate many of the crops we eat, and predators control the population of other animals. If one species disappears, the whole system can be affected. Many medicines have also been developed from plants and animals, and species that are lost may hold the key to future treatments. Beyond practical reasons, many people believe we have a **moral duty** to protect other living creatures and to pass on a rich natural world to future generations.

Wildlife can also **support local economies**. National parks and wildlife reserves attract tourists, who pay for guides, hotels and food, creating jobs for local people. When communities earn money from living animals, they have a strong reason to protect them.

However, some people argue that governments spend too much money on wildlife while there are more urgent human problems, such as poverty, hunger and poor healthcare. From their point of view, a hospital or school should come before a tiger reserve. This argument is understandable, especially in poorer countries. Yet the money spent on conservation is usually a small part of national budgets, and protecting forests and rivers also protects the clean water, food and climate that humans depend on.

Practical measures include creating protected areas, enforcing **strict anti-poaching laws**, banning trade in wildlife products, and educating people about endangered species. Involving local communities, for example by employing them as forest guards or tourist guides, is often the most effective approach.

In conclusion, protecting wildlife is not a luxury but a necessity, because healthy ecosystems support human life as well. Governments should spend wisely, combining conservation with programmes that also **benefit local people**.

### Renewable Energy and Fossil Fuels

Energy is essential for modern life. It powers homes, factories, hospitals, transport and communication. For more than two centuries, most of the world's energy has come from fossil fuels such as coal, oil and natural gas. Today, however, there is growing pressure to switch to **renewable energy**, such as solar, wind and hydroelectric power, which comes from natural sources that do not run out.

The main reason for this change is that fossil fuels are the **leading cause of climate change**. Burning them releases large amounts of carbon dioxide, which traps heat in the atmosphere. Fossil fuels also cause local air pollution, which damages people's lungs and hearts. In addition, these fuels are limited, and countries that depend on imported oil and gas can face sudden price rises, which harm their economies.

Renewable energy offers several clear advantages. First, it produces **little or no pollution** while generating electricity, so it can help to reduce both global warming and local air pollution. Second, sources such as sunlight and wind will never run out. Third, renewable energy can improve **energy security**, because countries can produce their own power instead of buying fuel from abroad. For countries with plenty of sunshine, such as Bangladesh, solar power is particularly suitable.

Renewable energy can also bring electricity to remote areas. In many villages that are far from the national grid, small solar panels on rooftops now allow families to have lights at night, charge mobile phones and run small businesses. Children can study after dark, and shops can stay open longer. This shows that clean energy can improve people's lives as well as the environment.

The renewable energy industry also **creates new jobs** in manufacturing, installation and maintenance. As technology improves and production increases, the cost of solar panels and wind turbines has fallen significantly, making clean energy more affordable than before.

However, renewable energy still faces some challenges. The initial cost of building solar farms or wind turbines can be high, and poorer countries may struggle to afford the investment. Solar and wind power also depend on the weather, so electricity is not always available when it is needed unless it is stored in batteries, which can be expensive. Large projects, such as dams for hydroelectric power, can also damage rivers, farmland and wildlife habitats.

To overcome these problems, governments can offer **financial incentives**, such as subsidies or tax reductions, for households and businesses that install solar panels. They can invest in better storage and power networks and gradually reduce support for coal and oil. Richer countries can help poorer countries by providing funding and technology.

In conclusion, renewable energy is cleaner, safer and more sustainable than fossil fuels. Although the transition requires investment and planning, the **long-term benefits** for the environment, public health and the economy are much greater than the costs.

### Cities: Air Pollution, Traffic and Green Spaces

More than half of the world's population now lives in cities, and this number continues to grow. Cities offer jobs, education and services, but rapid and poorly planned growth often damages the urban environment. Many large cities, including Dhaka, suffer from heavy traffic, dirty air, noise and a shortage of parks and open spaces. These problems have a direct effect on the health and quality of life of millions of people.

**Air pollution** is one of the most serious urban problems. It comes from vehicle exhaust, factories, construction dust, brick kilns and the burning of waste. Breathing polluted air every day can cause asthma, lung disease and heart problems, and children and elderly people are especially vulnerable. On some days, the air in large cities is so polluted that people are advised to stay indoors.

**Traffic congestion** makes the situation worse. As more people buy cars and motorcycles, roads become crowded, journeys take longer, and vehicles release more fumes while they wait in traffic. Workers may spend several hours a day commuting, which increases stress and reduces the time they can spend with their families.

Another problem is the loss of **green spaces**. As land in cities becomes more valuable, parks, playgrounds, lakes and trees are often replaced by apartment buildings, shopping centres and roads. Some people argue that this is necessary, because a growing population needs housing and businesses need space to grow. However, parks are not a luxury. Trees absorb pollution, provide shade and reduce temperatures in hot weather. Parks give children a safe place to play and adults a place to exercise, relax and meet neighbours, which improves both physical and mental health.

There are several ways to tackle these problems. Governments can **invest in public transport**, such as metro rail systems and reliable bus networks, so that people do not need private cars. Safe footpaths and cycle lanes can encourage people to walk or cycle for short journeys. Strict rules on vehicle emissions and factory pollution, together with fines for those who break them, can reduce dirty air. Some cities have tried car-free days or charges for driving in the city centre, which raise awareness and reduce traffic.

City planning is also essential. Governments should **protect existing parks** by law and include green spaces in new housing areas. Housing needs can often be met by building upwards or developing unused industrial land, rather than destroying parks. Planting trees along roads and on rooftops can make a city greener at a relatively low cost.

Individuals can contribute too, by using public transport, sharing cars, keeping their vehicles well maintained and taking part in tree-planting or clean-up activities.

In conclusion, cities face serious environmental problems, but they can be solved with good planning and political will. A city with clean air, efficient transport and plenty of green space is not only healthier but also a more **pleasant place to live and work**.

### Air Travel, Tourism and the Environment

International travel has become far more common than in the past. Cheaper flights, higher incomes and the growth of the tourism industry mean that millions of people now fly abroad every year for holidays, business and family visits. While this has many benefits, it also has a significant impact on the environment, both in the air and at tourist destinations.

The main environmental problem with air travel is its **high carbon emissions**. Aeroplanes burn large amounts of fuel, and a single long-distance return flight can produce as much carbon per passenger as several months of driving. Although aviation is not the largest source of greenhouse gases overall, it is growing quickly, and there are few clean alternatives for long-distance flights at present. Airports also create noise pollution for people who live nearby and take up large areas of land.

Tourism itself can damage the natural environment. Popular beaches, mountains and islands may be crowded with hotels, restaurants and roads, which destroy natural habitats. Tourists produce large amounts of waste and use a great deal of water, often in places where water is scarce. Coral reefs, forests and historic sites can be **damaged beyond repair** when too many visitors arrive without proper management.

On the other hand, travel and tourism bring important benefits. Tourism **generates income and employment** for millions of people, including hotel staff, guides, drivers and craftspeople, and it is a vital part of the economy in many developing countries. International travel also helps people understand other cultures and allows families separated by migration to stay connected. In addition, money from tourism can be used to protect natural areas; national parks often depend on entry fees from visitors.

Several measures can reduce the environmental impact of travel. Some people suggest raising the cost of flights through **environmental taxes**, especially for people who fly very frequently. Higher prices could reduce unnecessary flights and raise money for clean energy. However, critics argue that this would make travel a privilege for the rich and would hurt families who need to visit relatives abroad, so such taxes should be designed fairly.

Other solutions include investing in high-speed trains for shorter journeys, developing cleaner aircraft fuels and replacing some business trips with online meetings. At tourist destinations, governments can limit the number of visitors to sensitive areas, require hotels to manage waste and water responsibly, and promote **eco-tourism**, which focuses on protecting nature and supporting local communities.

Travellers can also make responsible choices, such as taking fewer but longer trips, choosing trains when possible, respecting local environments and avoiding products made from endangered animals.

In conclusion, air travel and tourism offer real economic and cultural benefits, but they also place growing pressure on the environment. Restricting travel completely would be neither realistic nor fair. Instead, a combination of fair taxes, cleaner technology and **responsible tourism** can allow people to keep travelling while protecting the planet.

### Deforestation and Natural Places

Forests cover a large part of the Earth's land, and they are among the most valuable natural resources we have. They provide oxygen, absorb carbon dioxide, protect soil, regulate rainfall and are home to most land animals and plants. Yet forests are being cut down at an alarming rate, and many other natural places, such as hills, wetlands, rivers and beaches, are being lost to development.

The main causes of deforestation are economic. Large areas of forest are cleared to create farmland for crops and cattle, or to grow plantation crops such as palm oil. Trees are also cut down for timber and firewood, and forests are cleared to build roads, mines, factories and housing for growing populations. In many cases, people who cut down forests are poor and need land or fuel to survive, while in others, large companies are driven by profit.

The consequences are serious. Without tree roots to hold the soil, heavy rain can wash it away, causing **soil erosion**, floods and landslides; in hilly areas, these disasters can destroy homes and take lives. When forests are burned or cleared, the carbon stored in the trees is released, which **contributes to global warming**. Deforestation also destroys the habitat of countless species, pushing many towards extinction, and it can change local rainfall patterns, harming farmers.

Some people argue that global warming is the most urgent environmental problem, while others believe deforestation is more damaging. In reality, the two are closely connected: cutting down forests increases global warming, and global warming in turn makes forests more vulnerable to drought and fire. Deforestation, however, has more immediate and visible local effects, such as landslides and the loss of wildlife.

Natural beauty spots, such as lakes, waterfalls, coastlines and mountains, are also under pressure as cities expand and tourism grows. Protecting these places brings many benefits. They provide **clean air and water**, protect biodiversity and offer people a place to relax and escape the stress of city life, which improves mental health. They also attract tourists, creating income and jobs for local communities. Once a natural place is covered with concrete, it is almost impossible to restore it.

There are several ways to protect forests and natural places. Governments can **declare protected areas** and national parks where cutting trees and building are banned, and they must enforce these laws strictly. Large-scale tree-planting programmes, especially with native species, can restore damaged land. Farmers can be supported to use land more efficiently so that they do not need to clear new forest. Consumers can choose products that are certified as sustainable, such as paper and furniture from responsibly managed forests.

Local communities should also be involved. When local people benefit from forests, for example through eco-tourism or the sustainable collection of honey and fruits, they become the best guardians of the land.

In conclusion, forests and natural places are essential for the health of the planet and of human beings. Protecting them requires strong laws, sustainable farming and the involvement of local communities, because once they are lost, they **cannot easily be replaced**.

### Water, Farming and Food

Water and food are the most basic human needs, and both depend on a healthy environment. As the world's population grows, the demand for food and water is increasing, yet many rivers, lakes and underground water sources are being overused or polluted. At the same time, modern farming methods, while producing more food than ever, have created their own environmental problems.

**Water pollution** is a major concern in many countries. Factories, especially textile and leather industries, sometimes release untreated chemical waste directly into rivers, turning the water dark and toxic. Household sewage and plastic waste add to the problem. Polluted water kills fish, harms people who use it for washing and drinking, and spreads diseases such as diarrhoea and cholera. Farmers who use polluted river water for irrigation may also contaminate their crops.

Water shortages are another growing problem. In many regions, farmers pump large amounts of groundwater for irrigation, causing water levels to fall year after year. Climate change is making rainfall less predictable, leading to droughts in some areas and floods in others. In coastal areas, rising sea levels push salt water into rivers and farmland, making the soil less fertile.

Modern agriculture has greatly increased food production, which has helped to **reduce hunger** in many parts of the world. However, the heavy use of chemical fertilisers and pesticides can pollute rivers and harm insects, birds and fish. Growing the same crop on the same land year after year can reduce soil quality, and large-scale cattle farming produces greenhouse gases and requires huge areas of land.

**Food waste** is a further issue. A large proportion of the food that is produced is never eaten; it spoils during transport, is thrown away by shops because it does not look perfect, or is left uneaten on plates. Wasting food also wastes the water, energy and land used to produce it.

There are many ways to address these problems. Governments must **enforce pollution laws** strictly, requiring factories to treat their waste water and fining those that do not. Investment in sewage treatment and clean water supplies can protect public health. Farmers can be trained and supported to use water-saving methods such as drip irrigation, and to use fertilisers and pesticides more carefully or adopt organic methods. Developing crops that can survive drought or salty soil can help farmers adapt to climate change.

Individuals can help by **saving water** at home, reducing food waste by planning meals and storing food properly, and choosing locally produced, seasonal food where possible.

In conclusion, clean water and sustainable farming are essential for feeding a growing world population. By controlling pollution, using water efficiently and reducing food waste, societies can protect these resources for **future generations**.

### Economic Growth versus Environmental Protection

Every country wants economic growth. Growth creates jobs, raises incomes and allows governments to spend more on education, healthcare and infrastructure. However, the industries that drive growth, such as manufacturing, mining, construction and energy production, often damage the environment. This has led to a long debate: should countries focus on growing their economies first and deal with the environment later, or should they protect the environment even if this slows growth?

Supporters of putting the economy first argue that poverty is the most urgent problem in many developing countries. People who do not have enough food, clean water, housing or healthcare need jobs and income before anything else. Factories, roads and power stations provide these jobs. They also point out that today's rich countries became rich by industrialising, often with little concern for the environment, so it seems unfair to ask poorer countries to follow stricter rules. Some also argue that money spent on protecting animals or forests could be better spent on hospitals and schools.

However, ignoring the environment has **serious economic costs**. Polluted air and water make people ill, which increases healthcare spending and reduces productivity. Floods, droughts and storms, made worse by climate change, destroy crops, roads and homes, costing huge sums to repair. When rivers are poisoned, fishing communities lose their income, and when soil is damaged, farmers produce less food. In other words, environmental damage can **undermine the very growth** it was supposed to support.

Moreover, the choice between growth and the environment is increasingly a false one. **Green industries**, such as renewable energy, energy-efficient buildings, recycling and sustainable tourism, are creating millions of jobs around the world. Countries that invest in clean technology early may gain a competitive advantage in the future global economy. Many international buyers also prefer products made in environmentally responsible ways, which encourages exporters to clean up their factories.

The idea of **sustainable development** offers a balanced approach. It means meeting the needs of the present without damaging the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. In practice, this means allowing industries to grow while requiring them to control pollution, use resources efficiently and pay for the damage they cause. Governments can use taxes, fines and incentives to guide businesses towards cleaner methods.

For poorer countries, international support is important. Richer nations, which have caused most past pollution, can provide funding and technology so that developing countries can grow without repeating the same mistakes.

In conclusion, economic growth and environmental protection should not be seen as enemies. In the short term, there may be difficult choices, but in the long term, a healthy environment is the foundation of a healthy economy. The goal should be **growth that is clean, fair and sustainable**.

### Consumerism and Lifestyle

Modern society is often described as a consumer society. People today buy more clothes, electronic devices, furniture and packaged goods than any previous generation. Advertising, social media and easy online shopping constantly encourage us to buy new things. While this has brought comfort and convenience to many people, it has also placed enormous pressure on the planet's resources.

Every product we buy has an environmental cost. Making a single pair of jeans or a smartphone requires raw materials, water and energy, and often involves transporting materials across the world. When products are thrown away, they become waste that must be buried or burned. The more we consume, the more resources we use and the more pollution we create. **Overconsumption**, especially in wealthy countries, is therefore one of the root causes of environmental problems.

**Fast fashion** is a clear example. Cheap clothing is produced quickly in large quantities so that people can follow the latest trends. Many items are worn only a few times before being thrown away. The textile industry uses large amounts of water and chemicals, and in some producing countries, factory waste pollutes rivers. Similarly, many people replace their mobile phones and other devices every year or two, even when the old ones still work, which creates growing amounts of electronic waste.

Many products are also designed not to last. When a cheap appliance breaks, it is often cheaper to buy a new one than to repair the old one. This **throw-away culture** wastes resources and encourages people to see objects as disposable.

However, consumer choices can also be part of the solution. When people **buy less but better**, choosing durable, repairable products, they reduce waste and often save money in the long run. Buying second-hand clothes, repairing items, sharing tools and borrowing rather than buying are all becoming more popular. Consumers who choose environmentally friendly products also put pressure on companies to change their practices.

Governments and businesses have responsibilities too. Governments can introduce rules requiring companies to make products that last longer and can be repaired, and to take back and recycle old products. They can also control misleading advertising that encourages unnecessary buying. Businesses can design products for durability, offer repair services and use recycled materials.

Changing lifestyles is not only about sacrifice. Many people find that a simpler life, with fewer possessions and more time spent on relationships, hobbies and nature, is more satisfying. Happiness does not necessarily increase with the number of things we own.

In conclusion, consumerism has made life more comfortable, but our current level of consumption is **not sustainable**. By buying less, choosing better and valuing what we already have, individuals, together with responsible businesses and governments, can reduce the pressure on the planet.

### Global Problems and International Cooperation

Many environmental problems do not respect national borders. Greenhouse gases released in one country warm the whole planet, air pollution can travel to neighbouring countries, and plastic dumped in one river can end up on beaches thousands of kilometres away. Because of this, many people argue that environmental problems must be solved through **international cooperation** rather than by individual countries acting alone.

The case for an international approach is strong. If only some countries reduce their emissions while others continue to pollute heavily, the overall problem will not be solved. Countries may also worry that strict environmental rules will make their industries less competitive, so they are more willing to act if they know that others are doing the same. **International agreements**, in which countries commit to reducing emissions and protecting forests and oceans, can create common goals and shared responsibility.

A key question in this debate is whether rich or poor nations should take the main responsibility. Rich countries have produced the largest share of greenhouse gases over the past two centuries, as they industrialised and built their wealth. Their citizens also tend to consume more energy and goods per person. From this point of view, it is fair that wealthy nations should **take the lead** in reducing emissions and help poorer countries cope with the effects of climate change.

Poorer countries, meanwhile, often suffer the most serious consequences, even though they contributed least to the problem. Low-lying and agricultural nations, including Bangladesh, face floods, cyclones, droughts and rising seas, but they have fewer resources to protect themselves. Rich countries can support them by providing **financial aid and clean technology**, so that they can build flood defences, adopt renewable energy and grow their economies without polluting heavily.

However, it would be wrong to say that poorer nations have no responsibility at all. Some developing countries are now among the fastest-growing sources of emissions as they industrialise. Deforestation, river pollution and waste problems also occur in poorer countries, and their own governments have the power to address them. Every country, rich or poor, needs to protect its own environment for the sake of its own people.

Domestic action also remains essential. International agreements only work if each government turns its promises into national laws and policies, such as investing in clean energy, protecting forests and controlling pollution. Local problems, like a polluted river or a lack of parks in a city, are usually best solved by national and local authorities, who understand local conditions.

In conclusion, global environmental problems require global solutions, and rich nations should bear the greater responsibility because of their history and wealth. Nevertheless, all countries must play their part, and international cooperation must be supported by strong action **at the national and local level**.

### Environmental Education and Individual Action

Laws, technology and international agreements are essential for protecting the environment, but they are not enough on their own. Real change also depends on the knowledge, attitudes and daily habits of ordinary people. This is why environmental education and individual action are so important.

Many people still **lack awareness** of how their behaviour affects the environment. They may not know how much energy their appliances use, where their waste goes, or how their food and clothing are produced. Others understand the problems but feel that their individual actions are too small to make any difference, so they do nothing. Education can change both of these attitudes.

Schools have a vital role to play. When children learn about pollution, climate change and wildlife from an early age, they are more likely to develop **environmentally friendly habits** that last a lifetime. Practical activities, such as planting trees, starting a school garden, organising recycling programmes or visiting a local river, make the lessons real and memorable. Children also often take what they learn home and influence their parents' behaviour, for example by reminding them to switch off lights or avoid plastic bags.

Public awareness campaigns can reach adults. Governments, charities and the media can use television, social media and posters to explain simple ways of saving energy, reducing waste and protecting nature. Campaigns are especially effective when they show people how environmentally friendly choices can also save them money, such as using energy-efficient light bulbs or reducing food waste.

Individual actions may seem small, but they **add up** when millions of people take part. Using public transport, walking or cycling for short journeys, saving electricity and water, buying fewer unnecessary products, recycling and avoiding single-use plastic all reduce pressure on the environment. Individuals can also join community activities, such as cleaning up a local park or canal, and support organisations that work to protect nature.

Perhaps most importantly, individuals influence the decisions of businesses and governments. When consumers choose environmentally responsible products, companies change their practices to keep their customers. When voters show that they care about the environment, politicians are more likely to introduce green policies. In this way, **individual choices** can lead to wider social change.

However, it is important not to place all the responsibility on individuals. People can only make green choices if those choices are available and affordable. A person cannot take the bus if there is no reliable bus service, or recycle if there is no recycling collection. Governments must therefore create the conditions that make sustainable living easy.

In conclusion, environmental education and individual action are essential parts of the solution. By **raising awareness** and changing daily habits, people can make a real difference, especially when their efforts are supported by government policies and responsible businesses.

| food security (n) | খাদ্য নিরাপত্তা | **threaten / ensure** food security | Droughts threaten food security in many regions. |

| Positive phrases | Negative phrases |
|---|---|
| **play a key role in** protecting | **pose a serious threat to** |
| **bring long-term benefits** | **cause irreversible damage to** |
| **reduce pressure on** natural resources | **put** wildlife **at risk** |
| **pave the way for** a cleaner future | **come at the expense of** the environment |

## Part 5 — Sentence Structure Toolkit {: .part}

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Paragraph mix formula:** প্রতিটি body paragraph-এ রাখার চেষ্টা করো → **1টি long simple + 1–2টি compound + 2–3টি complex + 1টি compound-complex** sentence। 5–6টি বাক্য × গড়ে 20 শব্দ = **100–120 words**।

</div>

#### 1. Long simple sentence (rich noun phrase)

**Formula:** [long noun phrase] + verb + object
*একটাই clause, কিন্তু subject-টা বড় ও তথ্যপূর্ণ।*

- The burning of coal, oil and gas in power stations and vehicles **is** the main cause of global warming.
- The rapid loss of forests in many developing countries **has pushed** numerous species towards extinction.

#### 2. Compound with *and / but / so / yet*

- Plastic is cheap and convenient, **but** it takes hundreds of years to break down.
- Rich countries polluted the most in the past, **so** they should pay more to solve the problem.

#### 3. Compound with semicolon + linker

- Renewable energy is expensive to install**; however,** it saves money in the long run.
- Factories release untreated waste into rivers**; as a result,** fish die and people become ill.

#### 4. Complex — concession (*Although / Even though / While*)

- **Although** individuals can recycle and save energy, only governments can build clean power stations.
- **Even though** tourism damages some natural sites, it also provides money to protect them.

#### 5. Complex — reason (*Because / Since / As*)

- **Since** pollution crosses national borders, no country can solve the problem alone.
- **Because** trees hold the soil in place, cutting them down on hills causes landslides.

#### 6. Complex — condition (*If / Unless / Provided that*)

- **If** governments do not act now, future generations **will** pay a much higher price.
- **Unless** illegal hunting is stopped, many species **may** disappear completely.

#### 7. Relative clause (*which / who / where / whose*)

- Many factories release smoke into the air, **which** causes serious breathing problems.
- Farmers **whose** land has been flooded by salt water can no longer grow rice.

#### 8. Compound-complex

**Formula:** Although/Because/If + clause, Clause A, and/but Clause B

- **Although** solar panels are costly at first, they reduce electricity bills, **and** they produce no pollution.
- **If** cities invest in public transport, fewer people will drive, **and** the air will become cleaner.

#### 9. Participle phrase

- **Faced** with more frequent floods, many coastal families have moved to the cities.
- **Using** public transport instead of private cars, commuters can significantly reduce air pollution.

#### 10. *Not only … but also …*

- Parks **not only** absorb pollution **but also** give people a place to relax and exercise.
- Deforestation **not only** destroys wildlife habitats **but also** contributes to global warming.

#### 11. *The more …, the more …*

- **The more** we consume, **the more** waste we produce.
- **The more** trees a city plants, **the cooler and cleaner** its air becomes.

#### 12. Cleft sentence (emphasis)

- **It is** governments, not individuals, **that** have the power to change national energy policy.
- **What** the world needs most **is** cooperation rather than blame.

#### 13. Impersonal passive

- **It is widely accepted that** human activity is the main cause of climate change.
- **It is often argued that** environmental protection slows down economic growth.

#### 14. Contrasting views

- **While some people believe that** individuals should solve environmental problems, **others argue that** this is the government's job.
- **While** economic growth creates jobs, **environmental damage** can destroy livelihoods.

#### 15. Result / consequence

- Many people now carry reusable bags, **which means that** fewer plastic bags end up in rivers.
- Forests are being cleared for farmland. **As a result,** many animals have lost their homes.

#### Introduction (35–45 words)

কেউ কেউ বিশ্বাস করেন যে কেবল সাধারণ নাগরিকেরাই পরিবেশের ক্ষতি কমাতে পারে, অন্যদিকে অনেকে যুক্তি দেন যে এমন বৈশ্বিক সমস্যা সরকারের দায়িত্ব। <span class="tag">Complex · Passive</span> আমি শেষের মতটিকে সম্পূর্ণ সমর্থন করি, কারণ প্রয়োজনীয় ক্ষমতা কেবল সরকারের আছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — বিপরীত মত (90–120 words)

একদিকে, যারা ব্যক্তিগত উদ্যোগকে সমর্থন করেন, তারা যুক্তি দেন যে প্রতিটি মানুষ তার ব্যবহৃত জ্বালানি ও কেনা পণ্যের মাধ্যমে এই সমস্যায় ভূমিকা রাখে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> ব্যক্তিগত উদ্যোগ ছোট মনে হতে পারে, কিন্তু লক্ষ লক্ষ মানুষ অংশ নিলে তা বড় হয়ে ওঠে (add up)। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> গণপরিবহন ব্যবহার করে এবং একবার ব্যবহারযোগ্য প্লাস্টিক এড়িয়ে চলে সাধারণ পরিবারগুলো পরিবেশের ওপর চাপ কমাতে পারে। <span class="tag">Simple · Verb+ing</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, ঢাকার যে পরিবার বাসে যাতায়াত করে এবং কাপড়ের ব্যাগ ব্যবহার করে, তারা কম বর্জ্য তৈরি করে, এবং এই পরিবর্তনে তাদের খুব কম খরচ হয়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> এ কারণে অনেকে বিশ্বাস করেন যে পরিবেশ রক্ষার কাজ সাধারণ নাগরিক থেকেই শুরু হওয়া উচিত। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — আমার মত (90–120 words)

অন্যদিকে, আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে এই সমস্যাগুলো সরকারেরই মোকাবিলা করা উচিত, কারণ বড় পরিসরে কাজ করার ক্ষমতা কেবল তাদেরই আছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> দূষণ যখন মূলত বিদ্যুৎকেন্দ্র ও কারখানা থেকে আসে, তখন কঠোর আইন এবং পরিচ্ছন্ন জ্বালানিতে বড় বিনিয়োগ প্রয়োজন হয়, এবং একজন মানুষের পক্ষে তা দেওয়া সম্ভব নয়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex · Passive</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, নদী দূষণকারী কারখানার মালিক এলাকাবাসীর অভিযোগ উপেক্ষা করতে পারেন, কিন্তু ভারী জরিমানাসহ একটি আইন প্রতিটি কারখানাকে বর্জ্য পরিষ্কার করতে বাধ্য করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> যদিও কেউ কেউ যুক্তি দেন যে ছোট ছোট উদ্যোগ মিলে বড় হয়, পরিবেশবান্ধব বিকল্পগুলো সহজলভ্য ও সাশ্রয়ী হলেই কেবল মানুষ সেগুলো বেছে নিতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> ফলে সরকার নেতৃত্ব না দিলে (take the lead) ব্যক্তিগত চেষ্টা সফল হতে পারে না। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Conclusion (35–45 words)

পরিশেষে, পরিবেশের ক্ষতি মোকাবিলা করবে নাগরিকেরা নাকি জাতীয় নেতারা, এ নিয়ে মানুষের মধ্যে ভিন্ন ভিন্ন মত আছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যদিও ব্যক্তিগত সিদ্ধান্ত বর্জ্য ও দূষণ কমাতে পারে, আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে এই সমস্যাগুলোর সমাধান সরকারেরই করা উচিত, কারণ প্রয়োজনীয় পরিসরে পরিবর্তন আনতে কেবল তারাই পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Introduction (30–40 words)

বৈশ্বিক উষ্ণায়ন (global warming) আজ প্রাকৃতিক বিশ্বের সামনে থাকা সবচেয়ে গুরুতর বিপদগুলোর একটি। <span class="tag">Simple</span> এর মূল কারণ জীবাশ্ম জ্বালানি পোড়ানো ও বন ধ্বংস করা, কিন্তু নবায়নযোগ্য জ্বালানি ও বন সংরক্ষণ এই সমস্যার মোকাবিলা করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — 2টি কারণ (90–120 words)

বৈশ্বিক উষ্ণায়নের প্রধান কারণ হলো জীবাশ্ম জ্বালানি (fossil fuels) পোড়ানো। <span class="tag">Simple</span> বিদ্যুৎকেন্দ্র, কারখানা, গাড়ি ও উড়োজাহাজে যখন কয়লা, তেল ও প্রাকৃতিক গ্যাস পোড়ানো হয়, তখন তা বায়ুমণ্ডলে কার্বন ডাই-অক্সাইড ও অন্যান্য গ্রিনহাউস গ্যাস ছাড়ে। <span class="tag">Complex · Passive</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, একটি বড় শহরে বিদ্যুৎ সরবরাহের জন্য কয়লা পোড়ানো একটি বিদ্যুৎকেন্দ্র প্রতিদিন কার্বন ডাই-অক্সাইড ছাড়ে, এবং এই গ্যাস কম্বলের মতো পৃথিবীর চারপাশে তাপ আটকে রাখে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> আরেকটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ কারণ হলো বন উজাড় (deforestation), যা প্রায়ই কৃষিজমি তৈরির জন্য করা হয়। <span class="tag">Complex · Passive</span> গাছ কার্বন ডাই-অক্সাইড শোষণ করে; যখন বন কেটে ফেলা হয় বা পুড়িয়ে দেওয়া হয়, তখন এই প্রাকৃতিক সুরক্ষা হারিয়ে যায়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> ফলে প্রতি বছর পৃথিবীর চারপাশে আরও বেশি তাপ আটকে পড়ে। <span class="tag">Simple · Passive</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — 2টি সমাধান (90–120 words)

সবচেয়ে কার্যকর সমাধান হলো, সরকার জীবাশ্ম জ্বালানির বদলে নবায়নযোগ্য জ্বালানি (renewable energy) চালু করবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> সরকার যখন ভর্তুকি বা কর ছাড়ের মতো আর্থিক প্রণোদনা দেয়, তখন পরিবার ও ব্যবসাপ্রতিষ্ঠান solar panel কিনতে পারে, ফলে কম কয়লা ও তেল পোড়ানো হয়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> যেমন, সরকারের সহায়তায় solar panel বসানো একটি গ্রামীণ বাংলাদেশি পরিবারকে আর কয়লা থেকে তৈরি বিদ্যুতের ওপর নির্ভর করতে হয় না, যা তাদের কার্বন নিঃসরণ কমায়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এছাড়া, সরকার পৃথিবীর অবশিষ্ট বনগুলো রক্ষা করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> তারা সংরক্ষিত এলাকা ও জাতীয় উদ্যান ঘোষণা করতে পারে, যেখানে গাছ কাটা ও নির্মাণকাজ নিষিদ্ধ, এবং বড় আকারের বৃক্ষরোপণ কর্মসূচি, বিশেষ করে দেশীয় প্রজাতির গাছ দিয়ে, ক্ষতিগ্রস্ত জমি আবার সবুজ করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> এই পদক্ষেপগুলো একসাথে গ্রিনহাউস গ্যাস কমাবে এবং পৃথিবীর উষ্ণ হওয়ার গতি ধীর করবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Conclusion (30–40 words)

পরিশেষে, বৈশ্বিক উষ্ণায়ন মূলত কয়লা, তেল ও গ্যাসের ব্যবহার এবং গাছ হারানোর ফল। <span class="tag">Simple</span> পরিচ্ছন্ন জ্বালানি এবং বনভূমির আরও কঠোর সুরক্ষা পৃথিবীর জন্য এই হুমকি উল্লেখযোগ্যভাবে কমিয়ে দেবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

### Essay 3 — Agree / Disagree

<div class="question">Both governments and individuals are spending vast amounts of money protecting animals and their habitats. This money could be better spent dealing with fundamental issues in society, such as poverty and health care. To what extent do you agree?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · ENV-11 · Common topic ★ Protecting wildlife*

**Essay type:** Agree / Disagree · **Position:** I completely disagree.<br>
**অবস্থান:** আমি সম্পূর্ণ দ্বিমত পোষণ করি — প্রকৃতি সংরক্ষণ মানুষের বেঁচে থাকার উপাদান রক্ষা করে এবং দারিদ্র্যও কমায়।<br>
**কাঠামো:** IELTS_Agree_Disagree_Essay_Guide.md অনুযায়ী — শুরু থেকে শেষ পর্যন্ত একটিই স্পষ্ট পক্ষ, কখনো আংশিক উত্তর নয়। মোট 270–345 শব্দ।

**পরিকল্পনা (Plan)**

- **Introduction (35–50):** প্রশ্নের paraphrase → I completely disagree + Idea 1 (মানুষের বেঁচে থাকার উপাদান রক্ষা) + Idea 2 (দারিদ্র্য কমায়)।
- **Body Paragraph 1 (100–120) — Idea 1:** বন ও নদী = পানি, খাদ্য, জলবায়ু → দূষণে অসুখ, চিকিৎসার খরচ → উদাহরণ: বন হারানো গ্রাম → মানুষের স্বাস্থ্যও রক্ষা পায়।
- **Body Paragraph 2 (100–120) — Idea 2:** প্রকৃতি থেকে জীবিকা, পর্যটন → উদাহরণ: অভয়ারণ্যের পাশে guesthouse → Response: দারিদ্র্য বেশি জরুরি, কিন্তু সংরক্ষণ বাজেটের ছোট অংশ → আয়ের উৎস নষ্ট হবে।
- **Conclusion (35–55):** In conclusion, I firmly believe… → Idea 1 ও Idea 2 নতুন শব্দে।

#### Introduction (35–50 words)

প্রায়ই যুক্তি দেওয়া হয় যে প্রাণী ও তাদের আবাসস্থল রক্ষায় যে বিপুল অর্থ ব্যয় হয়, তা বরং দারিদ্র্য দূর করা ও স্বাস্থ্যসেবা উন্নত করার কাজে লাগানো উচিত। <span class="tag">Complex</span> আমি এই মতের সাথে সম্পূর্ণ দ্বিমত পোষণ করি, কারণ প্রকৃতি সংরক্ষণ (conservation) মানুষের বেঁচে থাকার জন্য প্রয়োজনীয় জিনিসগুলো রক্ষা করে এবং এটি দারিদ্র্যও কমাতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — Idea 1 (100–120 words)

এই ধারণার সাথে আমার দ্বিমতের প্রধান কারণ হলো, প্রাণী ও তাদের আবাসস্থল (habitats) রক্ষা করলে মানুষের বেঁচে থাকার জন্য যা প্রয়োজন, তা-ও রক্ষা পায়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এর কারণ হলো, বন ও নদী রক্ষা করলে মানুষ যে বিশুদ্ধ পানি, খাদ্য ও জলবায়ুর ওপর নির্ভর করে, সেগুলোও রক্ষা পায়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> ক্ষতিগ্রস্ত বন ও দূষিত নদী মানুষকে অসুস্থ করে; ফলে চিকিৎসার খরচ বাড়ে এবং সবচেয়ে দরিদ্র পরিবারগুলোই প্রথমে কষ্ট পায়। <span class="tag">Compound</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, যে গ্রাম তার বন হারায় এবং দূষিত নদীর পানি পান করে, সেখানকার মানুষ প্রায়ই অসুস্থ থাকতে পারে, এবং এর ফলে দরিদ্র পরিবারগুলো তাদের সামান্য সঞ্চয় ওষুধের পেছনে খরচ করতে বাধ্য হয়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> তাই প্রকৃতি সংরক্ষণ শুধু প্রাণীদের নয়, মানুষের স্বাস্থ্যকেও সুরক্ষা দেয়। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — Idea 2 (100–120 words)

আমার দ্বিমতের আরেকটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ কারণ হলো, প্রকৃতি সংরক্ষণ আসলে দারিদ্র্য কমাতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> মানুষ যখন প্রকৃতি থেকে জীবিকা অর্জন করে, তখন তা রক্ষা করার একটি শক্ত কারণ খুঁজে পায়, কারণ জাতীয় উদ্যান ও বন্যপ্রাণী অভয়ারণ্য পর্যটক আকর্ষণ করে, যারা গাইড, হোটেল ও খাবারের জন্য টাকা খরচ করে এবং এতে স্থানীয়দের কর্মসংস্থান হয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যেমন, অভয়ারণ্যের কাছে বাস করা যে পরিবার একটি ছোট guesthouse খোলে, তারা হয়তো সন্তানদের স্কুলে পাঠাতে পারে, যা তাদের দারিদ্র্য থেকে বেরিয়ে আসতে সাহায্য করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যদিও কেউ কেউ যুক্তি দেন যে দারিদ্র্য দূর করা বেশি জরুরি, সংরক্ষণে ব্যয় করা অর্থ সাধারণত জাতীয় বাজেটের খুব ছোট একটি অংশ। <span class="tag">Complex</span> ফলে এই অর্থ অন্য খাতে সরিয়ে নিলে দারিদ্র্যের সমাধান প্রায় হবে না, বরং আয়ের একটি উপকারী উৎস নষ্ট হবে। <span class="tag">Compound</span>

#### Conclusion (35–55 words)

পরিশেষে, আমি দৃঢ়ভাবে বিশ্বাস করি যে বন্যপ্রাণী ও তাদের আবাসস্থল রক্ষার অর্থ অন্য সামাজিক সমস্যার দিকে সরিয়ে নেওয়া উচিত নয়। <span class="tag">Complex · Passive</span> সুস্থ প্রকৃতি মানুষের সমাজকে বাঁচিয়ে রাখে, এবং এটি দরিদ্র পরিবারগুলোকে উপকারী কাজ ও আয়ের সুযোগও দেয়। <span class="tag">Compound</span>

</div>

| 4 | Did I use at least **5 complex** and **2 compound-complex** sentences? | |

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Common mistakes in environment essays (এগুলো এড়িয়ে চলো):**

- ❌ *Environment is getting polluted.* → ✅ **The** environment is becoming polluted. ("the environment" সবসময় *the* সহ)
- ❌ *pollutions, wastes, rubbishes, equipments* → ✅ pollution, waste, rubbish, equipment (uncountable)
- ❌ *Global warming is caused by human.* → ✅ … caused by **humans / human activity**.
- ❌ *Government should take step.* → ✅ **Governments** should take **steps** / **The government** should take **action**.
- ❌ *Trees give us oxygen and make environment good.* (too simple) → ✅ *Trees absorb carbon dioxide and provide habitats for wildlife.*
- **"environment / pollution" প্রতিশব্দ:** the natural world, nature, the planet, ecosystems, environmental damage, contamination, emissions

### Homework 1 — Double Question

<div class="question">With deforestation, urban development and illegal hunting, many animal species are becoming endangered as they lose their habitat, and some are even threatened with extinction. Do you think it is important to protect animals? What measures can be taken to deal with this problem?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · ENV-09 · ★ protecting wildlife*

**Essay type:** Double Question — Opinion + List · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–300 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 30–40 · BP1 = Q1 (Opinion block) 90–120 · BP2 = Q2 (List block) 90–120 · Conclusion 30–40<br>
**Use ideas from:** Wildlife & Biodiversity; Deforestation & Natural Places; International Cooperation

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- Q1 "Do you think it is important…?" পক্ষ বেছে নিতে বলে, তাই Opinion block: হ্যাঁ, গুরুত্বপূর্ণ — একটি কারণ (বাস্তুতন্ত্রের ভারসাম্য) ও একটি উদাহরণ (মৌমাছি ও ফসল)।
- Opinion block-এর Response: Although some people argue that মানুষের প্রয়োজন আগে, [তোমার পক্ষ]।
- Q2 "What measures…?" নাম জানতে চায়, তাই List block: 2টি পদক্ষেপ (কঠোর আইন ও বনরক্ষী; স্থানীয়দের কাজে লাগানো), Body 2-তে "I" নয়।
- Introduction: paraphrase + [উত্তর 1], and [উত্তর 2]। Conclusion-এ দুটি উত্তর নতুন শব্দে, নতুন idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** lose their natural habitat · on the brink of extinction · biodiversity · play a vital role in the ecosystem · crack down on poaching · declare protected areas · involve local communities · for future generations

**Sentence target:** use *not only… but also…* once and *Unless…, …* once.

</div>

### Homework 2 — Advantages–Disadvantages

<div class="question">Many people around the world now take regular international flights. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages to the environment?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · TOU-09*

**Essay type:** Advantages–Disadvantages, Type 2 (outweigh) · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 330–360 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 40–50 · BP1 advantages 120–135 · BP2 disadvantages 120–135 · Conclusion 35–45<br>
**Use ideas from:** Air Travel & Tourism; Climate Change; Economic Growth vs Environment

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- একটিই রায় দাও: সুবিধা বেশি অথবা অসুবিধা বেশি। "সুবিধা বেশি, যদি দায়িত্বশীলভাবে ভ্রমণ করে" ধরনের শর্ত লিখবে না।
- Body 1 (ঠিক 7টি বাক্য): 2টি সুবিধা (পর্যটন আয় ও চাকরি; প্রবাসী পরিবারের যোগাযোগ)।
- Body 2 (ঠিক 7টি বাক্য): 2টি অসুবিধা (কার্বন নিঃসরণ; পর্যটন এলাকায় প্রকৃতির ক্ষতি)। সুবিধার পক্ষে হলে শেষ বাক্য "Although these…"।
- Although ও While বাক্যে যে দিক সমর্থন করো তা comma-র পরে; Introduction ও Conclusion-এ একই রায়।

</div>

**Try to use:** high carbon emissions · generate income and employment · damage beyond repair · environmental taxes · responsible tourism · eco-tourism · in the long run · outweigh

**Sentence target:** use *While…, …* once to contrast, and *which means that…* once.

</div>

### Homework 3 — Discussion

<div class="question">While some people consider global warming to be the most pressing environmental problem we have at the moment, others believe that deforestation has a more devastating impact on our world. Discuss both sides and give your opinion.</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · ENV-05*

**Essay type:** Discussion (both views + opinion) · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–299 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 35–45 · BP1 (the other view) 90–120 · BP2 (your view) 90–120 · Conclusion 35–45<br>
**Use ideas from:** Climate Change; Deforestation & Natural Places; Wildlife & Biodiversity

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- বৈশ্বিক উষ্ণায়ন কেন সবচেয়ে জরুরি — এর প্রভাব কি সারা পৃথিবীতে (বন্যা, খরা, সমুদ্রপৃষ্ঠ)?
- বন উজাড় কেন বেশি ধ্বংসাত্মক — এর প্রভাব কি দ্রুত ও সরাসরি (ভূমিধস, প্রাণী বিলুপ্তি)?
- তোমার মত: একটিই পক্ষ বেছে নাও, কখনো আংশিক নয়। সেটি সবসময় BP2-তে রাখো।
- উপসংহারে নতুন কোনো idea বা সুপারিশ নয়: প্রশ্ন নতুন শব্দে, দুই মতের মূল কথা, আর একই মত।

</div>

**Try to use:** the most pressing problem · have a devastating impact on · greenhouse gases · absorb carbon dioxide · soil erosion and landslides · loss of biodiversity · closely connected · take urgent action

**Sentence target:** use *While some people believe that…, others argue that…* in the introduction and one cleft sentence (*It is… that…*).

</div>

### Homework 4 — Agree / Disagree

<div class="question">Global environmental issues are the responsibility of rich nations, not of poorer nations. Do you agree with this opinion?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · ENV-14*

**Essay type:** Agree / Disagree — one clear side · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 270–345 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 35–50 · BP1 Idea 1 100–120 · BP2 Idea 2 100–120 · Conclusion 35–55<br>
**Use ideas from:** International Cooperation; Economic Growth vs Environment; Climate Change

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- একটিই পক্ষ নাও: সম্পূর্ণ একমত বা সম্পূর্ণ দ্বিমত। "ধনী দেশের দায়িত্ব বেশি, কিন্তু একমাত্র নয়" লিখবে না।
- তোমার পক্ষের 2টি idea কী (যেমন অতীতের দূষণ ও বেশি ভোগ; অর্থ ও প্রযুক্তি)? প্রতিটির একটি বাস্তব উদাহরণ।
- বিপরীত যুক্তিটি (দরিদ্র দেশের বন উজাড় ও দূষণ) BP2-এর Response বাক্যে Although অংশে রাখো।
- উপসংহার: In conclusion, I firmly believe that… + দুটি idea নতুন শব্দে। নতুন কোনো সুপারিশ বা idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** take the lead · historical emissions · financial aid and clean technology · bear the greater responsibility · collective responsibility · industrialise · international cooperation · at the national and local level

**Sentence target:** use *Although…, …* twice and one relative clause with *whose*.

</div>
