## Part 1 — Idea Generation Flow {: .part}

<div class="flow">Health → Fast Food &amp; Modern Diets → Obesity &amp; Responsibility → Exercise &amp; Sedentary Lifestyle → Mental Health &amp; Stress → Prevention vs Treatment → Free Healthcare &amp; Who Pays → Health Taxes &amp; Laws → Children's Health &amp; School Sport → Technology &amp; Health → Ageing Population → Traditional vs Modern Medicine → Media, Advertising &amp; Body Image</div>

When you see **any** health question, run through this chain from left to right and ask: *"Does this question touch this area?"* Then **stop at the 2 links that fit the question best**. Those 2 links become your two body paragraphs.

**Memory trick — the 4 levels of health responsibility**

Almost every health question asks, directly or indirectly, *"Who should be responsible for people's health?"* Think from the smallest level to the largest:

| Level | Who? | Typical ideas |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Me** | the individual | diet, exercise, sleep, smoking, stress, personal choice |
| **2. Family & School** | parents, teachers | children's meals, screen time, PE lessons, healthy habits |
| **3. Doctors & Health System** | doctors, hospitals | treatment, check-ups, health education, prevention |
| **4. Government & Society** | state, companies, media | laws, taxes, free healthcare, parks, advertising rules |

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Example:** *"The number of overweight people is increasing. What are the reasons? How can this problem be solved?"*

Run the chain → **Fast Food** ✔ → **Exercise & Sedentary Lifestyle** ✔ → **Health Taxes & Laws** ✔.
BP1 (reasons) = Level 1–2: fast food + less exercise. BP2 (solutions) = Level 2–4: school PE, sugar tax, food labels, parks.

</div>

## Part 2 — Exam-Hall Brainstorming System {: .part}

### 2A. The 5-Minute Plan

| Minute | What to do | What you write on the question paper |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | **Decode the question.** Underline the topic words and the instruction words. Decide which of your five essay guides it uses (Part 2G). | e.g. *prevention / treatment / funding / to what extent → Agree / Disagree guide* |
| 1–3 | **4-level grid.** Me – Family & School – Doctors – Government: one note each. | 4–6 one-line notes |
| 3–4 | **Choose the 2 strongest ideas.** Add one reason and one example to each. | Idea 1 + why + example; Idea 2 + why + example |
| 4–5 | **Decide your position (one clear side, only if the question asks) and use the guide's word plan.** | Thesis / BP1 / BP2 / Conclusion in 4 lines |

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**পরীক্ষার হলে মনে রাখো:** Health essay-তে খুব সাধারণ কথা ("health is wealth", "exercise is good") লিখে নম্বর পাওয়া যায় না। প্রতিটি idea-র সাথে **কারণ** (কেন?) এবং **নির্দিষ্ট উদাহরণ** (কে, কোথায়, কী হয়?) দাও। যেমন: *"office workers who sit for eight hours a day and drive home"* — এটাই Band 7-এর উদাহরণ।

### 2E. How One Idea Becomes a Full Paragraph (PEEL)

**P**oint → **E**xplain (why? how?) → **E**xample → **L**ink / Result

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### বাংলায় চিন্তা (Bangla thinking)

**Point:** রোগ প্রতিরোধ চিকিৎসার চেয়ে সস্তা ও বেশি কার্যকর।
**Explain:** কারণ রোগ গুরুতর হওয়ার আগে ধরা পড়লে চিকিৎসা সহজ হয় এবং খরচও কম লাগে।
**Example:** যেমন, বিনামূল্যে রক্তচাপ পরীক্ষার মাধ্যমে একজন মানুষের উচ্চ রক্তচাপ আগেই ধরা পড়লে তাকে ব্যয়বহুল হৃদরোগের চিকিৎসা নিতে হয় না।
**Link/Result:** ফলে মানুষ ও সরকার — দুজনেরই অর্থ বাঁচে।

</div>

**The same idea in English (one paragraph):**

> **Point:** Preventing illness is usually cheaper and more effective than treating it.
> **Explain:** This is because diseases that are detected before they become serious are easier to treat and require less expensive care.
> **Example:** For example, if a free blood-pressure check reveals that a person has high blood pressure, they can control it with simple medicine and avoid costly heart treatment later.
> **Link:** As a result, both patients and the government save a great deal of money.

Count it: about **75 words** from one idea. Add one more explanation or a second short example and you reach the **100–120 words** that one body paragraph needs.

### 2F. Worked Brainstorm Demo

<div class="question">The amount of time spent on sport and exercise should be increased in schools in order to tackle the problem of overweight children. Do you think this is the best way to deal with the problem? What other solutions can you suggest?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · HEA-03 (also in the Part 7 Question Bank)*

**My 5-minute notes:**

```
Type: Double Question → Opinion + List (Double Question guide, Model Essay B)
Q1: "Is this the BEST way?" → OPINION: No  ("best" + "other solutions")
Q2: "What OTHER solutions?" → LIST, and NOT more sport ("other" trap)

Intro (30–40w): paraphrase + "this is not the best way, and healthier school
                meals and better habits at home would work better"
BP1 (90–120w) OPINION block – P E X R L:
   P: not the best way, because diet affects weight more than exercise
   E: more calories than the body needs → one hour of sport cannot balance them
   X: child plays football at school, then soft drinks + fried snacks at home
   R: Although some argue PE keeps children active, it cannot undo a bad diet
   L: Therefore, extra sport alone is not the most effective answer
BP2 (90–120w) LIST block – solutions (no "I"):
   S1 schools improve food → ex: pupil drinks water instead of a soft drink
   S2 parents build better habits → home-cooked meals, less screen time
   L: Together, these measures would tackle the main causes of obesity
Conclusion (30–40w): answer 1 + answer 2 in new words
```

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**কেন এই পরিকল্পনা?** প্রশ্নে "best" থাকলে সবচেয়ে সহজ উত্তর একটি স্পষ্ট "No", কারণ তখন Q2-এর "অন্য সমাধান" স্বাভাবিকভাবে চলে আসে। "Helpful, but NOT enough alone" লিখবে না, কারণ এটি আংশিক উত্তর। Q2-তে আবার খেলাধুলার কথা লিখবে না ("other" ফাঁদ)। BP1-এর soft drink উদাহরণটি BP2-এর সমাধানে ফিরে আসে, তাই দুটি paragraph সহজে যুক্ত হয়। Idea chain-এর **Children's Health & School Sport** ও **Fast Food** থেকে idea নেওয়া হয়েছে।

### 2G. Essay-Type Paragraph Plans (your five essay guides)

| Essay type (guide) | Introduction | Body Paragraph 1 | Body Paragraph 2 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agree / Disagree** (*to what extent? / do you agree?*) | **35–50:** paraphrase + *I completely agree/disagree with this view because* Idea 1 and Idea 2. Never "partly". | **100–120:** Idea 1 — P E E X L | **100–120:** Idea 2 — P E X R L (*Although some people argue that …, [my side]*) | **35–55:** *In conclusion, I firmly believe that …* + both ideas in new words |
| **Discuss Both Views** (*some think X, others think Y*) | **35–45:** *While some people argue that [other view], others believe that [my view]. I completely support the latter view because …* | **90–120:** the view you do **not** support, no "I" — P E E X L | **90–120:** **your view** — P E X R L | **35–45:** *In conclusion, people hold different views on whether … While …, I firmly believe that …* |
| **Advantages – Disadvantages** | **40–50:** paraphrase + *Although* [side you do not support], [side you support] + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **30–40**, 2 sentences) | **120–135:** advantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **120–135:** disadvantages — 7 sentences (T P E X P E S) | **35–45:** *While …* + opinion only if asked (no opinion asked: **25–35**) |
| **Cause/Problem + Solution** | **30–40:** paraphrase + *This is mainly because C1 and C2, but S1 and S2 can tackle this problem.* No opinion. | **90–120:** 2 causes or problems — P E X P E L | **90–120:** 2 solutions, each fixing one cause (WHO + WHAT + HOW) | **30–40:** causes + solutions in new words |
| **Double Question** (*two questions*) | **30–40:** paraphrase + *[Answer 1], and [answer 2].* | **90–120:** answer to Q1 — List block (P E X P E L) or Opinion block (P E X R L) | **90–120:** answer to Q2 — List or Opinion block | **30–40:** *In conclusion,* answer 1 + answer 2 in new words |

*Full rules: `Writing/IELTS_*_Essay_Guide.md`. Questions asking only for solutions or only for problems use the Cause/Problem + Solution guide, Section 14: one idea in each body paragraph (P E E X L), no opinion.*

- **শব্দসংখ্যা:** Agree / Disagree 270–345 · Discuss Both Views 260–299 · Advantages – Disadvantages 330–360 (মতামত না চাইলে 310–340) · Cause/Problem + Solution ও Double Question 260–300। সহজ হিসাব: 35 + 105 + 105 + 35 = 280।
- **Introduction ও Conclusion:** সবসময় 2টি বাক্য (Advantages – Disadvantages-এ মতামত চাইলে 3টি)। "This essay will…" ধরনের বাক্য কখনো লিখবে না।
- **Body paragraph:** প্রতিটি বাক্য গড়ে 18–22 শব্দ। Example সবসময় ঠিক আগের Explain বাক্যটিকেই দেখায়।

## Part 3 — Category-Wise Free Writing {: .part}

Each passage below is an **idea store**. Read it slowly, notice how each idea is explained and supported with an example, and pay attention to the **bold collocations**. After each passage, try to say 4–5 of its ideas aloud in your own words.

### Fast Food, Processed Food and Modern Diets

Diets around the world have changed dramatically in the last few decades. In the past, most families ate fresh, home-cooked meals made from local ingredients. Today, burgers, fried chicken, pizza, instant noodles, packaged snacks and sugary drinks are available on almost every street corner and can be delivered to the door within minutes. As the pace of modern life has increased, more and more people are turning to fast food and ready-made meals for their main meals.

There are understandable reasons for this trend. Fast food is **quick and convenient**, which is important for people who work long hours, study late or have long journeys to and from work. It is often cheap, and it is easy to find in cities. Ready-made and processed foods also have practical advantages: preservatives allow them to be stored for a long time, which reduces food waste and makes it easier to keep food at home. For busy parents, a frozen meal can be a quick solution on a difficult evening.

However, the health effects of a fast-food diet are serious. Many fast foods and processed meals are **high in fat, sugar and salt**, but low in vitamins, minerals and fibre. Eating them regularly can lead to weight gain, obesity, high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes and heart disease. Sugary soft drinks are especially harmful, because they contain a large amount of sugar but do not make people feel full. Some people also worry about the chemicals and additives used in processed foods, although most are tested and permitted in small amounts.

Fast food also affects family and cultural life. When families rely on takeaways and ready meals, they may stop cooking and eating together, and **traditional cooking skills** may not be passed on to the next generation. Children who grow up eating mainly fast food may never learn to enjoy vegetables, fish or traditional dishes.

On the other hand, banning fast food completely would be unrealistic and would limit personal freedom. Most people can enjoy fast food occasionally without harm; the problem is eating it every day. Fast food restaurants also create many jobs, particularly for young people.

The best solutions focus on information, choice and price. Governments can require restaurants and food companies to show **clear nutritional information**, such as calories and sugar content, on menus and packaging. Taxes on sugary drinks can encourage companies to reduce sugar and customers to choose healthier options. Schools can teach children basic cooking skills and the principles of a **balanced diet**, and restaurants can be encouraged to offer healthier menu items, such as salads, grilled food and water instead of soft drinks.

In conclusion, fast food offers speed and convenience, but a diet based mainly on it can cause serious health problems. The answer is not to ban it, but to **eat it in moderation** and to make healthy food easier, cheaper and more attractive for everyone.

### Obesity: Causes and Responsibility

Obesity has become one of the most serious health problems of the modern world. The number of adults and children who are overweight has risen sharply in many countries, including developing countries where hunger was once the main concern. Being overweight greatly **increases the risk of** diabetes, heart disease, joint problems and some types of cancer, and it places a heavy burden on healthcare systems.

The causes of obesity are well known. The most important is an **unhealthy diet**, especially the regular consumption of fast food, sweets, fried snacks and sugary drinks. Portion sizes have also grown, and many people eat more calories than their bodies need. The second major cause is a **lack of physical activity**. Many jobs now involve sitting at a desk all day, people travel by car or motorbike instead of walking, and leisure time is often spent watching television or using smartphones.

Children are particularly affected. Many children spend hours playing video games instead of playing outside, and they are surrounded by advertisements for snacks and soft drinks. In some cities, there are few safe parks or playgrounds, so parents prefer to keep their children indoors. Busy parents may also buy ready-made food because they have little time to cook.

There is a long debate about who is responsible for this problem. Some people argue that individuals are responsible for their own weight. Adults choose what they eat and whether they exercise, and parents decide what their children eat at home. From this point of view, people should **take responsibility for their own health**, and the government should not interfere in private choices.

Others argue that the government must play a larger role. Individuals make their choices within an environment that is shaped by powerful food companies, advertising, city planning and prices. When unhealthy food is cheaper and more heavily advertised than healthy food, and when there are no parks or safe footpaths, it is difficult for people to make healthy choices. Moreover, obesity affects the whole of society because treating obesity-related diseases costs public healthcare systems a great deal of money.

In reality, both sides have a role. Governments can **create a healthier environment** by taxing sugary drinks, controlling junk-food advertising to children, requiring clear food labels, building parks and cycle lanes, and improving school meals. Schools can provide daily physical education and teach children about nutrition. Parents can cook healthy meals, limit screen time and set a good example by being active themselves. Individuals, finally, must make use of these opportunities.

In conclusion, obesity is caused mainly by poor diet and inactivity, which are encouraged by modern lifestyles. Tackling it requires **shared responsibility**: governments should make healthy choices easy and affordable, while families and individuals should make those choices every day.

### Exercise, Walking and the Sedentary Lifestyle

Human bodies are designed to move, yet modern life encourages people to sit for most of the day. Many people sit at a desk for eight hours at work, sit in a car or bus to travel, and then sit in front of a television or computer in the evening. This **sedentary lifestyle** is one of the main reasons for rising levels of obesity, heart disease and other health problems.

There are several reasons why people exercise less than in the past. Technology has removed much of the physical effort from daily life: machines do household tasks, lifts replace stairs, and online shopping means that people do not even need to walk to the shops. Many people also drive or take a rickshaw even for very short journeys. In large cities, walking can be unpleasant or dangerous because of heavy traffic, air pollution, broken footpaths and hot weather. Long working hours also leave people feeling **too tired or too busy** to exercise.

The benefits of regular exercise are enormous. Physical activity helps people to **maintain a healthy weight**, strengthens the heart and lungs, and reduces the risk of diabetes, high blood pressure and some cancers. It also improves mental health: exercise reduces stress, improves mood and helps people sleep better. Even simple activities, such as walking for thirty minutes a day, can make a significant difference. For older people, regular movement helps them stay independent for longer.

Some people claim that daily exercise is the best way to improve health. Exercise is certainly very important, but on its own it is not enough. A person who exercises every day but eats a poor diet, smokes, sleeps badly or suffers from constant stress will not be completely healthy. **A balanced approach**, combining exercise with good nutrition, enough sleep and regular medical check-ups, is the most effective way to stay healthy.

There are many ways to encourage people to be more active. Governments and city planners can build **safe footpaths, cycle lanes and parks**, so that walking and cycling become pleasant and practical. Public transport also encourages walking, because people walk to and from stations. Employers can provide exercise facilities, flexible breaks or "walking meetings", and schools can ensure that children have daily physical activity. Some workplaces encourage staff to use the stairs or stand up regularly during the day.

Individuals can also build exercise into their daily routines without joining a gym. They can walk or cycle for short journeys, take the stairs, play sports with friends or do simple exercises at home. Fitness apps and smartwatches can help people track their activity and set goals.

In conclusion, a lack of exercise is a major cause of modern health problems. Although exercise alone cannot guarantee good health, it is one of the most powerful and affordable ways to improve it. Making physical activity a **natural part of daily life** should be a priority for individuals, employers and governments.

### Mental Health and Stress

For a long time, health was seen mainly in physical terms. Today, however, there is growing recognition that **mental health** is just as important as physical health. Stress, anxiety and depression are common in modern societies, affecting students, workers, parents and elderly people alike. Mental health problems can damage relationships, reduce people's ability to work and study, and in serious cases put lives at risk.

There are many causes of mental health problems in modern life. **Work-related stress** is one of the most common. Long working hours, job insecurity, heavy workloads and the expectation to answer emails at any time can leave people exhausted. Students face intense pressure to achieve high exam results, often from their parents and teachers. Financial worries, such as debt or unemployment, also cause great anxiety.

Social media has added a new source of pressure, especially for young people. Constant comparison with the carefully edited lives of others can lead to low self-esteem and feelings of inadequacy. Cyberbullying can make young people feel unsafe even at home. At the same time, many people are more isolated than in the past. Families are smaller, relatives may live far away, and elderly people may live alone, which can cause loneliness.

One of the biggest problems is **the stigma attached to mental illness**. In many societies, people who suffer from depression or anxiety are afraid to talk about it, because they worry that others will see them as weak or "mad". As a result, many people never seek help, and their problems become worse. In addition, there are often very few trained counsellors and psychiatrists, especially outside big cities, and treatment can be expensive.

Poor mental health also affects physical health and the economy. People who suffer from long-term stress are more likely to sleep badly, eat unhealthily, smoke or drink alcohol, and they may develop high blood pressure or heart problems. Employees with untreated depression often find it hard to concentrate, take more sick days and may eventually lose their jobs, which harms both their families and their employers.

Fortunately, there are many ways to improve mental health. Governments can **raise awareness** through public campaigns that encourage people to talk openly about their feelings, and they can include mental health services in public healthcare. Schools can teach students how to manage stress and provide counsellors whom students can talk to in confidence. Employers can reduce unnecessary overtime, respect employees' free time and offer support to staff who are struggling.

Individuals can also protect their mental health through healthy habits. Regular exercise, enough sleep, a balanced diet and time spent in nature all help to reduce stress. Limiting time on social media, spending time with friends and family and talking to someone trusted when problems arise are simple but powerful steps.

In conclusion, mental health problems are a growing concern in modern societies, caused by work pressure, academic stress, social media and loneliness. By reducing stigma, improving services and building **a healthy work–life balance**, societies can help people to lead happier and more productive lives.

### Prevention versus Treatment: Government Health Spending

Every government has to decide how to spend its limited healthcare budget. One of the most important choices is between **prevention**, which means stopping people from becoming ill in the first place, and **treatment**, which means caring for people after they become ill. Some people argue that prevention is more important and that government funding should reflect this, while others believe that treating the sick must always come first.

There are strong arguments for prevention. First, it is usually **far cheaper** to prevent a disease than to treat it. A vaccination programme, for example, costs much less than treating thousands of patients with a serious infectious disease. Similarly, encouraging people to stop smoking, eat healthily and exercise can reduce the number of people who develop lung cancer, diabetes and heart disease, which are extremely expensive to treat.

Second, prevention improves people's **quality of life**. A person who never develops diabetes avoids years of medication, hospital visits and complications. Regular health check-ups, such as blood-pressure and blood-sugar tests, can identify problems early, when they are easier to manage. Prevention also benefits the economy, because healthy people can work, care for their families and contribute to society.

Prevention includes many different activities: vaccinations, clean water and sanitation, health education in schools, public campaigns against smoking and drug use, and laws on food safety and road safety. Doctors also play an important role by **advising patients** on how to improve their lifestyle, although they often have very little time during appointments.

However, treatment cannot be neglected. No matter how much money is spent on prevention, people will still have accidents, catch infections, develop genetic illnesses and grow old. These patients need hospitals, doctors, nurses, medicines and equipment. If treatment services are reduced, patients may face long waiting lists or be unable to get urgent care, which can cost lives. Many diseases, such as some types of cancer, cannot always be prevented, even with a healthy lifestyle.

Another difficulty is that the results of prevention are slow and less visible. A new hospital or a successful operation is easy to see, but the benefits of a health campaign may only appear after many years. As a result, politicians may prefer to spend money on treatment, which brings faster public approval.

The most sensible approach is to **strike a balance**. Governments should continue to fund high-quality treatment for those who need it, but gradually increase spending on prevention, because this will reduce the demand for treatment in the long term. In other words, prevention is an investment that makes treatment more affordable.

In conclusion, prevention is generally more cost-effective than treatment and leads to a healthier population. Although treatment will always be necessary, governments should **give greater priority to prevention** in their health budgets, so that fewer people become ill in the first place.

### Free Healthcare: Who Should Pay?

Health is one of the most important things in a person's life, yet medical care can be extremely expensive. In some countries, healthcare is free for all citizens and paid for through taxes. In others, people must pay for treatment themselves or buy health insurance. A common IELTS question is whether healthcare should be free for everyone, or whether individuals should pay because their health is their own responsibility.

Supporters of free healthcare argue that health is a **basic human right**. Illness can happen to anyone, rich or poor, and no one should be denied treatment because they cannot afford it. Without free healthcare, poor families may delay seeing a doctor until a problem becomes serious, or they may have to sell land, borrow money or take children out of school to pay medical bills. In many developing countries, a single serious illness can push a family into **long-term poverty**.

Free healthcare also benefits society as a whole. When people can see a doctor early, infectious diseases are controlled before they spread, and chronic illnesses are managed before they become expensive emergencies. A healthy population is also more productive, which supports economic growth. In addition, free healthcare creates a sense of fairness and social solidarity, because everyone contributes and everyone is protected.

On the other hand, critics argue that healthcare should not be free regardless of income. Free healthcare is **extremely costly**, and as populations age and medical technology becomes more advanced, the cost continues to rise. Taxes must be high to pay for it, and public hospitals may suffer from long waiting times and overcrowding. Some also argue that when services are free, a few people may use them unnecessarily.

Another argument is that people should be **held accountable** for their own health. A person who smokes, drinks heavily or eats a very unhealthy diet is choosing to increase their risk of illness, so, critics say, it is unfair for other taxpayers to pay for the consequences. However, this view ignores the fact that many illnesses are caused by genetics, accidents, poverty, pollution or old age, not by personal choices. It would also be very difficult and unfair to decide who "deserves" treatment.

A balanced system is possible. Essential and emergency healthcare should be free or very cheap for everyone, especially for children, elderly people and low-income families. People with higher incomes could contribute more through taxes or insurance, and small fees for non-urgent services could reduce misuse. Governments can also use **health taxes** on tobacco and sugary drinks to raise money for healthcare while discouraging unhealthy habits.

In conclusion, although free healthcare is expensive, access to basic medical care should not depend on a person's income. A fair system should guarantee essential treatment for all, while asking those who can afford it to contribute more and encouraging everyone to **look after their own health**.

### Health Taxes and Laws: Sugar, Tobacco and Banning Unhealthy Food

Governments have several tools to improve public health. Besides education and healthcare services, they can use **taxes and laws** to change people's behaviour. Many countries have raised taxes on cigarettes and alcohol, banned smoking in public places and introduced taxes on sugary drinks. Some people even suggest banning fast food or convenience foods. These measures are effective in some ways but controversial in others.

The main argument for health taxes is that they **discourage unhealthy habits**. When the price of a product rises, people usually buy less of it, especially young people and those on low incomes. Higher taxes on cigarettes, for example, have been followed by a fall in smoking in many countries. Similarly, a tax on sugary drinks can encourage people to choose water or milk instead, and it can push companies to reduce the amount of sugar in their products in order to avoid the tax.

Health taxes also **raise money** that can be spent on healthcare, sports facilities or health education. In this way, the people who choose unhealthy products help to pay for the health problems they cause. Laws can also protect people who do not make the unhealthy choice; bans on smoking in restaurants, offices and public transport protect non-smokers from second-hand smoke.

However, health taxes and bans have disadvantages. Critics argue that they **limit personal freedom**, because adults should be free to decide what they eat and drink, even if their choices are not healthy. They also point out that such taxes fall most heavily on poor families, who spend a larger share of their income on food. If the price of cheap snacks and drinks rises, poorer people may pay more without changing their habits.

Complete bans are even more problematic. Banning fast food or convenience food would be very difficult to enforce, would cause many people to lose their jobs and might simply create illegal markets. Moreover, the problem is not occasional fast food, but eating too much of it. A ban would also remove a convenient option for busy people who use it responsibly.

Another limitation is that taxes alone do not change the underlying causes of unhealthy behaviour, such as lack of time, lack of knowledge, stress or the absence of affordable healthy alternatives. People who are stressed may continue to smoke or eat sweets, even if they cost more.

For these reasons, health taxes work best as **part of a wider strategy**. The money they raise can be used to make healthy food cheaper, for example by supporting fruit and vegetable markets, and to fund education campaigns. Clear food labels, limits on advertising to children and healthier school meals can support the same goal.

In conclusion, taxes on sugar and tobacco can be an effective way to reduce harmful habits and raise money for health. However, outright bans on unhealthy food are unrealistic. Governments should combine moderate taxes with education and **healthier, affordable alternatives**, so that people are encouraged, rather than forced, to make better choices.

### Children's Health, School Sport and Physical Education

Children's health has become a major concern in many countries. More children are overweight than in the past, and many spend most of their free time indoors, using screens rather than playing. Because habits formed in childhood often last a lifetime, many people believe that schools and parents must do more to keep children active and healthy.

Schools are in a unique position to influence children's health. Almost all children attend school, so school programmes can reach every child, regardless of family background. **Physical education (PE)** lessons give children the chance to exercise, learn new sports and develop coordination and fitness. Team sports, such as football and cricket, also teach cooperation, discipline and how to cope with winning and losing. For children from poor families or crowded city areas, school may be the **only place** where they can play sport safely.

Some people argue that exercise is the responsibility of parents, and that schools should not "waste" valuable time on sport when they could focus on academic subjects. It is true that parents have an important role, but many parents work long hours, cannot afford sports clubs or live in areas without parks. If schools remove sport from the curriculum, many children will hardly exercise at all. In addition, physical activity can actually **improve academic performance**, because it helps children concentrate and reduces stress.

However, increasing school sport alone is not the best way to tackle childhood obesity. Children spend only part of their day at school, and diet has a stronger effect on weight than exercise. A child who plays football for an hour at school but drinks several cans of soft drink and eats fried snacks at home will still gain weight. **A combination of solutions** is needed.

Schools can improve the food they provide by offering healthy lunches, selling fruit instead of sweets and removing sugary drinks from vending machines. They can also teach children about nutrition and basic cooking. Parents can prepare home-cooked meals, limit screen time, encourage outdoor play and act as role models by being active themselves. Governments can support these efforts by building playgrounds and parks, controlling junk-food advertising aimed at children and funding school sports facilities.

Universities are another stage of life where young people often become less active, as they study for long hours and live independently for the first time. Some suggest making sport compulsory at university; while this may be difficult, universities can certainly offer cheap and attractive sports facilities to encourage students to stay active.

In conclusion, school sport and PE play a vital role in keeping children healthy and should not be reduced. However, to **tackle childhood obesity** effectively, schools, parents and governments must work together to improve both exercise and diet.

### Technology and Health

Technology has had a powerful influence on health, both positive and negative. On the one hand, medical technology has saved millions of lives and made treatment more accurate and accessible. On the other hand, everyday technology, such as smartphones, computers and televisions, has encouraged a less active lifestyle and created new health problems.

The benefits of medical technology are clear. Modern scanners allow doctors to **diagnose diseases** at an early stage, when they are easier to treat. New medicines and vaccines have controlled diseases that once killed large numbers of people. Robotic surgery and advanced equipment make operations safer and recovery faster. Telemedicine allows patients in rural areas to consult specialists through video calls without making long and expensive journeys.

Technology can also help people manage their own health. Smartwatches and fitness apps track steps, heart rate and sleep, and they can remind users to stand up, drink water or exercise. People with diabetes can monitor their blood sugar with small devices, and online information helps people understand their conditions and treatment options. Health apps can also make it easier to book appointments and receive test results.

Technology can also make life safer for vulnerable people. For elderly people who live alone, simple devices such as emergency alarms and video-calling apps can provide security and reduce loneliness, because help or a familiar face is only a button away.

However, everyday technology has contributed to a **sedentary lifestyle**. Many people spend hours sitting in front of screens at work and at home, which increases the risk of obesity, back pain, eye strain and poor posture. Children who spend too much time on video games and social media may play outside less and sleep less, because screen use late at night disturbs sleep patterns. Social media can also harm mental health through comparison and online bullying.

Another problem is **health misinformation**. The internet contains a great deal of false or misleading health advice, from dangerous diets to unproven cures. Some people search their symptoms online and either become unnecessarily worried or try to treat themselves instead of seeing a doctor. False information about vaccines, for example, has led some parents to refuse vaccinations for their children.

The key is to use technology **wisely and in moderation**. Governments and health authorities can provide reliable health information online and work with technology companies to reduce the spread of false health claims. Schools and parents can teach children to limit screen time and check information carefully. Individuals can use fitness apps and devices to support healthy habits, while making sure that screen time does not replace physical activity, sleep and face-to-face relationships.

In conclusion, technology is a double-edged sword for health. Medical technology has brought **remarkable benefits**, while everyday devices have encouraged inactivity and spread misinformation. If people use technology to support, rather than replace, healthy habits, its advantages can far outweigh its disadvantages.

### Ageing Population and Elderly Care

Thanks to better nutrition, hygiene and medical care, people are living much longer than in the past. In many countries, a growing proportion of the population is over sixty-five, and many people now live into their eighties and nineties. This is a great achievement, but an **ageing population** also creates new challenges for health systems, families and economies.

Older people are more likely to suffer from **chronic illnesses**, such as diabetes, heart disease, arthritis and dementia, which require long-term treatment and care. As the number of elderly people grows, the demand for doctors, nurses, medicines and care homes increases, putting pressure on healthcare budgets. At the same time, there may be fewer young workers paying taxes to support these services.

Families also face difficulties. In the past, especially in countries like Bangladesh, elderly parents usually lived with their children in large joint families, where they received care and respect. Today, many young people move to cities or abroad for work, and families are smaller, so elderly people may be left to live alone. **Loneliness and isolation** are serious problems that can affect both mental and physical health.

There is a debate about who should be responsible for caring for elderly people. Some believe it is the duty of the family, because children owe care to the parents who raised them, and elderly people are usually happier in a familiar home surrounded by loved ones. Others argue that the government should take responsibility, because many families cannot afford the cost of care or do not have the time and skills to look after someone with serious health needs.

In reality, both families and governments have a role. Families can provide love, company and daily support, while governments can provide **affordable healthcare**, pensions, home-care services and trained carers for those with complex needs. Support for family carers, such as paid leave or financial help, can make it easier for families to look after their elderly relatives.

An ageing population also affects the economy. As the number of retired people increases, governments must spend more on pensions and healthcare, while the share of working-age people who pay taxes may fall. Some countries have responded by gradually raising the retirement age or encouraging healthy older people to continue working part-time, which allows them to stay active and share their experience.

Prevention is also important. Encouraging **active ageing** — regular exercise, healthy eating, social activities and lifelong learning — can help older people stay healthy and independent for longer. Community centres, walking groups and volunteer programmes can reduce loneliness and allow elderly people to share their experience with younger generations.

In conclusion, an ageing population is a sign of progress, but it requires careful planning. By sharing responsibility between families and the state and by helping older people **stay active and connected**, societies can ensure that longer lives are also healthier and happier lives.

### Traditional Medicine and Modern Medicine

Before modern medicine developed, people in every culture relied on traditional remedies, such as herbs, special diets, massage and spiritual practices. In many parts of the world, including South Asia, traditional medicine is still widely used. Today, some people argue that because many modern health problems are caused by lifestyle and cannot be fully cured by modern medicine, a return to traditional medicine should be encouraged.

There are some reasons to value traditional medicine. Many traditional systems focus on **prevention and a balanced lifestyle**, encouraging healthy eating, rest, exercise and mental calm. This approach can be useful for modern lifestyle-related problems such as stress, poor sleep and digestive problems, which are often caused by the way people live. Some traditional remedies are cheap and easily available, which is important for poor communities with limited access to doctors. In addition, a number of modern medicines were originally developed from plants that were used in traditional healing.

Traditional practices can also provide **comfort and a sense of cultural identity**. Treatments such as massage, meditation and herbal teas may help people relax and feel cared for, and some patients feel that traditional healers spend more time listening to them than busy doctors do.

However, traditional medicine has serious limitations. Many traditional remedies have **not been scientifically tested**, so it is often unclear whether they work, what dose is safe or whether they interact dangerously with other medicines. Some products may even contain harmful substances. The greatest danger is that people may use traditional remedies instead of seeking proper medical treatment for serious illnesses, such as infections, cancer or heart disease, which can delay diagnosis until it is too late.

Modern medicine, by contrast, is based on scientific research and careful testing. Vaccines, antibiotics, surgery and modern drugs have dramatically increased life expectancy and saved countless lives. Although modern medicine cannot cure every lifestyle disease, it can control conditions such as diabetes and high blood pressure very effectively.

In addition, the solution to lifestyle diseases is not necessarily traditional medicine but **lifestyle change** itself: better diet, more exercise, less stress and no smoking. Both modern doctors and traditional practices can encourage these changes.

The best approach is to **combine the strengths** of both. Traditional remedies that are proven to be safe and effective can be used alongside modern treatment, under proper regulation. Governments should test and regulate traditional products, and people should be encouraged to see qualified doctors for serious health problems.

In conclusion, traditional medicine has some value, especially in promoting a balanced lifestyle, but it should not replace modern, scientifically tested medicine. A careful combination of both, supported by healthy living, is the most sensible way forward.

### Media, Advertising and Body Image

The media and advertising have a strong influence on how people eat, what they buy and how they see their own bodies. Television, magazines, social media and advertisements constantly show images of food, fashion and "perfect" bodies. While the media can spread useful health information, it can also encourage unhealthy habits and create unrealistic expectations.

Food advertising is a major concern. Fast food companies and producers of sweets and soft drinks spend huge amounts of money on advertising, often targeting children with cartoons, toys and celebrities. Children are easily influenced and often ask their parents to buy the products they see on television or online. As a result, **junk-food advertising** can contribute to poor diets and childhood obesity.

The media's image of the "ideal body" is another problem. For decades, magazines, films and advertisements have promoted very thin women and extremely muscular men as the standard of beauty. Many of these images are digitally edited and do not reflect real people. When young people constantly compare themselves with these images, they may develop **low self-esteem** and dissatisfaction with their bodies. In serious cases, this can lead to eating disorders, such as starving oneself or extreme dieting, which are dangerous to both physical and mental health.

Social media has intensified these effects. Influencers share carefully selected photos and promote diets, supplements and beauty products, sometimes without any medical knowledge. Teenagers may feel pressure to look a certain way in order to gain "likes" and followers.

Children are also exposed to advertising through games, videos and social media, where adverts are often mixed with entertainment and are harder to recognise. A popular online personality who drinks a particular soft drink in a video may influence young viewers more than a traditional television advertisement.

However, the media can also be a **powerful force for good**. Public health campaigns on television and social media can raise awareness about the dangers of smoking, the importance of exercise and the signs of mental illness. Documentaries and news programmes can inform people about healthy eating. Some brands and celebrities now promote more realistic and diverse body types, which can help people feel more confident about their appearance.

Several measures can reduce the negative effects. Governments can **restrict advertising** of unhealthy food to children, for example by banning such adverts during children's television programmes. Advertisers can be required to state when images have been digitally altered. Schools can teach **media literacy**, helping students understand how advertising works and why online images are often unrealistic. Parents can talk to their children about body image and set a positive example.

In conclusion, the media and advertising can damage health by promoting junk food and unrealistic body images, especially among young people. However, with sensible regulation, education and responsible content, the media can also become an effective tool for **promoting healthy lifestyles**.

| health tax / sugar tax (n) | স্বাস্থ্য কর / চিনি কর | **impose / introduce** a sugar tax | Some countries have introduced a sugar tax on soft drinks. |
| discourage (v) | নিরুৎসাহিত করা | **discourage** people **from** smoking | High prices discourage people from smoking. |
| take responsibility for | দায়িত্ব নেওয়া | **take responsibility for** their own health | Adults should take responsibility for their own health. |

## Part 5 — Sentence Structure Toolkit {: .part}

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Paragraph mix formula:** প্রতিটি body paragraph-এ রাখার চেষ্টা করো → **1টি long simple + 1–2টি compound + 2–3টি complex + 1টি compound-complex** sentence। 5–6টি বাক্য × গড়ে 20 শব্দ = **100–120 words**।

</div>

#### 1. Long simple sentence (rich noun phrase)

**Formula:** [long noun phrase] + verb + object
*একটাই clause, কিন্তু subject-টা বড় ও তথ্যপূর্ণ।*

- The regular consumption of fast food and sugary drinks **increases** the risk of obesity and diabetes.
- A lack of affordable healthcare in rural areas **forces** many poor families to borrow money.

#### 2. Compound with *and / but / so / yet*

- Fast food is cheap and convenient, **but** it is usually high in fat and salt.
- Many people work long hours, **so** they have little time to exercise.

#### 3. Compound with semicolon + linker

- Prevention is cheaper than treatment**; therefore,** governments should invest more in it.
- Free healthcare is expensive**; however,** it protects poor families from debt.

#### 4. Complex — concession (*Although / Even though / While*)

- **Although** exercise is important, it cannot replace a healthy diet.
- **Even though** sugar taxes are unpopular, they encourage companies to make healthier drinks.

#### 5. Complex — reason (*Because / Since / As*)

- **Since** many illnesses can be prevented, health education should start in primary school.
- **Because** office workers sit all day, they are more likely to suffer from back pain.

#### 6. Complex — condition (*If / Unless / Provided that*)

- **If** children eat junk food every day, they **are likely to** become overweight adults.
- **Unless** mental illness is discussed openly, many people **will** never seek help.

#### 7. Relative clause (*which / who / where / whose*)

- People **who** exercise regularly tend to sleep better and feel less stressed.
- Many soft drinks contain large amounts of sugar, **which** damages teeth and causes weight gain.

#### 8. Compound-complex

**Formula:** Although/Because/If + clause, Clause A, and/but Clause B

- **Although** fast food saves time, it is often unhealthy, **and** it can replace family meals.
- **If** governments build more parks, people will walk more, **and** their health will improve.

#### 9. Participle phrase

- **Working** at a desk for eight hours, many employees hardly move during the day.
- **Encouraged** by influencers, some teenagers follow dangerous diets.

#### 10. *Not only … but also …*

- Regular exercise **not only** strengthens the heart **but also** reduces stress.
- A sugar tax **not only** discourages unhealthy habits **but also** raises money for hospitals.

#### 11. *The more …, the more …*

- **The more** time people spend in front of screens, **the less** active they become.
- **The earlier** a disease is detected, **the easier** it is to treat.

#### 12. Cleft sentence (emphasis)

- **It is** poor diet, rather than lack of exercise, **that** is the main cause of obesity.
- **What** many patients need most **is** clear advice from their doctor.

#### 13. Impersonal passive

- **It is widely believed that** prevention is better than cure.
- **It is often argued that** health is a personal responsibility.

#### 14. Contrasting views

- **While some people argue that** individuals are responsible for their health, **others believe that** governments must play a larger role.
- **While** modern medicine is scientifically tested, **traditional remedies** often are not.

#### 15. Result / consequence

- Many people now drive everywhere, **which means that** they walk far less than in the past.
- Healthcare costs are rising. **As a result,** governments are looking for ways to prevent illness.

আধুনিক জীবনের গতি বাড়ার সাথে সাথে এখন আরও বেশি মানুষ মূল খাবারের জন্য fast food-এর ওপর নির্ভর করছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যদিও fast food ব্যস্ত মানুষের সময় ও টাকা বাঁচাতে পারে, এটি প্রায়ই তাদের স্বাস্থ্যের ক্ষতি করে এবং পরিবারের রান্নার ঐতিহ্য দুর্বল করে দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> আমার মতে, এই প্রবণতার অসুবিধাগুলো সুবিধার চেয়ে বেশি। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — সুবিধা (120–135 words)

এতে কোনো সন্দেহ নেই যে fast food ব্যস্ত মানুষদের জন্য উল্লেখযোগ্য সুবিধা বয়ে আনে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> প্রথমত, fast food দ্রুত ও সুবিধাজনক (quick and convenient), যা দীর্ঘ সময় কাজ করা মানুষদের জন্য গুরুত্বপূর্ণ। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এর কারণ হলো, delivery app-এর মাধ্যমে একটি গরম খাবার অর্ডার করা যায় এবং তা কয়েক মিনিটেই পৌঁছে যায়, তাই বাজার করা বা রান্নায় কোনো সময় খরচ হয় না। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex · Passive</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, যে কর্মী দেরিতে অফিস থেকে বের হন, তিনি বাড়ি ফেরার পথেই রাতের খাবার কিনে নিতে পারেন, যা তার সময় ও শক্তি বাঁচায়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এছাড়া, fast food শুধু সস্তাই নয়, শহর ও নগরের প্রায় প্রতিটি রাস্তার মোড়েই পাওয়া যায়। <span class="tag">Simple</span> এই কম দামের ফলে শিক্ষার্থী ও নিম্ন আয়ের কর্মীরা পেট ভরানো একটি খাবার কিনতে পারে, যা তাদের দৈনন্দিন খরচ কমায়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> সব মিলিয়ে, fast food ব্যস্ত মানুষদের দৈনন্দিন জীবন আরও সুবিধাজনক ও সাশ্রয়ী করে তোলে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — অসুবিধা (120–135 words)

অন্যদিকে, এটি অস্বীকার করা যায় না যে fast food-এর কিছু অসুবিধাও আছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> প্রথমত, অনেক মানুষ স্বাস্থ্য সমস্যায় ভোগে, কারণ fast food ও প্রক্রিয়াজাত খাবারে চর্বি, চিনি ও লবণ বেশি থাকে (high in fat, sugar and salt)। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যথেষ্ট ভিটামিন ও আঁশ ছাড়া তাদের ওজন প্রায়ই বেড়ে যায়; ফলে ডায়াবেটিস ও হৃদরোগের ঝুঁকিও বাড়ে। <span class="tag">Compound</span> যেমন, যে শিশু প্রতিদিন fried chicken খায়, সে অতিরিক্ত ওজনের হয়ে যেতে পারে, যা সারাজীবন তার স্বাস্থ্যের ক্ষতি করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> দ্বিতীয়ত, ঘরে রান্না কমে যাওয়া একটি গুরুতর সমস্যা হতে পারে, কারণ অনেক ব্যস্ত পরিবার একসাথে রান্না করা ও খাওয়া বন্ধ করে দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> বাবা-মা যদি খুব কমই রান্না করেন, তাহলে ঐতিহ্যবাহী রান্নার দক্ষতা পরবর্তী প্রজন্মের কাছে পৌঁছাবে না, এবং ফলস্বরূপ স্থানীয় খাদ্যসংস্কৃতি হারিয়ে যেতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> যদিও কেউ কেউ পরিমিতভাবে (in moderation) fast food উপভোগ করতে পারেন, এই স্বাস্থ্য ও পারিবারিক সমস্যাগুলো প্রায়ই দীর্ঘস্থায়ী ক্ষতি করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Conclusion (35–45 words)

পরিশেষে, fast food-এর ওপর নির্ভর করা এখন একটি সাধারণ আধুনিক অভ্যাসে পরিণত হয়েছে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> যদিও (While) কিনে আনা খাবার দ্রুত ও সাশ্রয়ী হতে পারে, এগুলো প্রায়ই অসুস্থতার কারণ হয় এবং পরিবারের একসাথে রান্না করার সময় কমিয়ে দেয়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> তাই আমি বিশ্বাস করি যে অসুবিধাগুলো সুবিধার চেয়ে বেশি। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

### Essay 3 — Cause and Solution

*Source: IELTS Liz · HEA-02 · Common topic ★ Fast food & exercise*

#### Introduction (30–40 words)

অতিরিক্ত ওজনের কারণে এখন ক্রমবর্ধমান সংখ্যক মানুষ গুরুতর স্বাস্থ্যঝুঁকির মুখে পড়ছে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এর মূল কারণ অস্বাস্থ্যকর খাদ্যাভ্যাস ও ব্যায়ামের অভাব, কিন্তু স্বাস্থ্যকর খাদ্যনীতি ও আরও সক্রিয় দৈনন্দিন রুটিন এই সমস্যার মোকাবিলা করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound</span>

#### Body Paragraph 1 — 2টি কারণ (90–120 words)

এই প্রবণতার একটি প্রধান কারণ হলো অস্বাস্থ্যকর খাদ্যাভ্যাস, বিশেষ করে নিয়মিত fast food, মিষ্টি, ভাজাপোড়া ও চিনিযুক্ত পানীয় খাওয়া। <span class="tag">Simple</span> খাবারের পরিমাণও (portion sizes) বেড়েছে, এবং অনেকে শরীরের প্রয়োজনের চেয়ে বেশি ক্যালরি গ্রহণ করে। <span class="tag">Compound</span> উদাহরণস্বরূপ, যে অফিসকর্মী প্রতিদিন দুপুরে একটি burger ও একটি চিনিযুক্ত পানীয় কেনেন, তিনি অতিরিক্ত ক্যালরি গ্রহণ করতে পারেন, এবং এই বাড়তি শক্তি চর্বি হিসেবে জমা হয়। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> আরেকটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ কারণ হলো শারীরিক পরিশ্রমের অভাব। <span class="tag">Simple</span> এখন অনেক চাকরিতে সারাদিন টেবিলে বসে কাজ করতে হয়, মানুষ হাঁটার বদলে গাড়ি বা মোটরসাইকেলে চলাফেরা করে, এবং অবসর সময় প্রায়ই টেলিভিশন দেখে বা smartphone ব্যবহার করে কাটে। <span class="tag">Compound</span> ফলে অনেক মানুষ যত ক্যালরি গ্রহণ করে, তার চেয়ে অনেক কম খরচ করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span>

#### Body Paragraph 2 — 2টি সমাধান (90–120 words)

সবচেয়ে কার্যকর সমাধান হলো, সরকার স্বাস্থ্যকর খাবারকে বেছে নেওয়ার সহজ বিকল্পে পরিণত করবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> সরকার যদি চিনিযুক্ত পানীয়ের ওপর কর বসায় এবং খাবারের গায়ে স্পষ্ট লেবেল (food labels) বাধ্যতামূলক করে, তাহলে মানুষ অস্বাস্থ্যকর পণ্য কম কিনবে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> যেমন, যে অফিসকর্মী স্পষ্ট লেবেলযুক্ত একটি পানীয়ের গায়ে চিনির পরিমাণ দেখেন, তিনি হয়তো এর বদলে পানি বেছে নেবেন, যা তার দৈনিক ক্যালরি কমায়। <span class="tag">Complex</span> এছাড়া, সরকার ও ব্যক্তি উভয়েই ব্যায়ামকে দৈনন্দিন জীবনের অংশ করে তুলতে পারে। <span class="tag">Simple</span> শহরে যখন পার্ক ও সাইকেল লেন তৈরি হয়, তখন মানুষ বেশি হাঁটতে বা সাইকেল চালাতে পারে, এবং দিনে ত্রিশ মিনিট হাঁটার মতো সহজ কাজও বড় পার্থক্য তৈরি করতে পারে। <span class="tag">Compound-Complex</span> এই পদক্ষেপগুলো একসাথে মানুষকে স্বাস্থ্যকর ওজন বজায় রাখতে সাহায্য করবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

#### Conclusion (30–40 words)

পরিশেষে, আরও বেশি মানুষ অতিরিক্ত ওজনের শিকার হচ্ছে, কারণ তারা খুব বেশি ক্যালরি গ্রহণ করে এবং খুব কম নড়াচড়া করে। <span class="tag">Complex</span> খাবার নিয়ে আরও স্পষ্ট নিয়ম এবং দৈনন্দিন শারীরিক কাজের বেশি সুযোগ মানুষকে সুস্থ থাকতে সাহায্য করবে। <span class="tag">Simple</span>

</div>

<div class="tip" markdown="1">

**Common mistakes in health essays (এগুলো এড়িয়ে চলো):**

- ❌ *Health is wealth, so everyone should be healthy.* (memorised cliché) → ✅ *Good health allows people to work, study and care for their families.*
- ❌ *Fast foods are unhealthy.* → ✅ **Fast food is** unhealthy. (usually uncountable)
- ❌ *Peoples are suffering from obesity.* → ✅ **People are** suffering from obesity.
- ❌ *Doctors give advices to patients.* → ✅ Doctors give **advice** to patients.
- ❌ *It is harmful for health.* → ✅ It is **harmful to** health / **bad for** health.
- ❌ *Do exercise* → ✅ **take** exercise / **do** exercise **regularly** / **exercise** (verb)
- **"healthy / unhealthy" প্রতিশব্দ:** nutritious, balanced, fit, in good shape / harmful, damaging, poor (diet), high in sugar

</div>

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- "4 levels of responsibility" (Part 1) ব্যবহার করো: কোন level-গুলো ব্যক্তির পক্ষে, কোনগুলো সরকারের পক্ষে?
- ব্যক্তির পক্ষে 2টি যুক্তি কী (খাদ্য ও ব্যায়াম ব্যক্তিগত পছন্দ, সরকার সবার ঘরে ঢুকতে পারে না)?

### Homework 2 — Cause–Solution

<div class="question">Walking is known to be beneficial for health, and yet fewer and fewer people are walking these days. What are the reasons for this? What can be done to tackle this problem?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · HEA-09 · ★ exercise & modern lifestyle*

**Essay type:** Cause and Solution · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 260–300 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 30–40 · BP1 2 causes/problems 90–120 · BP2 2 solutions 90–120 · Conclusion 30–40<br>
**Use ideas from:** Exercise, Walking & the Sedentary Lifestyle; Technology & Health; Cities (Chapter 03)

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- Cause + Solution: BP1-এ 2টি কারণ (শহরের রাস্তা অনিরাপদ, যেমন ভাঙা ফুটপাত ও যানজট; প্রযুক্তি শারীরিক পরিশ্রম কমায়) এবং একটি বাস্তব উদাহরণ।
- BP2 (Mirror rule): প্রতিটি সমাধান একটি কারণের (ভাঙা ফুটপাত ↔ নিরাপদ ফুটপাত)। প্রতিটিতে WHO + WHAT + HOW।
- Introduction: This is mainly because C1 and C2, but S1 and S2 can tackle this problem.
- মতামত বা "This essay will…" নয়; উপসংহারে নতুন idea নয়।

</div>

**Try to use:** a sedentary lifestyle · rely on cars and rickshaws · heavy traffic and air pollution · broken footpaths · build safe footpaths · public transport · build exercise into daily routines · maintain a healthy weight

**Sentence target:** use *The more…, the less…* once and *not only… but also…* once.

### Homework 3 — Agree / Disagree

<div class="question">Putting a tax on sugar is a good way to reduce the number of people incurring health problems from eating too many sugary foods and drinks. Do you agree?</div>

*Source: IELTS Liz · FOO-07*

**Essay type:** Agree / Disagree — one clear side · **Time:** 40 minutes · **Length:** 270–345 words<br>
**Word plan:** Intro 35–50 · BP1 Idea 1 100–120 · BP2 Idea 2 100–120 · Conclusion 35–55<br>
**Use ideas from:** Health Taxes & Laws; Obesity & Responsibility; Fast Food

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- একটিই পক্ষ নাও: চিনি কর একটি ভালো উপায় — সম্পূর্ণ একমত বা সম্পূর্ণ দ্বিমত। "ভালো, কিন্তু একমাত্র উপায় নয়" লিখবে না।
- একমত হলে 2টি idea: দাম বাড়লে মানুষ কম কেনে; কোম্পানি চিনি কমায় বা করের টাকা স্বাস্থ্যখাতে যায়। প্রতিটির একটি উদাহরণ।
- বিপক্ষের যুক্তি (দরিদ্রদের ওপর চাপ, ব্যক্তিস্বাধীনতা) BP2-এর Response বাক্যে Although অংশে রাখো।
- উপসংহারে নতুন পদক্ষেপ (food labels, শিক্ষা) যোগ করবে না: In conclusion, I firmly believe that… + দুটি idea নতুন শব্দে।

</div>

**Try to use:** impose a sugar tax · discourage unhealthy habits · reduce the sugar content · raise money for healthcare · limit personal freedom · fall most heavily on the poor · part of a wider strategy · affordable healthy alternatives

**Sentence target:** use *Although…, …* twice and one relative clause with *which*.

</div>

<div class="bn" markdown="1">

#### ভাবনার প্রশ্ন (Thinking prompts)

- প্রশ্নে দুটি দাবি একসাথে আছে, কিন্তু উত্তর একটিই: সম্পূর্ণ একমত বা সম্পূর্ণ দ্বিমত (দ্বিমত সহজ, কারণ সব রোগ মানুষের নিয়ন্ত্রণে নয়)।
