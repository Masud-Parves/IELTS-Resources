# IELTS Speaking: My Band 6.5 Guide (A to Z)

> **Target:** band 6.5 minimum (7 if possible)
> **Main problems:** fluency (long pauses), tense mistakes, subject–verb agreement, missing articles, missing -s/-es, he/she mix-ups
> **Plan:** 4 weeks of training → mock tests → final exam (December 2026)
> **Daily time:** about 45 minutes

---

## 0. Know Your Target

You get four marks, and **each one is 25% of your score**:

| Criterion | What the examiner checks | My focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluency & Coherence (FC)** | Can I keep talking without long pauses? Is my answer connected? | ⭐ **Biggest weak point** |
| **Lexical Resource (LR)** | Do I have enough words for every topic, including some less common ones? | Topic vocabulary |
| **Grammatical Range & Accuracy (GRA)** | Do I mix simple and complex sentences? How many mistakes do I make? | Tense, S–V agreement, articles, -s/-es, he/she |
| **Pronunciation (P)** | Is my speech easy to understand? (Clarity matters, not a British accent.) | Keep it clear |

**Band 6.5 = an average like 7 + 6 + 7 + 6.**

| Criterion | Band 6 sounds like… | Band 7 sounds like… |
|---|---|---|
| FC | Speaks at length but **sometimes stops**, repeats, self-corrects a lot | Speaks at length **without noticeable effort**; uses linking words naturally |
| LR | Enough words to discuss topics; meaning is clear | Uses **some less common words and collocations**; can paraphrase |
| GRA | Mix of simple and complex sentences, but **frequent errors** in complex ones | **Many error-free sentences**; some mistakes still OK |
| P | Mostly clear; some words are hard to follow | Easy to understand all the time; some intonation |

> **The plan:** turn **FC** and **GRA** into 7s. Grammar here does not mean fancy structures. It means **stopping the same five small mistakes** that come back again and again.

## 1. The Test in 3 Parts

| Part | Time | What happens | What wins marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Part 1** | 4–5 min | Questions about you: home, work/study, hobbies, daily life | Natural answers of **2–3 sentences**, no one-word replies |
| **Part 2** | 1 min prep + **2 min talk** | A cue card ("Describe a…"). The examiner stops you at 2 min. | **Keep talking until the examiner stops you.** Use the right tense. |
| **Part 3** | 4–5 min | Abstract questions linked to Part 2 (society, the future, comparisons) | Opinion + reason + example; this is where 6.5 vs 7 is decided |

## 3. Focus 1: Fluency (My Biggest Weak Point)

Fluency is **not** speaking fast. It is **not stopping**. Examiners punish long silences much more than small mistakes.

### 3.1 The golden rule
> **Don't stop to fix small mistakes.** If you notice a mistake, correct it once quickly ("…he go, sorry, he goes…") and keep going. If you're not sure, just carry on.

Train accuracy in **drills** (Sections 4–6). In the exam, **keep talking**.

### 3.2 Answer extenders: never give one-line answers

**Part 1: A + R + E (Answer + Reason + Example/Extra)**

> **Q:** Do you like cooking?
> ❌ *"Yes, I like."* (too short, and wrong grammar)
> ✅ *"Yes, I **do**, actually. I find it quite relaxing after a long day. For example, on Fridays I usually cook biryani for my family, and they **love** it."*

**Part 3: O + R + E + C (Opinion + Reason + Example + Contrast)**

> **Q:** Do you think children spend too much time on phones?
> ✅ *"**I'd say** yes, in most cases. **The main reason is that** phones are designed to be addictive, so kids find it hard to stop. **For instance,** my nephew **spends** almost four hours a day on YouTube. **Having said that,** phones **can** be useful for learning if parents **set** some limits."*

### 3.3 Thinking-time phrases (use these instead of silence)

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Need a second to think | *"Well, let me think…"* / *"That's an interesting question…"* |
| Never thought about it | *"I've never really thought about that, but I guess…"* |
| Hard question | *"That's a tricky one. I suppose it depends on…"* |
| Forgot a word | *"…the thing you use to… what's it called… the remote control."* (Explaining around a word is a **skill**, not a failure.) |
| Didn't understand | *"Sorry, could you repeat the question, please?"* (OK once or twice) |

> Don't use the same filler every time. "Actually" and "basically" in every sentence sound robotic.

### 3.4 Linking words (these connect your ideas, which is the "Coherence" half of FC)

| Job | Words |
|---|---|
| Add | *also, on top of that, what's more, another thing is* |
| Reason | *because, since, the main reason is that, that's why* |
| Example | *for example, for instance, like, such as* |
| Contrast | *but, although, however, having said that, on the other hand* |
| Result | *so, as a result, that's why* |
| Time (Part 2) | *at first, after that, then, eventually, in the end* |
| Opinion | *I'd say, personally, in my view, I think, it seems to me* |

**Linkers in a Part 2 answer (asked 7 Nov).** Too few linkers sound robotic, but **too many sound memorised**. If almost every sentence starts with *Moreover / On top of that / More importantly*, the examiner thinks you are reciting. Natural speakers mostly join ideas *inside* the sentence with **and, but, so, because, also**.

**The simple rule:** use a linker only when it does a real job.

| Job | Natural spoken linker |
|---|---|
| Start the talk | **Well,** I would like to talk about… |
| Move to a new prompt (only 1–2 per answer) | **As for his achievements,** … |
| Add one more point (once) | **Besides,** … or just use **also** inside the sentence: *He is also…* |
| Contrast | **but** / **Although** … |
| Example | **For example,** … |
| Personal comment | **Actually,** … / **To be honest,** … |
| Link to the "why" | **That is why** I'm a big fan of his. |

**Test:** remove the linker. If the meaning is still clear and the sentence still flows, you don't need it.

**Avoid in Speaking:** *Moreover, Furthermore, In addition, More importantly, Overall*. These are essay words, and too many of them sound memorised.

**About 6–8 linkers in a 2-minute answer is enough,** plus your normal *and / but / so / also*.

> Model answers: cards 105 and 117 in the Person Topics book, with the linkers in **bold**.

### 3.5 Daily fluency drills (pick 2 per day)

| Drill | How | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **1-minute non-stop** | Pick any topic (my phone, rain, breakfast). Talk for 60 seconds **without stopping**. Count your pauses. Goal: 0 pauses over 2 seconds. | 5 min |
| **4-3-2** | Tell the same Part 2 story in **4 minutes**, then **3**, then **2**. Each round gets smoother and faster. | 10 min |
| **Shadowing** | Play 1–2 minutes of Listening audio you've already done (Cambridge Part 1 or 3). Repeat **just behind** the speaker, copying rhythm and stress. (This also helps your Listening.) | 10 min |
| **Narrate your day** | While walking or cooking, describe what you're doing in English, in your head or out loud. "Now I'm cutting onions, which always **makes** me cry…" | any time |
| **Re-record** | Record an answer, listen, note the mistakes, record it **again**. The second version is always better, and that's how you improve. | 10 min |

### 3.6 Measuring my fluency (speed + pauses)

> There's **no official speed** in the band descriptors, and examiners don't count words. They listen for **effort, hesitation and connection**. These numbers are rough benchmarks for **checking myself**.

**Speed benchmarks**

| Speed (words/min) | How it usually sounds |
|---|---|
| under 80 | Slow and hesitant, which usually keeps fluency at band 5 |
| 80–100 | A bit slow; OK if there are very few long pauses |
| **100–140** | ✅ **Target zone for band 6.5–7** |
| 130–160 | Normal native-speaker conversation |
| 170+ | Too fast for me now: more mistakes and unclear words, which hurts Pronunciation |

**Minimum: about 100 words/min (200+ words in Part 2).** Comfortable target: **110–130 words/min (220–260 words in 2 minutes)**.

**What matters more than speed**

| Measure | How to count | Band 6.5 target |
|---|---|---|
| **Long pauses** (2–3+ sec) | Mark `...` in the transcript | **0–2 per minute** |
| **Where I pause** | Between ideas is fine. In the middle of a phrase is bad: *"I went to... the... market"* | Only between sentences or ideas |
| **Run length** (words between pauses) | Average across the answer | **6+ words** |
| **Restarts** (*"I go, I went…"*) | Count them | **2 or fewer per minute** |
| **Part 2 length** | Timer | **The full 2:00** |

> **Priority:** reduce **pauses** first, and speed will come naturally. 100 words/min with no long pauses sounds more fluent than 140 with a stop every 5 seconds.

## 4. Focus 2: Correct Tenses

### 4.1 The question tells you the tense
This is the most useful trick: **listen to the verb in the question and answer in the same tense.**

| The question says… | Use | Example answer |
|---|---|---|
| *Do you…? / What do you usually…?* | **Present simple** | *I **usually go** to the gym in the evening.* |
| *Are you…ing at the moment?* | **Present continuous** | *I**'m preparing** for IELTS right now.* |
| *Did you…? / When you were a child…?* | **Past simple** | *I **played** cricket every afternoon.* |
| *Have you ever…?* | **Present perfect**, then **past simple** for details | *Yes, I**'ve been** to Cox's Bazar. I **went** there last winter.* |
| *How long have you…?* | **Present perfect (continuous)** | *I**'ve lived** here **for** ten years / **since** 2016.* |
| *Will you…? / In the future…?* | **will / going to** | *I think I**'ll** live abroad. I**'m going to** apply next year.* |
| *How has … changed?* | **Present perfect + used to** | *Dhaka **has become** much busier. People **used to** walk more.* |
| *Would you like to…?* | **would** | *Yes, I**'d love** to. It **would** be amazing.* |
| **Part 2: "Describe a time when… / a trip you took"** | **Past simple** for the whole story | *It **was** two years ago. We **took** a bus…* |
| **Part 2: "Describe a person you admire"** | **Present simple** (they're still alive and still like that) | *She **is** my aunt. She **works** as a doctor.* |

### 4.2 My common tense mistakes (Bangla → English)

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Yesterday I **go** to market.* | *Yesterday I **went** to **the** market.* | Past time → past simple |
| *I **am going** there every day.* | *I **go** there every day.* | Habits use present simple, not -ing |
| *I **have gone** there last year.* | *I **went** there last year.* | A finished time (last year, in 2020) → past simple |
| *I **am living** here **since** 5 years.* | *I**'ve lived** here **for** 5 years.* | Duration up to now → present perfect + *for* |
| *I **am knowing** him.* | *I **know** him.* | *know, like, want, need, have (own)* don't take -ing |
| *When I was child, I **like** mangoes.* | *When I was **a** child, I **liked** mangoes.* | The whole sentence stays in the past |

### 4.3 Part 2 tense check
In the **1-minute prep**, write **PAST** or **PRESENT** at the top of your notes. Look at it while you speak.

**Mixed cards (asked 8 Nov):** some cards have a past event *and* a person who still exists, for example *"a person you met once"*. Use both tenses:

| What you describe | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The **event** (the meeting, what happened) | **Past simple** | *I **met** him last month. That meeting **was** like a breath of fresh air.* |
| The **person** (what they are like now) | **Present simple** | *He **is** very sociable. He **studies** music.* |
| Your experience **up to now** (no time given) | **Present perfect** | *I **have met** him only once.* |
| Your **feeling now** | **Present simple** | *I **feel** inspired by him.* |

> **Check the meaning too:** *"he **is** like a breath of fresh air in my hectic lifestyle"* is correct grammar, but it means he is part of your daily life. For someone you met once, *"that meeting **was** like a breath of fresh air"* is more logical.

### 4.4 Talking about past habits (used to / would + more)

**used to + V1 vs would + V1**

| | **used to + V1** | **would + V1** |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated **actions** | ✅ *I **used to** play cricket every afternoon.* | ✅ *I **would** play cricket every afternoon.* |
| **States** (be, have, live, like, know, love) | ✅ *I **used to** live in Comilla.* / *I **used to** be very shy.* | ❌ *I would live in Comilla.* / ❌ *I would be very shy.* |

> **Rule:** **used to** works for everything. **would** only works for **repeated actions**, never states.
> **Order:** start with **used to** to set the past scene, then switch to **would** for the details. If you start with *would*, it can sound like "imagine" instead of "past habit".

**Common mistakes**

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *I **use to** play…* | *I **used to** play…* | Always **-d** in positive sentences (it sounds like "yoos-tuh") |
| *I didn't **used to** like vegetables.* | *I didn't **use to** like vegetables.* | After *did / didn't*, drop the -d |
| *I **was used to** play football.* | *I **used to** play football.* | *was used to* means something different (see below) |
| *I would be very naughty.* | *I **used to** be very naughty.* | *be* is a state, so *would* isn't possible |
| *He **would** suffer from a hormone problem as a child.* | *He **suffered** from a growth hormone deficiency as a child.* | An illness or condition is a **state**, not a repeated action, so use past simple (or *used to suffer*), never *would* |
| *He **used to win** several music competitions.* | *He **won** several music competitions.* | When you give a **number** (*several, three, many times*), use past simple. *Used to* is for a habit with no count (8 Nov) |

> **Don't confuse:** *I **used to** wake up early* (I did it in the past, but not now) ≠ *I**'m used to** waking up early* (I'm accustomed to it, so it isn't difficult).

**More ways to describe past habits** (don't use *used to* 10 times in one answer, but mix these)

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Past simple + frequency word | *We **often / usually / always** played cricket after school.* |
| every / most + time | ***Every Friday**, we went to the river.* / ***Most weekends**, we…* |
| spent + time + -ing | *We **spent hours chatting** about cricket.* |
| whenever | ***Whenever** it rained, we **would** sit under the tree.* |
| Hardly a day went by without… | ***Hardly a day went by without** us meeting.* ⭐ band 7 |
| It was a habit of ours to… | *It was **a habit of ours to** have tea together.* |
| got into the habit of + -ing | *We **got into the habit of** studying together.* |
| tended to + V1 | *We **tended to** stay out until it got dark.* |
| always + past continuous (fun or annoying habits) | *We **were always getting** into trouble!* |

**Is past simple + *usually* correct for a past habit?** ✅ **Yes.** It's normal and natural.

> ❌ *I usually went to our **grandparent's** house **on** summer vacation.*
> ✅ *I usually went to **my grandparents'** house **during the summer holidays**.*

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| **grandparents'** | Plural noun ending in -s → apostrophe **after** the s: *my grandparents' house, my parents' room*. (*grandparent's* = only one grandparent) |
| **during the summer holidays** | Natural British English. *on summer vacation* is American, also OK: *on my summer vacation*. |
| Where *usually / often / always* goes | **Before** the main verb: *I **usually went**…* · **After** *was/were*: *I **was usually** very excited.* |

> **"My instructor said *usually* is only for the present tense."**
> That's a **simplified exam strategy**, not a grammar rule. *Usually* + past simple is correct, standard English and is **not** marked as a mistake.
> The strategy is still good: *used to / would* make the habit **clear straight away** (past simple alone can sound like a single event), and they show the examiner **grammatical range**.

**My approach:**

| Use | How much |
|---|---|
| **used to** and **would** | Main forms for past habits (instructor's method) |
| **past simple + usually / often** | Once or twice, for variety |
| **would usually** | ✅ natural: *We **would usually** leave early in the morning.* |
| ❌ **used to usually** | Avoid: *I used to usually go…* sounds awkward, so pick one |

**⭐ Simple rule card: past habits for band 6.5**

| Form | Example | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **used to + V1** | *I **used to** live in a village.* | **Start** the story · actions **and** states |
| **would + V1** | *We **would** swim in the pond.* | Repeated **actions** only |
| **would + usually + V1** | *We **would usually** leave early.* | Actions, "most of the time" |
| **would + often + V1** | *My grandmother **would often** cook pitha for us.* | Actions, "many times" |
| *(extra)* past simple + usually/often | *I **usually went** there in summer.* | Variety, once or twice (optional) |

> **The one rule:** ***would* never goes with states** (*be, have, live, like, know, love*), so use *used to* for those. ❌ *I would be very shy.* → ✅ *I **used to** be very shy.*
>
> *"When I was a child, I **used to** spend every summer in my grandparents' village. I **used to** love it there. My cousins and I **would** swim in the pond for hours, and we **would usually** come home only when we got hungry. My grandmother **would often** make pitha for us in the evening."*

**Recommended mix for one answer:** *used to* (once, at the start) → *would* (2–3 times) → past simple + *usually/often* → *spent hours -ing* → *whenever* → one band 7 phrase.

**Keep talking for 2 minutes: the 6 layers** (when you run out of ideas, move to the next layer)

| Layer | What to add | Tense |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Who + when** | the friend's name, your age, where you lived | *used to* |
| 2. **The routine** | what you did, how often, how long | *would*, past simple + *usually* |
| 3. **The place / details** | describe the place, people, food, sounds | past simple, *there was/were*, *who/which* clauses |
| 4. **One specific memory** ⭐ | one day that stands out | past continuous + past simple: *One day, **while** we **were** sitting there, he suddenly **told** me…* |
| 5. **Feelings** | why it mattered | *Looking back, …* / *so … that …* |
| 6. **Past vs now** | do you still do it? | present simple + *not as often as we used to* |

> Layer 4 makes the biggest difference. A single story always fills 30 seconds easily.

**Model: Part 2, *Describe something you often did with a friend*** (notes: **PAST** · **HE**)

> *"I'd like to talk about something I **used to** do with my best friend, Tanvir, when we were in secondary school.*
>
> *Almost every afternoon after school, we **would** ride our bicycles to a small tea stall near the river. We **usually** ordered two cups of milk tea and some singara, and we **spent hours chatting** about cricket, exams and our future plans.*
>
> *The place itself was quite simple. **There was** a wooden bench under a huge banyan tree, and the owner, an old man **who** knew us very well, **would always** save that bench for us. **Whenever** it rained, we **would** sit there and watch the river. Honestly, **hardly a day went by without** us going there, even during exam time.*
>
> *I remember one particular day **when we were sitting** there and Tanvir suddenly **told** me that his family **was moving** to Chittagong. I was **so** shocked **that** I couldn't say anything for a few minutes.*
>
> ***Looking back**, those afternoons were probably the happiest part of my teenage years. These days, Tanvir and I don't meet **as often as we used to**, because we're both busy, but **whenever** we do meet, we still go to that tea stall. It **has become** a kind of tradition for us."*

Notice: the friend is **he** every time (*Tanvir… his family*), and the tense moves from **past habit** to **one past story** to **now**. That change in time is what gives you variety.

**Part 1 version (2–3 sentences)**

> **Q:** What did you often do with your friends as a child?
> *"We **used to** play cricket in the field behind my house. Every evening, we **would** play until it got dark, and our mothers **would** have to call us home for dinner!"*

## 5. Focus 3: Subject–Verb Agreement and -s/-es

### 5.1 The one rule
> **He / She / It / one person / one thing → verb + s.**
> *I play, you play, we play, they play* → but *he play**s**, she play**s**, my brother play**s***.

### 5.2 Spelling the -s

| Verb ends in… | Add | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| most verbs | **-s** | works, likes, plays, reads |
| -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -o | **-es** | watches, washes, misses, fixes, goes, does |
| consonant + y | **-ies** | studies, tries, carries, cries |
| vowel + y | **-s** | plays, enjoys, buys |
| *have* | → **has** | she **has** two cats |

### 5.3 Traps

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| *My mother **cook** very well.* | *My mother **cooks** very well.* | "My mother" = she |
| *He **don't** like tea.* | *He **doesn't** like tea.* | do → **does** for he/she/it |
| *Does she **likes** it?* | *Does she **like** it?* | After *does/doesn't*, no -s |
| *People **is** very friendly.* | *People **are** very friendly.* | **People** is plural |
| *Everyone **have** a phone.* | *Everyone **has** a phone.* | *Everyone, everybody, nobody* are singular |
| *One of my friends **are** a doctor.* | *One of my friends **is** a doctor.* | The subject is **one** |
| *There **is** many parks.* | *There **are** many parks.* | There **is** + one / There **are** + many |
| *My family **are/is**…* | *My family **is** quite big.* | Both are OK; **is** is safest |
| *The news **are** bad.* | *The news **is** bad.* | *News* is singular |

### 5.4 Plural -s on nouns
Bangla often marks plurals with words (অনেক বন্ধু) instead of an ending. English needs **both**:
> *many friend**s***, *two brother**s***, *a lot of student**s***, *one of my **cousins***
> ❌ *many friend*, *few people**s*** (people is already plural)

## 6. Focus 4: Articles (a / an / the) and He/She

### 6.1 Articles: the 5 rules that cover 90%
Bangla has no articles, so you have to **add** them on purpose.

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| **a/an** + one countable thing, first mention, or a job | *I have **a** bike. My sister is **an** engineer.* |
| **the** = we both know which one | *I went to **the** park near my house. **The** park was crowded.* |
| **the** + only one / superlatives | ***the** sun, **the** internet, **the** best, **the** most popular* |
| **No article** + general plural/uncountable things | *I like **∅** dogs. **∅** Money isn't everything. **∅** Education is important.* |
| **No article** + meals, most countries, cities, languages, sports | *have **∅** breakfast, in **∅** Bangladesh, speak **∅** Bangla, play **∅** football* |

### 6.2 Fixed phrases (learn them as chunks)

| With **the** | With **no** article |
|---|---|
| in **the** morning / afternoon / evening | at night, at home, at work |
| go to **the** cinema / market / gym | go to school / university / bed / work |
| play **the** guitar / piano | play cricket / football |
| on **the** internet, on **the** phone | by bus, by car, on foot |
| **the** same, **the** USA, **the** UK | watch TV, listen to music |
| at **the** weekend | last year, next week |

> **Quick test:** before any singular countable noun (*phone, friend, house, job*), you **must** have something in front: *a / the / my / this / one*. ❌ *I bought phone.* ✅ *I bought **a** phone.*

### 6.3 He/She mix-ups
Bangla uses **সে** for both men and women, so your brain doesn't separate them automatically.

| Fix | How |
|---|---|
| **Part 2 notes** | Write a big **HE** or **SHE** at the top of the card when it's about a person. |
| **Match the whole set** | **she – her – hers – herself** / **he – him – his – himself** |
| **Drill** | Describe your mother for 1 minute, then your father for 1 minute. Record it and count every wrong pronoun. |
| **Use the name** | If you feel unsure, repeat the name once: *"…and Rina, she's my cousin…"*. It resets your brain. |

Common mix-ups: ❌ *My mother… **he** is a teacher.* ❌ *My father loves **her** job.* ✅ *My father loves **his** job.*

## 7. Focus 5: Topic Vocabulary

### 7.1 How to learn vocabulary for Speaking
- **Learn chunks, not single words.** *Heavy traffic*, not "traffic". *A hectic schedule*, not "hectic".
- **Make it about YOU.** For every chunk, say one sentence about your own life. *"I have a hectic schedule these days because of IELTS."*
- **5–8 chunks a day** is enough. More than that, and you'll forget them.
- **Review** the chunks on Day 1, Day 3 and Day 7.
- **Say it, don't just read it.** A word you've only read won't come out under pressure.

### 7.2 What makes vocabulary "band 7"

| Feature | Band 6 | Band 7 |
|---|---|---|
| Precise words | *very good, very bad* | *beneficial, harmful, convenient, stressful* |
| Collocations | *do a mistake, big rain* | *make a mistake, heavy rain* |
| Idioms (a few, used correctly) | none | *once in a blue moon, not my cup of tea* |

## 8. Pronunciation (Quick Fixes)

You don't need a foreign accent. You need to be **clear**. These sounds are the usual problems for Bangla speakers:

| Sound | Practise | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| **v** (teeth on lip) vs **w** (round lips) | *very, village, video* / *west, water, wonderful* | "bhery", "wery" |
| **z** | *zero, zoo, easy, busy* | "jero", "eaji" |
| **s** vs **sh** | *sea / she, save / shave* | mixing them up |
| **s + consonant** at the start | *school, station, sport, speak* | "ishkul", "isteshon" |
| **th** | *think, three / this, mother* | "tink", "dis" (OK if clear, but try) |
| **Word endings** | *work**ed**, play**s**, friend**s*** | dropping the ending also sounds like a grammar mistake |
| **Word stress** | *PHO-to-graph, pho-TO-gra-phy, e-co-NO-mic* | flat stress on every syllable |

> **Intonation:** let your voice go up and down. A flat, monotone voice sounds memorised and keeps P at 6. Shadowing (Section 3.5) fixes this better than anything else.

## 10. Answer Frameworks

### Part 1: A + R + E (15–25 seconds per answer)
> **Q:** Do you prefer mornings or evenings?
> *"**I'd say** evenings, **definitely**. **The thing is,** I'm not really a morning person, so I feel more energetic later in the day. **For example,** I **do** most of my IELTS practice after 8 pm."*

### Part 2: The 1-minute notes plan
Don't write sentences. Write **keywords** in 5 boxes:

```
[ PAST / PRESENT ]          [ HE / SHE ]
1. What / who:  ...
2. When / where: ...
3. Details (cue-card points): ...
4. Story / example: ...
5. Feeling / why it matters: ...
```

**Talk structure (fill the 2 minutes):**
1. **Intro (10 sec):** *"I'd like to talk about…"*
2. **Cue-card points (60 sec):** answer each bullet point, adding a detail to each.
3. **A small story or example (30 sec):** *"I remember one time when…"*
4. **Feelings / why it's important (20 sec):** *"The reason this means so much to me is…"*
5. **If you still have time:** add more detail. **Never stop early.** Let the examiner stop you.

**How much content is enough for Part 2?**

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **2 minutes ≈ 220–260 words** (see 3.6) | About **4 ideas** fully developed. 10 ideas in one sentence each runs over time *and* sounds like a list. |
| **Depth beats breadth** | Develop each idea with details, feelings and one short story (the 6 layers in 4.4). This is what band 7 sounds like. |
| **Read the card carefully** | *"Describe **a** childhood memory"* = **one** story in depth. *"your childhood"* = 3–4 memories is fine. |
| **Save the extra ideas** | Put them in **Section 15 (My Story Bank)**, because each one becomes another cue card answer. |

### Part 3: O + R + E + C (30–45 seconds per answer)

| Question type | Opening phrase |
|---|---|
| Opinion | *I'd say… / Personally, I think…* |
| Compare (past vs now) | *Compared to the past, … has changed a lot. People used to… but now…* |
| Future | *I imagine that in the future… / It's likely that…* |
| Advantages / disadvantages | *The biggest benefit is… but on the downside…* |
| Why? | *There are a couple of reasons. Firstly… Another reason is…* |
| Not sure | *It's hard to say, but I suppose it depends on…* |

**Range of grammar to use naturally in Part 3:**
- *If* clauses: *If the government **invested** more in transport, traffic **would** improve.*
- Relative clauses: *People **who** live in cities…*
- Passive: *A lot of trees **have been cut down**.*
- Comparatives: *…much **more expensive than** it **used to** be.*

**Before you go in**
- [ ] Speak English for 15–20 minutes before the test (warm up your mouth and brain)
- [ ] Remember: **the examiner is not your enemy.** It's a conversation, not an interrogation.

**During the test**
- [ ] Part 1: **2–3 sentences** every time (A + R + E)
- [ ] Listen to the **tense of the question** and answer in the same tense
- [ ] Part 2: write **PAST/PRESENT** and **HE/SHE** on your notes
- [ ] Part 2: **don't stop** before the examiner says so
- [ ] Part 3: **opinion + reason + example + contrast**
- [ ] Forgot a word? **Explain around it.** Don't stop.
- [ ] Made a small mistake? Fix it once quickly or ignore it, and **keep going**
- [ ] He → **his**, she → **her**; my mother → **she**; people → **are**; everyone → **has**

**Never**
- [ ] ❌ Give memorised answers (examiners are trained to spot them and lower your score)
- [ ] ❌ Go silent for more than a few seconds
- [ ] ❌ Answer with just "Yes" or "No"

### 15.3 My 2-stage method

| Stage | What I do | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1: simple sentences** (now) | Write my ideas as a Bangla list → turn each idea into 1–3 **simple English sentences** → say them aloud | Build sentence making and **fluency** with correct basic grammar |
| **Stage 1.5: join** | Join 2 ideas with *and / but / so / because / when* | Make longer sentences without stopping |
| **Stage 2: cue card version** (later) | Pick 4 ideas → develop them in depth with the 6 layers (4.4) → about 270 words | Band 6.5–7 answers for specific cue cards and questions |

**How to practise a Stage 1 list:**
1. Read one idea, cover it, and **say it aloud** in your own words.
2. Check three things: **past tense** (*fought, rode, caught, hid*), **he/she**, and **a/the**.
3. Once single ideas feel easy, **join two ideas** (Stage 1.5).
4. Then **talk through the whole list** without looking. Aim for 2 minutes without a long pause.
5. When that feels comfortable, move to Stage 2.

### 15.4 Childhood: Stage 1 (simple sentences)

1. When I was a child, I **played** cricket almost every day after school.
2. On Fridays, my whole family **watched** a movie together. We all **enjoyed** those moments a lot.
3. I regularly **watched** my favourite cartoons, like *Tom and Jerry* and *Pokémon*.
4. I **was** very naughty. I often **fought** with my brother and sister over small things.
5. We **had** a power cut almost every day. During power cuts, we **played** chor-police in the dark. One person **was** the thief and **hid** somewhere. The others **were** the police, and they **tried** to find the thief.
6. In the summer holidays, I **used to** visit my grandparents' village. There **were** many fruit trees there, like mango, jackfruit and black plum. My cousins and I **climbed** the trees and **picked** the fruit.
7. On holidays, I often **rode** my bicycle with my friend for hours.
8. There **was** a river near my grandparents' house. My cousins and I often **went** swimming there.
9. In the rainy season, I **went** fishing with my youngest maternal uncle. We **caught** many kinds of fish.
10. Sometimes we **had** small picnics on our rooftop. We **organised** dancing, singing and other events. Everyone in the family **took** part.
11. At night, my maternal grandmother **told** us stories, usually ghost stories. We **were** scared, but we **enjoyed** them a lot.

**Irregular past verbs in this list:** fight → **fought** · ride → **rode** · catch → **caught** · hide → **hid** · take → **took** · go → **went** · tell → **told** · have → **had**

### 15.5 Childhood: Stage 2 (cue card model, *Describe your childhood memories*, notes: **PAST**)

Built from my Bangla ideas: **4 ideas in depth**, about 270 words, 2 minutes.

>
> *When I was a kid, I **used to** play cricket with the boys in my area almost every afternoon after school. To be honest, I was quite **naughty**, and I **would often** squabble with my brother and sister over silly things like the TV remote.*
>
> *Back then, we **used to** have **power cuts** almost every evening. **Whenever** the electricity went off, my cousins and I **would** play a game called chor-police in the dark. It's a bit like **hide-and-seek**: one person, the "thief", hides, and the others, the "police", have to find them. It was a little scary, but really exciting.*
>
> *My favourite memories, though, are from the summer holidays, when we **would** visit my **grandparents' village**. > *At night, my **maternal grandmother** **would** tell us ghost stories. We **were always getting** scared, but we never wanted her to stop!*
>
> ***Looking back**, I think those memories are so special because life was simple and the whole family spent time together. These days, children are busy with their phones, so they don't really have experiences like that **as often as we used to**."*

Notice: *used to* starts each new scene → *would* gives the details → *whenever*, *spent hours -ing* and *were always getting* add variety → it ends with **feelings + past vs now**.
