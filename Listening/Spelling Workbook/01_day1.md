## [Day 1] -ANT vs -ENT

### Learning objectives

By the end of today you will be able to:

- spell **43 common -ant / -ent words** used in IELTS Listening;
- choose the ending using **5 rules** instead of guessing;
- turn every adjective into its noun (**important → importance**), which prepares you for Day 2;
- understand why **pronunciation alone cannot tell you the ending**.

### Why your ear can't help you

> ⚠️ **-ant and -ent sound exactly the same.** Both are unstressed, so both are pronounced /ənt/ (or just /nt/).

| Word | IPA | Ending you hear | Ending you write |
|---|---|---|---|
| pleas**ant** | /ˈpleznt/ | /nt/ | **-ant** |
| pres**ent** | /ˈpreznt/ | /nt/ | **-ent** |
| dist**ant** | /ˈdɪstənt/ | /ənt/ | **-ant** |
| consist**ent** | /kənˈsɪstənt/ | /ənt/ | **-ent** |
| import**ant** | /ɪmˈpɔːtnt/ | /nt/ | **-ant** |
| pat**ient** | /ˈpeɪʃnt/ | /nt/ | **-ent** |

*Pleasant* and *present* almost rhyme, but one is -ant and the other is -ent. So **you need a rule, a word family or a memory trick.**

### The 5 rules

**Rule 1: Use the word family (the most reliable rule).**
If you know a related word ending in **-ance, -ancy or -ation**, write **-ant**. If you know one ending in **-ence or -ency**, write **-ent**.

| You know… | so you write… | You know… | so you write… |
|---|---|---|---|
| import**ance** | import**ant** | differ**ence** | differ**ent** |
| toler**ation** | toler**ant** | frequ**ency** | frequ**ent** |
| vac**ancy** | vac**ant** | effici**ency** | effici**ent** |
| hesit**ation** | hesit**ant** | intellig**ence** | intellig**ent** |

**Rule 2: Verbs ending in -ate or -y usually give -ant.**
toler**ate** → toler**ant** · domin**ate** → domin**ant** · hesit**ate** → hesit**ant** · particip**ate** → particip**ant** · migr**ate** → migr**ant** · vac**ate** → vac**ant**

appl**y** → applic**ant** · rel**y** → reli**ant** · compl**y** → compli**ant** · def**y** → defi**ant**

**Rule 3: A hard c or g sound before the ending gives -ant; a soft c or g sound gives -ent.**
English **c** and **g** sound soft (/s/ and /dʒ/) before **e**, and hard (/k/ and /ɡ/) before **a**. The ending keeps the sound you hear.

| Hard sound → -ant | Soft sound → -ent |
|---|---|
| signifi**c**ant /k/ | re**c**ent /s/ |
| appli**c**ant /k/ | inno**c**ent /s/ |
| va**c**ant /k/ | de**c**ent /s/ |
| ele**g**ant /ɡ/ ★ | intelli**g**ent /dʒ/ |
| arro**g**ant /ɡ/ | ur**g**ent /dʒ/ |
| extrava**g**ant /ɡ/ | dili**g**ent /dʒ/ ★ |

💡 *Significent* would be pronounced "signifi-SENT". The **a** keeps the /k/ sound.

**Rule 4: After *qu* and after *-id-*, write -ent.**
fre**qu**ent · conse**qu**ent · subse**qu**ent · elo**qu**ent

conf**id**ent · ev**id**ent · res**id**ent · pres**id**ent · acc**id**ent · inc**id**ent

**Rule 5: Verbs ending in *-ist* give -ent, except two.**
consist → consist**ent** · insist → insist**ent** · persist → persist**ent** · exist → (non-)exist**ent**

⚠️ **Exceptions:** ass**ist** → assist**ant**, res**ist** → resist**ant**.

💡 *"The **A**ssistant and the **R**esistant soldier are **A**nts."*

### Important exceptions and traps

- **People nouns are mixed.** Many end in -ant (*assistant, applicant, participant, accountant, consultant, tenant, servant, inhabitant, immigrant, attendant*), but some end in -ent (*student, resident, president, agent, opponent, client*). Learn these as a list.
- **Same sound, different word (British English):**
  - **dependant** (*n*, a person who depends on you, e.g. a child) / **dependent** (*adj*). *I have two dependants. She is dependent on her parents.*
  - **confidant** (*n*, a person you tell secrets to) / **confident** (*adj*, sure of yourself).
  - **currant** (*n*, a small dried fruit) / **current** (*adj*, happening now; *n*, a flow of water or electricity).
- **One word, two pronunciations:** **present** /ˈpreznt/ (*adj*, here now; *n*, a gift) and **present** /prɪˈzent/ (*v*, to show). The spelling stays the same.

### Word families: adjective → noun

You will use this table again tomorrow. **The letter never changes inside a family:** -ant gives -ance/-ancy, and -ent gives -ence/-ency.

| -ANT adjective | Noun | -ENT adjective | Noun |
|---|---|---|---|
| important | import**ance** | different | differ**ence** |
| significant | signific**ance** | independent | independ**ence** |
| distant | dist**ance** | confident | confid**ence** |
| resistant | resist**ance** | intelligent | intellig**ence** |
| relevant | relev**ance** | excellent | excell**ence** |
| brilliant | brilli**ance** | patient | pati**ence** |
| elegant | eleg**ance** | absent | abs**ence** |
| tolerant | toler**ance** | present | pres**ence** |
| dominant | domin**ance** | convenient | conveni**ence** |
| abundant | abund**ance** | competent | compet**ence** |
| reluctant | reluct**ance** | permanent | perman**ence** |
| vacant | vac**ancy** | frequent | frequ**ency** |
| hesitant | hesit**ancy** | efficient | effici**ency** |
| constant | const**ancy** | consistent | consist**ency** |
| assistant (*n*) | assist**ance** | urgent | urg**ency** |

### Common mistakes and corrections

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| importent | **important** | import**ance** → -ant (Rule 1) |
| independant | **independent** | No **a** anywhere after *ind-*: i-n-d-**e**-p-**e**-n-d-**e**-n-t |
| significent | **significant** | Hard /k/ sound → -ant (Rule 3) |
| inteligent | **intelligent** | Double **ll**, soft g → -ent |
| assistent | **assistant** | Exception to the *-ist* rule (Rule 5) |
| consistant | **consistent** | consist → -ent (Rule 5) |
| frequant | **frequent** | After *qu* → -ent (Rule 4) |
| elegent ★ | **elegant** | Hard /ɡ/ → -ant (Rule 3) |
| convienont ★ | **convenient** | con + **ven** + **i** + **ent** |
| deligent ★ | **diligent** | **di**-li-gent; soft g → -ent |

### Memory techniques

- 💡 **Hard sound → a, soft sound → e.** Say the letter before the ending: signifi**c**ant (/k/) → **a**; re**c**ent (/s/) → **e**.
- 💡 **"An Assistant, An Applicant and An Accountant are All Ants."** They work hard, like ants, and they all end in -ANT.
- 💡 **independent has no a.** Every vowel after *ind-* is **e**: ind**e**p**e**nd**e**nt.
- 💡 **per-MAN-ent.** Find the *man* in *permanent*, then add **-ent**.
- 💡 **EXCEL + LENT.** *Excellent* = *excel* + *lent*.
- 💡 **An ELEGANT ANT.** ★ Picture an ant in a suit.
- 💡 **con-VEN-I-ENT.** ★ Say all four syllables slowly when you write it.
- 💡 **Ask yourself "what's the noun?"** If you know *frequency*, *frequent* must end in -ent.
