## [Day 2] -ANCE vs -ENCE

### Learning objectives

By the end of today you will be able to:

- spell **42 common -ance / -ence nouns**;
- use yesterday's adjectives to choose the ending (**different → difference**);
- choose the ending from the **verb** (**appear → appearance**, **exist → existence**);
- tell apart sound-alike pairs that are a big IELTS trap: **patience / patients**, **assistance / assistants**.

### Why your ear can't help you (again)

> ⚠️ Unstressed **-ance** and **-ence** are both pronounced /əns/ or /ns/. The difference is only on paper.

| Word | IPA | Word | IPA |
|---|---|---|---|
| dist**ance** | /ˈdɪstəns/ | exist**ence** | /ɪɡˈzɪstəns/ |
| guid**ance** | /ˈɡaɪdns/ | evid**ence** | /ˈevɪdəns/ |
| perform**ance** | /pəˈfɔːməns/ | refer**ence** | /ˈrefrəns/ |

**When the ending is stressed, you *can* hear the a:** adv**ance** /ədˈvɑːns/, enh**ance** /ɪnˈhɑːns/, rom**ance** /rəʊˈmæns/, fin**ance** /ˈfaɪnæns/. These are easy. The difficult ones are the unstressed endings.

### The 5 rules

**Rule 1: Follow the Day 1 family.** The letter never changes inside a family.

- -**ant** → -**ance**: import**ant** → import**ance**, dist**ant** → dist**ance**, signific**ant** → signific**ance**
- -**ent** → -**ence**: differ**ent** → differ**ence**, confid**ent** → confid**ence**, independ**ent** → independ**ence**

**Rule 2: Learn the -ence verb groups. Most other verbs take -ance.**

| Verb ends in… | Noun ends in | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| stressed **-er / -ur** | **-ence** | ref**er** → reference · pref**er** → preference · conf**er** → conference · occ**ur** → occurrence |
| **-ist** | **-ence** | ex**ist** → existence · ins**ist** → insistence · pers**ist** → persistence ⚠️ *but* assist → assist**ance**, resist → resist**ance** |
| **-ere** | **-ence** | interf**ere** → interference · adh**ere** → adherence |
| **-ide** | **-ence** | res**ide** → residence · conf**ide** → confidence · coinc**ide** → coincidence ⚠️ *but* guide → guid**ance** |
| **-ge** (soft) | **-ence** | emer**ge** → emergence · conver**ge** → convergence |
| **most other verbs** | **-ance** | appear → appear**ance** · perform → perform**ance** · attend → attend**ance** · accept → accept**ance** · allow → allow**ance** · insure → insur**ance** · avoid → avoid**ance** · disturb → disturb**ance** · annoy → annoy**ance** |

**Rule 3: Hard c/g → -ance; soft c/g → -ence (same as Day 1).**
signifi**c**ance, ele**g**ance, extrava**g**ance (hard) · inno**c**ence, intelli**g**ence, ne**g**ligence, dili**g**ence (soft).

**Rule 4: -iance comes only from verbs ending in -y.**
var**y** → var**iance** · rel**y** → rel**iance** · appl**y** → appl**iance** · compl**y** → compl**iance** · all**y** → all**iance** · def**y** → def**iance**.
Every other "-ee-ens" ending is **-ience**: exper**ience** ★, conven**ience**, aud**ience**, sc**ience**, pat**ience**, obed**ience**.

**Rule 5: Watch the spelling changes when you add the ending.**

| Verb | Noun | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| maint**ai**n | maint**e**n**ance** | *ai* becomes *e*, and you add *-en-* |
| ent**er** | ent**r**ance | the *e* of *-er* goes |
| rememb**er** | rememb**r**ance | the *e* of *-er* goes |
| hind**er** | hind**r**ance | the *e* of *-er* goes |
| occ**ur** | occu**rr**ence | **double r** (stress stays on *-cur-*) |
| ref**er** | ref**e**rence | **single r** (stress moves to *RE-*) |
| pref**er** | pref**e**rence | **single r** (stress moves to *PRE-*) |
| exc**el** | exce**ll**ence | **double l** |
| obe**y** | obed**ience** | *y* becomes *-dience* |

### British English: -ce noun, -se verb

| Noun (-ce) | Verb (-se) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| licen**ce** | licen**se** | You need a driving **licence**. The shop is **licensed** to sell food. |
| practi**ce** | practi**se** | I need more **practice**. I **practise** every day. |
| advi**ce** /ədˈvaɪs/ | advi**se** /ədˈvaɪz/ | Can you give me some **advice**? I **advise** you to book early. |
| defen**ce**, offen**ce**, preten**ce** | (*AmE: defense, offense, pretense*) | Use the **-ce** spelling throughout. |

💡 **ICE is a noun, IS(E) is a verb.** *Advice* and *ice* are both nouns.

### The IELTS sound-alike trap: -ance/-ence or -ants/-ents?

These pairs are pronounced **exactly the same**. Only the grammar tells you which to write.

| Uncountable noun (a quality or idea) | Plural noun (people or things) | IPA (same for both) |
|---|---|---|
| **patience**: *Learning takes patience.* | **patients**: *The doctor sees 30 patients a day.* | /ˈpeɪʃns/ |
| **assistance**: *Do you need assistance?* | **assistants**: *We need two shop assistants.* | /əˈsɪstəns/ |
| **presence**: *Your presence is required.* | **presents**: *She opened her presents.* | /ˈprezns/ |
| **attendance**: *Attendance is compulsory.* | **attendants**: *Flight attendants serve meals.* | /əˈtendəns/ |
| **residence**: *a hall of residence* | **residents**: *Local residents complained.* | /ˈrezɪdəns/ |
| **innocence**: *He proved his innocence.* | **innocents**: *Many innocents died.* | /ˈɪnəsns/ |

💡 **Grammar test:** after a **number, *many*, *several*, *a few*, *these*** or before **are/were** → plural **-ants/-ents**. After ***a lot of, some, need, show, require*** with a singular verb → **-ance/-ence**.

### -ancy and -ency: the same rule

Like *-ance* and *-ence*, the **-ancy / -ency** endings follow the family.

| -ANCY | -ENCY |
|---|---|
| vac**ancy** (*a job vacancy*) | emerg**ency** (*emergency exit*) |
| accountancy (from *accountant*) | frequ**ency** (from *frequent*) |
| consult**ancy** (from *consultant*) | effici**ency** (from *efficient*) |
| pregn**ancy** | curr**ency** (*foreign currency*) |
| hesit**ancy** (from *hesitant*) | ag**ency** (*travel agency*) |
| const**ancy** (from *constant*) | flu**ency** (*IELTS: Fluency and Coherence*) |
| | profici**ency** (*English proficiency*) |
| | consist**ency**, tend**ency**, urg**ency** |

### Common mistakes and corrections

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| maintainance | **maintenance** | *ai* → *e*: MAIN + TEN + ANCE |
| occurence | **occurrence** | **cc** and **rr**: stress stays on *-cur-* |
| referrence | **reference** | single **r**: stress moves to *RE-* |
| existance | **existence** | exist → -ence (Rule 2) |
| independance | **independence** | still no *a* anywhere (Day 1) |
| attendence | **attendance** | attend a *dance* |
| apperance | **appearance** | appear + ance: keep *appear* whole |
| perfomance | **performance** | per + **form** + ance |
| enterance | **entrance** | enter loses its *e* |
| sentance ★ | **sentence** | three *e*'s and no *a* |
| experiance ★ | **experience** | -ience group (Rule 4), like *science* |

### Memory techniques

- 💡 **MAIN + TEN + ANCE.** The **main ten ants** do the **maintenance**.
- 💡 **atTEND a DANCE.** at + ten + **dance** = attendance.
- 💡 **perFORMance:** you fill in a **form** before a performance.
- 💡 **appEARance:** *appear* keeps its *ear*, then add *-ance*.
- 💡 **SCIENCE needs EXPERIENCE.** ★ Both end in *-ience*.
- 💡 **SENT-EN-CE.** ★ Three syllables, three *e*'s.
- 💡 **ocCURRence:** two **c**'s, two **r**'s. A *current* of two letters.
- 💡 **Is it a quality or a person?** Patience is a quality; patients are people.
