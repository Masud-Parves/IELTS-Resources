## 2. The 6 Tools

Every day uses some of these six tools. Learn them once here.

### Tool A: Mistake Log (5 min, after every test)
Write **every** wrong answer in the log (Section 10) and give it **one reason**:

| Code | Reason |
|---|---|
| **SP** | Spelling (I heard it but spelled it wrong) |
| **GR** | Grammar: plural -s, -ed, wrong word form |
| **WL** | Broke the word limit |
| **DIS** | Distractor: I wrote the first answer, but the speaker changed it |
| **LOST** | Lost my place and missed the answer |
| **HEAR** | Couldn't hear the word clearly |
| **VOC** | Didn't know the word |

At the end of each week, count the codes. **The biggest code is next week's focus.**

### Tool B: Sentence Dictation (15 min)
This trains your ear for fast, connected speech and also trains your spelling.

1. Choose **2–3 minutes** of Part 3 or Part 4 audio from a test you have done.
2. Play **one sentence**, then pause.
3. Write every word. You can replay it **up to 3 times**.
4. If a word is still unclear, write `____` and move on.
5. Check against the audioscript. Mark each mistake:
   - **Didn't hear it** → play that spot again 3 times while looking at the script
   - **Spelled it wrong** → add it to your spelling list
   - **Didn't know it** → learn it
6. Play the whole clip once more while reading the script.

> ⚠️ Stop after 15–20 minutes. Dictation is tiring, and a tired brain learns very little.

### Tool C: Spelling Practice (10 min)
Follow the routine in [IELTS_Spelling_Fix_List.md](../Writing/IELTS_Spelling_Fix_List.md) (Look, Say, Cover, Write, Check).
Each day: **3 words from the Listening Word Bank (Section 5) + 2 of your own mistakes**.

### Tool D: Transcript Method (20–30 min)
This is the best training for Part 3.

1. Do the section under test conditions and mark it.
2. Open the audioscript. For **every** question, highlight:
   - where the answer is
   - the **paraphrase** (the question says *"cost"*, the speaker says *"it's not cheap"*)
   - the **distractor** (the wrong answer that was mentioned first)
3. Play the audio again **with** the script.
4. Play it again **without** the script. You should now hear every answer.

### Tool E: Shadowing (5 min)
Play one sentence, pause, and **say it aloud**, copying the speed and the way words join together. Do 2–3 minutes of audio.
This teaches your brain the real speed of English, so fast speech stops surprising you.

### Tool F: Full Test (40 min + 10 min)
Strict exam conditions:
- No pausing, no replaying
- Write on an answer sheet in **CAPITAL LETTERS**
- **10 minutes to transfer** answers at the end (paper test)
- Then mark it and fill in the Mistake Log

> **Computer test?** Type your answers during the audio and keep only **2 minutes** at the end for checking. Practise the same way.

## 3. Part-by-Part Strategy

### Part 1: Conversation, form completion (aim for 9–10/10)
This is where spelling and numbers decide your score.

- **Spelled names:** practise the letters that sound alike:
  **A / E / I · G / J · B / P / V · M / N · S / F · Q / U**
- **"Double"** means write the letter twice: *"double L"* = LL.
- **Numbers:** **13 / 30 · 14 / 40 · 15 / 50**. In 13 the stress is at the end (thir-**TEEN**), and in 30 it is at the start (**THIR**-ty).
- **Corrections:** *"It's 15… oh no, sorry, 16."* The **last** answer counts.
- **Prices and dates:** write *£15.50* or *15 March*. Both *March 15* and *15 March* are accepted.

### Part 2: Monologue, maps and multiple choice (aim for 8/10)
- **Maps:** before the audio, find **where you are standing** (*"You are here"*) and the entrance. Learn these words: *opposite, next to, behind, in front of, at the end of, on the corner, to the left/right of, beyond, past, between.*
- Follow the speaker with your pencil on the map.

### Part 3: Discussion (aim for 6–7/10, now about 4–5)
**See Section 4 below: the full Part 3 system.**

### Part 4: Lecture, note completion (aim for 7–8/10)
- There is **no break** in the middle. Read all 10 questions before it starts.
- The notes follow the lecture in order. Use the **headings** as signposts.
- Listen for signpost phrases: *"Firstly…", "Moving on to…", "Another factor is…", "However…", "The main reason…", "To sum up…"*
- Usually **one word only**, and often an academic word, so spelling matters a lot here.

### Question types

| Type | How to do it |
|---|---|
| **Form / note completion** | Predict the word type (noun? number? plural?). Check the word limit. |
| **Multiple choice** | All 3 options are usually mentioned. Cross out each one as it is rejected. |
| **Choose TWO** | The two answers can come in any order, and the speakers may reject options between them. |
| **Matching** | The options list (A–G) is in random order; the questions are in order. Keep your eyes on the questions, not the options list. |
| **Map / diagram** | Find the starting point first. Follow with your pencil. |

## 4. The Part 3 System

Part 3 is not faster than the other parts. It **feels** faster because 2–3 people are discussing, changing their minds, and disagreeing. The answer is almost never the first idea you hear.

### Before the audio (use every second of reading time)
1. Underline **1–2 keywords** in each question.
2. Underline **who** the question is about: the student, the tutor, or both.
3. For multiple choice, think of other ways each option could be said.
4. Notice question words like *main*, *most*, *first*, *disagree*, *both*.

### While listening: the 2-question rule
**Keep your eyes on two questions: the current one and the next.**
If you hear the answer to the next question, you have missed the current one. **Leave it blank, move on, and guess at the end.**
Never chase a lost answer. That is how one missed answer becomes three.

### Listen for signal phrases (this is where answers change)

| Signal | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Rejecting an idea** | *I'm not sure about that · Actually… · That won't work because… · Yes, but… · The trouble is… · I don't think so · That's not really…* |
| **Agreeing / deciding** | *That's a good point · Exactly · Let's go with… · So we agree that… · I think you're right · OK, so that's settled* |
| **Changing mind** | *On second thought… · Actually, maybe we should… · Now that you mention it…* |
| **Emphasis (answer is coming)** | *The main problem was… · What really surprised me was… · The most important thing is…* |

### The 4 distractor traps

| Trap | Example |
|---|---|
| **Suggested then rejected** | *"We could use a survey." "Hmm, that would take too long. Interviews are better."* → interviews |
| **Past vs now** | *"We used to meet on Mondays, but now it's Thursdays."* → Thursdays |
| **Not X but Y** | *"It's not the cost that worries me, it's the time."* → time |
| **Correction** | *"The deadline is the 10th… no, the tutor changed it to the 12th."* → 12th |

### Part 3 training each day
Do one Part 3 with **Tool D (Transcript Method)**, and in the script, **highlight every signal phrase in a different colour**. After 10–15 Part 3s you will start to hear these phrases automatically.

## 5. Spelling for Listening

### The rules for answers
- Wrong spelling = **wrong answer**. No half marks.
- British and American spellings are both accepted (*colour / color*).
- **Write in CAPITAL LETTERS** on the paper test. This is allowed, it makes letters clear, and you never worry about capitals for names.
- **Plurals matter.** If you hear *"tables"*, writing *table* is wrong.
- Hyphenated words count as **one** word (*part-time*).
- Numbers can be written as digits (*15*, not *fifteen*).

### Your biggest spelling problem
In your own mistake list, **most errors are weak vowels**. You write the sound you hear, but in English the unstressed "uh" sound can be spelled *a, e, i, o* or *u* (*similer → similar, santance → sentence, grammer → grammar*).

**Fix: learn a "spelling voice."** Say every hard word in your head the way it is *spelled*, not the way it sounds:

| Word | Say it like this |
|---|---|
| Wednesday | **Wed-nes-day** |
| February | **Feb-ru-ar-y** |
| vegetable | **veg-e-ta-ble** |
| comfortable | **com-fort-a-ble** |
| temperature | **tem-per-a-ture** |
| library | **lib-rar-y** |
| separate | **sep-a-rate** (there is *a rat* in separate) |
| environment | **en-vi-ron-ment** |
| government | **gov-ern-ment** |
| restaurant | **res-tau-rant** |

### Listening-specific spelling rules

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Plural -es** after s, x, ch, sh, and some o-words | buses, boxes, matches, dishes, potatoes, tomatoes |
| **Consonant + y → ies** | facility → facil**ities**, activity → activ**ities**, library → librar**ies** |
| **f / fe → ves** | shelf → shel**ves**, knife → kni**ves**, life → li**ves** |
| **Double letters** | accommodation (cc, mm), committee (mm, tt, ee), address, questionnaire (nn), recommend (one c, mm), opportunity (pp), beginning (nn) |
| **i before e, except after c** | believe, field, achieve · but rec**ei**pt, rec**ei**ve, c**ei**ling |
| **Silent letters** | recei**p**t, **k**nowledge, **w**rite, i**s**land, Wedn**e**sday, colum**n** |

### Listening Word Bank (learn 3 a day)
These words appear again and again in IELTS Listening answers.

| Topic | Words |
|---|---|
| **Days & months** | Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday, February, August |
| **Places & buildings** | library, laboratory, museum, gallery, theatre, centre, restaurant, cafeteria, stadium, hospital, pharmacy, apartment, reception, entrance, corridor, basement, cathedral |
| **Accommodation & forms** | accommodation, address, surname, postcode, occupation, nationality, signature, application, reference, licence, insurance, deposit, receipt, guarantee |
| **Money** | payment, discount, refund, budget, salary, purchase, cheque, rent, fee, cash |
| **Travel** | journey, departure, arrival, luggage, passenger, vehicle, ferry, tunnel, destination, itinerary, reservation, schedule, souvenir, excursion, brochure, platform |
| **Study** | lecture, seminar, tutorial, assignment, dissertation, questionnaire, research, survey, statistics, bibliography, presentation, semester, campus, graduate, experiment, analysis |
| **Health** | medicine, nutrition, vitamin, protein, exercise, muscle, injury, allergy, diet, symptom, temperature |
| **Nature & environment** | environment, pollution, climate, habitat, species, conservation, recycling, wildlife, desert, glacier, volcano, drought, fertiliser, agriculture, coast, island, vegetation |
| **Science & history (Part 4)** | technology, equipment, frequency, oxygen, carbon, evidence, theory, architecture, archaeology, sculpture, colony, ancient, mineral, population |
| **Everyday hard words** | necessary, convenient, comfortable, available, beginner, colleague, committee, business, especially, recommend, opportunity, competition, separate, government |

## 8. The Mock Test Phase (after Week 3)

Use **unseen** tests only: Cambridge 13 or 14 (or 17–19 if you have them).

| Day | Task |
|---|---|
| **Mock 1** | Full test in exam conditions. If possible, do it at the same time of day as your real exam. |
| **Next day** | Transcript Method on lost sections only. Spelling review. |
| **Mock 2** | Full test. |
| **Next day** | Fix day. |
| **Mock 3** | Full test. |
| **Before the exam** | Light listening, read your Mistake Log, rest. **No new full test the day before the exam.** |

### Are you ready?

| Mock results | What it means |
|---|---|
| 2 of 3 mocks at **30+** | ✅ Ready for band 7 |
| Mocks at **28–29** | Close. Spend the remaining days only on your biggest log code. |
| Mocks below **27** | Look at the log: if most marks are SP/GR, do extra spelling; if most are LOST/DIS, do extra Part 3 transcript work. |

## 9. Exam Day Checklist

**Before each part:**
- [ ] Read ahead during every pause. Don't go back to the previous section.
- [ ] Underline keywords and the word limit.
- [ ] Predict the word type (noun, number, plural?).

**During the audio:**
- [ ] Answers come **in order**. Stay with the audio.
- [ ] Two-question rule in Part 3. If you're lost, skip and move on.
- [ ] The **last** answer counts when the speaker corrects themselves.

**Transfer / final check:**
- [ ] CAPITAL LETTERS (paper test).
- [ ] Every spelling, letter by letter.
- [ ] Plural -s and -ed endings.
- [ ] Word limit (*NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS*).
- [ ] **No blanks.** Guess every missing answer.
